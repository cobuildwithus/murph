Evening Screen Curfew is the family for experiments that reduce **personal screen use near bedtime or during the sleep window**.

The family should stay narrow. A screen curfew is not the same as all-day screen reduction, social-media detox, blue-light filters, Night Shift or f.lux-style tools, amber glasses, room-light redesign, CBT-I, sleep restriction, melatonin supplementation, shift-work scheduling, pediatric household media rules, or psychiatric virtual-darkness protocols. Those may be adjacent variants or clinical contexts, but they should not be collapsed into the Murph canonical Digital Sunset protocol.

The core question for this family is whether a time-bounded screen boundary changes bedtime behavior, arousal, notifications, light exposure, or sleep opportunity enough to produce a useful personal signal. The evidence is mixed and population-specific, so family pages should preserve null, mixed, safety-only, and population-mismatch findings rather than implying that screens are universally harmful.
