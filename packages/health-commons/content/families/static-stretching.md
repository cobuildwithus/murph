Static Stretching is the family for **held stretch training** where the primary outcome is flexibility or joint range of motion.

## What belongs here

Protocols belong in this family when the routine is mostly self-applied static holds: moving slowly into a position, holding still at mild tension or slight discomfort, and repeating enough exposure over days or weeks to test a matched ROM outcome.

## What stays separate

Keep these as sibling or adjacent variants unless a page explicitly changes scope: PNF or hold-relax, dynamic stretching, ballistic stretching, yoga or Pilates, foam rolling or myofascial release, eccentric or loaded mobility work, athletic warm-up routines, post-exercise recovery protocols, vascular-stretching programs, pain-treatment plans, pregnancy-specific plans, hypermobility care, and clinical rehabilitation.

Safety-only sources set boundaries rather than efficacy claims. Post-surgical or repaired tissue, pregnancy/postpartum contexts, symptomatic hypermobility or EDS/HSD, neurologic red flags, high fall-risk or floor-limited users, clinical pain patterns, severe post-exertion symptoms, and forceful assisted stretching should stay outside the ordinary wellness starter unless a separate guided variant is built.

## How to read the evidence

The direct family claim is narrower than common internet phrasing: static stretching can be a reasonable way to test measured ROM/flexibility. It should not be automatically promoted as injury prevention, pain treatment, posture correction, recovery, or performance enhancement.
