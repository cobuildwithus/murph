Added Sugar Reduction is the family for protocols that reduce added sugars or closely related free-sugar exposures while preserving adequate energy, carbohydrates, hydration, and food flexibility.

## Included

- Label-defined no-added-sugar experiments.
- Free-sugar reduction variants when the boundary is explicitly declared.
- Sugar-sweetened beverage reduction variants.
- Substitution-support variants when replacements are logged and not treated as equivalent.

## Not the same thing

This family is not ketogenic dieting, fasting, total-carbohydrate elimination, diabetes medication management, pediatric treatment, pregnancy nutrition care, NAFLD treatment, dental treatment, or a claim that non-sugar sweeteners are inherently safe or harmful.

## Safety boundary

Ordinary added-sugar-reduction wellness variants must preserve food access, adequate energy, carbohydrate availability, hydration, regular meals, and non-moralizing flexibility. Diabetes medication management, pregnancy or breastfeeding nutrition, pediatric feeding, eating-disorder care, frailty or long-term-care nutrition, high-training athlete fueling, kidney or other medical nutrition therapy, NAFLD treatment, cystic fibrosis nutrition, dental or periodontal treatment, ketogenic dieting, fasting, and total-carbohydrate restriction belong in separate clinician-guided or clinical variants.

## Current canonical protocol

- `protocol_variant:added-sugar-reduction/no-added-sugar-diet` — Murph’s default label-defined, 14-day baseline plus 21-day intervention self-experiment.

## Evidence posture

The strongest landing claim is about measuring and reducing added/free-sugar intake. Downstream weight, glucose, lipid, blood-pressure, dental, craving, taste, and long-term disease outcomes are mixed or adjacent and must stay source-scoped.
