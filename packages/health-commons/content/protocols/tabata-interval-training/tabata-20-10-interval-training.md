Tabata 20/10 Interval Training is a Murph canonical, conservative self-experiment built around one eight-round block of 20 seconds hard work followed by 10 seconds true rest. It is meant to test cardio-fitness trend and protocol tolerance, not to reproduce every detail of the original supervised cycle-ergometer literature or every public workout that uses the word “Tabata.”

## What to do

Use a modality you can dose and stop safely. For a first exposure, prefer a stationary bike, rower, elliptical, or another low-impact controlled setup. Attempt up to eight 20/10 rounds after a 5-10 minute warm-up, using true 10-second rests. Stopping early for symptoms, unsafe form, unsafe environment, or poor recovery is correct protocol execution, not failure.

## What counts as this protocol

This Murph protocol counts as the canonical starter version when the session uses 20 seconds of work and 10 seconds of true rest, aims for one eight-round block, logs completed rounds and modality, and avoids stacking extra high-intensity sets unless they are explicitly recorded as a different variant.

## What does not count

Do not silently merge this with 40/20 intervals, AMRAP or EMOM workouts, metcon finishers, CrossFit WODs, Wingate/SIT protocols, clinical rehabilitation HIIT, or marketing “four-minute fat-loss” workouts. Those may be useful adjacent records, but they are not the same protocol without dose, modality, intensity, volume, population, comparator, and outcome matching.

## How to interpret results

A useful result is not round completion at any cost. A better result is a tolerable six-week pattern: completed or safely stopped sessions, stable or improving recovery, no safety flags, and a plausible trend in wearable cardio-fitness or work capacity. If safety or recovery worsens, that outweighs the efficacy hypothesis.
