  relationship: root
  rationale: Default Murph alcohol-free challenge variant covering 7-, 14-, and 30-day complete-abstinence durations while keeping Dry January campaigns, alcohol reduction, intermittent abstinence, detox, AUD treatment, pregnancy, liver-disease treatment, and medication-supported care separate.
attribution:
  ownerType: murph
protocol:
  doseSignature: Alcohol-free · choose 7, 14, or 30 days · 7-day baseline preferred · daily adherence, symptom, craving, sleep, and social-context logs · end-of-challenge review and next-week plan
  target: Complete abstinence from alcoholic beverages for the selected calendar-day window.
  frequency:
    sessionsPerWeek: 7
  interventionSessionsMinimum: 6
  interventionSessionsTarget: 30
  steps:
    - "Complete safety screen; do not self-start if withdrawal, dependence, high/uncertain intake, pregnancy, liver, seizure, mental-health, medication, or substance risks exist."
    - "Choose duration: 7 days for starter check, 14 days for midpoint commitment, or 30 days for strongest challenge fit."
    - "Baseline 7 days when practical; log alcohol timing/amount, sleep, RHR/HRV, mood, cravings, social plans, caffeine, and meds."
    - "Set start/end dates; remove easy-access alcohol, plan social friction, and choose a non-moral lapse plan."
    - "During challenge, avoid alcoholic drinks; use no/low-alcohol products only if they do not trigger cravings or extra drinking occasions."
    - "Daily, log alcohol-free status; if drinking happens, record amount, context, symptoms, craving, and next-step decision."
    - "Days 1–7, watch for withdrawal-like symptoms: tremor, sweats, nausea, severe anxiety, racing heart, confusion, hallucinations, seizure, or unsafe feelings."

## Question this experiment answers

After a stable baseline, what changes when alcohol is removed for a defined **7-, 14-, or 30-day** window—and are those changes useful enough to guide your next drinking pattern?

## Simple version

1. Screen for safety first. This self-guided version is only for adults who can reasonably stop drinking without withdrawal risk. Possible dependence or loss of control, current or past withdrawal symptoms, high or uncertain intake, prior severe withdrawal, pregnancy/trying to conceive/breastfeeding questions, high-risk or complex medication use, known or suspected liver disease or abnormal liver tests, active AUD treatment/recent detox, unstable mental health, self-harm risk, co-occurring substance use, seizure disorder, or significant cardiovascular/medical instability means clinician-guided care first.
2. Choose one duration: **7 days** for a starter check, **14 days** for a midpoint commitment, or **30 days** for the best-evidenced full challenge.
3. Log a 7-day baseline if practical. During the challenge, log each calendar day as alcohol-free or not.
4. Watch the first week carefully for withdrawal-like symptoms, sleep disruption, mood changes, and cravings.
5. At the end, review what changed, what did not, what felt hard, and what you want the next week to look like.

## What to measure

Use a layered plan. Everyone logs adherence, any drinks, symptoms, cravings, mood, sleep notes, social friction, and replacement behaviors. If a wearable is already available, compare baseline versus intervention trends for resting heart rate, HRV/RMSSD, sleep timing, sleep efficiency, and sleep-onset context. If you already use a home cuff, morning blood pressure can be useful. For a 30-day version, optional labs such as GGT/liver enzymes, liver biochemistry, HOMA/insulin-resistance-style markers, lipids, blood pressure/heart-rate/weight, or selected glucose markers can be reviewed, but they should not be treated as disease treatment or guaranteed improvement; fasting-glucose signals from a tiny 7-day pilot are exploratory only.

## How to read the result

Treat the outcome as a decision aid, not a pass/fail score. A useful result might be fewer alcohol days, better sleep continuity, lower resting heart rate, easier social planning, better refusal confidence, or a clear realization that cravings, stress, or social friction need more support. A null or mixed result is also useful: it may mean the duration was too short, the baseline was unstable, the main outcome was social rather than biometric, or this is better handled with clinician-guided support.

## Off-ramp

Before the last day, choose the next-week plan: continue abstinence, resume with a lower-risk target, return to usual drinking while tracking, or seek clinician-guided help. Do not use the final day as a reward-drinking trigger. If you drank during the challenge, log amount, context, symptoms, craving, and next-step decision without shame; do not make up for it with bingeing, extra restriction, medication changes, or unsafe substitution, and route to clinician/urgent care if the event involved symptom relief, binge/loss of control, unsafe behavior, severe craving, medication interaction, or withdrawal-like symptoms.

## Not this protocol

This page is not medical detoxification, AUD treatment, medication-supported alcohol treatment, liver-disease care, pregnancy counseling, adolescent care, medication-interaction management, or a claim that everyone should stop drinking. It is a bounded Murph self-experiment for appropriately screened adults who want to test a temporary alcohol-free interval and can reasonably stop drinking without withdrawal risk.
