protocol:
  doseSignature: daily oral preformed EPA/DHA · exact EPA+DHA logged · 14-day baseline + 12-week intervention
  target: Stable product and actual labeled EPA mg/day + DHA mg/day, chosen before the run and kept fixed
  frequency:
    sessionsPerWeek: 7
    sessionsPerDay: 1
  interventionSessionsMinimum: 56
  interventionSessionsTarget: 84
  steps:
  - Pick one preformed EPA/DHA product, such as fish oil or algal EPA/DHA, and read the Supplement Facts or product label before starting; do not use cod liver oil, krill oil, DHA-only products, prescription products, or multi-gram/high-dose therapy as the starter variant.
  - Record the brand, product name, source type, formulation if known, serving size, EPA mg, DHA mg, total EPA+DHA per planned day, capsule or serving count, batch/lot if available, expiration date, storage conditions, and any third-party testing or certificate-of-analysis information.
  - 'Complete the safety screen before starting or increasing intake: age under 18, pregnancy/trying to conceive/lactation, fish/shellfish or ingredient hypersensitivity, antithrombotic or regular NSAID use, bleeding disorder or abnormal bruising/bleeding, planned procedures, atrial fibrillation/flutter or unexplained palpitations/syncope, severe hypertriglyceridemia or pancreatitis history, liver disease/elevated ALT/AST, LDL-C requiring monitoring, gout, immunosuppression, prescription/high-dose omega-3 use, and clinician-managed lipid or cardiovascular care.'
  - Run a 14-day baseline without starting the supplement. Log seafood servings and type, high-mercury fish exposure, ALA-rich foods, other omega-3 sources, medications/supplements, symptoms, and baseline labs if using omega-3 index/RBC EPA+DHA or a fasting lipid panel.
  - Start the planned daily serving and keep product, dose, timing, whether taken with food/fat, background seafood intake, diet pattern, alcohol, weight-change efforts, exercise load, and other supplements as stable as reasonably possible for 12 weeks.
  - Log each day whether the serving was taken, missed, doubled, or changed; total EPA+DHA taken; dose time; whether it was taken with food/fat; GI or fishy-taste symptoms; palpitations or irregular heartbeat; dizziness/syncope; unusual bleeding/bruising; nosebleeds/gum bleeding/heavier menstrual bleeding; allergic symptoms; gout flares or edema; and major diet/medication/supplement changes.
  - Retest with the same lab and sample matrix after the intervention window. Treat omega-3 index/RBC EPA+DHA as an exposure/status endpoint and lipid results as clinical-context information, not proof of disease-risk reduction or justification to change prescribed therapy.
  tips:
  - One product, not stacked — keeps dose and source interpretable.
  - Save a label photo with exact EPA and DHA mg; capsule count alone is not enough.
  - Same meal pattern each day if taking with food.
  - Swallow intact per product instructions — do not crush, chew, or open unless labeled safe.
  - Keep seafood intake stable unless the experiment is explicitly about changing it.
  - Prefer products with transparent third-party quality testing.
  keepInMind:
  - This protocol is a measurable-status and lipid-context experiment, not a cardiovascular-event-prevention or disease-treatment claim.
  - EPA and DHA amounts, source, and formulation should not be collapsed into “fish oil” without logging details.
  - Omega-3 index/RBC EPA+DHA, whole blood, and plasma measures are not interchangeable; use the same matrix and method for before/after comparisons.
  - Triglycerides are the most consistent lipid signal, but LDL-C, HDL-C, non-HDL-C, ApoB, ALT/AST, and medication context can require clinical interpretation when abnormal.
  - Prescription omega-3 products, multi-gram high-dose therapy, cod liver oil, krill oil, ALA foods, pregnancy/lactation protocols, pediatric use, severe hypertriglyceridemia/pancreatitis care, arrhythmia/bleeding-risk pathways, allergy management, liver/LDL monitoring, gout, and immunosuppressed or immunosuppressive-therapy contexts are separate boundaries.

  stopConditions:
  - Stop immediately and seek urgent medical help for hives or rash with swelling, swelling of lips/tongue/throat, wheezing, trouble breathing, chest pain, fainting, severe shortness of breath, stroke-like symptoms, or a rapid/irregular heartbeat with weakness, dizziness, or near-syncope.
  - Stop immediately and seek urgent medical help for vomiting blood, coughing blood, black/tarry stools, red blood in stool or urine, uncontrolled bleeding, severe headache or neurologic symptoms after head injury, or any severe bleeding symptom.
  - Stop and contact a clinician promptly for new or worsening palpitations, irregular heartbeat, diagnosed AF/flutter, recurrent unexplained dizziness, unusual bruising, prolonged bleeding from cuts, recurrent nosebleeds, bleeding gums, heavier-than-usual menstrual bleeding, or any bleeding change while using anticoagulants, antiplatelets, regular NSAIDs, or bleeding-relevant supplements.
  - Stop and contact a clinician promptly for repeated vomiting, severe diarrhea, severe reflux/heartburn, severe abdominal pain, inability to tolerate the product, new rash/urticaria, gout flare, marked joint swelling, new edema, dark urine, jaundice, or clinician-directed abnormal labs such as rising ALT/AST or LDL-C.
  - Pause before surgery, dental extraction, endoscopy, biopsy, or any invasive procedure unless the treating clinician says to continue; also pause when starting or increasing anticoagulants, antiplatelets, regular NSAIDs, or bleeding-relevant supplements, or when pregnancy is discovered or breastfeeding begins.
  - Do not stop, replace, or reduce prescribed medications because of this protocol; medication changes belong with the prescribing clinician.

    unit: mg/dL

  biomarkerKey: biomarker:omega-3-index

whyItWorks:
  - "## EPA and DHA replace other fatty acids in cell membranes\n\nPreformed EPA and DHA enter red-blood-cell membranes over weeks of consistent intake, raising omega-3 index proportionally to dose, duration, and baseline status. A 12-week window with same-matrix retesting separates real tissue change from acute plasma noise."
  - "## Triglycerides fall when liver export slows\n\nEPA and DHA reduce hepatic VLDL assembly and triglyceride secretion. Fasting triglycerides are the most consistent lipid signal, especially from an elevated baseline. LDL-C and non-HDL-C move in different directions depending on dose, EPA/DHA ratio, and starting phenotype."
  - "## Biomarker movement is not disease prevention\n\nRising omega-3 index and falling triglycerides confirm exposure and metabolic shift. They do not establish cardiovascular-event reduction, mood improvement, dry-eye relief, or inflammation control in a self-experiment."

## Question this experiment answers

Can a stable oral preformed EPA/DHA supplement, taken daily and logged precisely, change your measured omega-3 status and/or lipid-panel context over a 14-day baseline plus 12-week intervention window?

## Simple version

Pick one EPA/DHA product, write down the actual EPA and DHA amounts, screen for safety boundaries, hold diet and other supplements as stable as possible, and retest with the same lab method after the intervention. Fasting triglycerides are the clearest downstream lipid signal; omega-3 index or erythrocyte EPA+DHA is a status check that the product and dose changed tissue fatty-acid exposure.

## What to watch

Prioritize a fasting lipid-panel comparison: triglycerides can fall when EPA/DHA reduces liver triglyceride output, while LDL-C and non-HDL-C show whether particle remodeling helped or offset the lipid-panel signal. Use omega-3 index or RBC EPA+DHA as a body-status readout, not as proof of cardiovascular, mood, cognition, dry-eye, recovery, soreness, or inflammation benefit.

## What not to claim

Do not market this as proof of heart-attack or stroke prevention, treatment for depression or anxiety, dry-eye treatment, athletic recovery therapy, or a substitute for clinician-managed lipid care. Do not blend this with seafood, ALA/flax/chia, krill oil, cod liver oil, prescription products, pregnancy/lactation/pediatric protocols, allergy management, arrhythmia or bleeding-risk pathways, gout, immunosuppression, or clinical nutrition unless a separate variant is created.

## Safety posture

This is a moderate-caution supplement protocol. The starter version is intended for low-risk adults who are not on antithrombotic therapy or regular NSAIDs, do not have bleeding/procedure or rhythm-history flags, are not pregnant/trying to conceive/lactating, are not under 18, do not have fish/shellfish allergy or product-hypersensitivity concerns, and are not using high-dose or prescription omega-3 therapy. Liver/LDL monitoring, gout, immunosuppression, severe hypertriglyceridemia, pancreatitis history, and clinician-managed cardiovascular or lipid care route out of the starter variant. Safety screening takes priority over efficacy when the evidence is uncertain.

## Evidence snapshot

The direct evidence supports EPA/DHA exposure/status measurement and a triglyceride-focused lipid signal. The broader landscape is mixed: cardiovascular event prevention, mood/cognition, dry-eye, recovery, and inflammation outcomes are either population-specific, heterogeneous, null, or adjacent to this protocol rather than default claims. The evidence section preserves those distinctions without turning adjacent sources into default protocol claims.
