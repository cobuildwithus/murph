Burnout is what chronic workplace stress produces: exhaustion, cynicism, and reduced effectiveness. Recovery usually takes two things at once, **less exposure to the conditions that caused it and a gradual rebuilding of capacity**. Rest alone is a short refill if the work stays the same.

Treat low capacity as real. A depleted person often cannot run a wellness schedule, make a big career decision, or "catch up" during leave.

## What to do

- **Reduce load before adding goals.** Work out what can stop, wait, move to someone else, or shrink.
- **Restore the physical floor.** Protect sleep opportunity, regular food, prescribed care, daylight, gentle movement, and time away from work. These are foundations, not proof that bad habits caused the burnout.
- **Create real psychological detachment.** Write a shutdown note, take work apps off personal devices where you can, and decide who handles true emergencies.
- **Name the workplace change required.** Fewer cases, clearer priorities, a different supervisor, schedule changes, protected leave, role redesign, or eventually a different job. "Manage time better" is not a change.
- **Rebuild activity gradually.** Start with short, low-cost activities that bring pleasure, competence, or connection. Add duration once energy is stable, not on the first good day.
- **Stay connected.** Burnout brings withdrawal and shame. Tell at least one trusted person what is happening and what help would be useful.
- **Plan the return, not just the break.** On leave or reduced duties, agree on hours, responsibilities, check-ins, and what happens if symptoms worsen.

## A simple plan

Use three phases rather than a fixed countdown.

**Phase 1: stabilize.** For one or two weeks, keep a consistent sleep and meal rhythm, get brief outdoor movement most days, and remove one source of work intrusion outside agreed hours. List your current demands and mark each one stop, reduce, delegate, or keep. If even this floor is out of reach, get more support rather than adding self-improvement tasks.

**Phase 2: rebuild.** Pick one low-pressure activity in each of three areas: body, connection, and competence. A 15-minute walk, coffee with a friend, and one finished household task would do. Repeat them at a level that leaves some energy afterward. Increase only one thing per week: frequency, duration, or difficulty.

**Phase 3: redesign work.** Write down the top three conditions that fed the burnout and a concrete protection for each. "Too much work" becomes a weekly priority decision with a manager. "Always available" becomes an on-call rule. "No control" becomes defined decision rights. Decide which protections must exist before you take on the previous load.

Review weekly: Is energy improving? Can I detach from work? Is cynicism easing? Are the work conditions actually changing?

## How to know it is working

Recovery often shows up outside work first: enjoying a conversation, finishing basic tasks with energy to spare, sleeping without replaying the day, feeling curious again. Later, work feels more proportionate, and you can be effective without constant urgency or stretching every day.

A better day does not prove full capacity is back, and a hard day is not failure. Track the weekly pattern of usable energy, detachment, and functioning.

## If you get stuck

If rest is not helping, look for ongoing work contact, caregiving load, financial stress, sleep disruption, substances, pain, or an untreated health condition. If the organization will not change the conditions, you may need occupational health, leave, formal accommodations, a union, or a job change.

Burnout can look like or sit alongside depression, anxiety, sleep disorders, anemia, thyroid problems, and other medical conditions. Get a clinical evaluation when exhaustion is severe, spreads across every part of life, or comes with major changes in sleep, appetite, concentration, or hope.

## A quick note

This plan should lighten the load, not turn recovery into another performance project. If you feel unsafe, are thinking about self-harm, or cannot manage basic daily needs, get prompt local professional or crisis support.
