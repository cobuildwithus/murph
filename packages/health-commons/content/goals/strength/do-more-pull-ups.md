More pull-ups require both a stronger single repetition and the endurance to repeat it. Testing a maximum set every session usually turns practice into fatigue. Most training should use sets that end before technique changes, with enough weekly repetitions to improve the pattern.

Define your pull-up: grip, starting hang, chin height, and whether kipping is allowed. A strict overhand pull-up, a neutral-grip pull-up, and a chin-up are related but distinct skills. Use one as the anchor so the count means the same thing from month to month.

## What to do

- Practice pull-ups two to four times per week depending on current ability and total training.
- Keep most sets at roughly half to two-thirds of your current maximum.
- End sets before swinging, shortened range, or slow grinding takes over.
- Build maximal strength with weighted pull-ups or harder low-repetition variations when you can already do several clean repetitions.
- Use assisted pull-ups to add quality volume after unassisted sets.
- Train rows and elbow flexors while managing total shoulder and elbow load.

If your maximum is two or three, use many small sets of one or two plus assisted work. If your maximum is 15, larger submaximal sets and occasional added weight may be more useful. The plan should reflect the current ceiling.

## A simple plan

Test one clean maximum after a warm-up. On three weekly sessions, perform five sets at about half that number. For a maximum of eight, start with sets of four. Rest two minutes or longer. Add one repetition to one set at a time until all sets increase.

On the first session, add two heavier sets using a small amount of weight or a harder variation for 3 to 5 repetitions. On the third session, add two assisted sets of 6 to 10 after the unassisted work. Keep the middle session simple and stop while fresh.

Retest after four weeks following a lighter day. If the maximum rises, reset the training sets from the new number. If it does not but total clean volume rose, continue another block rather than changing everything. Strength and endurance do not always peak on the same day.

Use assistance to keep quality high on days when unassisted repetitions fall below the plan. For example, complete the planned clean unassisted sets, then use a machine or band for the remaining volume. This is usually more useful than turning the final repetitions into short-range swings. During a high-volume block, rotate grips only if each remains comfortable and track the anchor grip separately.

Schedule a lighter week after several hard weeks by cutting sets in half. Elbows and shoulders may benefit even when the muscles feel ready for more.

## How to know it is working

Track total clean repetitions across the same number of sets, added weight on a low-repetition set, and the standardized maximum. A rising total with stable form is a strong leading signal. Note grip and range because small changes can alter the result.

Do not treat body weight as the only explanation. It changes relative strength, but pulling strength can improve at any size. Measure what you can act on: training volume, assistance, load, and recovery.

## If you get stuck

If the first repetitions feel strong but endurance collapses, add assisted back-off volume. If every repetition is slow, build maximal strength with short weighted or low-assistance sets. If grip fails first, train carries or hangs, but do not layer them onto already exhausted elbows every day.

Pull-ups overlap with climbing, rows, pulldowns, curls, and manual work. Persistent elbow irritation often means total pulling and gripping volume is too high. Reduce the aggravating work and rebuild. More frequency helps only when each exposure is small enough to recover from.

## A quick note

Use a secure bar and avoid dropping from a height onto a cluttered or hard surface. Sudden shoulder or elbow injury, marked weakness, or persistent numbness is a reason to stop. A lower count with full control is more useful than an inflated number built on a changing standard.
