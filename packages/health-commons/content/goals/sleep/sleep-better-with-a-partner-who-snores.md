A partner's snoring affects two people's sleep, but it may also be a sign of obstructive sleep apnea for the person making the sound. A good plan protects your sleep tonight and turns repeated loud snoring, gasping, or breathing pauses into a calm health conversation—not a nightly cycle of nudging and resentment.

## What to do

- Talk during the day, not at 2 a.m. Describe what you hear and how often it disrupts sleep without blaming the person for something happening while unconscious.
- Notice breathing pauses, choking, gasping, long silent gaps, morning headaches, and excessive daytime sleepiness. These strengthen the case for a sleep evaluation.
- Encourage side sleeping when snoring is clearly position dependent. Use a comfortable positional aid rather than repeated elbowing.
- Reduce shared late alcohol when it worsens snoring. The partner can also address nasal congestion and smoking where relevant.
- Protect your side with well-fitted earplugs and a low, steady masking sound, while keeping emergency and wake alarms detectable.
- Agree on a temporary backup sleep location for severe nights or high-stakes mornings. Planned separate sleep can protect the relationship better than an exhausted argument.

## A simple plan

For one week, note whether snoring was mild, disruptive, or included concerning pauses. A few short audio clips can help the partner understand the pattern, but do not record every night or post intimate recordings to a commercial service without consent.

Have one daytime conversation using a shared goal: “I want both of us to sleep well, and I noticed pauses that may be worth checking.” If symptoms suggest apnea, help arrange a primary-care or sleep appointment. Offer the observations without trying to diagnose severity.

While waiting, test side sleeping, less late alcohol, and appropriate congestion care. Set up your earplugs and sound masking before bed. Decide in advance when either person can move to the backup room without interpreting it as rejection.

## How to know it is working

Your awakenings decrease, both people feel more rested, and the snoring partner completes evaluation when indicated. A lower-volume night is useful but does not prove apnea is absent or controlled.

Phone snoring scores can show a rough pattern but cannot reliably distinguish simple snoring from airway obstruction. If the partner starts treatment, follow clinical data and symptoms, not only your report of sound.

## If you get stuck

If the partner feels criticized, focus on observable facts and your own sleep need. Invite them to listen to a short consensual recording and choose the next step. If access to care is slow, primary care can often start screening and order appropriate testing.

Avoid mouth taping, random anti-snoring gadgets, and unmonitored boil-and-bite devices. A fitted oral appliance can be useful for selected people, but it should be provided by a qualified dentist and its effect on apnea should be confirmed.

If resentment has become the main problem, treat sleep logistics and the relationship conversation separately. A temporary bedroom plan is a practical accommodation, not a verdict on intimacy.

## A quick note

Breathing pauses, choking, severe daytime sleepiness, drowsy driving, or significant heart and blood-pressure conditions make evaluation more urgent. If the partner stops breathing for a prolonged period or has acute distress, seek emergency help.
