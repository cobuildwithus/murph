This source is included for **Alcohol-reduction comparator reviews and public-health guideline context**.

**Findings:** The Cochrane review included 69 studies with 33,642 participants. At one year, brief intervention reduced alcohol consumption by about 20 g/week versus minimal or no intervention (95% CI 12 to 28 lower). Effects on binges, drinking days, and drinking intensity were small or unclear. Extended intervention did not clearly outperform brief intervention.

**Why it matters:** Most directly useful comparator review in this batch for primary-care brief alcohol reduction and safety-reporting gaps.

**Potential experiment signals:** weekly alcohol intake, binge episodes, drinking days, adverse events.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as high-priority adjacent comparator evidence and safety-reporting context; do not label it direct abstinence evidence.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness:** `adjacent_variant`.

**Population mismatch:** Primary-care brief intervention is an adjacent alcohol-reduction intervention, not a self-directed abstinence challenge.

**Limitations and safety notes:** Moderate-quality evidence for consumption reduction with substantial heterogeneity; adverse-event reporting was sparse. Only five studies reported adverse effects; two reported none, one reported increased binge drinking in women, and two reported equivalent driving-related adverse events.
