This source is included for **Direct protocol and duration evidence**.

**Findings:**
- `finding:pmid-30016350-vagal-tone-moderates-craving` — The extracted source reported that craving increased across the day and decreased after drinking, and that respiratory-sinus-arrhythmia patterns moderated craving trajectories.
- `finding:pmid-30016350-not-wearable-efficacy` — This source is mechanistic/autonomic context and should not be used as evidence that consumer wearable HRV improves or validates a short-term alcohol-free challenge.

**Why it matters:** Adds mechanistic evidence that autonomic regulation may moderate craving during brief abstinence, relevant to optional HRV/autonomic hypotheses but not direct wearable validation.

**Potential experiment signals:** alcohol craving, respiratory sinus arrhythmia, cardiac vagal tone, HRV hypothesis, time-of-day craving.

**Protocol takeaway:** Cardiac vagal function moderated daily craving patterns during brief abstinence/usual-drinking windows; use only as mechanistic measurement context.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Limitations and population mismatch:** n=29; mechanistic and exploratory; not a consumer wearable HRV validation study; only three-day windows; no health-outcome efficacy test. Population mismatch: Moderate-heavy consumers in an autonomic lab/EMA study may not represent broad challenge users or high-risk withdrawal populations.
