This source is included for **Alcohol-reduction comparator reviews and public-health guideline context**.

**Findings:** Thirty-four studies were included. All studies reported significant alcohol reductions at 3 months after brief intervention, but between-group effects were inconsistent, and most studies did not find significant between-group differences at 6 or 12 months. The review reported lower alcohol-related injury at 6 or 12 months for intervention recipients but little evidence for reduced subsequent hospitalizations.

**Why it matters:** Preserves mixed setting-specific evidence and helps avoid overstating brief-intervention effects across all care settings.

**Potential experiment signals:** weekly alcohol intake, alcohol-related injury, risky driving, health-care utilization.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as mixed clinical setting context and safety-boundary evidence, not as direct abstinence-challenge support.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness:** `clinical_supervised`.

**Population mismatch:** Emergency-department alcohol misuse populations differ from self-selected users trying a short alcohol-free challenge.

**Limitations and safety notes:** Emergency-department studies may show assessment effects or regression to the mean, and longer-term between-group differences were often absent. Alcohol-related injury and risky driving outcomes were considered; adverse events from the intervention itself were not extracted.
