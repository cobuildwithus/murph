This source is included for **Sleep, autonomic, wearable, and acute-alcohol measurement context**.

**Findings:** Alcohol intake was associated with dose-dependent increases in nocturnal RHR, lower HRV, shorter sleep, and lower next-day physical activity; one-drink-more versus one-drink-less contrasts increased RHR by about 2.8 bpm in females and 2.4 bpm in males and reduced HRV by about 3.8 ms in females and 3.3 ms in males.

**Why it matters:** Largest wearable-context source in the batch, directly linking alcohol intake to RHR, HRV, sleep duration, and next-day activity.

**Potential experiment signals:** nocturnal resting heart rate, HRV, sleep duration, next-day physical activity, sex and age modifiers.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as high-priority observational wearable context; do not infer causality or guarantee individual response.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness:** `measurement_context`.

**Population mismatch:** Alcohol-exposure days in a wearable cohort are not a randomized abstinence challenge.

**Limitations and safety notes:** Retrospective observational design; self-reported alcohol; proprietary wearable context; residual confounding possible. No adverse events were extracted; physiologic and behavior disruption after alcohol was observational.
