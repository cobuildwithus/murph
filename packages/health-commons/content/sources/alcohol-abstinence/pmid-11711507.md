This source is included for **Alcohol-reduction comparator reviews and public-health guideline context**.

**Findings:** Fifteen randomized trials with 2,234 participants reported pooled reductions of about 3.31 mmHg systolic blood pressure and 2.04 mmHg diastolic blood pressure, with a dose-response pattern and larger effects among participants with higher baseline blood pressure.

**Why it matters:** Provides mechanistic and clinical context that reducing alcohol intake can lower blood pressure, especially in higher baseline BP or heavier alcohol contexts.

**Potential experiment signals:** morning blood pressure, weekly alcohol intake, baseline blood pressure subgroup.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use for blood-pressure endpoint rationale and dose-response context, not as direct evidence for 7-, 14-, or 30-day alcohol-free challenge efficacy.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness:** `same_mechanism`.

**Population mismatch:** Trials of alcohol reduction among drinkers differ from voluntary short abstinence challenges and may include heavier-drinking or hypertensive populations.

**Limitations and safety notes:** Alcohol-reduction dose and baseline drinking level matter; this meta-analysis does not test complete short-term abstinence or Murph-style challenge adherence. Adverse events were not extracted from the accessible abstract.
