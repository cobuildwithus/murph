This source is included for **Direct protocol and duration evidence**.

**Findings:**
- `finding:pmid-39489405-feasible-safe-perceived-benefits` — The extracted report described the 30-day challenge as feasible and safe, with no serious adverse events; about two thirds completed successful 30-day abstinence and 94.1% reported at least one perceived health benefit.
- `finding:pmid-39489405-pain-no-significant-change` — Pain intensity did not significantly change in the extracted report, so this source should not be used to claim that a 30-day alcohol-free challenge improves pain.

**Why it matters:** Closest direct 30-day challenge source, with explicit feasibility, safety, perceived benefits, sleep, fatigue, self-efficacy, and pain endpoints in heavier drinkers.

**Potential experiment signals:** completion, adverse events, sleep, fatigue, withdrawal symptoms, alcohol abstinence self-efficacy, pain intensity, perceived benefits.

**Protocol takeaway:** A supported 30-day challenge was feasible and had no serious adverse events in a small heavy-drinking sample, with perceived benefits and sleep/fatigue/self-efficacy signals; pain intensity did not significantly change.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

**Limitations and population mismatch:** Small single-arm study; substantial support via motivational interviewing and weekly calls; heavy-drinking and chronic-pain-enriched population; perceived benefits are not blinded outcomes; no control arm. Population mismatch: Chronic-pain and heavy-drinking participants may have different risks, motivations, and benefits than lower-risk self-guided Murph users.
