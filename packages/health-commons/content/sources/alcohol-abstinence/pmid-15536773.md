This source is included for **Alcohol-reduction comparator trials, digital interventions, and liver-focused protocols**.

**Findings:**
- `finding:pmid-15536773-heavy-drinking-reduced` — The intervention group’s proportion of heavy drinkers decreased from 35.8% at baseline to 24.7% at 12 months, with no corresponding control-group change reported in the extracted abstract material.
- `finding:pmid-15536773-cdt-marker-improved` — %CDT decreased in both groups, with a larger extracted change in the intervention group and significantly more intervention participants achieving at least a 25% CDT reduction.
- `finding:pmid-15536773-medical-comorbidity-boundary` — The chronic-disease primary-care setting makes this useful for comorbidity and biomarker context, but the population and intervention differ from a self-directed 7-, 14-, or 30-day alcohol-free challenge.

**Why it matters:** It connects alcohol-reduction counseling to medical-comorbidity endpoints and a biochemical alcohol-use marker (%CDT), but does not test complete abstinence.

**Potential experiment signals:** heavy-drinking status, carbohydrate-deficient transferrin, medical comorbidity context, primary-care feasibility.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as adjacent biomarker-feedback evidence for reduced drinking and CDT monitoring in a medical-comorbidity population; do not promote it to direct abstinence-challenge efficacy.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Limitations and population mismatch:** Small pilot; medical-comorbidity population; reduction/brief-intervention strategy rather than abstinence; detailed adverse-event extraction was not available in the batch; endpoints are partly biomarker feedback rather than challenge outcomes.
