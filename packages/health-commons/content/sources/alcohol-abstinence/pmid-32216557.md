This source is included for **Dry January and temporary abstinence campaign evidence**.

**Findings:**
- Participation was associated with increased wellbeing and general self-efficacy at one-month follow-up, with larger changes among people who completed the challenge.
- Greater use of email support was correlated with Dry January completion, but the observational design does not establish that emails caused completion.

**Why it matters:** Direct 30-day evidence for wellbeing, general self-efficacy, and the potential role of campaign emails.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- wellbeing
- general self-efficacy
- challenge completion
- email-support use

**Protocol takeaway:** Supports measuring wellbeing and confidence at the end of a 30-day challenge and offering lightweight support emails.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.
