This source is included for **Direct protocol and duration evidence**.

**Findings:**
- `finding:pmid-29726886-ggt-decreased-resumed` — GGT, although within the normal range, decreased significantly after one month of abstinence and increased again after alcohol resumption in the extracted report.
- `finding:pmid-29726886-small-uncontrolled-boundary` — The small uncontrolled design limits claims about causality, magnitude, and generalizability beyond liver-biochemistry signals.

**Why it matters:** Direct one-month abstinence evidence for liver-biochemistry endpoints in moderate consumers, closer to a wellness challenge population than clinical detox cohorts.

**Potential experiment signals:** GGT, ALT, AST, liver enzymes, liver stiffness, adherence.

**Protocol takeaway:** One month of abstinence reduced GGT in a small moderate-consumer study, with GGT rising again after drinking resumed; do not overstate broader liver benefit from this small uncontrolled study.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

**Limitations and population mismatch:** n=16; no non-abstinence control; endpoints largely liver-biochemistry; normal-range GGT was reported; not a 7- or 14-day study. Population mismatch: Moderate consumers without structural liver disease may not represent dependent drinkers, people with active liver disease, or very low-risk drinkers.
