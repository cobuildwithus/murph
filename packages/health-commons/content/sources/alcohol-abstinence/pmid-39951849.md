This source is included for **Dry January and temporary abstinence campaign evidence**.

**Findings:**
- For males, higher campaign-material exposure was associated with lower follow-up alcohol consumption but not through measured psychosocial determinants; for females, the exposure association was not significant, with small mediation via habit and subjective norm.

**Why it matters:** Important boundary evidence: more campaign exposure does not automatically translate into broad consumption change through targeted psychosocial determinants.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- campaign-material exposure
- alcohol consumption
- habit
- subjective norm
- sex differences

**Protocol takeaway:** Do not assume exposure to support materials is a sufficient mechanism; measure engagement and actual behavior.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
