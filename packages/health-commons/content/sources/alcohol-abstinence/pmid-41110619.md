This source is included for **Dry January and temporary abstinence campaign evidence**.

**Findings:**
- Prior participation, registration, higher baseline social drinking-refusal self-efficacy, and support email engagement were associated with higher completion, while smoking and perceived overdrinking were associated with lower completion odds.
- Participation was associated with short-term improvements in drinking-refusal self-efficacy, mental wellbeing, sleep quality, and physical health, especially among completers.

**Why it matters:** Direct 30-day Dry January evidence from a non-UK setting with completion predictors and short-term wellbeing/sleep/health outcomes.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- completion predictors
- drinking-refusal self-efficacy
- mental wellbeing
- sleep quality
- physical health

**Protocol takeaway:** Supports tracking completion, baseline DRSE, support engagement, sleep, wellbeing, and physical-health self-ratings in the 30-day variant.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.
