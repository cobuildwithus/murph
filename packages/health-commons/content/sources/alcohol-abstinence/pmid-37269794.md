This source is included for **Alcohol-reduction comparator reviews and public-health guideline context**.

**Findings:** Among 760 surveyed drinkers, 65% recognized the campaign. Among recognizers, 22% reported reducing drinking frequency or amount due to the campaign, and 73% considered the alcohol-cancer message believable. Above-guideline drinkers were less positive but more likely to report harm-reduction behaviours.

**Why it matters:** Useful for communication endpoints and the distinction between recognition, belief, reported reduction, and actual abstinence.

**Potential experiment signals:** campaign recognition, risk perception, reported drinking reduction, actual alcohol-free days.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as cross-sectional communication context only; do not infer causal abstinence effects.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness:** `measurement_context`.

**Population mismatch:** Campaign recognition and self-reported reductions are not direct alcohol-free challenge outcomes.

**Limitations and safety notes:** Cross-sectional and self-reported; cannot establish causality or sustained abstinence. No intervention adverse-event data were extracted; this is campaign evaluation research.
