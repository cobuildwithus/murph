This source is included for **Dry January and temporary abstinence campaign evidence**.

**Findings:**
- The time-series analysis estimated a campaign-associated reduction in population-level alcohol consumption during the Buddhist Lent abstinence campaign months, with model estimates around 8–10% lower consumption.

**Why it matters:** Adjacent population-level evidence that culturally embedded temporary abstinence campaigns can affect sales, while duration and context differ markedly from Dry January.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- per-capita alcohol sales
- population-level consumption
- seasonal campaign effect

**Protocol takeaway:** Use only as adjacent campaign context; do not equate a 3-month national religious campaign with a 7/14/30-day self-experiment.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
