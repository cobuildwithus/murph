This source is included for **Alcohol-reduction comparator reviews and public-health guideline context**.

**Findings:** Twelve controlled trials in general adult primary-care patients reported that good-quality, brief, multi-contact counseling reduced drinks per week by about 13% to 34% more than controls at 6 to 12 months, and increased the proportion drinking at moderate or safe levels by about 10% to 19%. One study maintained improved drinking patterns for 48 months.

**Why it matters:** Provides foundational adult primary-care reduction context and highlights the limits of self-reported drinking outcomes.

**Potential experiment signals:** drinks per week, safe drinking threshold, self-report validation.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as adjacent comparator context for alcohol-reduction counseling; do not infer direct abstinence challenge effects.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness:** `adjacent_variant`.

**Population mismatch:** Counseling in primary care is not the same intervention as a self-directed alcohol-free challenge.

**Limitations and safety notes:** Most outcomes were self-reported, and the evidence summary did not directly prove morbidity or mortality benefit. The accessible evidence summary emphasized drinking outcomes; adverse events were not extracted.
