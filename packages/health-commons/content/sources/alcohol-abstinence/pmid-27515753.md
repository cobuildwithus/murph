This source is included for **Alcohol-reduction comparator reviews and public-health guideline context**.

**Findings:** Fifty-two trials with 29,891 individuals were included. Alcohol brief interventions reduced alcohol quantity by about 0.15 standardized mean difference units. Setting and content did not significantly moderate effectiveness; practitioner group mattered in some analyses, with small effects for nurse-delivered and brief-advice interventions.

**Why it matters:** Helps frame that brief-intervention delivery mode and setting may have modest effects but no single content feature guarantees improvement.

**Potential experiment signals:** weekly alcohol intake, delivery mode, intervention intensity.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as comparator and implementation context; avoid causal claims about specific challenge-message components.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness:** `adjacent_variant`.

**Population mismatch:** Brief-intervention reductions across heterogeneous settings are not direct evidence for self-directed alcohol-free challenge variants.

**Limitations and safety notes:** Small average effect; meta-regression is observational across trials and cannot establish causal moderator effects. Adverse events were not extracted from accessible metadata.
