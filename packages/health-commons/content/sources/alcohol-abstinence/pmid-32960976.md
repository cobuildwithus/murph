This source is included for **Alcohol-reduction comparator reviews and public-health guideline context**.

**Findings:** The review included one trial with 269 participants followed for 24 months. Alcohol intake decreased more in the intervention group, but there were no clear differences in mortality, cardiovascular events, systolic blood pressure, or diastolic blood pressure; certainty was low or very low.

**Why it matters:** Important negative/mixed evidence: reducing alcohol intake did not clearly improve BP outcomes in the hypertension-control Cochrane review.

**Potential experiment signals:** morning blood pressure, alcohol intake, cardiovascular events, serious adverse events.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use to temper BP claims and highlight uncertainty; do not imply guaranteed BP improvement from abstinence.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness:** `same_mechanism`.

**Population mismatch:** Hypertension treatment context with psychological assistance differs from a short voluntary abstinence challenge.

**Limitations and safety notes:** Single included trial, low/very-low certainty, and insufficient safety data. The included evidence did not provide quality-of-life or serious adverse-event data; mortality and cardiovascular-event estimates were imprecise.
