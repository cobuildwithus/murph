This source is included for **Direct protocol and duration evidence**.

**Findings:**
- `finding:pmid-29730627-cardiometabolic-growth-factor-improved` — The abstinence group had extracted reductions in HOMA (-25.9%), systolic BP (-6.6%), diastolic BP (-6.3%), weight (-1.5%), VEGF (-41.8%), and EGF (-73.9%), while the control group had no significant changes in the extracted abstract data.
- `finding:pmid-29730627-excluded-dependence-liver-disease` — The study excluded alcohol dependence and known liver disease, so its short-term abstinence findings should not be used as evidence that abrupt self-guided abstinence is safe for dependent or medically complex drinkers.

**Why it matters:** Core direct one-month abstinence source with a comparison group and cardiometabolic, liver, weight, and cancer-related growth-factor endpoints.

**Potential experiment signals:** HOMA-IR, blood pressure, body weight, liver function tests, VEGF, EGF, adherence.

**Protocol takeaway:** One month abstinence was associated with improvements in insulin resistance, blood pressure, weight, and growth-factor markers relative to no significant control changes, but it was non-randomized.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

**Limitations and population mismatch:** Non-randomized; self-selected abstinence group; single tertiary centre; short follow-up; excluded alcohol dependence and known liver disease, so not a detoxification or liver-disease treatment study. Population mismatch: The study excluded alcohol dependence and known liver disease; results are not a safety guarantee for high-risk drinkers or a clinical withdrawal protocol.
