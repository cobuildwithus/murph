This source is included for **Direct protocol and duration evidence**.

**Findings:**
- `finding:pmid-28067302-fasting-glucose-egp-decreased` — After one week of abstinence, extracted values showed fasting plasma glucose decreased about 7% (105.5 to 98.2 mg/dL, p<0.01), fasting endogenous glucose production decreased about 6% (84.1 to 77.6 mg/m2/min, p<0.01), and hepatic insulin sensitivity improved.
- `finding:pmid-28067302-muscle-insulin-not-improved-boundary` — The extracted abstract did not report improved muscle insulin sensitivity, and the authors flagged no control group and small, selected population as limitations.

**Why it matters:** Direct 7-day abstinence evidence with quantified glucose-metabolism physiology, useful for the 7-day alcohol-free variant.

**Potential experiment signals:** fasting plasma glucose, endogenous glucose production, hepatic insulin sensitivity, muscle insulin sensitivity, liver enzymes.

**Protocol takeaway:** A one-week abstinence pilot reported lower fasting plasma glucose and endogenous glucose production and improved hepatic insulin sensitivity, but was tiny, male-only, and uncontrolled.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

**Limitations and population mismatch:** n=8; open-label nonrandomized single-arm design; men only; Japanese non-obese elevated-fasting-glucose sample; not a 14- or 30-day variant; further randomized trials needed. Population mismatch: Metabolic effects may not generalize to women, other ethnic groups, normoglycemic users, people with obesity, or dependent drinkers.
