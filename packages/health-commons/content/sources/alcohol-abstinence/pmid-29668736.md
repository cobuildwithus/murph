This source is included for **Direct protocol and duration evidence**.

**Findings:**
- `finding:pmid-29668736-stress-craving-ema-mixed` — The extracted source reported time-of-day and drinking-context differences in stress and craving; drinking reduced craving relative to pre-drink states, while the 3-day abstinence condition did not show a simple uniform craving increase.
- `finding:pmid-29668736-ciwa-no-positive` — A positive CIWA-Ar score never occurred in the extracted sample, but this does not establish safety for dependent or higher-risk drinkers.

**Why it matters:** Direct brief-abstinence evidence for craving, stress, and withdrawal-symptom monitoring, even though it is shorter than the 7-, 14-, and 30-day variants.

**Potential experiment signals:** alcohol craving, stress, EMA burden, withdrawal symptoms, adverse childhood experiences, social/contextual drinking cues.

**Protocol takeaway:** Three-day imposed abstinence did not simply produce a uniform craving increase; craving and stress varied by time of day, drinking context, and ACE history, with no positive CIWA-Ar cases in the selected sample.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Limitations and population mismatch:** Only 3 days; selected moderate-heavy consumers; not a 7/14/30-day outcome study; cue exposure unmeasured; health outcomes not tested. Population mismatch: Moderate-heavy volunteers in a structured EMA study may differ from self-guided challenge users and from dependent drinkers at withdrawal risk.
