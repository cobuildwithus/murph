This source is included for **Alcohol-reduction comparator reviews and public-health guideline context**.

**Findings:** Seven publications with 14 datasets were included. Very brief interventions were not significant by sex-stratified analyses. Extended brief interventions reduced weekly alcohol intake among women by about 51 g/week (95% CI 29 to 74 lower); the male estimate was heterogeneous and not considered meaningful. GGT estimates were also heterogeneous.

**Why it matters:** Shows that alcohol-reduction counseling can change alcohol intake in some primary-care contexts, but it does not establish benefits of a short alcohol-free challenge.

**Potential experiment signals:** weekly alcohol intake, gamma-glutamyltransferase, sex-stratified response.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as adjacent brief-intervention context; do not promote it to direct support for abstinence variants.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness:** `adjacent_variant`.

**Population mismatch:** Alcohol-reduction counseling in primary care is adjacent to, but not the same as, self-directed 7-, 14-, or 30-day abstinence.

**Limitations and safety notes:** Older meta-analysis; heterogeneous intervention intensity, sex-stratified results, and biomarker heterogeneity. Adverse events were not extracted from the accessible abstract.
