This source is included for **Alcohol-reduction comparator trials, digital interventions, and liver-focused protocols**.

**Findings:**
- `finding:pmid-9622267-alcohol-lipid-effects-mixed` — The controlled trial found regular alcohol intake increased HDL-C and apolipoproteins A-I/A-II and reduced Lp(a), while also increasing triglycerides; interpretation for abstinence challenge lipid claims is mixed.
- `finding:pmid-9622267-pattern-not-confirmed` — The study did not confirm a more atherogenic lipid profile from episodic weekend-pattern drinking versus regular daily drinking in this controlled sample.

**Why it matters:** It is lipid-mechanism and pattern context for alcohol reduction/abstinence but not a behavioral abstinence challenge.

**Potential experiment signals:** HDL cholesterol, apolipoprotein A-I, apolipoprotein A-II, triglycerides, lipoprotein(a), LDL cholesterol.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as mechanistic/context evidence on alcohol intake and serum lipids. Do not claim that short abstinence improves lipids from this pattern trial alone.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Limitations and population mismatch:** Healthy male regular drinkers only; alcohol substitution/pattern trial rather than abstinence challenge; cardioprotective lipid changes and triglyceride increases make interpretation mixed; no consumer abstinence protocol.
