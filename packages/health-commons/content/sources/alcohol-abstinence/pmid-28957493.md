This source is included for **Dry January and temporary abstinence campaign evidence**.

**Findings:**
- Dry January registrations grew from just over 4,000 in 2013 to nearly 60,000 in 2016; in the 2015 registrant survey, participants reported high uptake and perceived helpfulness of emails, SMS, and social-media support.
- Dry January participants had lower AUDIT scores and larger improvements in social, emotional, and opportunistic drinking-refusal self-efficacy at 6 months in the extracted study summary.

**Why it matters:** Direct 30-day Dry January evidence for campaign reach, support usability, and post-challenge self-reported drinking-confidence outcomes.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- registration growth
- support engagement
- AUDIT score
- drinking-refusal self-efficacy

**Protocol takeaway:** Supports including optional structured support and tracking post-challenge drinking-confidence outcomes in the 30-day variant.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.
