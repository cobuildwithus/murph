This source is included for **Medication, pregnancy, liver disease, and mental-health safety boundaries (part 2)**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Cross-sectional outpatient cohort analysis (2019).
- **People studied or addressed:** Kaiser Permanente Northern California adult primary-care outpatients screened for unhealthy alcohol use.
- **Role in Murph:** safety-boundary evidence only.
- **Most relevant Murph signals:** unhealthy alcohol use screen, benzodiazepine exposure flag, sedation risk flag, medication-review referral.

## Findings

- **safety** — In this cross-sectional outpatient analysis, 7.5% of screened adults used benzodiazepines in the 12-month period centered on the first alcohol-screening visit. Patients with unhealthy alcohol use or alcohol use disorder were more likely to use benzodiazepines than those with low-risk or no use, although among those dispensed benzodiazepines, doses were lower and durations shorter.
- **context** — The study is observational and does not test alcohol abstinence, alcohol reduction, or withdrawal management. Its reusable value is a sedative-medication safety boundary: benzodiazepine exposure and unhealthy alcohol use can overlap in outpatient care and should trigger clinician-guidance prompts rather than protocol efficacy claims.

## Why it matters

Shows that unhealthy alcohol use can overlap with benzodiazepine exposure in routine care, supporting a clear sedative-medication caution before a short alcohol-free experiment.

## Protocol takeaway

Flag benzodiazepine use as a clinician-guidance boundary rather than treating short-term abstinence as a stand-alone safety intervention.

## Claim use

`safety-only`.

## Important limits

Adult outpatients in an integrated health system; not a prospective abstinence-challenge intervention. Observational design; associations do not establish causality or show what happens after a 7-, 14-, or 30-day abstinence challenge.
