This source is included for **Alcohol-reduction comparator reviews and public-health guideline context**.

**Findings:** The review included 201 trials with 94,753 participants. Practitioner interventions reduced alcohol consumption more than digital interventions at 1 month and 6 months, but there was no evidence of a difference at 12 months. No evidence of delivery-mode difference was found for high-intensity drinking episodes; effects were small and heterogeneity was a concern.

**Why it matters:** High-recall comparator source for digital/practitioner delivery when considering Murph support features.

**Potential experiment signals:** weekly alcohol intake, high-intensity drinking episodes, digital versus practitioner support.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as adjacent digital/practitioner comparator evidence; avoid direct efficacy claims for Murph abstinence variants.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness:** `adjacent_variant`.

**Population mismatch:** Hazardous/harmful drinkers in intervention trials may differ from general wellness users choosing a short alcohol-free challenge.

**Limitations and safety notes:** Network comparisons and heterogeneity limit certainty; this is alcohol-reduction delivery evidence rather than abstinence-challenge evidence. Adverse events were not extracted from accessible metadata.
