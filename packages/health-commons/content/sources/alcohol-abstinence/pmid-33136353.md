This source is included for **Alcohol-reduction comparator reviews and public-health guideline context**.

**Findings:** The analysis included 74,388 adults. The revised guideline announcement and limited promotion were not associated with a clearly detectable decrease in AUDIT-C score or alcohol consumption; observed hospitalisation reductions were considered unlikely to be attributable to the guideline announcement.

**Why it matters:** Important negative/context source showing low-risk guideline promotion did not clearly change alcohol consumption at population level.

**Potential experiment signals:** AUDIT-C score, guideline awareness, weekly alcohol intake, hospitalisation context.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use to avoid assuming guideline messages alone change drinking behavior.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness:** `general_guideline`.

**Population mismatch:** Population-level guideline promotion differs from an opt-in short alcohol-free challenge.

**Limitations and safety notes:** Guidelines were not subject to large-scale promotion; interrupted time-series design cannot prove individual-level causality. Population-level hospitalisation and assault outcomes were considered, but this is not an adverse-event study of an abstinence challenge.
