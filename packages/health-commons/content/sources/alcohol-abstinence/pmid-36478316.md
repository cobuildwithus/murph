This source is included for **Alcohol-reduction comparator reviews and public-health guideline context**.

**Findings:** The campaign evaluation followed 4,002 drinkers, with 2,538 completing all three waves. Campaign exposure was associated with greater awareness of the maximum two-drinks-per-day guideline and some long-term harm risks, and with a short-term decrease in at-risk drinking in some segments; favorable effects were no longer significant around six months.

**Why it matters:** Shows that public-health messages can raise knowledge and may temporarily affect at-risk drinking, while effects may fade.

**Potential experiment signals:** guideline awareness, risk perception, weekly alcohol intake, follow-up durability.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use for communication and durability context, not direct abstinence efficacy.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness:** `measurement_context`.

**Population mismatch:** Mass-media campaign evaluation is not a protocolized abstinence intervention.

**Limitations and safety notes:** Campaign exposure and self-reported consumption may be confounded; effects were short-term and segment-specific. No adverse-event evidence was extracted; this is public-health communication research.
