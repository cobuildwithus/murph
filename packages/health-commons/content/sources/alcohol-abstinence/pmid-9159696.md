This source is included for **Alcohol-reduction comparator reviews and public-health guideline context**.

**Findings:** The accessible summary states that heavy drinkers receiving brief interventions were about twice as likely to moderate their drinking at 6 to 12 months compared with controls. Trial and participant counts were not extracted in this batch run.

**Why it matters:** Foundational comparator source, but its age and population mismatch require conservative use.

**Potential experiment signals:** moderation of drinking, weekly alcohol intake, baseline severity.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as historical comparator context only; avoid using it as direct support for alcohol-free challenge variants.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness:** `adjacent_variant`.

**Population mismatch:** Heavy drinkers in intervention trials may include people needing clinical evaluation rather than self-directed wellness challenges.

**Limitations and safety notes:** Older meta-analysis; heavy-drinker population and brief-intervention context are not direct short abstinence evidence; count fields were not extracted here. Adverse events were not extracted from the accessible metadata.
