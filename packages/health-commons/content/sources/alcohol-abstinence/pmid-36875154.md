This source is included for **Sleep, autonomic, wearable, and acute-alcohol measurement context**.

**Findings:** Accessible source title and candidate notes indicate childhood neglect was associated with lower affect and higher stress in habitual drinkers; no universal abstinence benefit was extracted.

**Why it matters:** Preserves population vulnerability evidence and cautions against universal mood-benefit claims.

**Potential experiment signals:** stress, affect, desire to drink, childhood neglect subgroup.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as vulnerability/context evidence; do not generalize abstinence challenge effects across trauma histories.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness:** `measurement_context`.

**Population mismatch:** Habitual drinkers with childhood-neglect subgroup analysis differ from broad Murph users.

**Limitations and safety notes:** Secondary analysis; participant count and detailed effect estimates not extracted here; trauma subgroup may not generalize. Stress/affect vulnerability is a safety and support consideration; no adverse events were extracted.
