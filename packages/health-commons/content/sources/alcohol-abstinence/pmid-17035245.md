This source is included for **Alcohol-reduction comparator trials, digital interventions, and liver-focused protocols**.

**Findings:**
- `finding:pmid-17035245-modest-reduction-versus-usual-care` — Participants in all groups drank less by three months, but reductions were greater in both brief-intervention groups than control; differential weekly-consumption reductions remained significant at 12 months in the extracted abstract.
- `finding:pmid-17035245-implementation-costs-low` — Average incremental costs were extracted as $4.16 per patient for licensed practitioners and $2.82 for mid-level specialists, with no significant difference between the two intervention conditions.

**Why it matters:** It provides implementation and cost context for brief alcohol-reduction counseling, including a less costly mid-level professional delivery model.

**Potential experiment signals:** weekly alcohol consumption, proportion reducing by at least one drink per week, quality of life, incremental intervention cost.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as adjacent implementation context: brief interventions can produce modest reductions, but this is managed-care reduction evidence rather than short-term abstinence evidence.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Limitations and population mismatch:** Not an abstinence challenge; quasi-experimental pre-post implementation design; managed-care clinic setting; self-reported drinking; quality-of-life difference not detected.
