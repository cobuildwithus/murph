This source is included for **Direct protocol and duration evidence**.

**Findings:**
- `finding:pmid-7334801-volunteers-drank-more-after` — The extracted abstract reported that students volunteering to abstain spent similar time at drinking-related social functions before, during, and after abstinence, and drank more after than before the abstinence period.
- `finding:pmid-7334801-unwilling-heaviest-selection` — The extracted abstract reported that students unwilling to volunteer for 10 days of abstinence were the heaviest drinkers, highlighting selection bias in voluntary challenge studies.

**Why it matters:** Older direct evidence on post-abstinence drinking behavior, relevant to rebound/off-ramp concerns for short challenges.

**Potential experiment signals:** post-challenge drinking, social exposure, volunteering/selection, rebound drinking.

**Protocol takeaway:** College students who volunteered to abstain for 10 days reportedly drank more after than before abstinence, while those unwilling to volunteer were the heaviest drinkers; use as cautionary context, not modern efficacy evidence.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Limitations and population mismatch:** Older college sample; exact sample size not extracted; social context and campus drinking norms may not generalize; not a biomarker or safety study. Population mismatch: College students in 1981 may differ substantially from current Murph users and nonstudent adults.
