This source is included for **Medication, pregnancy, liver disease, and mental-health safety boundaries (part 2)**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Clinical narrative review (2022).
- **People studied or addressed:** Patients with psychiatric disorders and hepatic or gastrointestinal diseases.
- **Role in Murph:** safety-boundary evidence only.
- **Most relevant Murph signals:** hepatic impairment screen, psychotropic medication review, sedation or hepatic encephalopathy risk, bleeding-risk co-medication flag.

## Findings

- **safety** — The clinical review describes hepatic-disease prescribing concerns for psychotropics, including altered protein binding and higher free-drug fractions. It recommends hepatic-aware dosing approaches such as lower maintenance doses in chronic liver disease and flags specific medication cautions, including avoiding duloxetine in hepatic impairment, avoiding venlafaxine in decompensated liver disease or seizure risk, using reduced/cautious doses for several antidepressants, and avoiding bupropion in severe liver disease.
- **safety** — The review states that SSRIs are generally believed to be safe in chronic liver disease but notes uncontrolled observations associating sertraline with fatal liver injury and highlights bleeding-risk concerns mainly when SSRIs are co-prescribed with antiplatelet agents. It also flags sedation-sensitive contexts, such as avoiding trazodone in hepatic encephalopathy.

## Why it matters

Supports an explicit liver-disease and psychotropic-medication boundary before a short alcohol-free experiment, especially when hepatic impairment or sedating medications may change risk.

## Protocol takeaway

Use as safety-only guidance: people with liver disease, hepatic encephalopathy risk, or complex psychotropic prescribing should not be handled as routine self-guided abstinence challengers.

## Claim use

`safety-only`.

## Important limits

Patients with hepatic or gastrointestinal disease and psychiatric comorbidity; not general wellness abstinence-challenge users. Narrative clinical review; not an abstinence intervention trial and not a source for estimating protocol effect sizes.
