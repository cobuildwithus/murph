This source is included for **Alcohol-reduction comparator reviews and public-health guideline context**.

**Findings:** The online experiment randomized 3,718 drinkers to message conditions. Alcohol harm-reduction advertisements increased intentions to reduce consumption, and guideline-message variants improved correct estimation of drinking levels associated with long-term harms in accessible summaries.

**Why it matters:** Useful for communications design and outcome boundaries: intention/knowledge is not the same as sustained alcohol abstinence.

**Potential experiment signals:** intention to reduce drinking, guideline knowledge, risk perception.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use for risk-communication context only; do not treat message effects as abstinence-challenge efficacy.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness:** `measurement_context`.

**Population mismatch:** Risk-communication experiment does not test actual 7-, 14-, or 30-day abstinence behavior.

**Limitations and safety notes:** Immediate intentions and knowledge outcomes are not behavior change or abstinence outcomes. No intervention adverse events were extracted; this is a message-exposure study.
