This source is included for **Alcohol-reduction comparator trials, digital interventions, and liver-focused protocols**.

**Findings:**
- `finding:pmid-38685934-primary-analysis-nonsignificant` — The pre-registered conservative intention-to-treat analysis found a non-significant 0.98-unit/week greater reduction in the app group, with 95% CI from -2.67 to 0.70 units/week.
- `finding:pmid-38685934-sensitivity-analysis-positive` — A multiple-imputation sensitivity analysis estimated a 2.00-unit/week greater reduction with Drink Less, with 95% CI from -3.76 to -0.24 units/week.
- `finding:pmid-38685934-trial-related-adverse-events-rare` — Trial-related adverse events were rare, reported as less than 0.1% in both arms in the extracted publication.

**Why it matters:** This is the largest current digital alcohol-reduction comparator in the batch and gives effect-size and safety context for app-supported behavior change.

**Potential experiment signals:** weekly alcohol consumption, AUDIT score, app engagement, health-related quality of life, adverse events.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as adjacent digital reduction evidence: the primary conservative analysis was not statistically significant, while sensitivity analysis favoured Drink Less; do not frame as direct abstinence evidence.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Limitations and population mismatch:** Reduction rather than abstinence; UK adults motivated to reduce drinking; primary conservative analysis was non-significant; self-report alcohol outcomes; missing-data handling changed interpretation.
