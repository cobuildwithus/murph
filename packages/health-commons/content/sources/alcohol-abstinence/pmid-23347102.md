This source is included for **Sleep, autonomic, wearable, and acute-alcohol measurement context**.

**Findings:** Accessible summaries report reduced sleep-onset latency at all dosages, more consolidated first-half sleep, and increased disruption in the second half of the night.

**Why it matters:** Provides sleep architecture guardrails for claims that alcohol-free nights may change sleep latency, REM, or second-half sleep quality.

**Potential experiment signals:** sleep onset latency, sleep efficiency, REM sleep, slow-wave sleep, wake after sleep onset.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as sleep-mechanism context and to avoid oversimplifying alcohol as purely sleep-promoting.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness:** `same_mechanism`.

**Population mismatch:** Acute alcohol sleep studies do not directly represent voluntary alcohol-free challenge participants.

**Limitations and safety notes:** Review-level evidence; older literature; not an abstinence intervention. Review context notes sleep disruption but no protocol-specific adverse events were extracted.
