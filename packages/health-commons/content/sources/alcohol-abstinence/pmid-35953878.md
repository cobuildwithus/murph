This source is included for **Sleep, autonomic, wearable, and acute-alcohol measurement context**.

**Findings:** Alcohol increased nocturnal heart rate and reduced sleep quality, total sleep time, and sleep efficiency while altering sleep architecture; relationships to morning-after effects were modest or not strong.

**Why it matters:** Preserves both positive physiologic disruption findings and null/mixed morning-after relationships.

**Potential experiment signals:** sleep quality, total sleep time, sleep efficiency, sleep architecture, nocturnal heart rate, morning-after mood and performance.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as acute alcohol context for sleep and RHR endpoints and to avoid assuming nocturnal disruption explains all next-day effects.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness:** `same_mechanism`.

**Population mismatch:** Alcohol-placebo dosing study does not directly represent voluntary abstinence.

**Limitations and safety notes:** High-dose acute exposure; laboratory sample; not a short abstinence challenge. Acute intoxication and sleep/cardiovascular disruption were part of the exposure; no abstinence adverse events were extracted.
