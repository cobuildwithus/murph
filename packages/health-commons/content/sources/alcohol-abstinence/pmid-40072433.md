This source is included for **Registry, external protocol, and miscellaneous context records**.

**Findings:** Alcohol cessation was associated with increased LDL-C and decreased HDL-C versus continued drinking; reported cessation-associated LDL-C increases ranged from about 1.10 to 6.53 mg/dL by prior drinking level and HDL-C decreases from about 1.25 to 5.65 mg/dL.

**Why it matters:** Important lipid boundary source: stopping alcohol may change HDL and LDL in directions that can be misread if the protocol over-simplifies cholesterol outcomes.

**Potential experiment signals:** LDL cholesterol, HDL cholesterol, triglycerides, non-HDL cholesterol.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as observational lipid-context evidence only; do not claim causality or simple cardiovascular benefit/harm from short abstinence based on lipid changes alone.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness:** `measurement_context`.
