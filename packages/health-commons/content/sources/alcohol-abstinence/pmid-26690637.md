This source is included for **Dry January and temporary abstinence campaign evidence**.

**Findings:**
- Participation was associated with reduced alcohol consumption and increased drinking-refusal self-efficacy at 6-month follow-up, with stronger changes among people who completed the challenge.
- More moderate baseline drinking and greater social drinking-refusal self-efficacy predicted success; lower pre-January drunkenness frequency was the best multivariable predictor, and only a small minority reported rebound increases.

**Why it matters:** Directly informs the 30-day variant and shows post-challenge drinking change without a rebound signal in most participants.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- alcohol use after challenge
- frequency of drunkenness
- drinking-refusal self-efficacy
- abstinence success

**Protocol takeaway:** Supports cautious 30-day protocol claims about possible post-challenge drinking reduction and confidence gains in self-selected participants.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.
