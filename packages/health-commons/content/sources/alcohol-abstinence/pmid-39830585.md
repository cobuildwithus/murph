This source is included for **Medication, pregnancy, liver disease, and mental-health safety boundaries (part 2)**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Retrospective cohort medication-chart review (2025).
- **People studied or addressed:** Hospitalized patients with alcohol use disorder receiving withdrawal therapy on an addiction-specific ward.
- **Role in Murph:** safety-boundary evidence only.
- **Most relevant Murph signals:** current medication list, alcohol-medication interaction count, drug-drug interaction severity, psychotropic medication interaction flag.

## Findings

- **safety** — The retrospective study found that 66.3% of patient cases had at least one potential alcohol-medication interaction and that 50.0% of prescribed drugs could potentially interact with alcohol. Of the potential alcohol-medication interactions, 4.0% were classified as severe, 91.8% as moderate, and 4.3% as mild; severe interactions were most often associated with analgesics and diabetes drugs.
- **safety** — The study identified 811 potential drug-drug interactions: 3.3% severe and 96.5% moderate, with 21.7% of patient cases affected. Psychoanaleptics and psycholeptics were frequently associated with moderate-to-severe drug-drug interactions. The study screened for potential interactions and did not prospectively confirm actual adverse events from the flagged interactions.

## Why it matters

Shows medication-interaction risk is common in a clinical AUD/withdrawal setting, supporting medication-review prompts and clinician guidance boundaries.

## Protocol takeaway

Use as a medication safety boundary: short-term abstinence content should prompt medication review and clinician guidance rather than trying to manage drug interactions itself.

## Claim use

`safety-only`.

## Important limits

Hospitalized AUD withdrawal patients, not generally healthy adults doing a self-directed 7-, 14-, or 30-day alcohol-free challenge. Potential interactions were identified by screening tools; the study did not prospectively confirm actual adverse events from the flagged interactions.
