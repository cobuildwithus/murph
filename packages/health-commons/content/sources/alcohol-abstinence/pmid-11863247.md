This source is included for **Sleep, autonomic, wearable, and acute-alcohol measurement context**.

**Findings:** Alcohol intake fell from roughly 70 to 19 mL ethanol/day; daytime systolic blood pressure was about 4 mmHg lower, heart rate was lower, pNN50 and high-frequency HRV were higher, and LF/HF was lower in the restriction period.

**Why it matters:** Provides adjacent short-term alcohol-reduction evidence for blood pressure, resting heart rate, and HRV endpoints that Murph users may track during alcohol-free challenges.

**Potential experiment signals:** ambulatory systolic blood pressure, daytime and nighttime heart rate, pNN50, high-frequency HRV, LF/HF ratio.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as adjacent alcohol-reduction physiology evidence, not as proof that complete 7-, 14-, or 30-day abstinence has the same effect.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness:** `adjacent_variant`.

**Population mismatch:** Japanese male habitual drinkers may not generalize to mixed-sex global wellness users.

**Limitations and safety notes:** Small male-only sample; alcohol restriction was not complete abstinence; no direct 7-, 14-, or 30-day abstinence challenge arm. No adverse events were extracted from accessible source text.
