This source is included for **Alcohol-reduction comparator reviews and public-health guideline context**.

**Findings:** The review included 14 RCTs/CCTs with 4,041 mainly male adults. Alcohol consumption was lower at 6 months and 9 months in pooled analyses, but this was not maintained at 1 year. Fewer deaths were reported at 6 months and 1 year, but this hospital-ward context is clinically specific.

**Why it matters:** Useful as a population-boundary source: hospital heavy-drinker interventions should not be generalized to low-risk self-experiments.

**Potential experiment signals:** weekly alcohol intake, alcohol-related admission context, mortality safety boundary.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use for clinical-supervised comparator and referral context, not direct support for self-directed abstinence variants.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness:** `clinical_supervised`.

**Population mismatch:** Clinical hospital-ward patients with heavy alcohol use are higher acuity than typical Murph abstinence-challenge users.

**Limitations and safety notes:** Hospitalized heavy alcohol users differ from wellness self-experimenters; optimal intervention content remained unclear. Mortality signals favored brief intervention in the review, but the source does not make this a safety claim for self-directed abstinence challenges.
