This source is included for **Direct protocol and duration evidence**.

**Findings:**
- `finding:pmid-3360951-no-subsequent-drinking-change` — The extracted abstract reported that voluntary 3-week abstinence neither increased nor decreased subsequent alcohol consumption compared with control.
- `finding:pmid-3360951-rebound-boundary` — This source is useful for off-ramp/rebound context but not for claims about biomarker, sleep, mood, or safety benefits.

**Why it matters:** Directly tests whether a short abstinence interval produces rebound drinking afterward, an important off-ramp concern for challenge design.

**Potential experiment signals:** post-challenge drinking frequency, post-challenge drinking quantity, rebound drinking, off-ramp plan.

**Protocol takeaway:** In female social drinkers, voluntary 3-week abstinence neither increased nor decreased subsequent alcohol consumption in extracted abstract data; use as context for rebound claims.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Limitations and population mismatch:** Older study; female social drinkers only; small sample; self-monitoring; not a modern Dry January or Murph protocol; health endpoints not measured. Population mismatch: Female social drinkers may not generalize to heavy drinkers, dependent drinkers, or mixed-gender challenge populations.
