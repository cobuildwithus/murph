This source is included for **Alcohol-reduction comparator reviews and public-health guideline context**.

**Findings:** Nineteen trials with 5,639 individuals were included. Seventeen trials reported alcohol consumption; eight reported significant intervention effects. The adjusted intention-to-treat pooled estimate favored brief intervention by about 38 g ethanol/week lower intake (95% CI 24 to 51 lower). Other outcomes were inconclusive.

**Why it matters:** Foundational meta-analysis quantifying how much primary-care brief interventions reduced weekly alcohol intake.

**Potential experiment signals:** weekly alcohol intake, brief-intervention comparator, intention-to-treat outcome.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as alcohol-reduction comparator evidence; do not claim direct abstinence-challenge efficacy.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness:** `adjacent_variant`.

**Population mismatch:** Primary-care outpatient intervention evidence is adjacent to a Murph alcohol-free challenge.

**Limitations and safety notes:** Effects are for reduction counseling and other outcomes were inconclusive; setting and intervention format differ from self-directed abstinence. Adverse events were not extracted from the accessible abstract.
