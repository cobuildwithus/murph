This source is included for **Direct protocol and duration evidence**.

**Findings:**
- `finding:pmid-39155051-four-participant-classes` — The extracted abstract/snippets identified four classes: ordinary drinkers (49.0%), drinkers in control (21.4%), habitual drinkers with perceived control to refuse (18.4%), and drinkers not in control (11.2%).
- `finding:pmid-39155051-heterogeneity-boundary` — This typology supports heterogeneous adherence and support assumptions, but it is not direct evidence that abstinence improves biomarkers or symptoms.

**Why it matters:** Shows that challenge participants are heterogeneous, supporting segmentation rather than one-size-fits-all claims about adherence or benefits.

**Potential experiment signals:** participant subgroup, baseline alcohol use, drinking-refusal self-efficacy, craving, behavioral automaticity, adherence support needs.

**Protocol takeaway:** TAC participants fell into four empirically derived classes, so protocol copy should avoid assuming a single participant profile or uniform support need.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Limitations and population mismatch:** Self-selected Dutch challenge participants; typology/context source; does not directly estimate health benefits of 7/14/30-day abstinence; rights should be verified before committing PDFs. Population mismatch: Dutch TAC participants may differ from Murph users in country, campaign context, motivation, and baseline drinking pattern.
