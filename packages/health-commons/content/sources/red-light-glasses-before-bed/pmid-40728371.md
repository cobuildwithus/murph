This source is included for **Product characterization and implementation**.

**Findings:** This review found that commercial blue-blocking products vary widely in melanopic filtering strength. Lens color and marketing copy were not enough to know whether the product meaningfully reduced circadian-relevant light. Timing, fit, edge leakage, and use conditions matter, so spectral adequacy becomes a protocol guardrail rather than a nice-to-have detail.

**Why it matters:** Shows why lens color and marketing labels are not enough; spectral filtering, fit, timing, and usage determine whether melanopic input is actually reduced.

**Potential experiment signals:** melanopic daylight filtering density, spectral transmittance, implementation timing

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as the product-quality guardrail and avoid brand recommendations unless spectral data is available.
