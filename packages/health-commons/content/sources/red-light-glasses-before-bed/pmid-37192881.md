This source is included for **Broad systematic review**.

**Findings:** This review and meta-analysis found that reducing short-wavelength light before bed may improve sleep, especially in studies using blue-blocking glasses. The evidence was heterogeneous and not strong enough for broad routine clinical use. It supports a cautious self-experiment, not a guaranteed sleep treatment.

**Why it matters:** Useful umbrella context: subjective sleep signals are often more consistent than objective signals across heterogeneous interventions.

**Potential experiment signals:** sleep efficiency, total sleep time, Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index, sleep onset latency

**Protocol takeaway:** Do not merge glasses, screen filters, and room-light dimming into one causal claim.
