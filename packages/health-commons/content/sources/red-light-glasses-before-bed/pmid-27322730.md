This source is included for **Clinical circadian disorder variant**.

**Findings:** In a small open-label delayed-sleep-phase study, evening amber glasses advanced sleep timing and actigraphic sleep onset. Dim-light melatonin onset also shifted earlier but did not reach statistical significance. This suggests a circadian-timing effect in a clinical disorder, not direct proof for healthy sleepers.

**Why it matters:** Reinforces that delayed-sleep-phase protocols need their own framing and should not be merged into a general bedtime eyewear experiment.

**Potential experiment signals:** sleep timing, actigraphy sleep onset, circadian phase

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as an adjacent variant, not as direct proof for the ordinary bedtime eyewear page.

**Claim use:** `context-only`. Keep this classification when wiring claims so circadian-disorder studies do not become direct support for the ordinary consumer protocol.
