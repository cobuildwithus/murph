This source is included for **Product and home-lighting context**.

**Findings:** This modeling study showed that household lamp spectra and brightness can change estimated melatonin suppression. Cool LED and CFL lighting produced higher modeled suppression than warmer or incandescent sources, and tunable warm settings reduced disruption. It supports logging room lighting and treating lighting changes as distinct from eyewear.

**Why it matters:** Shows why room-light brightness and lens spectra can matter as much as the fact that a lens is amber or red.

**Potential experiment signals:** estimated melatonin suppression, spectral transmittance, home-lighting conditions

**Protocol takeaway:** Supports logging room-light brightness and treating room-light redesign as a separate intervention from eyewear.

**Claim use:** `context-only`. Keep this classification when wiring claims so home-lighting modeling work does not get mistaken for direct bedtime sleep efficacy evidence.
