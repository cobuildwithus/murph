This source is included for **Shift-work adjacent variant**.

**Findings:** In simulated overnight shift work, lenses blocking wavelengths below 530 nm prevented bright-light suppression of melatonin. They did not meaningfully impair the performance measures used in the study. This supports the short-wavelength mechanism, but the setting was night-shift work, not ordinary bedtime sleep.

**Why it matters:** Supports the short-wavelength mechanism under shift-work conditions without turning shift-work findings into a general bedtime protocol claim.

**Potential experiment signals:** melatonin suppression, alertness, performance, sleepiness

**Protocol takeaway:** Keep shift-work lighting questions separate from the ordinary pre-bed eyewear protocol.

**Claim use:** `context-only`. Keep this classification when wiring claims so adjacent mechanism papers do not become direct support for the consumer bedtime protocol.
