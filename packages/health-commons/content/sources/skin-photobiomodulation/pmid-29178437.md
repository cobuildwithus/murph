This source is included for **skin safety and screening boundary**.

**Findings:** 808 nm had greater transmittance than 635 nm, though the effect was partly reduced with darker skin and thicker tissue. Dose increased skin temperature by about 0.7-1.6°C across 6-12 J doses; subjects were not aware of the temperature increase in extracted records.

**Why it matters:** Supports conservative dose/heat monitoring and skin-type-aware assumptions for red/NIR exposure.

**Potential experiment signals:** skin temperature delta, skin tone/thickness context, warmth awareness.

**Protocol takeaway:** Skin color, tissue thickness, wavelength, and dose altered red/NIR laser penetration and temperature behavior. Laser optical data should not be directly converted into LED protocol efficacy claims.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

### Extraction notes

- **Population:** Forty volunteers assessed for skin color and skin-fold thickness at a standardized site near the elbow.
- **Intervention/exposure:** 635 nm and 808 nm low-intensity laser diodes at energy doses of 2, 6, 9, and 12 J within ANSI safety guidelines.
- **Comparator/control:** Wavelength, skin color, tissue thickness, and dose comparisons.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Acute measurement during/after exposure.
- **Endpoints:** Transmittance, reflectance, absorption, and skin temperature.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No symptom awareness of temperature increase in extracted record; thermal rise is still relevant for monitoring.
- **Limitations:** Adjacent laser variant, not LED PBM.; Acute measures near elbow, not face.; Low-intensity lasers and optical setup differ from consumer panels/masks.
- **Population mismatch/directness:** Adjacent red/NIR optical and thermal safety context.
- **Artifact/rights status:** paywalled.
