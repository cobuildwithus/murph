This source is included for **skin safety and screening boundary**.

**Findings:** Extracted records report at least fair improvement in fine wrinkles in all patients and no effect on hyperpigmented lesions; authors suggested potential benefit on skin texture/wrinkles via collagen and elastin increases.

**Why it matters:** Use cautiously as infrared context and do not conflate far-IR or heat exposure with nonthermal NIR PBM.

**Potential experiment signals:** skin temperature, texture score, pigment lesion tracking.

**Protocol takeaway:** Older infrared study suggested wrinkle/texture effects but did not improve hyperpigmented lesions. Small older study cannot define modern home-device safety or efficacy.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

### Extraction notes

- **Population:** Patients with mild to moderate facial wrinkles/photoaged skin, with accompanying mechanistic skin/fibroblast assessments.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Infrared radiation treatment.
- **Comparator/control:** Baseline and mechanistic comparisons; no sham comparator identified in extracted record.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Daily infrared treatments were described in review excerpts; exact follow-up should be verified in full text before detailed protocol use.
- **Endpoints:** Wrinkles, skin texture, collagen and elastin content, fibroblast response, and hyperpigmented lesions.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No hyperpigmentation improvement signal; thermal/infrared context requires caution separate from low-irradiance PBM.
- **Limitations:** Older small study.; Infrared protocol differs from modern red/NIR LED devices.; No robust sham-controlled photoaging design identified in extracted record.
- **Population mismatch/directness:** Adjacent infrared/photoaging context, not direct target-protocol safety boundary.
- **Artifact/rights status:** open_access.
