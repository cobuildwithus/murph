This source is included for **skin-of-color, visible-light pigmentation, melasma, or PIH safety boundary**.

**Findings:** Extracted record reports significant subjective and objective improvements in both skin-type groups, but with non-random assignment and limited design detail.

**Why it matters:** Use as skin-of-color pigment-disorder context and avoid claiming uniform pigmentation safety from photoaging PBM.

**Potential experiment signals:** Fitzpatrick V-VI subgroup watch, melasma symptom tracking, skin-type-specific tolerability.

**Protocol takeaway:** LED treatment was reported to improve melasma in Fitzpatrick V-VI participants, but design limits causal claims. Do not promote as direct support for the target protocol.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

### Extraction notes

- **Population:** Sixty female participants aged 25-60 years with melasma and Fitzpatrick skin types V and VI.
- **Intervention/exposure:** LED treatment delivered over 36 treatments during nine months.
- **Comparator/control:** Participants were purposely assigned to two groups by skin type; no randomized sham control identified in extracted record.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Nine months of treatment.
- **Endpoints:** Subjective and objective melasma improvement outcomes.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** Adverse-event detail not captured in extracted record; use cautiously for safety claims.
- **Limitations:** No randomized sham comparator identified.; Melasma treatment study, not skin texture/photoaging protocol.; Device parameters and adverse-event detail need full-text verification.
- **Population mismatch/directness:** Adjacent skin-of-color melasma context; not direct red/NIR photoaging evidence.
- **Artifact/rights status:** unknown.
