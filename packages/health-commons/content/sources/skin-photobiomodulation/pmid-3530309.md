This source is included for **photosensitizing-medication and retinoid safety boundary**.

**Findings:** Extracted record describes abnormal phototesting from 300±5 nm to 365±30 nm that returned close to normal within one month of stopping treatment in a case; low incidence suggests idiosyncrasy.

**Why it matters:** Use to avoid overgeneralizing retinoid cautions; screen and refer rather than asserting all retinoids are red/NIR contraindications.

**Potential experiment signals:** retinoid use history, irritation vs photosensitivity distinction, clinician/pharmacist review.

**Protocol takeaway:** Older retinoid photosensitivity evidence suggests rare or idiosyncratic reactions rather than a uniform retinoid rule. Modern topical retinoids/formulations require separate evidence.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

### Extraction notes

- **Population:** Patients receiving retinoids in clinical and laboratory photosensitivity assessments.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Retinoid exposure with phototesting.
- **Comparator/control:** Phototesting before/after stopping the retinoid in reported cases.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Extracted record notes phototesting abnormality returning close to normal within one month after stopping a retinoid in one report.
- **Endpoints:** Phototesting abnormality across UV wavelengths and clinical photosensitivity.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** Retinoid-associated photosensitivity appears possible but uncommon/idiosyncratic in extracted records.
- **Limitations:** Older study with limited extracted quantitative detail.; UV phototesting, not red/NIR PBM exposure.; Participant count not captured here.
- **Population mismatch/directness:** Retinoid-specific medication safety context only.
- **Artifact/rights status:** paywalled.
