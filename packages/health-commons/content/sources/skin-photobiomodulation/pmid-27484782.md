This source is included for **red-light skin safety and dose-escalation boundary**.

**Findings:** Protocol source only; it defines dose-escalation logic and safety stopping rules but does not report completed efficacy outcomes.

**Why it matters:** Use for protocol-defined safety boundaries and planned stop conditions, not as outcome evidence.

**Potential experiment signals:** dose cohort rules, mock-control safety comparison, photosensitivity pre-screen.

**Protocol takeaway:** The STARS 1 protocol established planned high-fluence red LED dose-escalation and adverse-event rules. Reported effects must come from the completed trial rather than this protocol.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

### Extraction notes

- **Population:** Healthy adult volunteers planned for high-fluence red LED exposure on skin.
- **Intervention/exposure:** High-fluence LED red light in sequential dose cohorts of 160, 320, 480, and 640 J/cm².
- **Comparator/control:** Mock phototherapy within each dose cohort.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Planned three times weekly exposure for three consecutive weeks.
- **Endpoints:** Planned safety endpoints included adverse events, dose-limiting toxicity, maximum tolerated dose, erythema, hyperpigmentation, blistering, and photosensitivity screening.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No completed adverse-event results in the protocol; adverse events were prospectively defined for the later safety study.
- **Limitations:** Protocol paper, not completed trial results.; Safety-focused and not designed for facial photoaging efficacy.; Planned healthy-volunteer forearm exposure limits generalization to home use.
- **Population mismatch/directness:** Direct for red LED dose-escalation safety design; indirect for photoaging efficacy.
- **Artifact/rights status:** open_access.
