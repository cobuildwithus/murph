This source is included for **skin-of-color, visible-light pigmentation, melasma, or PIH safety boundary**.

**Findings:** 415 nm exposure produced dose-dependent hyperpigmentation in skin types III-IV and was reported as more pronounced than UVB with persistence up to three months; 630 nm did not induce hyperpigmentation in the extracted record.

**Why it matters:** Supports careful wavelength-specific safety wording rather than treating all visible light as equivalent.

**Potential experiment signals:** wavelength audit, blue-light exclusion, persistent hyperpigmentation monitoring.

**Protocol takeaway:** Blue/violet visible light induced persistent pigmentation, whereas 630 nm red light did not in this wavelength-comparison study. Absence of hyperpigmentation with 630 nm here does not prove all red-light devices are pigment-neutral across all doses and populations.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

### Extraction notes

- **Population:** Healthy volunteers with Fitzpatrick skin types III and IV.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Visible-light wavelength exposures including blue/violet 415 nm and red 630 nm, compared with UVB and unexposed skin.
- **Comparator/control:** Unexposed skin and UVB-exposed skin; 415 nm versus 630 nm visible light.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Pigmentation assessed through a follow-up window reported up to three months.
- **Endpoints:** Clinical pigmentation, dose response, persistence of hyperpigmentation, histologic epidermal effects, keratinocyte necrosis, and p53 staining.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** Key safety signal is wavelength-specific visible-light hyperpigmentation, especially at 415 nm; 630 nm was not associated with induced hyperpigmentation in this study.
- **Limitations:** Not a red/NIR PBM treatment trial.; Participant count was not captured in extracted records.; Acute experimental exposures may not match consumer device schedules.
- **Population mismatch/directness:** Highly relevant for separating blue/violet pigmentation risks from red-light safety; indirect for red/NIR photoaging protocol.
- **Artifact/rights status:** paywalled.
