This source is included for **methods, dose-reporting, measurement, or device-geometry context**.

**Findings:** The review identified 27 dermatology LED studies and emphasized wide parameter variability, including fluence and wavelength differences large enough to make cross-study comparison unreliable. It also noted limited dose validation and reporting concerns such as assessor blinding and sponsorship.

**Why it matters:** It directly informs dose-reporting and methodological caution for red/NIR consumer or clinical LED protocols.

**Potential experiment signals:** wavelength and bandwidth, fluence, irradiance at skin, dose-validation method, assessor blinding, sponsor or device-manufacturer involvement.

**Protocol takeaway:** Treat LED studies as difficult to compare unless wavelength, irradiance, fluence, exposure geometry, validation, and blinding are explicit.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
