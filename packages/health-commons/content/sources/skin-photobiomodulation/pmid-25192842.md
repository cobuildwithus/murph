This source is included for **photosensitizing-medication and retinoid safety boundary**.

**Findings:** The paper argues that guidance on drug avoidance for laser/IPL users can be overly rigid when based on limited data; extracted records note retinoids and steroids are discussed but are not photosensitizers in the same way as true photosensitizing agents.

**Why it matters:** Use to make medication screening nuanced: identify high-risk drugs and refer uncertain cases, but avoid unsupported blanket exclusions.

**Potential experiment signals:** drug list triage, test patch/hold decision, clinician referral.

**Protocol takeaway:** Laser/IPL medication guidance cautions against rigid overinterpretation of photosensitizing-drug lists. Laser/IPL thermal/high-energy context differs from red/NIR PBM.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

### Extraction notes

- **Population:** People undergoing laser/IPL/light-based procedures while using medications that may affect photosensitivity or healing.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Laser/IPL procedures and medication screening.
- **Comparator/control:** Not applicable.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Not applicable.
- **Endpoints:** Photosensitizing agents, radiation tolerance, medication-related procedure cautions, and practical safety recommendations.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** Medication-related photosensitivity and healing impairment are the safety concern; evidence quality varies by drug and device wavelength.
- **Limitations:** Laser/IPL focus, not low-irradiance PBM.; Guidance/commentary rather than prospective adverse-event trial.
- **Population mismatch/directness:** Adjacent procedure safety guidance only.
