This source is included for **skin-of-color, visible-light pigmentation, melasma, or PIH safety boundary**.

**Findings:** Review context supports that standard UV filters do not fully protect against visible light; tinted/opaque strategies are emphasized for visible-light photoprotection.

**Why it matters:** Use for counseling around melasma/PIH and continued photoprotection during a cosmetic PBM protocol.

**Potential experiment signals:** photoprotection adherence, tinted sunscreen use, sun-exposure diary.

**Protocol takeaway:** Visible-light photoprotection differs from UV-only photoprotection and may matter for pigment-prone users. Photoprotection claims should remain separate from red/NIR treatment effects.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

### Extraction notes

- **Population:** Dermatology photoprotection literature, especially for pigmentary disorders and visible-light effects.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Photoprotection strategies against visible and ultraviolet light.
- **Comparator/control:** Organic/inorganic UV filters, tinted sunscreens, clothing, shade, and avoidance strategies.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Not applicable.
- **Endpoints:** Visible-light protection, pigment darkening, erythema, melasma/PIH-relevant photoprotection.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** Not an adverse-event trial; used for pigmentation-prevention context.
- **Limitations:** Photoprotection review, not PBM intervention evidence.; Does not test red/NIR devices.
- **Population mismatch/directness:** Background/safety boundary only.
- **Artifact/rights status:** open_access.
