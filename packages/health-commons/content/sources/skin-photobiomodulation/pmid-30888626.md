This source is included for **photosensitizing-medication and retinoid safety boundary**.

**Findings:** Review emphasizes prevention, patient counseling, and sun-protective measures; once a reaction occurs, discontinuing the culprit medication and corticosteroid treatment may be needed.

**Why it matters:** Use to justify medication screening, conservative pause/clinician review language, and stop rules for sunburn-like reactions.

**Potential experiment signals:** medication checklist, sunburn-like reaction stop rule, clinician review trigger.

**Protocol takeaway:** Photosensitizing medications warrant screening before elective light exposure. Do not assume every listed medication reacts to red/NIR wavelengths; use as conservative screening only.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

### Extraction notes

- **Population:** Patients taking medications with photosensitizing potential.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Photosensitizing medications plus ultraviolet or visible radiation exposure.
- **Comparator/control:** Not applicable; review of mechanisms and clinical management.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Not applicable.
- **Endpoints:** Phototoxic/photoallergic eruptions, diagnosis, prevention, medication discontinuation, corticosteroid treatment, and sun protection.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** Photosensitive drug eruptions can occur with UV or visible radiation; commonly implicated medications include tetracyclines/doxycycline, quinolones, thiazides, NSAIDs, amiodarone, antipsychotics, and others in extracted records.
- **Limitations:** Medication photosensitivity evidence is largely UV/visible oriented and not specific to red/NIR PBM.; Review does not quantify risk for a cosmetic LED protocol.
- **Population mismatch/directness:** Medication safety boundary, not protocol efficacy evidence.
- **Artifact/rights status:** paywalled.
