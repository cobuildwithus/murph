This source is included for **skin-of-color, visible-light pigmentation, melasma, or PIH safety boundary**.

**Findings:** For therapeutic irradiation, 830 nm reduced ΔEI and ΔMI versus control; 590 nm did not significantly differ from control. For prevention, both 830 nm and 590 nm lowered ΔEI versus control. The authors concluded 830 nm could improve PIE and PIH and both wavelengths had preventive effects for PIE.

**Why it matters:** Use as adjacent pigment-response context and endpoint inspiration, not as direct photoaging evidence.

**Potential experiment signals:** erythema index, melanin index, PIE/PIH watch.

**Protocol takeaway:** In a small induced PIE/PIH model, 830 nm LED showed therapeutic effects on erythema and melanin indices; 590 nm was more limited therapeutically. Short induced model and small sample limit generalization.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

### Extraction notes

- **Population:** Ten healthy subjects in an induced erythema/pigmentation model on thigh skin.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Therapeutic/preventive LED irradiation with 830 nm at 60 J/cm² and 590 nm at 20 J/cm² around an induced 308 nm LED erythema/pigmentation model.
- **Comparator/control:** Control model sites without LED intervention.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Ten-day follow-up with repeated measurement visits.
- **Endpoints:** Erythema index, melanin index, TEWL, C-Cube photography, preventive and therapeutic effects.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No detailed adverse-event signal captured in extracted abstract; study used induced UVB/308 nm model and short follow-up.
- **Limitations:** Pilot with n=10.; Induced thigh model, not spontaneous facial PIH or photoaging.; Uses 590/830 nm pigment/erythema protocol, not standard red/NIR texture protocol.
- **Population mismatch/directness:** Adjacent PIE/PIH model; informative for pigment and erythema endpoints only.
- **Artifact/rights status:** unknown.
