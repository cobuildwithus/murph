This source is included for **skin-of-color, visible-light pigmentation, melasma, or PIH safety boundary**.

**Findings:** The review describes visible light as able to penetrate skin, interact with chromophores, and induce pigmentation and erythema.

**Why it matters:** Use for cautious visible-light boundary language and for distinguishing red/NIR PBM from broader visible-light exposure.

**Potential experiment signals:** wavelength disclosure, erythema/darkening diary, device light-spectrum audit.

**Protocol takeaway:** Visible light has biologic skin effects, including pigmentation and erythema. Does not define safe home red/NIR dosing by itself.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

### Extraction notes

- **Population:** Cutaneous photobiology literature relevant to visible light from sun and artificial light sources, including LEDs.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Visible light (400-700 nm) exposure.
- **Comparator/control:** Contextual comparison across wavelengths and chromophores.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Not applicable.
- **Endpoints:** Visible-light penetration, chromophore activation, pigmentation, erythema, edema, and fibrosis.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** Pigmentation and erythema are the key safety/context endpoints.
- **Limitations:** Review, not direct red/NIR trial.; Visible-light category includes wavelengths not used in the target protocol.
- **Population mismatch/directness:** Background safety context only.
- **Artifact/rights status:** open_access.
