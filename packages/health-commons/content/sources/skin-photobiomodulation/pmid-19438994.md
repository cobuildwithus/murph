This source is included for **photosensitizing-medication and retinoid safety boundary**.

**Findings:** No phototoxic or photoallergic reactions occurred with tretinoin 0.05% in the extracted record; findings supported the conclusion that tretinoin appeared neither phototoxic nor photoallergenic in the tested formulation.

**Why it matters:** Use as a negative/null retinoid source to avoid blanket claims that topical tretinoin is photosensitizing.

**Potential experiment signals:** retinoid irritation screen, photosensitivity distinction, null safety evidence.

**Protocol takeaway:** Controlled trials reported no tretinoin phototoxicity or photoallergy for a tested 0.05% gel formulation. Still screen for irritation, barrier damage, and clinician instructions.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

### Extraction notes

- **Population:** Healthy volunteers in four controlled clinical trials; two phototoxicity trials and two photoallergenicity trials.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Tretinoin 0.05% gel formulation under phototoxicity/photoallergy testing conditions.
- **Comparator/control:** Controlled vehicle/comparator testing in trial designs.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Phototoxicity after 24-hour exposure under occlusion; photoallergenicity after three-week six-dose induction followed by challenge.
- **Endpoints:** Phototoxic reactions and photoallergic reactions.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** Null finding for phototoxic/photoallergic reactions in tested formulation; irritation from retinoids is a separate issue not resolved by this finding.
- **Limitations:** Specific tretinoin 0.05% gel formulation and trial conditions.; Does not cover all retinoids or irritated/barrier-impaired skin.; UV/photoallergy testing context differs from red/NIR PBM.
- **Population mismatch/directness:** Medication boundary source; not direct PBM evidence.
- **Artifact/rights status:** paywalled.
