This source is included for **skin safety and screening boundary**.

**Findings:** Twenty-eight subjects achieved the primary outcome; 93.3% of enrolled subjects were reported as achieving 60% SS-10 reduction, with benefit persisting at two months. Two reported side effects were allergic reactions to nickel in protective goggles.

**Why it matters:** Use for sensitive-skin tolerability and protective-equipment adverse-event language, not as primary photoaging efficacy evidence.

**Potential experiment signals:** Sensitive Scale-10, irritation/stinging log, goggle contact dermatitis.

**Protocol takeaway:** Small uncontrolled pilot found red LED phototherapy tolerable for sensitive skin, with nickel-goggle allergy as a reported side effect. No sham control and different population/outcome.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

### Extraction notes

- **Population:** Adults with sensitive skin and Sensitive Scale-10 score ≥40; 28 completed according to protocol.
- **Intervention/exposure:** ~630 nm red LED exposure to cheeks, 7 J/cm² for 3 minutes at 10 cm, twice weekly until response or up to eight weeks.
- **Comparator/control:** No control group.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Up to eight weeks of treatment plus two-month post-treatment follow-up.
- **Endpoints:** Sensitive Scale-10 reduction, tolerability, trigger sensitivity, and side effects.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** Nickel-related allergic reactions from goggles were reported; no control group limits adverse-event interpretation.
- **Limitations:** Small uncontrolled pilot.; Sensitive-skin symptom study, not photoaging texture/wrinkle trial.; Goggle allergy is procedure-adjacent rather than LED skin phototoxicity.
- **Population mismatch/directness:** Safety/tolerability context for sensitive skin; not direct anti-aging evidence.
- **Artifact/rights status:** open_access.
