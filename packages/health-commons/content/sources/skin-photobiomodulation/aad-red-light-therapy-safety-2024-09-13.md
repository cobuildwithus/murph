This source is included for **skin safety and screening boundary**.

**Findings:** AAD guidance states short-term red light therapy appears safe, notes research has not found red light causes cancer unlike UV, and highlights mild pain/irritated skin as common side effects; it cautions darker skin users about possible hyperpigmentation/dark spots and advises dermatologist discussion for photosensitive conditions or medications.

**Why it matters:** Use for user-facing safety boundaries and referral language.

**Potential experiment signals:** skin irritation log, darker-skin hyperpigmentation watch, photosensitive condition screen, eye-protection adherence.

**Protocol takeaway:** AAD guidance supports cautious red/NIR PBM use with dermatologist review for darker skin, photosensitive conditions, medications, and eye protection. FDA-cleared/public-risk language should not be interpreted as proof of effectiveness.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

### Extraction notes

- **Population:** People considering red light therapy for skin.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Red and near-infrared photobiomodulation/red light therapy.
- **Comparator/control:** In-office/professional versus at-home context; no trial comparator.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Not applicable.
- **Endpoints:** Safety, skin irritation, pain, hyperpigmentation concerns, photosensitive conditions, photosensitizing medications, eye protection, and long-term uncertainty.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** Mild pain/irritated skin, possible hyperpigmentation/dark spots in darker skin tones, medication/photosensitivity concerns, and need for eye protection when required.
- **Limitations:** Public guidance, not a systematic evidence synthesis.; Does not define a specific device dose or efficacy estimate.; Long-term effects remain uncertain.
- **Population mismatch/directness:** Consumer-facing safety guidance; not direct clinical efficacy evidence.
