This source is included for **skin-of-color, visible-light pigmentation, melasma, or PIH safety boundary**.

**Findings:** Review source supports visible light as clinically relevant in photodermatology, including pigmentation and photosensitivity contexts.

**Why it matters:** Use to keep visible-light safety language in scope for skin-of-color and photosensitive users.

**Potential experiment signals:** photosensitivity history screen, melasma/PIH history screen.

**Protocol takeaway:** Visible light has dermatologic relevance beyond UV, including pigmentary and photosensitivity concerns. Does not establish red/NIR anti-aging effectiveness or a device dose.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

### Extraction notes

- **Population:** Photodermatology literature on visible light, photosensitivity, and pigmentary disorders.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Visible-light exposure from solar and artificial sources.
- **Comparator/control:** Not applicable; narrative context.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Not applicable.
- **Endpoints:** Photosensitivity, pigmentation, photodermatoses, and visible-light effects.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** Pigmentation and photosensitivity boundaries, especially for susceptible photodermatoses and darker skin types.
- **Limitations:** Review, not direct PBM trial.; Visible-light focus is broader than red/NIR protocol.
- **Population mismatch/directness:** Background safety context only.
- **Artifact/rights status:** paywalled.
