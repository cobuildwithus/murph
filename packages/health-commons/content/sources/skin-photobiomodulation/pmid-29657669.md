This source is included for **skin-of-color, visible-light pigmentation, melasma, or PIH safety boundary**.

**Findings:** The PBM-treated side showed statistically significant pigment reduction versus control in the extracted record; MASI on the treated side improved from 11.4 to 4.7 at week 12 (p<0.001), while melanin-index findings were less impressive.

**Why it matters:** Use only as adjacent melasma context and a reminder that pigment-disorder protocols are distinct from texture/photoaging protocols.

**Potential experiment signals:** MASI-like pigment score, melanin index, melasma flare watch.

**Protocol takeaway:** Small split-face melasma pilot suggested 940 nm pulsed PBM plus microdermabrasion reduced pigment versus control. Small combined-treatment design cannot establish general red/NIR photoaging safety or efficacy.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

### Extraction notes

- **Population:** Seven female patients with bilateral dermal melasma and prior unsuccessful topical/chemical-peel treatments.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Microdermabrasion plus low-energy pulsed 940 nm PBM photons weekly for eight weeks on the treated side.
- **Comparator/control:** Split-face control side receiving microdermabrasion without PBM.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Baseline to week 12, with limited longer follow-up reported for a subset.
- **Endpoints:** White-light/UV photos, Melasma Area and Severity Index (MASI), melanin index, and physician-rated pigment reduction.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No tissue damage emphasized in extracted conclusion; adverse-event extraction should remain cautious because the pilot was very small.
- **Limitations:** Very small pilot (n=7).; Melasma disease-treatment protocol, not photoaging texture protocol.; Combined microdermabrasion plus PBM makes isolated PBM attribution difficult.
- **Population mismatch/directness:** Adjacent pigment-disorder PBM; do not use as direct photoaging evidence.
- **Artifact/rights status:** open_access.
