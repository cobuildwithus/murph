This source is included for **skin-of-color, visible-light pigmentation, melasma, or PIH safety boundary**.

**Findings:** The review highlights that visible light can cause erythema in light skin and pigmentary changes in darker skin types; it focuses on disorders including melasma and post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation.

**Why it matters:** Supports screening and monitoring for melasma/PIH-prone users without overgeneralizing to all red/NIR wavelengths.

**Potential experiment signals:** PIH history screen, melasma flare tracking, skin-tone-specific counseling.

**Protocol takeaway:** Visible light can interact with skin differently by skin type, with pigmentary concerns in darker skin. Use as visible-light context, not protocol dosing evidence.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

### Extraction notes

- **Population:** Dermatology literature on visible-light interactions with skin and dermatologic diseases.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Visible light exposure across skin types and disease contexts.
- **Comparator/control:** Narrative comparison to other photobiologic exposures.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Not applicable.
- **Endpoints:** Erythema, pigmentary change, melasma, post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation, and visible-light photoprotection.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** Visible-light pigmentary change and erythema boundaries.
- **Limitations:** Review article rather than trial.; Not red/NIR-specific and not a photoaging efficacy source.
- **Population mismatch/directness:** Safety/background only; adjacent visible-light context.
- **Artifact/rights status:** unknown.
