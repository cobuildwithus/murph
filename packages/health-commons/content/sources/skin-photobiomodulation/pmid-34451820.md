This source is included for **photosensitizing-medication and retinoid safety boundary**.

**Findings:** Review describes how photoexcited drugs can trigger oxidative stress, inflammation, and melanin-related changes that manifest as erythema, blisters, burning, itching, and hyperpigmentation.

**Why it matters:** Use for adverse-event symptom language and medication screening, with wavelength caveats.

**Potential experiment signals:** burning/itching stop rule, blistering stop rule, PIH after reaction.

**Protocol takeaway:** Drug photosensitivity mechanisms can produce inflammatory and pigmentary skin reactions after light exposure. Do not claim red/NIR PBM triggers these reactions without direct evidence.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

### Extraction notes

- **Population:** Cellular, biochemical, and clinical drug-induced photosensitivity literature.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Photoexcitation and photoconversion of photosensitizing drugs under light exposure.
- **Comparator/control:** Not applicable.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Not applicable.
- **Endpoints:** Oxidative stress, inflammation, melanin synthesis changes, erythema, swelling, blisters, peeling, burning, itching, and hyperpigmentation.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** Symptoms include erythema, swelling, blisters, exudation, peeling, burning, itching, and hyperpigmentation.
- **Limitations:** Mechanistic review, not a red/NIR device study.; Much evidence concerns UV or broad visible light rather than the protocol wavelengths.
- **Population mismatch/directness:** Safety mechanism only.
- **Artifact/rights status:** open_access.
