This source is included for **skin-of-color, visible-light pigmentation, melasma, or PIH safety boundary**.

**Findings:** Review context indicates visible light can induce erythema and tanning particularly in darker skin, while infrared erythema is likely thermal in nature.

**Why it matters:** Use for boundary language that distinguishes visible-light pigmentation and thermal infrared effects from nonthermal red/NIR PBM claims.

**Potential experiment signals:** erythema stop rule, thermal sensation log, skin-of-color pigment monitoring.

**Protocol takeaway:** Review frames erythema and pigmentation risks across UV, visible, and infrared bands. Do not derive protocol dosing from this review alone.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

### Extraction notes

- **Population:** Human photobiology literature across UV, visible-light, and infrared exposures.
- **Intervention/exposure:** UV radiation, visible light, and infrared radiation exposures.
- **Comparator/control:** Narrative comparison across radiation bands and skin types.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Not applicable; review of varied studies.
- **Endpoints:** Erythema, tanning, pigmentation, infrared erythema, and photobiologic mechanisms.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** Pigmentation and erythema boundaries; thermal contribution for infrared exposure.
- **Limitations:** Narrative review with heterogeneous included evidence.; Not specific to consumer red/NIR PBM devices.; No pooled effect estimates.
- **Population mismatch/directness:** Background/safety boundary rather than direct protocol evidence.
- **Artifact/rights status:** paywalled.
