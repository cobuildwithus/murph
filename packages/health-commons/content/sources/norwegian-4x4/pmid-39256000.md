This source is included for **Adjacent variants to split**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Metabolic-syndrome RCT systematic review and meta-analysis (2024).
- **People studied or addressed:** Adults with metabolic syndrome (1,374 participants / 23 included studies).
- **Role in Murph:** adjacent-variant or recovery-context evidence; context only evidence; a related HIIT variant that should stay separate from standard 4x4.
- **Most relevant Murph signals:** estimated VO2max / cardio-fitness proxy, session heart-rate fidelity, heart-rate recovery, symptoms and adherence.

## Why it matters for Norwegian 4x4

Recent metabolic-syndrome HIIT synthesis; useful to separate metabolic HIIT and low-volume variants from standard 4x4.

## What it found

**Findings:** The review found HIIT can improve cardiometabolic outcomes in metabolic-syndrome populations compared with control or moderate-training comparators. The included protocols and populations were broader than Norwegian 4x4. The finding supports adjacent metabolic-HIIT context while keeping the standard 4x4 protocol separate.

## How Murph should use it

Use it for population and variant context, not as direct Norwegian 4x4 proof.

Use for variant separation, recovery context, or secondary-signal interpretation; do not count it as direct proof for the standard 4x4 self-experiment.

## Important limits

Metabolic-syndrome trials mix protocols, outcomes, and clinical populations.

The safe interpretation is narrower than “4x4 is always better.” Keep the population, supervision level, comparator, and exact interval dose visible before applying this source to a home wearable experiment.

## Plain-language takeaway

This is useful nearby evidence, but it should not be mixed into the direct claim that the standard four-interval recipe will improve a user's wearable fitness score.
