This source is included for **Adjacent variants to split**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Metabolic-syndrome RCT systematic review and meta-analysis (2024).
- **People studied or addressed:** Adults with metabolic syndrome (about 1,374 pooled participants / 23 included studies).
- **Role in Murph:** adjacent cardiometabolic context and variant separation.
- **Most relevant Murph signals:** estimated VO2max / cardio-fitness proxy, population fit, and variant separation.

## Why it matters for Norwegian 4x4

This is useful nearby evidence, but it should not be mistaken for direct proof of the exact four-interval recipe.

## What it found

**Findings:** Across 23 metabolic-syndrome randomized trials and about 1,374 pooled participants, HIIT improved several cardiometabolic outcomes versus controls or moderate training. The included programs mixed interval formats; subgroup analyses suggested lower-volume HIIT was not clearly inferior for some outcomes. That makes the paper useful for variant separation and cardiometabolic context, not as proof for the exact four-interval recipe.

## How Murph should use it

Use it to keep metabolic-risk and lower-volume HIIT papers in the right bucket instead of quietly importing them into the direct 4x4 claim.

## Important limits

The protocols, outcomes, and clinical populations are mixed. That breadth is exactly why this is context, not direct recipe proof.

## Plain-language takeaway

Useful nearby evidence, but it cannot tell you that classic Norwegian 4x4 is the only—or best—choice for metabolic-risk users.
