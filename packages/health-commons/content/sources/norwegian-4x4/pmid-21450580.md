This source is included for **Clinical lineage and population mismatch**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Essential-hypertension interval-training trial (2012).
- **People studied or addressed:** Adults with essential hypertension (88 participants).
- **Role in Murph:** clinical context and overclaim control; mixed evidence; screened or supervised clinical exercise, not unsupervised home training.
- **Most relevant Murph signals:** morning blood pressure context, estimated VO2max / cardio-fitness proxy, clinician-supervised symptoms and adherence.

## Why it matters for Norwegian 4x4

Supports why morning blood pressure can be tracked as context, but also why hypertension treatment claims need clinician-supervised framing.

## What it found

**Findings:** This hypertension trial reported blood-pressure and myocardial-function improvements with aerobic interval training. That makes blood pressure a useful context signal, but the study should not turn the Murph protocol into a hypertension treatment plan.

## How Murph should use it

Supports tracking blood pressure as context, but not promising blood-pressure treatment from a home 4x4 trial.

Use for blood-pressure context and hypertension guardrails only; the Murph protocol is not a hypertension treatment plan.

## Important limits

Uncontrolled hypertension is a clinician-guidance boundary for vigorous intervals.

The safe interpretation is narrower than “4x4 is always better.” Keep the population, supervision level, comparator, and exact interval dose visible before applying this source to a home wearable experiment.

## Plain-language takeaway

This helps explain where the 4x4 idea came from in supervised clinical exercise, but adults with essential hypertension are not the same as unscreened wellness users.
