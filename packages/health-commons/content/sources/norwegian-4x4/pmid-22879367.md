This source is included for **Safety and contraindications**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Supervised cardiac-rehabilitation safety registry (2012).
- **People studied or addressed:** Coronary heart disease cardiac-rehabilitation patients (4,846 participants).
- **Role in Murph:** safety and clinician-guidance boundary; supports safety boundaries; screened or supervised clinical exercise, not unsupervised home training.
- **Most relevant Murph signals:** estimated VO2max / cardio-fitness proxy, session heart-rate fidelity, heart-rate recovery, symptoms and adherence.

## Why it matters for Norwegian 4x4

Quantified supervised cardiac-rehabilitation safety registry; key low-but-not-zero risk framing.

## What it found

**Findings:** The registry compared adverse events during supervised high-intensity and moderate-intensity exercise in coronary heart disease rehabilitation. Serious events were rare across many training hours, but they were not zero. The finding supports a low-risk-with-supervision frame, not a blanket claim that high intensity is safe for everyone.

## How Murph should use it

Supports a low-risk-with-supervision frame and justifies strong stop-condition language.

Use to set screening, clinician-guidance, and stop-condition language; do not use it as evidence that unsupervised vigorous intervals are risk-free.

## Important limits

Screened supervised patients are not the same as unscreened home users.

The safe interpretation is narrower than “4x4 is always better.” Keep the population, supervision level, comparator, and exact interval dose visible before applying this source to a home wearable experiment.

## Plain-language takeaway

This is mainly a guardrail source: it helps decide who should get medical guidance, when to stop, and why screened supervision matters.
