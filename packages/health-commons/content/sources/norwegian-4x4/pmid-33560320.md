This source is included for **Mixed and null clinical evidence**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Randomized HFpEF exercise-mode trial (2021).
- **People studied or addressed:** Heart failure patients with preserved ejection fraction (180 participants).
- **Role in Murph:** clinical context and overclaim control; does not confirm evidence; screened or supervised clinical exercise, not unsupervised home training.
- **Most relevant Murph signals:** estimated VO2max / cardio-fitness proxy, session heart-rate fidelity, heart-rate recovery, symptoms and adherence.

## Why it matters for Norwegian 4x4

HFpEF clinical trial showing no significant primary peak-VO2 advantage for HIIT over comparators.

## What it found

**Findings:** The HFpEF randomized trial found no statistically significant, clinically meaningful advantage for HIIT or moderate training over guideline-based physical-activity advice on peak VO2. Short-term changes did not become strong group differences at 12 months. The finding argues against applying healthy-adult 4x4 claims to HFpEF treatment.

## How Murph should use it

Keeps the Murph claim bounded to a fitness experiment, not a treatment claim for heart failure.

Use to calibrate clinical context and avoid overclaiming superiority; do not treat supervised disease-population results as direct home-protocol proof.

## Important limits

HFpEF is a clinical population with different risks and supervision needs.

The safe interpretation is narrower than “4x4 is always better.” Keep the population, supervision level, comparator, and exact interval dose visible before applying this source to a home wearable experiment.

## Plain-language takeaway

This helps explain where the 4x4 idea came from in supervised clinical exercise, but heart-failure patients with preserved ejection fraction are not the same as unscreened wellness users.
