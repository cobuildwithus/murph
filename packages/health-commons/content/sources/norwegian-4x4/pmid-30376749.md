This source is included for **Safety and contraindications**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Cardiovascular-disease HIIT safety systematic review (2018).
- **People studied or addressed:** Cardiovascular-disease rehabilitation patients (about 1,117 pooled participants / 23 included studies).
- **Role in Murph:** safety boundary; helps explain who should not treat vigorous intervals casually.
- **Most relevant Murph signals:** symptoms and adherence, clinician-guidance boundaries, and stop-condition language.

## Why it matters for Norwegian 4x4

Low risk in cardiac rehab is not the same thing as no risk at home.

## What it found

**Findings:** Across 23 studies and more than 17,000 HIIT sessions in screened cardiovascular-disease populations, major adverse events were rare in supervised settings. That is reassuring as rehabilitation-context evidence, but it does not erase risk once screening, monitoring, medication review, and escalation plans disappear.

## How Murph should use it

Use it to justify strict screening questions and clinician-guidance boundaries for higher-risk users.

## Important limits

The participants were screened, supervised, and medically managed. That is exactly why this review is a boundary-setting source rather than a home-clearance source.

## Plain-language takeaway

Low risk in cardiac rehab is encouraging, but it is not permission to ignore symptoms or skip medical guidance when vigorous exercise may be risky.
