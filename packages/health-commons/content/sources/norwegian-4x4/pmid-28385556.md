- **Source type:** Narrative review and practical HIIT guidance (2017).
- **People studied or addressed:** Clinical and general aerobic HIIT literature.
- **Role in Murph:** framing and language calibration, not direct proof.
- **Most relevant Murph signals:** estimated VO2max / cardio-fitness proxy, implementation context, symptoms and adherence.

## Why it matters for Norwegian 4x4

It helps explain the broader story around 4x4 without pretending that one narrative review can answer the whole question.

## What it found

**Findings:** This CERG-lineage narrative review explains how HIIT is discussed across clinical and health-outcome settings, including home-based interest. It is helpful framing material, but the direct effect claim should still come from the underlying trials and meta-analyses.

## How Murph should use it

Use it to explain where the protocol fits in the literature, not to replace the direct intervention evidence.

## Important limits

Narrative reviews are useful for framing but do not settle protocol-specific effect size, population fit, or safety by themselves.

## Plain-language takeaway

Good for understanding the story around 4x4, not for replacing the actual trials.
