This source is included for **Clinical lineage and population mismatch**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Small supervised heart-failure interval-training trial (2007).
- **People studied or addressed:** Stable post-infarction heart failure patients (27 participants).
- **Role in Murph:** clinical context and overclaim control; mixed evidence; screened or supervised clinical exercise, not unsupervised home training.
- **Most relevant Murph signals:** estimated VO2max / cardio-fitness proxy, left-ventricular function context, clinician-supervised symptoms and adherence.

## Why it matters for Norwegian 4x4

Important early positive 4x4-lineage RCT, but its heart-failure population and supervision level make it inappropriate as direct wellness evidence.

## What it found

**Findings:** This randomized heart-failure study reported stronger cardiovascular effects from aerobic interval training than from moderate continuous training. The positive physiologic signal is useful lineage evidence, but the disease population and supervision requirements keep it from being direct home-protocol proof.

## How Murph should use it

Useful for mechanism and lineage, but not for unsupervised disease-treatment claims.

Use as clinical lineage and overclaim guardrail; do not treat heart-failure rehabilitation evidence as permission for unsupervised vigorous intervals.

## Important limits

Heart-failure exercise protocols require clinical screening and supervision.

The safe interpretation is narrower than “4x4 is always better.” Keep the population, supervision level, comparator, and exact interval dose visible before applying this source to a home wearable experiment.

## Plain-language takeaway

This helps explain where the 4x4 idea came from in supervised clinical exercise, but stable post-infarction heart-failure patients are not the same as unscreened wellness users.
