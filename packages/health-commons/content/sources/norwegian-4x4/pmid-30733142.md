This source is included for **Protocol dose and design**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Meta-analysis of randomized HIIT protocol trials (2019).
- **People studied or addressed:** Healthy, overweight/obese, and athletic adults (1,514 participants / 53 included studies).
- **Role in Murph:** direct or close support for the cardio-fitness claim; supports evidence; same aerobic-HIIT mechanism, but not always this exact Murph recipe.
- **Most relevant Murph signals:** estimated VO2max / cardio-fitness proxy, session heart-rate fidelity, heart-rate recovery, symptoms and adherence.

## Why it matters for Norwegian 4x4

Meta-analysis used to justify long-interval HIIT as a plausible VO2max-oriented design class.

## What it found

**Findings:** The meta-analysis found HIIT improved VO2max across adult populations and protocol types. Longer intervals, higher total interval volume, and multi-week programs showed stronger effects. The finding supports the long-interval design class behind 4x4 without proving every implementation is superior.

## How Murph should use it

Supports the design choice to use repeated 4-minute aerobic intervals rather than treating all HIIT as interchangeable.

Use as support for a bounded cardio-fitness experiment when the hard intervals actually reach the intended aerobic intensity.

## Important limits

It pools many HIIT protocols and adults, so it is support for the protocol family, not only Norwegian 4x4.

The safe interpretation is narrower than “4x4 is always better.” Keep the population, supervision level, comparator, and exact interval dose visible before applying this source to a home wearable experiment.

## Plain-language takeaway

For a generally healthy user, this belongs in the evidence pile that makes a 6-week 4x4 fitness test plausible, as long as the session is actually hard enough and recovery stays reasonable.
