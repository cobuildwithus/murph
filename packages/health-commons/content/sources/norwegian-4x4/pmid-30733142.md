This source is included for **Broader HIIT and VO2max context**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Meta-analysis of randomized HIIT protocol trials (2019).
- **People studied or addressed:** Healthy, overweight/obese, and athletic adults (about 1,514 pooled participants / 53 included studies).
- **Role in Murph:** broader programming context, not exact-dose proof.
- **Most relevant Murph signals:** estimated VO2max / cardio-fitness proxy and the design logic behind using long aerobic intervals.

## Why it matters for Norwegian 4x4

It helps explain why 4-minute intervals are a plausible programming choice instead of just another arbitrary HIIT format.

## What it found

**Findings:** Across 53 randomized HIIT trials and about 1,514 pooled participants, HIIT improved VO2max overall. Subgroup analyses favored longer intervals, higher total interval volume, and programs lasting at least several weeks, which makes the 4-minute interval design more plausible—but the paper pooled many HIIT recipes rather than testing only Norwegian 4x4.

## How Murph should use it

Use it to support the design logic behind 4x4, not to claim that all HIIT evidence is exact 4x4 evidence.

## Important limits

This meta-analysis mixes protocols, populations, and comparators. It supports the broader family, not one exact home-experiment recipe.

## Plain-language takeaway

This is useful background for why long intervals make sense, but it is still one step removed from the exact “does Norwegian 4x4 work for me?” question.
