This source is included for **Public health context**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** World Health Organization physical-activity guideline (2020).
- **People studied or addressed:** Children, adolescents, adults, older adults, pregnant/postpartum people, and people living with chronic conditions or disability.
- **Role in Murph:** safety and clinician-guidance boundary; context only evidence; broad guideline context, not protocol-specific efficacy evidence.
- **Most relevant Murph signals:** estimated VO2max / cardio-fitness proxy, session heart-rate fidelity, heart-rate recovery, symptoms and adherence.

## Why it matters for Norwegian 4x4

Public-health context showing vigorous aerobic exercise as one option within weekly activity guidance.

## What it found

**Findings:** The WHO guideline recommends regular aerobic activity for adults and recognizes vigorous activity as one valid way to meet weekly targets. It also emphasizes reducing sedentary behavior and adapting activity to health status. The finding supports public-health context, not a specific 4x4 efficacy claim.

## How Murph should use it

Use as background that movement matters, while keeping Norwegian 4x4 as one bounded experiment.

Use to set screening, clinician-guidance, and stop-condition language; do not use it as evidence that unsupervised vigorous intervals are risk-free.

## Important limits

Public-health guidance is not evidence that high-intensity intervals are the best fit for every user.

The safe interpretation is narrower than “4x4 is always better.” Keep the population, supervision level, comparator, and exact interval dose visible before applying this source to a home wearable experiment.

## Plain-language takeaway

This is mainly a guardrail source: it helps decide who should get medical guidance, when to stop, and why screened supervision matters.
