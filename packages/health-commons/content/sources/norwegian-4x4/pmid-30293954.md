This source is included for **Wearable or testable signals**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Systematic review of cardiac autonomic-control outcomes (2019).
- **People studied or addressed:** Healthy adults and cardiometabolic-risk participants (6 included studies).
- **Role in Murph:** recovery-context evidence, not a primary outcome source.
- **Most relevant Murph signals:** HRV RMSSD, resting heart rate, and recovery interpretation.

## Why it matters for Norwegian 4x4

It helps stop people from turning HRV into the headline outcome when the literature is much messier than that.

## What it found

**Findings:** The autonomic-control review found HIIT-related changes in HRV and related markers were small, inconsistent, and hard to interpret across a small, heterogeneous literature. That is exactly why HRV should stay a recovery-context metric rather than the main verdict on whether 4x4 “worked.”

## How Murph should use it

Use it to keep recovery metrics in the right role: helpful context, not the main promise.

## Important limits

Autonomic measures are highly sensitive to sleep, illness, alcohol, stress, and overall training load, so a flat or worse HRV signal is not automatically proof that the protocol failed.

## Plain-language takeaway

HRV can help you notice under-recovery, but it is too noisy to be the headline outcome.
