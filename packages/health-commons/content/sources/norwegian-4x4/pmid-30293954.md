This source is included for **Wearable or testable signals**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Systematic review of cardiac autonomic-control outcomes (2019).
- **People studied or addressed:** Healthy adults and cardiometabolic-risk participants (6 included studies).
- **Role in Murph:** adjacent-variant or recovery-context evidence; mixed evidence; useful for measurement and logging, not for efficacy proof.
- **Most relevant Murph signals:** estimated VO2max / cardio-fitness proxy, session heart-rate fidelity, heart-rate recovery, symptoms and adherence.

## Why it matters for Norwegian 4x4

HRV/autonomic-control synthesis; useful for setting cautious expectations around HRV.

## What it found

**Findings:** The review found limited evidence that HIIT may change cardiac autonomic-control markers such as HRV in some populations. The evidence base was small and heterogeneous. The finding makes HRV an exploratory signal, not a primary verdict for Norwegian 4x4.

## How Murph should use it

Keep HRV and sleep as recovery context rather than promised outcomes.

Use to improve logging and intensity interpretation; do not treat measurement-context evidence as proof of a health outcome.

## Important limits

Autonomic measures are sensitive to illness, alcohol, stress, sleep, and total training load.

The safe interpretation is narrower than “4x4 is always better.” Keep the population, supervision level, comparator, and exact interval dose visible before applying this source to a home wearable experiment.

## Plain-language takeaway

This is useful nearby evidence, but it should not be mixed into the direct claim that the standard four-interval recipe will improve a user's wearable fitness score.
