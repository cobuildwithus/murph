This source is included for **Clinical lineage and population mismatch**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Small supervised coronary artery disease intensity-comparison trial (2004).
- **People studied or addressed:** Stable coronary artery disease patients (21 participants).
- **Role in Murph:** clinical context and overclaim control; mixed evidence; screened or supervised clinical exercise, not unsupervised home training.
- **Most relevant Murph signals:** estimated VO2max / cardio-fitness proxy, clinician-supervised intensity fidelity, symptoms and adverse events.

## Why it matters for Norwegian 4x4

Adds the early CAD 4x4-lineage trial so positive disease-treatment data is visible without being mistaken for unscreened self-experiment evidence.

## What it found

**Findings:** The trial reported that supervised high-intensity aerobic interval exercise improved aerobic capacity more than moderate exercise in stable coronary artery disease patients. Because the population was screened and clinically supervised, the finding belongs in the clinical-lineage evidence rather than the ordinary unsupervised wellness claim base.

## How Murph should use it

Supports the clinical lineage of 4x4-style training while staying population-bounded.

Use as clinical lineage and population-mismatch context only; do not use it as direct support for unscreened users self-treating coronary disease.

## Important limits

Coronary disease exercise should not be self-prescribed from this trial.

The safe interpretation is narrower than “4x4 is always better.” Keep the population, supervision level, comparator, and exact interval dose visible before applying this source to a home wearable experiment.

## Plain-language takeaway

This helps explain where the 4x4 idea came from in supervised clinical exercise, but stable coronary artery disease patients are not the same as unscreened wellness users.
