This source is included for **Wearable or testable signals**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Single-group crossover implementation study (2014).
- **People studied or addressed:** Cardiac-rehabilitation participants (10 participants).
- **Role in Murph:** direct or close support for the cardio-fitness claim; supports evidence; useful for measurement and logging, not for efficacy proof.
- **Most relevant Murph signals:** estimated VO2max / cardio-fitness proxy, session heart-rate fidelity, heart-rate recovery, symptoms and adherence.

## Why it matters for Norwegian 4x4

Implementation caution source: RPE-only guidance can miss the intended HR target during interval training.

## What it found

**Findings:** The crossover study tested whether Borg-scale perceived exertion kept cardiac-rehabilitation participants in the target interval intensity. RPE-guided sessions tended to undershoot the desired high-intensity heart-rate zone. The finding supports heart-rate monitoring when the protocol depends on hitting a specific intensity.

## How Murph should use it

Prefer heart-rate feedback plus symptoms and RPE when testing the actual 4x4 dose.

Use to improve logging and intensity interpretation; do not treat measurement-context evidence as proof of a health outcome.

## Important limits

Heart-rate estimates and wrist sensors can also be wrong, especially during vigorous movement.

The safe interpretation is narrower than “4x4 is always better.” Keep the population, supervision level, comparator, and exact interval dose visible before applying this source to a home wearable experiment.

## Plain-language takeaway

For a generally healthy user, this belongs in the evidence pile that makes a 6-week 4x4 fitness test plausible, as long as the session is actually hard enough and recovery stays reasonable.
