This source is included for **Dose, target zone, and implementation**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Single-group crossover implementation study (2014).
- **People studied or addressed:** Cardiac-rehabilitation participants (10 participants).
- **Role in Murph:** implementation caution; useful for making sure the session actually hits the planned intensity.
- **Most relevant Murph signals:** estimated VO2max / cardio-fitness proxy, session heart-rate fidelity, heart-rate recovery, symptoms and adherence.

## Why it matters for Norwegian 4x4

Feeling “pretty hard” does not always mean the interval really landed in the intended 4x4 zone.

## What it found

**Findings:** In supervised cardiac rehabilitation, Borg-scale perceived exertion alone tended to undershoot the intended high-intensity interval target compared with heart-rate monitoring. For a true 4x4 experiment, that means effort feeling matters, but heart-rate feedback makes it much easier to know whether the planned dose really happened.

## How Murph should use it

Use it as a reminder that the goal is not just to feel challenged—it is to actually reach the intended training zone safely.

## Important limits

Heart-rate estimates and wrist sensors can be wrong too, especially during vigorous movement, so the answer is better monitoring and judgment, not blind faith in one number.

## Plain-language takeaway

Feeling “pretty hard” does not always mean you actually reached the 4x4 target.
