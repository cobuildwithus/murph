This source is included for **Mixed and null clinical evidence**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Multicenter randomized heart-failure trial (2017).
- **People studied or addressed:** Heart failure patients with reduced ejection fraction (261 participants).
- **Role in Murph:** clinical context and overclaim control; does not confirm evidence; screened or supervised clinical exercise, not unsupervised home training.
- **Most relevant Murph signals:** estimated VO2max / cardio-fitness proxy, session heart-rate fidelity, heart-rate recovery, symptoms and adherence.

## Why it matters for Norwegian 4x4

Larger HFrEF trial that did not confirm clear HIIT superiority and highlighted intensity-fidelity issues.

## What it found

**Findings:** This heart-failure trial did not confirm a clear durable superiority of HIIT over moderate training or recommended regular exercise. It also highlighted that participants often missed the prescribed high-intensity target. The finding warns that clinical 4x4-style protocols need supervision and intensity checks.

## How Murph should use it

Use it as a guardrail: the intended dose matters, and clinical outcomes do not automatically transfer to home 4x4.

Use to calibrate clinical context and avoid overclaiming superiority; do not treat supervised disease-population results as direct home-protocol proof.

## Important limits

Heart-failure participants need clinician-directed exercise decisions.

The safe interpretation is narrower than “4x4 is always better.” Keep the population, supervision level, comparator, and exact interval dose visible before applying this source to a home wearable experiment.

## Plain-language takeaway

This helps explain where the 4x4 idea came from in supervised clinical exercise, but heart-failure patients with reduced ejection fraction are not the same as unscreened wellness users.
