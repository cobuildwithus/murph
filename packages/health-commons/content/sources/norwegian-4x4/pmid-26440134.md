This source is included for **Exact or close 4x4 trials**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Three-arm 4x4 versus 1x4 versus moderate-continuous trial (2016).
- **People studied or addressed:** Overweight and obese adults (30 participants).
- **Role in Murph:** direct fit for the exact 4x4 claim and the clearest direct variant-separation paper.
- **Most relevant Murph signals:** estimated VO2max / cardio-fitness proxy, session heart-rate fidelity, variant separation.

## Why it matters for Norwegian 4x4

This is the direct paper that most clearly says classic 4x4 and low-volume 1x4 are not the same thing.

## What it found

**Findings:** Thirty overweight and obese adults were randomized to 6 weeks of 4x4, 1x4, or 45-minute moderate continuous training. Only the 4x4 group improved VO2max clearly more than the other two groups—about a 10% gain versus about 3% in the 1x4 and moderate groups—and estimated stroke volume improved only after 4x4. That makes this one of the strongest direct reasons to keep the standard four-interval dose separate from low-volume HIIT.

## How Murph should use it

Use it to support both the main fitness claim and the rule that 4x4 and 1x4 should not be merged into one bucket.

## Important limits

The comparison was still a small structured training study in a specific population, not a free-living wearable experiment.

## Plain-language takeaway

This is the paper that most directly says “4x4 is not the same as 1x4.”
