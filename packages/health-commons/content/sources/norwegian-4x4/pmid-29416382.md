This source is included for **Safety and contraindications**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Cardiac-rehabilitation systematic review and meta-analysis (2018).
- **People studied or addressed:** Cardiac-rehabilitation participants (about 953 pooled participants / 17 included studies).
- **Role in Murph:** supervised-setting context and safety boundary.
- **Most relevant Murph signals:** screening context, symptoms, and stop-condition language.

## Why it matters for Norwegian 4x4

It helps explain why rehab evidence should make the safety language more careful, not more casual.

## What it found

**Findings:** This cardiac-rehabilitation synthesis found that HIIT and moderate continuous training both improved fitness inside supervised rehabilitation programs. That is useful when writing safety language, but the whole point is that screening, monitoring, and clinical escalation were built into the setting.

## How Murph should use it

Use it to reinforce that "worked in rehab" and "appropriate for an unscreened home user" are two different claims.

## Important limits

Cardiac rehab is a clinical care environment. That changes both the risk calculation and the meaning of a successful session.

## Plain-language takeaway

This is more about the boundary around safe supervision than about telling a healthy home user what result to expect.
