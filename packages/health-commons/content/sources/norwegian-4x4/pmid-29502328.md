This source is included for **Clinical synthesis and overclaim guardrails**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Updated CAD and heart-failure aerobic-interval meta-analysis (2018).
- **People studied or addressed:** Patients with coronary artery disease or heart failure (about 1,080 pooled participants / 24 included studies).
- **Role in Murph:** clinical lineage and overclaim control; important context, but not direct home-protocol proof.
- **Most relevant Murph signals:** estimated VO2max / cardio-fitness proxy, clinical secondary outcomes, safety and supervision context.

## Why it matters for Norwegian 4x4

It helps explain why 4x4 shows up so often in cardiac-rehab discussions without letting rehab evidence turn into general self-treatment advice.

## What it found

**Findings:** Across 24 studies and about 1,080 pooled clinical participants with coronary artery disease or heart failure, aerobic interval training often improved peak VO2 more than continuous training, especially in some CAD and HFrEF subgroup analyses, but many secondary outcomes were similar and the authors called for more evidence on safety and longer-term sustainability. That makes this useful lineage context, not proof that home 4x4 treats cardiovascular disease.

## How Murph should use it

Use it to calibrate the strength of the claim: interesting and sometimes favorable in supervised clinical care, but not clean permission to oversell home 4x4.

## Important limits

Clinical synthesis is valuable context, but the participants, medications, supervision, and escalation paths are not the same as those in an unscreened home experiment.

## Plain-language takeaway

This helps explain where 4x4 came from in cardiac rehab, but rehab patients are not the same as unscreened wellness users.
