This source is included for **Clinical synthesis and overclaim guardrails**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Updated CAD and heart-failure aerobic-interval meta-analysis (2018).
- **People studied or addressed:** Patients with coronary artery disease or heart failure (1,080 participants / 24 included studies).
- **Role in Murph:** clinical context and overclaim control; mixed evidence; screened or supervised clinical exercise, not unsupervised home training.
- **Most relevant Murph signals:** estimated VO2max / cardio-fitness proxy, clinical secondary outcomes, safety and supervision context.

## Why it matters for Norwegian 4x4

Adds a clinical synthesis around CAD/HF trials so early positive disease-population studies do not dominate the practical claim set.

## What it found

**Findings:** This updated CAD/HF systematic review and meta-analysis synthesizes interval-versus-continuous training evidence in clinical cardiovascular populations. It keeps disease-population outcomes visible while preventing those results from standing in for unscreened home 4x4 evidence.

## How Murph should use it

Use it to calibrate the strength of superiority claims and keep population boundaries visible.

Use as clinical synthesis and overclaim guardrail; do not cite it as direct proof for unscreened home 4x4.

## Important limits

Clinical synthesis is valuable context, but not direct evidence for unscreened users.

The safe interpretation is narrower than “4x4 is always better.” Keep the population, supervision level, comparator, and exact interval dose visible before applying this source to a home wearable experiment.

## Plain-language takeaway

This helps explain where the 4x4 idea came from in supervised clinical exercise, but patients with coronary artery disease or heart failure are not the same as unscreened wellness users.
