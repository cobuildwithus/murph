This source is included for **Context-only rationale**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Meta-analysis of HIIT and VO2max trainability (2013).
- **People studied or addressed:** Healthy sedentary or recreationally active adults under 45 (334 participants / 37 included studies).
- **Role in Murph:** direct or close support for the cardio-fitness claim; supports evidence; same aerobic-HIIT mechanism, but not always this exact Murph recipe.
- **Most relevant Murph signals:** estimated VO2max / cardio-fitness proxy, session heart-rate fidelity, heart-rate recovery, symptoms and adherence.

## Why it matters for Norwegian 4x4

Open-access meta-analysis supporting the general concept of VO2max trainability from HIIT while showing response variability.

## What it found

**Findings:** This meta-analysis found that HIIT generally improves VO2max over short interventions. It also showed meaningful response variation across studies and participants. The finding supports estimated VO2max as a plausible signal while keeping expectations individualized.

## How Murph should use it

Use as broader mechanism support for why cardio-fitness can move over weeks.

Use as support for a bounded cardio-fitness experiment when the hard intervals actually reach the intended aerobic intensity.

## Important limits

This is not specific to the Murph recipe and should not override direct 4x4 evidence.

The safe interpretation is narrower than “4x4 is always better.” Keep the population, supervision level, comparator, and exact interval dose visible before applying this source to a home wearable experiment.

## Plain-language takeaway

For a generally healthy user, this belongs in the evidence pile that makes a 6-week 4x4 fitness test plausible, as long as the session is actually hard enough and recovery stays reasonable.
