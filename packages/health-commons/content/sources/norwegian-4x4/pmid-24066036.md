This source is included for **Broader HIIT and VO2max context**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Meta-analysis of HIIT and VO2max trainability (2013).
- **People studied or addressed:** Healthy sedentary or recreationally active adults under 45 (about 334 pooled participants / 37 included studies).
- **Role in Murph:** broader context, not exact-dose proof.
- **Most relevant Murph signals:** estimated VO2max / cardio-fitness proxy and realistic expectations about response variability.

## Why it matters for Norwegian 4x4

It helps explain why cardio fitness is a sensible thing to measure, while reminding you that people do not all respond the same way.

## What it found

**Findings:** Across 37 pooled studies and about 334 participants, HIIT increased VO2max on average, with a mean gain around 0.5 L/min, but response size varied. Longer work intervals and multi-week programs were among the patterns associated with larger gains. This supports VO2max as a plausible target while reminding you that individual responses are not identical.

## How Murph should use it

Use it to keep expectations honest: cardio fitness can move, but effect size is not one fixed number and the exact recipe still matters.

## Important limits

This is not specific to the Murph recipe and should never override the direct 4x4 trials or the dose-fidelity papers.

## Plain-language takeaway

This tells you that interval training can improve cardio fitness, but it does not prove the exact 4x4 recipe is necessary—or guarantee your wearable score will budge.
