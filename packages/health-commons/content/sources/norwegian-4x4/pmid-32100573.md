This source is included for **Safety and contraindications**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** American Heart Association scientific statement (2020).
- **People studied or addressed:** Adults considering moderate-to-vigorous or high-volume exercise.
- **Role in Murph:** safety and clinician-guidance boundary; supports safety boundaries; broad guideline context, not protocol-specific efficacy evidence.
- **Most relevant Murph signals:** estimated VO2max / cardio-fitness proxy, session heart-rate fidelity, heart-rate recovery, symptoms and adherence.

## Why it matters for Norwegian 4x4

AHA scientific statement for acute cardiovascular event risk and progression language.

## What it found

**Findings:** The AHA statement emphasizes that regular physical activity lowers cardiovascular risk, while strenuous exercise can transiently raise acute event risk in susceptible people. That risk is especially relevant for inactive people or people with unrecognized disease starting vigorous exercise. The finding supports gradual progression and screening language.

## How Murph should use it

Supports caution language for symptoms, recent illness, heart disease, and other higher-risk situations.

Use to set screening, clinician-guidance, and stop-condition language; do not use it as evidence that unsupervised vigorous intervals are risk-free.

## Important limits

Guidelines frame risk management; they do not test the Murph 4x4 recipe.

The safe interpretation is narrower than “4x4 is always better.” Keep the population, supervision level, comparator, and exact interval dose visible before applying this source to a home wearable experiment.

## Plain-language takeaway

This is mainly a guardrail source: it helps decide who should get medical guidance, when to stop, and why screened supervision matters.
