This source is included for **Mixed and null clinical evidence**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Multicenter randomized cardiac-rehabilitation trial (2015).
- **People studied or addressed:** Stable coronary artery disease patients (200 participants).
- **Role in Murph:** clinical context and overclaim control; does not confirm evidence; screened or supervised clinical exercise, not unsupervised home training.
- **Most relevant Murph signals:** estimated VO2max / cardio-fitness proxy, session heart-rate fidelity, heart-rate recovery, symptoms and adherence.

## Why it matters for Norwegian 4x4

Larger CAD trial that prevents overclaiming HIIT superiority over moderate continuous training.

## What it found

**Findings:** The SAINTEX-CAD trial found that aerobic interval training and continuous training both improved exercise capacity in stable coronary artery disease patients. It did not show a clear superiority of interval training over continuous training. The finding is an overclaim guardrail for disease-treatment or superiority claims.

## How Murph should use it

This prevents claiming 4x4 is always better than moderate cardio.

Use to calibrate clinical context and avoid overclaiming superiority; do not treat supervised disease-population results as direct home-protocol proof.

## Important limits

The setting was supervised clinical rehabilitation, not a general wellness experiment.

The safe interpretation is narrower than “4x4 is always better.” Keep the population, supervision level, comparator, and exact interval dose visible before applying this source to a home wearable experiment.

## Plain-language takeaway

This helps explain where the 4x4 idea came from in supervised clinical exercise, but stable coronary artery disease patients are not the same as unscreened wellness users.
