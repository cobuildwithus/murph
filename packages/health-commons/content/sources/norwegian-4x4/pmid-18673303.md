This source is included for **Clinical lineage and population mismatch**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Overweight adolescent interval-training trial (2009).
- **People studied or addressed:** Overweight adolescents (54 participants).
- **Role in Murph:** clinical context and overclaim control; mixed evidence; screened or supervised clinical exercise, not unsupervised home training.
- **Most relevant Murph signals:** estimated VO2max / cardio-fitness proxy, cardiovascular risk-factor context, adherence and supervision context.

## Why it matters for Norwegian 4x4

Adds pediatric/adolescent lineage evidence so the bibliography does not silently generalize adolescent clinical-risk findings to adult self-experiments.

## What it found

**Findings:** This adolescent overweight/obesity trial reported larger cardiovascular-risk-factor improvements with aerobic interval training than with a multitreatment comparison. The result is adjacent pediatric clinical context, not direct evidence for an adult home 4x4 recipe.

## How Murph should use it

Use as population-specific context, not as proof for the adult home protocol.

Use as adjacent clinical context only; adolescent overweight trials should not directly set adult unsupervised protocol claims.

## Important limits

Youth, obesity-treatment, and program-supervision context are not interchangeable with Murph's general experiment.

The safe interpretation is narrower than “4x4 is always better.” Keep the population, supervision level, comparator, and exact interval dose visible before applying this source to a home wearable experiment.

## Plain-language takeaway

This helps explain where the 4x4 idea came from in supervised clinical exercise, but overweight adolescents are not the same as unscreened wellness users.
