This source is included for **Interactions, contraindications, and population boundaries**.

**Findings:**
- `finding:pmid-22389767-polar-fractions-cyp-pgp` — The study reports that polar fractions of red yeast rice extracts enhanced P-glycoprotein activity and inhibited CYP1A2, CYP2C9, and CYP3A4 in mechanistic assays, creating plausible herb-drug interaction risk.
- `finding:pmid-22389767-untreated-fractions-verapamil-pk` — In Caco-2 and animal-model testing, untreated red yeast rice extract produced greater pharmacokinetic modulation than extract treated to remove the interaction-active polar fractions, indicating product-processing differences can affect interaction potential.

**Why it matters:** Mechanistically supports medication-interaction screening even when clinical event rates are unknown.

**Potential experiment signals:** CYP/P-gp-medication-screen, concurrent-verapamil-or-substrate-screen, drug-interaction-review-required.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as mechanistic safety evidence: red yeast rice fractions can affect CYP/P-gp pathways, but this does not establish human clinical event risk or efficacy.

**Limitations:** Non-human and mechanistic; product fractions and extraction methods may not match commercial supplements; no clinical adverse-event incidence is estimated.

**Population mismatch:** No human protocol population; applies to mechanism and interaction plausibility only.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.
