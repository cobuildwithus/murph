## Quick read

- **Source type:** Narrative review of nonfasting versus fasting lipid profiles for risk prediction (2019).
- **People or setting studied:** People undergoing lipid-profile testing for cardiovascular-risk prediction.
- **Exposure or intervention:** Nonfasting lipid-profile testing.
- **Comparator or control:** Fasting lipid-profile testing.
- **Duration or follow-up:** Narrative review; no intervention follow-up.
- **Role in Murph:** context-only / measurement_context.

## Findings

This review argues that nonfasting lipid profiles are useful for cardiovascular-risk prediction and that normal food intake changes many lipid values only minimally. It supports labeling fasting state rather than treating nonfasting panels as unusable.

**Effect estimate or direction extracted:** Search-extracted review summaries state that normal food intake causes minimal lipid/lipoprotein changes and that nonfasting triglycerides have been reported to predict cardiovascular risk; the review argues there is no sound evidence that fasting is uniformly superior.

## Why it matters

Useful for protocol text that avoids telling every user a nonfasting lipid panel is invalid.

## Protocol takeaway

Nonfasting panels can be acceptable context, but matched fasting status remains preferred for pre/post experimental comparisons.
