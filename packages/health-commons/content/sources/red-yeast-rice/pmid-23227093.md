This source is included for **Interactions, contraindications, and population boundaries**.

**Findings:**
- `finding:pmid-23227093-ryr-extracts-cyp-pgp` — At equivalent lovastatin concentrations, several red yeast rice extracts inhibited CYP450/P-glycoprotein pathways more effectively than pure lovastatin, with especially strong signals for CYP1A2 and CYP2C19 in the reported assays.
- `finding:pmid-23227093-nifedipine-no-effect` — In the healthy-volunteer nifedipine substudy, LipoCol Forte pretreatment did not significantly change nifedipine pharmacokinetic parameters, showing that not all CYP3A4/P-gp-related probes translated into a measurable clinical PK effect in this small sample.
- `finding:pmid-23227093-gemfibrozil-increases-lovastatin-acid` — Gemfibrozil coadministration significantly increased lovastatin acid exposure from red yeast rice: reported AUC0-12 increased from 31.1 ± 16.5 to 65.5 ± 31.6 ng·h/mL and Cmax from 6.79 ± 3.78 to 14.31 ± 7.43 ng/mL, while lovastatin itself was not significantly changed.

**Why it matters:** Provides direct human pharmacokinetic safety context for interacting drugs: a fibrate raised lovastatin-acid exposure from red yeast rice, while a nifedipine probe was null.

**Potential experiment signals:** fibrate-medication-screen, CYP3A4/P-gp-medication-screen, lovastatin-like-exposure-risk, CK, muscle-symptom-log.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as same-mechanism safety evidence: avoid or require clinician review for fibrates such as gemfibrozil and interpret CYP/P-gp concerns with nuance because not every probe showed a clinical PK change.

**Limitations:** Small healthy-volunteer pharmacokinetic substudies; specific product LipoCol Forte may not match commercial supplements; not powered for clinical adverse events.

**Population mismatch:** Healthy-volunteer PK data do not directly estimate risk in older, comorbid, statin-intolerant, or polypharmacy populations.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.
