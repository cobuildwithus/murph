## Quick read

- **Source type:** Narrative review on fasting versus nonfasting lipid profiles (2017).
- **People or setting studied:** Patients and clinicians using lipid-profile testing for cardiovascular-risk assessment.
- **Exposure or intervention:** Fasting versus nonfasting lipid-profile workflow.
- **Comparator or control:** Traditional fasting lipid testing versus nonfasting lipid testing.
- **Duration or follow-up:** Narrative review; no participant follow-up.
- **Role in Murph:** context-only / measurement_context.

## Findings

This review explains fasting versus nonfasting lipid-profile workflow and why nonfasting profiles can be clinically practical, while fasting may still be useful in specific measurement situations.

**Effect estimate or direction extracted:** Review context notes that nonfasting conditions predominate through most of the day and may reflect usual circulating atherogenic lipoproteins; it also frames nonfasting testing as simpler for many workflows.

## Why it matters

Gives practical language for a protocol test plan: fasting status is a workflow choice that should be matched or logged.

## Potential experiment signals

fasting status, lipid profile, triglycerides, calculated LDL-C

## Protocol takeaway

Use fasting or nonfasting lipid panels consistently; repeat fasting if triglycerides are very high or calculated LDL-C is uncertain.
