## Quick read

- **Source type:** Prospective cohort comparison of fasting and nonfasting lipid markers (2008).
- **People or setting studied:** Healthy women in a prospective cardiovascular outcomes cohort.
- **Exposure or intervention:** Baseline lipid and apolipoprotein measurements taken in fasting or nonfasting states.
- **Comparator or control:** Fasting versus nonfasting measurement status.
- **Duration or follow-up:** 11-year cardiovascular-event follow-up.
- **Role in Murph:** context-only / measurement_context.

## Findings

In a prospective cohort of 26,330 healthy women, fasting and nonfasting lipid/apolipoprotein measurements were compared for incident cardiovascular events. The reusable finding is that fasting status can matter for LDL-C, non-HDL-C, apoB, and ratio interpretation even though several lipid markers still predict risk in nonfasting samples.

**Effect estimate or direction extracted:** Among 26,330 women, 19,983 were fasting and 6,347 were nonfasting; 754 fasting and 207 nonfasting participants had incident cardiovascular events over follow-up. Search-extracted abstract details report stronger CVD associations for fasting total cholesterol, LDL-C, non-HDL-C, apoB100, and apoB100/apoA1 ratio, while HDL-C, triglycerides, total cholesterol/HDL-C, and apoA1 performed similarly or better in nonfasting analyses.

## Why it matters

A home cholesterol experiment may compare pre/post lipid panels. Fasting status should be logged or standardized because some LDL-C and apoB comparisons can be affected by fasting state.

## Potential experiment signals

## Protocol takeaway

Standardize fasting status across baseline and follow-up panels when using LDL-C or apoB as primary protocol endpoints.
