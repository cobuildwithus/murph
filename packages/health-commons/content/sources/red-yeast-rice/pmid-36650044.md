## Quick read

- **Source type:** Narrative best-practice review for LDL-C calculation (2023).
- **People or setting studied:** Clinicians and laboratories deciding when LDL-C should be calculated or directly measured.
- **Exposure or intervention:** LDL-C calculation equations and calculation thresholds.
- **Comparator or control:** Friedewald, Martin-Hopkins, extended Martin-Hopkins, Sampson/NIH equations, and direct measurement contexts.
- **Duration or follow-up:** Best-practice review; no intervention follow-up.
- **Role in Murph:** context-only / measurement_context.

## Findings

This review summarizes when and how LDL-C can be calculated, including triglyceride-range limitations for different equations. It supports lipid-test-plan instructions to record triglycerides and LDL-C calculation method.

**Effect estimate or direction extracted:** PubMed/search summaries report practical triglyceride thresholds: Sampson-NIH2 can be used with triglycerides up to about 9 mmol/L; Martin-Hopkins is commonly used up to 4.5 mmol/L, with extended Martin-Hopkins up to about 9.04 mmol/L.

## Why it matters

When a protocol participant has elevated triglycerides, LDL-C calculation may become the main interpretive weakness.

## Potential experiment signals

calculated LDL-C, triglycerides, lab-fidelity

## Protocol takeaway

Flag high triglycerides and LDL-C calculation method before interpreting LDL-C response.
