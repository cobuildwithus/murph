This source is included for **Lipid measurement and test-plan context**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** General-population fasting/nonfasting lipid study with outcomes context (2008).
- **People or setting studied:** Adults aged 20 to 95 years in the Copenhagen General Population Study, with additional cardiovascular-event context from the Copenhagen City Heart Study.
- **Exposure or intervention:** Normal food intake and time since last meal at lipid testing.
- **Comparator or control:** Fasting lipid profile versus nonfasting lipid profile at different post-meal intervals.
- **Duration or follow-up:** Post-meal lipid comparisons plus 14-year cardiovascular-event follow-up in a separate cohort of 9,319 participants.
- **Role in Murph:** context-only / measurement_context.

## Findings

In 33,391 general-population participants, normal food intake produced small mean lipid changes; triglycerides rose modestly, LDL-C and total cholesterol fell slightly, and non-HDL-C/apoB ratios were stable. Nonfasting markers still predicted cardiovascular events in a separate cohort.

**Effect estimate or direction extracted:** The abstract reports maximal post-meal changes of total cholesterol -0.2 mmol/L, LDL-C -0.2 mmol/L, HDL-C -0.1 mmol/L, and triglycerides +0.3 mmol/L; non-HDL-C, apoA1, apoB, and lipid ratios did not change. Highest versus lowest tertiles of nonfasting markers predicted 1.7- to 2.4-fold increased cardiovascular-event risk.

## Why it matters

Supports practical test-plan flexibility while reminding users to document fasting status and time since last meal.

## Protocol takeaway

Nonfasting panels can be informative, but pre/post experiment panels should still match fasting status when possible.
