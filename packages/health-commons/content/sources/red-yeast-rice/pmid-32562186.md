## Findings

This review argues apoB is often preferable to non-HDL-C as a target or interpretive marker in hypertriglyceridemia, diabetes, obesity/metabolic syndrome, and very low LDL-C contexts, while non-HDL-C remains useful when apoB is unavailable.

**Effect estimate or direction extracted:** Search-extracted review summary recommends apoB as the preferred secondary target in mild-to-moderate hypertriglyceridemia, diabetes, obesity/metabolic syndrome, and very low LDL-C contexts; non-HDL-C is a useful supplement when apoB is unavailable.

## Why it matters

A person’s LDL-C can fall while apoB or non-HDL-C discordance remains clinically relevant; this affects how protocol endpoints are interpreted.

## Protocol takeaway

If available, apoB can be a useful secondary endpoint; if not, non-HDL-C is a practical backup.
