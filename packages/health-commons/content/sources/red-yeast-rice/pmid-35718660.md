This source is included for **Interactions, contraindications, and population boundaries**.

**Findings:**
- `finding:pmid-35718660-statin-intolerance-definition` — The NLA statement defines statin intolerance as adverse effects associated with statin therapy that lead to dose reduction or discontinuation, classified as complete or partial intolerance, and requires attempts with at least two statins, including one at the lowest approved daily dose.
- `finding:pmid-35718660-evidence-based-nonstatin-risk-reduction` — The statement emphasizes assessing modifiable contributors to statin intolerance and using evidence-based approaches for ASCVD risk reduction, which sets a boundary against substituting red yeast rice without clinician guidance.

**Why it matters:** Prevents over-labeling users as statin intolerant and keeps red yeast rice discussion subordinate to evidence-based ASCVD risk management.

**Potential experiment signals:** statin-intolerance-criteria, ASCVD-risk-review, LDL-C, clinician-review-required, current-lipid-therapy-screen.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context-only safety boundary: do not claim statin intolerance unless criteria are met, and do not use red yeast rice as a substitute for clinician-guided ASCVD risk reduction.

**Limitations:** Guideline/scientific statement on statin intolerance, not a red yeast rice intervention study; red yeast rice-specific conclusions should come from other sources.

**Population mismatch:** Focuses on statin-intolerant patients requiring ASCVD risk reduction, not the broader population experimenting with red yeast rice for cholesterol.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.
