This source is included for **Lipid measurement and test-plan context**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** LDL-C equation development and validation for normolipidemia and hypertriglyceridemia (2020).
- **People or setting studied:** Patients/samples spanning normolipidemia and hypertriglyceridemia for LDL-C calculation validation.
- **Exposure or intervention:** Sampson LDL-C calculation equation.
- **Comparator or control:** Friedewald equation and other LDL-C estimation methods.
- **Duration or follow-up:** Analytical validation; no intervention follow-up.
- **Role in Murph:** context-only / measurement_context.

## Findings

The Sampson equation source reports improved LDL-C calculation across normolipidemia and hypertriglyceridemia, including triglycerides up to 800 mg/dL. It supports a protocol caveat that calculated LDL-C is less straightforward when triglycerides are high.

**Effect estimate or direction extracted:** PubMed summary reports the new equation calculates LDL-C in patients with triglycerides up to 800 mg/dL as accurately as Friedewald does for triglycerides under 400 mg/dL. Secondary summary reports fewer misclassifications in triglyceride 400-800 mg/dL treatment groups.

## Why it matters

Red-yeast-rice protocols often use LDL-C as the main endpoint, but triglyceride levels and equation choice can change calculated LDL-C.

## Potential experiment signals

calculated LDL-C, triglycerides, hypertriglyceridemia, LDL-C classification

## Protocol takeaway

When triglycerides are elevated, check whether LDL-C is calculated and which equation is used; consider direct LDL-C or apoB/non-HDL-C context.
