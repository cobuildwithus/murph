This source is included for **Lipid measurement and test-plan context**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Double-blind randomized placebo-controlled trial of a mixed plant-extract supplement (2013).
- **People or setting studied:** Adults aged 21 to 55 years with moderate hypercholesterolemia and no lipid-lowering drug treatment.
- **Exposure or intervention:** Dietary supplement containing red yeast rice, sugar-cane-derived policosanols, and artichoke leaf extracts.
- **Comparator or control:** Placebo in a double-blind randomized parallel controlled study.
- **Duration or follow-up:** 16 weeks, with lipid and oxidative-marker measurements at baseline and weeks 4, 8, 12, and 16.
- **Role in Murph:** context-only / measurement_context.

## Findings

In a 39-person, 16-week double-blind placebo-controlled trial, a supplement containing red yeast rice, sugar-cane-derived policosanols, and artichoke leaf extracts reduced LDL-C and total cholesterol in the supplement group. This is a combination-product finding and cannot isolate red yeast rice as the causal component.

**Effect estimate or direction extracted:** Search-extracted article text reports LDL-C decreased 21.4% and total cholesterol decreased 14.1% at week 16 versus baseline in the supplement group; triglycerides decreased 12.2% after 16 weeks. Because the supplement combined RYR with other plant extracts, this is adjacent-variant context rather than isolated RYR evidence.

## Why it matters

Shows why formulation boundaries matter: lipid changes from mixed nutraceutical stacks cannot be cleanly attributed to red yeast rice alone.

## Potential experiment signals

LDL-C, total cholesterol, triglycerides, HDL-C, vitamin E/total cholesterol ratio, safety labs

## Protocol takeaway

Do not promote combination-supplement effects as direct evidence for this red-yeast-rice protocol without clearly labeling the formulation mismatch.

## Safety and adverse events

The abstracted source notes measured antioxidant and oxidative parameters; no specific adverse-event signal was extracted in this batch.

## Limitations and population mismatch

Small trial; mixed supplement stack prevents attribution to red yeast rice alone; baseline-versus-within-group percentages should not be treated as a direct isolated-RYR effect.

**Population or modality mismatch:** RYR-containing combination product rather than a single red-yeast-rice protocol; included here as measurement/adjacent-variant context.
