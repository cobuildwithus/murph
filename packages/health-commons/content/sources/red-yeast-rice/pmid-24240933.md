This source is included for **Lipid measurement and test-plan context**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** LDL-C equation development and validation study (2013).
- **People or setting studied:** Large clinical lipid sample datasets used to compare calculated LDL-C estimation methods.
- **Exposure or intervention:** Novel adjustable triglyceride:VLDL-C factor method for estimating LDL-C.
- **Comparator or control:** Friedewald equation using a fixed triglyceride:VLDL-C factor.
- **Duration or follow-up:** Method-development and validation dataset; no intervention follow-up.
- **Role in Murph:** context-only / measurement_context.

## Findings

This LDL-C estimation-method study reported that an adjustable triglyceride:VLDL-C factor method improved guideline risk classification compared with the Friedewald equation. It supports recording LDL-C calculation method when interpreting cholesterol-protocol results.

**Effect estimate or direction extracted:** The PubMed summary reports the adjustable-factor method provided more accurate guideline risk classification than the Friedewald equation.

## Why it matters

Self-experiment protocols often rely on calculated LDL-C. Equation choice can create apparent LDL-C differences unrelated to supplement effect.

## Potential experiment signals

calculated LDL-C, guideline risk classification, LDL-C estimation accuracy

## Protocol takeaway

Record whether LDL-C was calculated and, if possible, which equation the lab used.
