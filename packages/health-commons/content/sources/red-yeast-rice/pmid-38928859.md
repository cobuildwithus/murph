This source is included for **Product quality, contamination, and dose uncertainty**.

**Findings:** Quality-control assessment of 35 online-purchased RYR supplements with concerns for monacolin-limit compliance, adulteration, contamination, and labeling.

**Extracted details:**

- **Population / sample:** Thirty-five RYR food supplements bought from an EU-bound e-commerce platform or registered online pharmacies; no human participants
- **Intervention or exposure:** Quality-control and safety assessment of online-purchased RYR supplements
- **Comparator / control:** No clinical comparator; comparison to legal/safety expectations
- **Duration / follow-up:** Single online product survey
- **Endpoints:** total monacolin K legal-limit compliance; citrinin compliance; adulteration; microbiological contamination; label compliance
- **Effect estimates or direction:** At least 4 of 35 products raised concern due to high monacolin K exceeding limits or severe bacterial contamination; 3 of 35 were likely adulterated; 6 of 17 EU e-commerce samples had label noncompliance.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** Flags high active-dose, adulteration, bacterial contamination, and label warnings as real-world implementation hazards.
- **Limitations:** Online-purchase sample; findings may not generalize to all markets, regulated pharmacies, or later lots.
- **Population mismatch:** No human efficacy or adverse-event outcomes.
- **Directness:** measurement_context/same_mechanism product-quality evidence

**Why it matters:** Directly supports implementation restrictions for product selection, supplier quality, and label verification.

**Potential experiment signals:** total monacolin K legal-limit compliance; citrinin compliance; adulteration; microbiological contamination; label compliance

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as current product-quality boundary evidence, not as efficacy magnitude evidence.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.
