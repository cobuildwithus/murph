This source is included for **Lipid measurement and test-plan context**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Meta-analysis of lipid and apolipoprotein cardiovascular-risk markers (2011).
- **People or setting studied:** Participants from prospective cardiovascular-risk studies comparing LDL-C, non-HDL-C, and apoB markers.
- **Exposure or intervention:** Higher LDL-C, non-HDL-C, or apoB levels as cardiovascular-risk markers.
- **Comparator or control:** Comparative marker performance: apoB versus non-HDL-C versus LDL-C.
- **Duration or follow-up:** Follow-up duration varied across included cohorts; not extracted for this batch.
- **Role in Murph:** context-only / measurement_context.

## Findings

This comparative marker meta-analysis found apoB to be a stronger cardiovascular-risk marker than LDL-C, with non-HDL-C also generally more informative than LDL-C. It supports apoB/non-HDL-C as context for interpreting cholesterol-protocol lab results.

**Effect estimate or direction extracted:** Search-extracted summary reports apoB as the strongest marker, LDL-C as the weakest, and non-HDL-C intermediate/superior to LDL-C. Reported relative-risk ratios included apoB 1.43 (95% CI 1.35 to 1.51) and LDL-C 1.25 (95% CI 1.18 to 1.33).

## Why it matters

A red-yeast-rice experiment may lower LDL-C but still need context from apoB or non-HDL-C, especially when triglycerides or particle burden are discordant.

## Potential experiment signals

LDL-C, non-HDL-C, apoB, cardiovascular risk

## Protocol takeaway

Consider non-HDL-C and apoB as interpretation aids, not as direct evidence that red yeast rice works.

## Limitations and population mismatch
