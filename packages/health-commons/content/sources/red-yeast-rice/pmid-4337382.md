This source is included for **Lipid measurement and test-plan context**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Foundational LDL-C calculation method paper (1972).
- **People or setting studied:** Patients or plasma samples used to estimate LDL-C from standard lipid-panel components.
- **Exposure or intervention:** Friedewald LDL-C estimation from total cholesterol, triglycerides, and HDL-C.
- **Comparator or control:** Preparative ultracentrifuge or direct/reference LDL-C measurement.
- **Duration or follow-up:** Analytical method; no intervention follow-up.
- **Role in Murph:** context-only / measurement_context.

## Findings

The Friedewald source established a practical way to estimate LDL-C from standard lipid-panel components without preparative ultracentrifuge. It is the foundational context for why calculated LDL-C can be method-dependent.

**Effect estimate or direction extracted:** Foundational method estimates LDL-C from standard lipid-panel components without preparative ultracentrifuge; later literature commonly expresses this in mg/dL as LDL-C = total cholesterol - HDL-C - triglycerides/5 under applicable conditions.

## Why it matters

A protocol that tracks LDL-C must know whether the LDL-C value is directly measured or derived from this older calculation.

## Potential experiment signals

calculated LDL-C, triglycerides, total cholesterol, HDL-C

## Protocol takeaway

Calculated LDL-C is not independent of triglycerides and HDL-C; record method and triglycerides.

## Safety and adverse events

No intervention adverse events.

## Limitations and population mismatch

Older method; calculation has known limitations, especially with high triglycerides and nonstandard lipid states; not an intervention study.

**Population or modality mismatch:** Measurement method only.
