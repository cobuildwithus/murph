This source is included for **Safety case reports and adverse-event signals**.

**Findings:**
- `finding:pmid-36779111-rhabdomyolysis-self-directed-use`: A 50-year-old woman using red yeast rice after a dyslipidemia diagnosis presented with symptoms and laboratory findings consistent with rhabdomyolysis; she improved after hydration and discontinuation, but the case cannot quantify risk.

**Why it matters:** Directly mirrors a real-world cholesterol self-management scenario where supplement use may not be disclosed to clinicians.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- Generalized myalgia and chest discomfort symptom prompts
- Creatine kinase if symptoms occur
- Kidney function and urinalysis if rhabdomyolysis suspected
- Clinician disclosure of supplement use

**Protocol takeaway:** Protocol onboarding should explicitly ask about undisclosed supplement use and warn that severe muscle symptoms require prompt evaluation.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

## Extraction details

**Population:** One 50-year-old woman with recently diagnosed dyslipidemia who ingested red yeast rice without informing clinicians.

**Exposure:** Red yeast rice ingestion for cholesterol/dyslipidemia self-management; dose and precise duration should be verified from full text.

**Comparator/control:** None; single-patient case report.

**Duration/follow-up:** Short clinical course with hospital evaluation and improvement after hydration and discontinuation; exact exposure duration should be verified from full text.

**Endpoints:**
- Rhabdomyolysis
- Generalized myalgia
- Creatine kinase / muscle injury
- Renal and liver function context

**Effect estimates or direction:** Adverse-event direction only: laboratory findings were consistent with rhabdomyolysis and clinical improvement occurred after hydration and stopping the exposure. No risk estimate was reported.

**Adverse events / safety notes:** Rhabdomyolysis with chest discomfort/generalized myalgia; accessible summaries note improvement after hydration and normalization of labs.

**Limitations:**
- Single case report; cannot establish incidence.
- Self-directed supplement use and product composition are not generalizable.
- Exact dose/duration and laboratory values should be checked in full text before high-specificity claims.

**Population mismatch:** Single self-directed user; useful for safety signals but not efficacy or event-rate claims.
