- **People or setting studied:** Clinical laboratories and clinicians using apoB, LDL-C, and non-HDL-C values.
- **Exposure or intervention:** Standardization status and analytical properties of apoB, LDL-C, and non-HDL-C.
- **Comparator or control:** ApoB standardization versus LDL-C and non-HDL-C measurement/estimation limitations.
- **Duration or follow-up:** Narrative review; no intervention follow-up.
- **Role in Murph:** context-only / measurement_context.

## Findings

This standardization review explains that apoB is a defined molecular analyte and discusses why apoB, LDL-C, and non-HDL-C differ in measurement standardization. It supports adding apoB as a secondary interpretation endpoint when available.

**Effect estimate or direction extracted:** PubMed/search summaries emphasize apoB as a more defined molecular target and discuss standardization advantages, while LDL-C and HDL-C/non-HDL-C standardization limitations remain important.

## Why it matters

Useful for explaining why apoB can be a robust secondary endpoint and why LDL-C/non-HDL-C still carry method caveats.
