This source is included for **Product quality, contamination, and dose uncertainty**.

**Findings:** Food-supplement quality study assessing dosage/formulation performance and citrinin in 14 marketed RYR products.

**Extracted details:**

- **Population / sample:** Fourteen marketed RYR food supplements in tablet or capsule form; no human participants
- **Intervention or exposure:** Quality-profile testing including dosage uniformity, disintegration, bioaccessibility, and citrinin method application
- **Comparator / control:** Comparison to pharmacopeial-style quality expectations; no clinical comparator
- **Duration / follow-up:** Single product-quality study
- **Endpoints:** dosage-form uniformity; tablet disintegration; bioaccessibility; citrinin detection
- **Effect estimates or direction:** The study reported significant variation in quality profile; cited extraction notes indicate 44% of tablets had longer disintegration and citrinin was not detected with the described method.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** Null citrinin result but quality variation and disintegration differences can affect dose delivery and interpretation.
- **Limitations:** Small marketed-product sample; not a human bioavailability or outcome study.
- **Population mismatch:** No human cholesterol protocol data.
- **Directness:** measurement_context for same product family

**Why it matters:** Shows that product quality includes dosage-form behavior, not only monacolin or contaminant concentration.

**Potential experiment signals:** dosage-form uniformity; tablet disintegration; bioaccessibility; citrinin detection

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as product-performance QC context and preserve the null citrinin finding.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.
