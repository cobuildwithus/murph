This source is included for **Safety case reports and adverse-event signals**.

**Findings:**
- `finding:pmid-37637685-rhabdomyolysis-aki-abiraterone-context`: A 76-year-old man on abiraterone developed rhabdomyolysis-induced acute kidney injury after adding a red yeast rice supplement; he improved after discontinuation and extracorporeal support, but abiraterone and cancer-treatment context limit causal inference.

**Why it matters:** Highlights kidney-injury escalation and possible interaction/comorbidity risk, beyond muscle symptoms alone.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- Creatine kinase, serum creatinine/eGFR, potassium when symptoms occur
- Muscle weakness/soreness and urine color symptoms
- Cancer therapy and CYP/interacting medication review
- Avoid self-experimentation in complex oncology-medication contexts

**Protocol takeaway:** Red yeast rice should be treated as a clinician-supervised exposure in patients with cancer therapies or other complex medication regimens.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

## Extraction details

**Population:** One 76-year-old man with advanced prostate cancer treatment context; he had used abiraterone for months and added a red yeast rice supplement before symptom onset.

**Exposure:** Red yeast rice supplement added while taking abiraterone; accessible summaries report supplement use for about 3 months before symptom onset.

**Comparator/control:** None; single-patient case report.

**Duration/follow-up:** About 3 months of red yeast rice supplement use before symptoms; clinical course included approximately 23 days until creatine kinase normalization and creatinine improvement in accessible summaries.

**Endpoints:**
- Rhabdomyolysis
- Acute kidney injury
- Creatine kinase
- Creatinine / renal recovery
- Possible drug-supplement interaction context

**Effect estimates or direction:** Adverse-event direction only: progressive limb weakness, muscle soreness, rhabdomyolysis-induced acute kidney injury, and improvement after drug/supplement discontinuation plus extracorporeal support were reported. No incidence or efficacy estimate was reported.

**Adverse events / safety notes:** Rhabdomyolysis-induced acute kidney injury requiring hemodialysis and hemoperfusion in the reported case.

**Limitations:**
- Single case report; cannot determine frequency or causal certainty.
- Abiraterone and cancer-treatment context are important confounders and population mismatches.
- Exact dose, monacolin content, and product characteristics should be verified before detailed dose claims.

**Population mismatch:** Older cancer patient on abiraterone; not representative of otherwise healthy cholesterol self-experimenters.
