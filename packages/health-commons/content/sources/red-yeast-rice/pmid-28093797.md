This source is included for **Product quality, contamination, and dose uncertainty**.

**Findings:** Retrospective surveillance analysis of 52 reports/55 adverse reactions to RYR supplements, including myalgia/CPK increase, rhabdomyolysis, liver injury, and hospitalizations.

**Extracted details:**

- **Population / sample:** 52 reports concerning 55 suspected adverse reactions to RYR dietary supplements in the Italian Surveillance System of Natural Health Products, April 2002-September 2015
- **Intervention or exposure:** Dietary supplements containing red yeast rice / monacolin K
- **Comparator / control:** No exposed-control comparator; spontaneous adverse-reaction reporting
- **Duration / follow-up:** Reports collected April 2002-September 2015
- **Endpoints:** myalgia and/or creatine phosphokinase increase; rhabdomyolysis; liver injury; gastrointestinal reactions; cutaneous reactions; hospitalization; causality classification
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Among 52 reports/55 reactions: myalgia and/or CPK increase 19, rhabdomyolysis 1, liver injury 10, gastrointestinal reactions 12, cutaneous reactions 9, other reactions 4; 13 cases required hospitalization; 28 patients were taking other medications.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** Raises safety signals for myopathy and liver injury similar to statins; spontaneous reports do not establish incidence.
- **Limitations:** Spontaneous-report surveillance is subject to underreporting, reporting bias, and incomplete exposure details; causality assessment is not the same as randomized evidence.
- **Population mismatch:** Adverse-event reports, not a controlled cholesterol protocol trial.
- **Directness:** same_mechanism safety signal

**Why it matters:** Supports monitoring and exclusion criteria for muscle/liver risk and concomitant medications.

**Potential experiment signals:** myalgia and/or creatine phosphokinase increase; rhabdomyolysis; liver injury; gastrointestinal reactions; cutaneous reactions; hospitalization; causality classification

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as adverse-event safety boundary; do not infer adverse-event incidence.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.
