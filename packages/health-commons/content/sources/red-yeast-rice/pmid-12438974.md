This source is included for **Safety case reports and adverse-event signals**.

**Findings:**
- `finding:pmid-12438974-rhabdomyolysis-renal-transplant`: A renal transplant recipient developed rhabdomyolysis while using an herbal preparation containing red yeast rice; the case suggests a serious statin-like adverse-event possibility in interaction-prone patients, but does not establish incidence or causality.

**Why it matters:** This early case report flags that red yeast rice can behave like a statin-class exposure in vulnerable patients and interaction-prone settings.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- Medication and supplement interaction screen before starting red yeast rice
- Muscle pain, weakness, and dark urine symptom tracking
- Creatine kinase if symptoms occur or if high-risk medications are present
- Kidney function and transplant-medication review in medically complex users

**Protocol takeaway:** For the cholesterol protocol, treat transplant status, immunosuppression, or strong interaction risk as a clinician-guidance boundary rather than a self-experiment setting.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

## Extraction details

**Population:** One stable renal transplant recipient with post-transplant hypercholesterolemia; medically vulnerable and likely using transplant immunosuppression, so not representative of typical self-directed cholesterol supplementation.

**Exposure:** An herbal preparation containing red yeast rice (Monascus purpureus), reported in the source abstract as the suspected component.

**Comparator/control:** None; single-patient case report.

**Duration/follow-up:** Exposure duration and follow-up details are not fully available in accessible source metadata; secondary summaries report use for about 2 months before abnormal creatine phosphokinase was detected.

**Endpoints:**
- Rhabdomyolysis
- Creatine kinase / creatine phosphokinase
- Renal transplant safety context
- Potential drug-supplement interaction

**Effect estimates or direction:** Adverse-event direction only: rhabdomyolysis was reported and attributed by the authors to the red-yeast-rice-containing preparation. No risk estimate, denominator, or efficacy endpoint was reported.

**Adverse events / safety notes:** Rhabdomyolysis / marked muscle-injury biomarker signal in a renal transplant recipient.

**Limitations:**
- Single case report; cannot estimate incidence or relative risk.
- Concomitant transplant medications and patient vulnerability are major confounders and population mismatches.
- Accessible metadata is limited; full text is paywalled, so case chronology and laboratory values should be verified before reuse in higher-specificity claims.

**Population mismatch:** Renal transplant recipient and likely immunosuppression context; substantially different from lower-risk adults considering red yeast rice for cholesterol.
