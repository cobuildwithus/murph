This source is included for **Safety case reports and adverse-event signals**.

**Findings:**
- `finding:pmid-30910808-acute-liver-injury`: A 64-year-old woman developed acute liver injury after approximately 6 weeks of red yeast rice supplementation for cholesterol; other causes were reportedly evaluated and biopsy was consistent with drug-induced liver injury, but the single-case design cannot estimate risk.

**Why it matters:** Preserves the liver-injury safety boundary for a cholesterol-oriented supplement whose active monacolin K is chemically statin-like.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- Baseline liver disease screening
- ALT, AST, alkaline phosphatase, bilirubin if clinician-guided or symptomatic
- Jaundice, dark urine, pale stools, fatigue, abdominal symptoms
- Alcohol and hepatotoxic co-exposure review

**Protocol takeaway:** Acute liver injury symptoms while using red yeast rice should trigger immediate discontinuation and medical evaluation.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

## Extraction details

**Population:** One 64-year-old woman with no prior medication use reported in accessible summaries, taking red yeast rice to lower cholesterol.

**Exposure:** Red yeast rice supplement; accessible summaries report 1200 mg/day for about 6 weeks before presentation.

**Comparator/control:** None; single-patient case report.

**Duration/follow-up:** About 6 weeks of supplement use before acute liver injury presentation, based on accessible abstract/summary text.

**Endpoints:**
- Acute liver injury
- Hepatitis symptoms
- Liver biopsy consistent with drug-induced liver injury
- Liver enzymes / jaundice context

**Effect estimates or direction:** Adverse-event direction only: acute liver injury occurred after red yeast rice use, with other infectious, toxic, and autoimmune causes reportedly ruled out and biopsy consistent with drug-induced liver injury. No event-rate or efficacy estimate was reported.

**Adverse events / safety notes:** Acute liver injury / acute hepatitis after red yeast rice supplementation.

**Limitations:**
- Single case report; cannot establish incidence.
- Alcohol intake and individual susceptibility may contribute and require full-text causal assessment.
- BMJ full text is permission-controlled; exact laboratory values and complete chronology should be verified before detailed reuse.

**Population mismatch:** Single older adult liver-injury case, not a representative cholesterol-protocol cohort.
