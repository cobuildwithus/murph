This source is included for **presleep_arousal_rumination_mechanisms**.

**Findings:** In 15 healthy volunteers, a pre-bed cognitive-arousal induction significantly prolonged objective sleep-onset latency compared with a reference night and was associated with changes in EEG, heart-rate, and temperature measures.

**Why it matters:** It experimentally links pre-sleep cognitive load to delayed sleep onset, strengthening the mechanistic rationale for reducing cognitive arousal before bed.

**Potential experiment signals:** Sleep-onset latency; Pre-sleep cognitive load; Resting heart rate; Proximal temperature; Deep-sleep EEG arousal

**Protocol takeaway:** Use for causal mechanism plausibility that cognitive arousal can delay sleep onset, not for meditation efficacy.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
