This source is included for **presleep_arousal_rumination_mechanisms**.

**Findings:** In a 31-person laboratory case-control study, ERP findings were interpreted as greater cortical arousal in insomnia and difficulty disengaging from wake processes at sleep onset.

**Why it matters:** The study supports cortical arousal and sleep-onset disengagement as relevant mechanisms for pre-sleep downshift practices.

**Potential experiment signals:** Sleep-onset latency; Pre-sleep cognitive arousal; EEG high-frequency arousal; Sleep efficiency

**Protocol takeaway:** Use this as physiology context for arousal/disengagement at sleep onset, not as evidence that meditation changes ERP or sleep.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Source kind:** journal_article.
- **Study design:** Laboratory ERP and polysomnography case-control physiology study.
- **Population:** 15 adults with primary chronic psychophysiological insomnia and 16 good sleepers.
- **Intervention or exposure:** Insomnia status and auditory ERP assessment during wake and sleep-onset states.
- **Comparator or control:** Good sleepers.
- **Duration or follow-up:** Acute laboratory assessment across four nights.
- **Endpoints:** ERP N1, P2, N350 components; polysomnographic sleep-onset context; cortical arousal and sleep-initiation processes.
- **Effect estimates or direction:** The insomnia group showed ERP patterns consistent with greater cortical arousal while awake and difficulty disengaging from wake processes at sleep onset..
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse events were highlighted in the accessible abstract..
- **Limitations:** Small physiology sample; laboratory setting; not an intervention and not a meditation study..
- **Population mismatch:** Clinical psychophysiological insomnia physiology rather than unsupervised bedtime meditation users..
