This source is included for **clinical_insomnia_mindfulness**.

**Findings:** `finding:pmid-32448712/community-mbsr-psqi-secondary-analysis` — In a secondary analysis of a randomized trial of 413 community-dwelling adults, exercise improved PSQI global score versus controls (mean change -0.98, 95% CI -1.56 to -0.41, p=.001), while MBSR was marginal on PSQI global score (mean change -0.53, 95% CI -1.10 to 0.04, p=.07). Both exercise and MBSR improved perceived sleep quality, and MBSR showed improvement in a daily disturbance factor; sleep efficiency did not improve for either active intervention.; `finding:pmid-32448712/healthy-sample-and-secondary-sleep-analysis-boundary` — The study was not a primary insomnia trial and enrolled healthy adults without known prior sleep problems, so its sleep findings are community context rather than clinical insomnia or direct pre-bed meditation evidence.

**Why it matters:** It tests MBSR outside a clinical insomnia sample and shows a mixed sleep pattern: perceived sleep quality may improve, but sleep efficiency did not.

**Potential experiment signals:** PSQI global score; perceived sleep quality; daily disturbance; sleep efficiency; follow-up durability

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as adjacent community context only; it does not show that brief silent meditation before bed improves sleep efficiency or treats insomnia.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
