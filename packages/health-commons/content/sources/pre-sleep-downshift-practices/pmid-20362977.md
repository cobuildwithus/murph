This source is included for **presleep_arousal_rumination_mechanisms**.

**Findings:** In 242 people with insomnia disorder, rumination and worry loaded as separate constructs; high versus low rumination groups differed on multiple sleep-log indices, whereas worry did not show the same main effect in the accessible abstract.

**Why it matters:** It separates rumination from worry and highlights rumination as a sleep-relevant cognitive process.

**Potential experiment signals:** Rumination before bed; Wake after sleep onset; Sleep efficiency; Subjective sleep quality

**Protocol takeaway:** Use to track rumination distinctly from worry in protocol experiments; do not infer meditation efficacy.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Source kind:** journal_article.
- **Study design:** Cross-sectional clinical insomnia cognition study using two-week sleep logs.
- **Population:** 242 people diagnosed with insomnia disorder by sleep experts.
- **Intervention or exposure:** Rumination and worry measures.
- **Comparator or control:** High versus low rumination and worry groupings.
- **Duration or follow-up:** Two-week sleep diary/log period.
- **Endpoints:** Sleep efficiency, wake after sleep onset, sleep quality, and other sleep-log indices.
- **Effect estimates or direction:** High and low ruminators differed on sleep-log indices including sleep efficiency, wake after sleep onset, and sleep quality; worry did not show the same main effect in the accessible abstract..
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** Adverse events were not assessed..
