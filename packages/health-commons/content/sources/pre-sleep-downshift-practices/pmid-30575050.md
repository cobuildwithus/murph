Across 18 RCTs (N=1654), mindfulness meditation improved sleep quality versus nonspecific active controls at post-treatment (ES 0.33, 95% CI 0.17 to 0.48) and follow-up (ES 0.54, 95% CI 0.24 to 0.84).

**Finding 1:** Across 18 RCTs (N=1654), mindfulness meditation improved sleep quality versus nonspecific active controls at post-treatment (ES 0.33, 95% CI 0.17 to 0.48) and follow-up (ES 0.54, 95% CI 0.24 to 0.84).

**Finding 2:** The same meta-analysis found no clear advantage versus specific active controls at post-treatment (ES 0.03, 95% CI -0.43 to 0.49) or follow-up (ES -0.14, 95% CI -0.62 to 0.34), preserving an important comparator boundary.

**Murph use:** Broad RCT synthesis for mindfulness meditation and sleep quality; useful as umbrella context rather than protocol-defining evidence.
