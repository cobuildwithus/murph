The pilot crossover trial found no main effects of slow breathing on basic polysomnographic sleep parameters; the authors concluded that neither slow breathing nor music listening had robust effects on sleep quality.

**Finding 1:** The pilot crossover trial found no main effects of slow breathing on basic polysomnographic sleep parameters; the authors concluded that neither slow breathing nor music listening had robust effects on sleep quality.

**Finding 2:** Both active pre-sleep interventions increased immediate HRV; the slow-breathing condition was associated with higher central delta power during N3 sleep, despite no robust basic sleep-parameter benefit.

**Finding 3:** The authors note a small sample, non-blinded design, mostly student population, uncorrected multiple testing, and only one intervention night; this limits generalization to clinical insomnia and repeated bedtime practice.
