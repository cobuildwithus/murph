This source is included for **presleep_arousal_rumination_mechanisms**.

**Findings:** In 202 young adults, worry and rumination factors were positively associated with pre-sleep arousal, and structural equation modeling indicated that pre-sleep arousal mediated relationships between repetitive thought and poorer sleep quality.

**Why it matters:** Repetitive thought and pre-sleep arousal are plausible targets for a bedtime downshift practice, but this paper only supports a mechanism and measurement rationale.

**Potential experiment signals:** Pre-sleep cognitive arousal; Pre-sleep somatic arousal; Subjective sleep quality; Sleep-onset latency; Sleep efficiency

**Protocol takeaway:** Use this source to justify tracking rumination/worry and pre-sleep arousal around the protocol, not to claim that silent meditation improves sleep.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Source kind:** journal_article.
- **Study design:** Cross-sectional mediation study of repetitive thought, pre-sleep arousal, and sleep quality.
- **Population:** Nonclinical young adults.
- **Intervention or exposure:** Trait worry, trait rumination, and pre-sleep cognitive/somatic arousal.
- **Comparator or control:** Higher versus lower repetitive-thought and pre-sleep arousal levels within a cross-sectional model.
- **Duration or follow-up:** Single assessment; no follow-up.
- **Endpoints:** Sleep quality, sleep latency, sleep efficiency, sleep disturbance, pre-sleep cognitive arousal, pre-sleep somatic arousal.
