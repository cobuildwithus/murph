**Finding 1:** In a 2-week daily-questionnaire study with 178 participants, indirect effects from stress to sleep quality through pre-sleep arousal were statistically significant at low and high levels of rumination and worry. Higher rumination and worry strengthened the stress-to-pre-sleep-arousal relationship, and cognitive arousal accounted for more variance in the stress-sleep relationship than somatic arousal across models.
