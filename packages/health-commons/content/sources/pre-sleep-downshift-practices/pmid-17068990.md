This source is included for **measurement_endpoints_and_wearables**.

**Findings:** Actigraphy was suboptimal as a stand-alone objective endpoint in older adults with chronic insomnia: it was good at detecting sleep but weak at detecting wake and may miss or distort clinically relevant changes in sleep efficiency and latency.

**Why it matters:** Preserves an important negative/mixed measurement caveat in a population where quiet wakefulness and fragmented sleep are common.

**Potential experiment signals:** sleep-onset-latency, sleep-efficiency, total-sleep-time, wake-after-sleep-onset.

**Protocol takeaway:** For older or insomnia-prone users, wearable improvements in sleep efficiency or sleep latency should be interpreted cautiously and paired with diary and symptoms.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
