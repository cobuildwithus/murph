## Extraction notes

This source is included for **safety_adverse_effects**.

**Findings:** The authors discuss when participation in MBSR may require screening, caution, or contraindication, especially for severe psychiatric vulnerability and distressing meditation-related reactions. [source_artifact:doi-10.1007-s12671-011-0079-9]

**Why it matters:** Narrative MBSR contraindication source for screening and safety boundaries rather than efficacy claims. [source_artifact:doi-10.1007-s12671-011-0079-9]

**Potential experiment signals:** adverse events, anxiety or panic activation, trauma activation, program discontinuation. [source_artifact:doi-10.1007-s12671-011-0079-9]

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as a safety-screening source: low-dose bedtime meditation should not be framed as universally benign or as a substitute for care when psychiatric symptoms are active. [source_artifact:doi-10.1007-s12671-011-0079-9]

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

**Safety notes:** Highlights caution for people with psychosis, severe psychological problems, anxiety/panic vulnerability, dissociation, or trauma-related activation; evidence is largely conceptual and case-based. [source_artifact:doi-10.1007-s12671-011-0079-9]

**Limitations and mismatch:** Narrative review; not a randomized safety trial and not specific to a low-dose bedtime protocol. MBSR is a multi-component clinical program, not silent meditation immediately before sleep. [source_artifact:doi-10.1007-s12671-011-0079-9]
