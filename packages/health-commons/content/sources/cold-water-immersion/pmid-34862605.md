This source is included for **Safety, adverse events, and cold-shock boundaries**.

**Findings:** In the COLD-CE study of 551 typical cold urticaria patients, 412 had positive cold-stimulation tests; cold anaphylaxis was reported in 37% of those positives, and complete cold-water immersion was a common provocation. This supports strong screening and medical-clearance boundaries for cold urticaria.

**Why it matters:** This is the strongest batch source for cold urticaria/anaphylaxis as a real whole-body immersion risk.

**Potential experiment signals:** cold-stimulation test history, hives/wheals, angioedema, laryngeal symptoms, anaphylaxis history.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use for high-priority screening and contraindication/medical-clearance language around cold urticaria and anaphylaxis risk.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.
