This source is included for **Open-water failure modes and deaths**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Narrative review (2014).
- **People studied or addressed:** Open-water swimming participants and event-safety context.
- **Role in Murph:** adjacent fatality/safety context.
- **Directness to Cold Plunge:** adjacent variant safety context rather than direct plunge evidence.
- **Population mismatch:** High: the review centers on open-water swimming, event organization, and exertional water movement rather than stationary plunges.
- **Most relevant Murph signals:** swimming or treading-water demands, panic, loss of coordination, cardiac symptoms, environmental supervision.

## Findings

- The review summarizes hazards of open-water swimming and presents event-organization guidance intended to reduce risk.
- It notes that better understanding of immersion responses and precursors to cardiac events may reduce deaths, because cardiac events appear to be a major cause of death in open-water swimming.
- That makes the source useful for safety boundaries, but it should remain clearly separate from the stationary cold-plunge protocol.

## Limitations

- Adjacent open-water endurance/swimming context, not static plunges.
- Narrative review rather than direct plunge trial.

## Why it matters

It helps keep the page from confusing open-water swimming fatalities and endurance problems with a controlled stationary plunge, while still preserving real fatality context.

## Potential experiment signals

- swimming or treading-water demands
- panic
- loss of coordination
- cardiac symptoms
- environmental supervision

## Protocol takeaway

Keep open-water swimming and static cold plunges separate. Use this source to support no-swim/no-solo warnings, not to make direct claims about brief home plunges.

## Claim use

`safety-only`. Keep this source on the safety/context boundary rather than using it as direct efficacy support for the protocol.
