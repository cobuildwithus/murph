This source is included for **Safety, adverse events, and cold-shock boundaries**.

**Findings:** Anxiety ratings predicted cardiac-frequency response components during initial cold-water immersion, supporting anxiety as a measurable cold-shock modifier. After habituation, anxiety ratings still predicted respiratory or cardiac cold-shock components depending on whether anxiety was lowered or increased.

**Why it matters:** Supports measuring subjective anxiety alongside heart and respiratory responses.

**Potential experiment signals:** self-reported anxiety; heart rate; respiratory rate; habituation status.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as safety-only psychophysiology evidence: anxiety can predict cold-shock components before and after habituation.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

**Population mismatch:** Acute survival-oriented cold-water immersion differs from routine voluntary cold plunge.

**Limitations:** Healthy volunteer laboratory context.; Predictive associations do not establish long-term benefit.; Safety/psychophysiology source rather than wellness efficacy evidence.
