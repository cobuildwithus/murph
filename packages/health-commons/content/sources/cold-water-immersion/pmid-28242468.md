This source is included for **Safety, adverse events, and cold-shock boundaries**.

**Findings:** The study reports that repeated anxiety inhibited habituation of the cold-shock response, supporting anxiety as a safety-relevant modifier of repeated exposure.

**Why it matters:** Highlights that psychological state can modify dose adaptation and safety risk.

**Potential experiment signals:** self-reported anxiety; heart rate; respiratory rate; minute ventilation; habituation status.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as safety-only evidence: repeated cold-water exposure may not blunt cold shock when anxiety is repeatedly high.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

**Population mismatch:** Accidental-immersion safety behavior context differs from routine cold-plunge wellness use.

**Limitations:** Exact participant count was not extracted in this batch.; Anxiety manipulation and accidental-immersion framing may not match voluntary wellness plunge.; Safety/physiology endpoint, not efficacy evidence.
