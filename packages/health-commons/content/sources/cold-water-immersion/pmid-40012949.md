This source is included for **Safety, adverse events, and cold-shock boundaries**.

**Findings:** The study reported no relationship between peak heart rate as the cold-shock index and divergent-thinking performance, while lower skin temperature was associated with worse performance. Subjective initial-gasp rating tracked peak heart rate, suggesting a simple subjective cold-shock signal may be useful in safety logging.

**Why it matters:** Adds safety context for decision-making, supervision, and cognitive impairment during cold stress.

**Potential experiment signals:** skin temperature; heart rate; subjective gasp; cognitive task performance; cold-stress symptoms.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as safety/context evidence that cold stress can impair cognition and decision-making; not as a benefit claim.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

**Population mismatch:** Very cold, operational, 13-minute group exposure differs from short wellness cold plunge.

**Limitations:** Operational service-member sample and extreme cold exposure.; Cognitive task context differs from wellness cold plunge.; No respiratory cold-shock component was measured in the study.
