This source is included for **Cold-shower adjacent variant**.

## Findings
- **Source kind / design:** Randomized controlled trial (journal_article).
- **Population:** Adults aged 18-65 without severe comorbidity and not routinely taking cold showers.
- **Participant count:** 3018 (reported).
- **Intervention or exposure:** Usual hot shower followed by 30, 60, or 90 seconds of cold water daily for 30 days, then optional continuation for 60 days.
- **Comparator or control:** No cold-shower control group.
- **Duration or follow-up:** 30 consecutive intervention days plus 60 optional follow-on days
- **Endpoints:** Illness days and sickness absence from work.
- **Effect estimate or direction:** Routine cold showering reduced self-reported sickness absence but not illness days; the reported incidence-rate ratio for sickness absence was about 0.71, and the different cold-duration arms did not clearly separate.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No related serious adverse events were reported in retrieved summaries.
- **Limitations:** Cold showering is a materially different modality from deliberate cold plunge in water depth, body surface area exposed, and likely thermal load.
- **Population mismatch:** This source does not cleanly match a default self-directed deliberate cold-plunge protocol.
- **Directness to Cold Plunge:** adjacent_variant.

## Why it matters
A large RCT gives more behavioral signal than most adjacent-variant studies, while still requiring strict modality separation.

## Protocol takeaway
Adjacent RCT only; do not upgrade shower evidence into cold-plunge claims.

## Claim use
Context only. Do not promote adjacent-variant, clinical-supervised, mechanistic, or review findings into direct cold-plunge claims.
