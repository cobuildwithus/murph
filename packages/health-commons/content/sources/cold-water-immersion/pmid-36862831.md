This source is included for **Sports recovery and training-adaptation boundary**.

**Findings:** The meta-analysis found a mixed time course: endurance recovery improved at 1 h, sprint at 1 h and jump at 6 h were impaired, and later jump, strength, CK, soreness, and perceived-recovery outcomes improved at selected later time points.

**Why it matters:** Shows that timing matters: the same CWI exposure can impair early explosive performance while supporting later recovery markers.

**Potential experiment signals:** performance:endurance; performance:sprint; performance:jump; performance:strength; biomarker:creatine_kinase; self_report:muscle_soreness; self_report:perceived_recovery.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as adjacent evidence for time-dependent exercise-recovery effects and for avoiding immediate high-power exercise claims after cold plunging.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness to Cold Plunge:** `adjacent_variant`.

**Population mismatch:** Exercise-recovery samples rather than general users; protocol differs from a standalone Murph Cold Plunge routine.

**Intervention or exposure:** Cold-water immersion after exercise.

**Comparator or control:** No-CWI or control recovery conditions in included studies.

**Duration or follow-up:** Temporal recovery profile across less than 1 h, 1-6 h, 24 h, 48 h, 72 h, and 96 h or more after immersion.

**Endpoints:** endurance performance; sprint performance; jump performance; muscle strength; creatine kinase; muscle soreness; perceived recovery.

**Effect estimates or direction:** CWI improved short-term endurance recovery at 1 h, impaired sprint at 1 h and jump at 6 h, and improved longer-term jump, strength, CK, soreness, and perceived-recovery outcomes at later time points.

**Adverse events or safety notes:** Early sprint/jump impairment is a performance boundary when cold exposure is followed by near-term explosive exercise.

**Limitations:** Meta-analysis is sports/exercise recovery rather than general wellness cold plunge evidence. Effects depend on timing after immersion, prior exercise type, and environmental conditions. Does not isolate a single standardized cold-plunge protocol. May not translate to long-term strength/hypertrophy adaptation goals.
