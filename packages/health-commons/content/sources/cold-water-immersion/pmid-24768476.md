This source is included for **Recovery dose trial**.



## Findings

Fifty healthy participants were randomized to seated rest or one of four cold/contrast recovery protocols after eccentric exercise: short contrast immersion, short intermittent cold-water immersion at 10°C, 10 minutes at 10°C, or 10 minutes at 6°C.

Outcomes included muscle soreness, pain on stretch, active range of movement, muscle strength, and serum creatine kinase through 96 hours. The 10-minute 6°C condition showed the lowest soreness and pain-on-stretch values, but differences were not statistically significant, and there were no significant between-group differences for the other outcomes.

No dropouts or adverse effects were reported in the abstracted record. The main limitation is that the trial addresses exercise-induced muscle damage management, not a general-purpose cold plunge protocol.



## Why it matters

Useful for implementation guardrails because it directly compares several cold-water dosing options after exercise, but it remains a sports-recovery DOMS study rather than a general cold-plunge wellbeing trial.

## Protocol takeaway

Use only as adjacent dose context for post-exercise recovery discussions. Do not treat it as direct evidence that an ordinary cold plunge improves soreness or recovery in non-exercise users.



## Claim use

Context only. This trial can inform adjacent recovery dose wording and endpoint choice, but it should not be used as direct support for general cold-plunge efficacy claims.
