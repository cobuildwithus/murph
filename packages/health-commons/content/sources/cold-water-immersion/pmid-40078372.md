This source is included for **Sports recovery and training-adaptation boundary**.

**Findings:** The network meta-analysis found medium-duration medium-temperature CWI (10-15 min, 11-15°C) reduced DOMS (SMD -1.45) and CK (SMD -0.85), while medium-duration low-temperature CWI (10-15 min, 5-10°C) reduced DOMS (SMD -1.12), improved jump (SMD 0.48), and reduced CK (SMD -0.90). Because the evidence is limited to acute EIMD recovery within short follow-up windows and uses network comparisons, it should not be generalized to routine wellness cold-plunge benefits or long-term adaptation.

**Why it matters:** Provides dose-structured adjacent evidence for soreness, jump, and CK recovery, but only in acute EIMD studies.

**Potential experiment signals:** self_report:doms; performance:jump; biomarker:creatine_kinase; protocol:temperature; protocol:duration.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use for adjacent dose-context language, not as direct proof that a Murph Cold Plunge dose improves recovery or health.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness to Cold Plunge:** `adjacent_variant`.

**Population mismatch:** Healthy/athlete acute EIMD recovery studies; not general cold-plunge users or long-term adaptation outcomes.

**Intervention or exposure:** CWI categorized by duration and temperature, including medium-duration low-temperature (10-15 min, 5-10°C) and medium-duration medium-temperature (10-15 min, 11-15°C) protocols.

**Comparator or control:** Control/passive recovery conditions and other CWI dose nodes.

**Duration or follow-up:** CWI within 1 h after a single exercise session; outcomes within 48 h in included studies.

**Endpoints:** delayed-onset muscle soreness; jump performance; creatine kinase.

**Effect estimates or direction:** MD-MT CWI reduced DOMS (SMD -1.45) and CK (SMD -0.85); MD-LT CWI reduced DOMS (SMD -1.12), improved jump (SMD 0.48), and reduced CK (SMD -0.90); rankings favored MD-MT for DOMS and MD-LT for jump/CK.

**Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse-event extraction available; dose findings should be interpreted in acute EIMD context rather than general safety.

**Limitations:** Network meta-analysis combines direct and indirect evidence. Focused on acute exercise-induced muscle damage within 48 h, not general wellbeing. Dose categories are broad and may not match a specific cold-plunge prescription. Authors called for further high-quality studies to verify conclusions.
