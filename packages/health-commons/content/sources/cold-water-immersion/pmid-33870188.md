This source is included for **Immersion-depth sleep study**.



## Findings

In a randomized, counterbalanced design, 12 well-trained male endurance runners completed a simulated trail run followed by 10 minutes of 13.3°C whole-body immersion with head immersed, partial-body immersion to the iliac crest, or an out-of-water control condition.

Whole-body immersion lowered core temperature more than control through 80 minutes, increased slow-wave sleep proportion in the first 180 minutes compared with partial immersion, and reduced arousals and limb movements versus control. During the first slow-wave-sleep sequence, RMSSD, LF, and HF were lower in the whole-body condition than in the other conditions.

Despite those sleep-architecture changes, there were no differences between conditions for maximal voluntary isometric contraction, countermovement jump, creatine kinase, or subjective fatigue through 48 hours. The paper is open access under CC BY.



## Why it matters

This is one of the clearest adjacent sleep-architecture trials, but it still sits in a narrow athlete-recovery setting and should not be promoted as direct general cold-plunge evidence.

## Protocol takeaway

Use only as athlete-specific sleep-architecture context. It can support a note that immersion depth may matter in post-exercise settings, but it should not be generalized into a broad sleep-optimization claim for everyday cold plunges.
