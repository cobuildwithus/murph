This source is included for **Safety, adverse events, and cold-shock boundaries**.

**Findings:** In Study 1, induced anxiety increased anxiety ratings and was associated with higher peak cardiac frequency and higher ventilation at some timepoints during cold-water immersion. Repeated immersion habituated cold-shock responses, but acute anxiety still increased cardiac and respiratory response components after habituation.

**Why it matters:** Shows psychological state is part of cold-shock safety, not just water temperature and time.

**Potential experiment signals:** self-reported anxiety; heart rate; respiratory rate; minute ventilation; first-exposure status.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as safety-only evidence that anxious/panicked users may have stronger cold-shock responses even after habituation.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

**Population mismatch:** Laboratory cold-shock survival paradigm differs from a voluntary wellness cold-plunge protocol.

**Limitations:** Small laboratory samples.; Anxiety manipulation may not match real-world panic or fear.; Safety/physiology outcome, not a wellness efficacy endpoint.
