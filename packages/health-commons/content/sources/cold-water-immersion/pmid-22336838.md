This source is included for **Sports recovery and training-adaptation boundary**.

**Findings:** Across 17 small trials totaling 366 participants, the review reported lower muscle soreness after CWI versus passive recovery at 24–96 hours, but the evidence was low quality and heterogeneous.; The review noted that most included trials did not adequately consider complications, leaving safety and optimal method uncertain.

**Why it matters:** This is a high-recall anchor for sports-recovery claims and for preserving uncertainty around adverse-event reporting and optimal dosing.

**Potential experiment signals:** delayed-onset muscle soreness; perceived recovery; fatigue; performance recovery; adverse-event reporting.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use for a cautious adjacent recovery statement only; do not use it to claim broad health benefits or an established optimal cold-plunge protocol.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Population mismatch:** Post-exercise soreness/recovery populations rather than general wellness users or clinical populations.

**Limitations:** Small trials and low-quality evidence.; Heterogeneous immersion dose, temperature, exercise stimulus, and comparator selection.; Adverse-event reporting was sparse.
