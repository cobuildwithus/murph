This source is included for **Adjacent and measurement context**.

**Findings:** The case report abstract describes immediate mood improvement after each swim, sustained and graduated reduction in depressive symptoms, reduced and then discontinued medication use, and remaining medication-free at one-year follow-up. Because this was one uncontrolled case and the exposure was open-water swimming rather than standardized cold plunge, it cannot establish causality or support a direct protocol claim for depression treatment.

**Why it matters:** Provides a frequently cited mental-health adjacent signal while clearly limiting it to uncontrolled open-water swimming context.

**Potential experiment signals:** depressive symptoms; anxiety symptoms; mood before/after exposure; antidepressant medication use; open-water safety context.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context-only adjacent clinical evidence; do not cite it as proof that cold plunge treats depression.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Population mismatch:** Clinical case of open-water swimming for major depressive disorder differs from a standardized cold-plunge wellness protocol.
