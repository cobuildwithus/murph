This source is included for **Cold-shock habituation and drowning-risk mitigation**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Systematic review and meta-analysis (2024).
- **People studied or addressed:** Previously unhabituated human participants exposed to repeated standardized cold-water immersion (17 included studies).
- **Role in Murph:** direct safety backbone.
- **Directness to Cold Plunge:** direct safety-boundary evidence for repeated cold plunges.
- **Population mismatch:** Minimal for the acclimation question, but the pooled literature is not a single standardized home cold-plunge protocol and underrepresents women and older adults.
- **Most relevant Murph signals:** entry breathing-rate spike, gasp/panic rating, palpitations or chest symptoms, cold-tolerance time with stable breathing, symptom log across first several exposures.

## Findings

- The review/meta-analysis identified 17 eligible groups and concluded that repeated standardized cold-water immersions blunt key cold-shock cardiorespiratory responses, with habituation usually apparent after about four exposures.
- Endpoint-specific pooled effects included lower initial heart rate (about -14 bpm), breathing frequency (about -8 breaths/min), and minute ventilation (about -21.3 L/min) after habituation.
- The authors explicitly cautioned that anxiety may partly contribute to apparent habituation and that female and older-participant evidence is limited.

## Limitations

- Endpoint-specific pooled sample sizes cannot be interpreted as one pooled participant total.
- The review highlighted underrepresentation of females and older adults.
- Reduced cold-shock physiology does not prove every downstream safety outcome in unsupervised real-world use.

## Why it matters

This is the strongest direct safety-backbone source for saying that repeated controlled exposures can blunt the most dangerous entry-phase cold-shock responses.

## Potential experiment signals

- entry breathing-rate spike
- gasp/panic rating
- palpitations or chest symptoms
- cold-tolerance time with stable breathing
- symptom log across first several exposures

## Protocol takeaway

Use this to justify acclimation-first dosing and beginner safety language, not to promise broad benefits from one hard plunge.

## Claim use

`safety-only`. Keep this source on the safety/context boundary rather than using it as direct efficacy support for the protocol.
