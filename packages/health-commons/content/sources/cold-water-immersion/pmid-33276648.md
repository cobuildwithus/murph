This source is included for **Winter-swimming risk and benefit overview**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Narrative review (2020).
- **People studied or addressed:** Winter swimmers, cold-water swimmers, and practical cold-water exposure context.
- **Role in Murph:** adjacent overview with strong safety content.
- **Directness to Cold Plunge:** adjacent winter-swimming safety context.
- **Population mismatch:** Moderate to high: much of the source concerns habitual winter/open-water swimmers rather than brief stationary plunges.
- **Most relevant Murph signals:** water temperature, acclimation status, entry symptoms, palpitations or chest symptoms, afterdrop/hypothermia signs.

## Findings

- The review states that regular, graded cold-water practice in experienced healthy people may be compatible with claimed benefits, but unfamiliar individuals remain at risk of death from cold shock or progressive impairment/hypothermia.
- It treats cold water as roughly below 15°C because many dangerous observable reactions peak around 15–10°C.
- The paper also recommends a step-by-step strategy to promote acclimatization and warns that people with cardiovascular disease are more vulnerable to arrhythmias and acute cardiovascular events.

## Limitations

- Narrative review with mixed field, observational, and practical literature.
- Winter/open-water swimmer populations do not map cleanly onto short home plunges.

## Why it matters

This is a broad overview source that helps preserve risk, threshold, and acclimation language even when the benefit literature is mixed and population-mismatched.

## Potential experiment signals

- water temperature
- acclimation status
- entry symptoms
- palpitations or chest symptoms
- afterdrop/hypothermia signs

## Protocol takeaway

Grade exposure, keep temperature and acclimation explicit, and do not treat winter-swimming narratives as direct proof that every cold plunge is beneficial or safe.

## Claim use

`safety-only`. Keep this source on the safety/context boundary rather than using it as direct efficacy support for the protocol.
