This source is included for **Sports recovery and training-adaptation boundary**.

**Findings:** The network meta-analysis found modality-specific effects: cold/long CWI showed significant power effects in resistance-training and team-sport networks, endurance results were uncertain, and moderate-medium CWI improved muscle-damage biomarkers. Passive recovery ranked highest for resistance-training strength, and the authors caution that CWI may be less desirable when strength adaptation is the priority; high indirectness and underrepresentation of female athletes limit certainty.

**Why it matters:** Provides the broadest modality-specific boundary in this batch, especially cautioning against CWI after resistance training when strength adaptation is prioritized.

**Potential experiment signals:** performance:resistance_strength; performance:resistance_power; performance:endurance_aerobic; performance:team_sport_power; biomarker:muscle_damage; self_report:perceived_recovery; protocol:temperature; protocol:duration.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as adjacent, modality-specific recovery evidence and explicitly separate resistance-strength adaptation cautions from team-sport or biomarker recovery signals.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness to Cold Plunge:** `adjacent_variant`.

**Population mismatch:** Exercise-modality-specific athlete/active-adult recovery evidence, not a direct Cold Plunge protocol trial.

**Intervention or exposure:** Post-exercise CWI protocols at 20°C or below, categorized by exercise modality, temperature, and duration.

**Comparator or control:** Passive recovery, active recovery, thermoneutral immersion, contrast water therapy, and alternative recovery modalities.

**Duration or follow-up:** CWI within 1 h after exercise; outcomes within 72 h post-exercise.

**Endpoints:** resistance-training strength; resistance-training power; endurance aerobic performance; endurance recovery; team-sport power; muscle damage biomarkers; perceived recovery.

**Effect estimates or direction:** Eighty-seven studies with 2,313 participants found modality-specific patterns: passive recovery ranked highest for resistance-training strength, cold/long CWI showed significant power effects in resistance and team-sport networks, endurance effects were uncertain, and moderate-medium CWI improved muscle-damage biomarkers.

**Adverse events or safety notes:** Resistance-training strength/adaptation boundary: passive recovery may be preferable when strength adaptation is the goal; no discrete adverse-event ledger extracted.

**Limitations:** Network structures were often star-shaped with high indirectness. Heterogeneity and design subgroup differences affected confidence. Female athletes were underrepresented. The review targets post-exercise recovery modalities, not general wellness cold plunging.
