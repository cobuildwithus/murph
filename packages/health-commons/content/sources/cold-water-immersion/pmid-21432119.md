This source is included for **Adjacent and measurement context**.

**Findings:** The review reports that cold-water immersion test results are influenced by water temperature, immersion time, room temperature, season, ischemia during immersion, and evaluation parameters; it discusses 12 °C for 5 min as an ISO-standardization proposal and reports one 12 °C/3 min rewarming-to-initial-temperature parameter with sensitivity 58% and specificity 100% in its diagnostic context. The source is a diagnostic-method paper, so it should inform measurement/tolerability context rather than direct claims about cold-plunge efficacy or whole-body outcomes.

**Why it matters:** Shows that even a localized cold-water test is highly protocol-sensitive, supporting careful documentation of temperature, duration, season, and measurement timing.

**Potential experiment signals:** finger skin temperature; rewarming time; cold pain/discomfort; water temperature; immersion duration; room temperature.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as measurement-context evidence only; do not treat hand diagnostic immersion as evidence for whole-body cold-plunge benefits.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Population mismatch:** HAVS diagnostic populations and hand-only exposure differ substantially from healthy adults using cold plunge.

**Limitations:** Hand-only diagnostic testing is not whole-body or head-out cold plunge.; Review conditions were designed for HAVS diagnosis, not wellness outcomes.; Diagnostic accuracy estimates depend on test condition and parameter choice..
