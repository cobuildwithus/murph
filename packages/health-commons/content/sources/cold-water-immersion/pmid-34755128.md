This source is included for **Winter-swimming thermogenesis mechanism**.

## Findings
- **Source kind / design:** Cross-sectional mechanistic study (journal_article).
- **Population:** 8 young healthy male winter swimmers and 8 age-matched male controls.
- **Participant count:** 16 (reported).
- **Intervention or exposure:** Habitual winter swimming exposure history (reported 2-3 times per week; at least ~2 years in retrieved summaries), with laboratory cold testing.
- **Comparator or control:** 8 winter swimmers versus 8 controls.
- **Duration or follow-up:** Cross-sectional lab testing; winter swimmers had at least 2 years of regular exposure
- **Endpoints:** Core temperature, BAT glucose uptake, cold-induced thermogenesis, supraclavicular skin temperature, and muscle activity.
- **Effect estimate or direction:** Winter swimmers had lower core temperature at thermal comfort and greater cold-induced thermogenesis than controls; BAT glucose uptake during cold increased similarly across groups.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse-event focus was reported in the retrieved full-text summary.
- **Limitations:** Cross-sectional design cannot establish causality; all participants were young healthy men, and winter swimming is not the same as an isolated plunge prescription.
- **Population mismatch:** This source does not cleanly match a default self-directed deliberate cold-plunge protocol.
- **Directness to Cold Plunge:** adjacent_variant.

## Why it matters
Useful for mechanism framing around thermogenesis, thermal comfort, and adaptation while preserving clear population and protocol mismatch.
