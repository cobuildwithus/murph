This source is included for **Safety, adverse events, and cold-shock boundaries**.

**Findings:** The study found reduced anxiety after thermoneutral-water exposure but negligible change in primary cold-shock response components; tidal volume was lower in the second cold-water immersion.

**Why it matters:** Separates perceived familiarity/anxiety from actual physiologic cold-shock reduction.

**Potential experiment signals:** anxiety; heart rate/cardiac frequency; respiratory rate; minute ventilation; tidal volume.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as safety context: feeling familiar with water exposure does not guarantee a blunted cold-shock response.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

**Population mismatch:** Perceptual manipulation and thermoneutral exposures are adjacent to, not the same as, a routine cold-plunge protocol.

**Limitations:** Small sample.; Laboratory sequence with thermoneutral-water exposures differs from repeated cold-plunge practice.; Safety/physiology endpoint, not efficacy evidence.
