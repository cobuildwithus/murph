This source is included for **Respiratory hazard and hyperventilation**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Focused physiology review (2006).
- **People studied or addressed:** Human cold-water immersion respiratory physiology.
- **Role in Murph:** direct respiratory safety mechanism.
- **Directness to Cold Plunge:** direct safety-boundary evidence for plunge entry breathing risk.
- **Population mismatch:** Low for the entry-respiratory question, although the article is a mechanistic review rather than a modern self-experiment trial.
- **Most relevant Murph signals:** gasp reflex, breathing-rate spike, coughing or aspiration, panic, chest tightness.

## Findings

- The review states that skin cooling can trigger the classic cold-shock pattern of initial gasp, hypertension, and hyperventilation even when hypocapnia would ordinarily suppress breathing.
- It argues that respiratory responses to skin cooling can override conscious and other autonomic respiratory controls.
- That loss of normal control is presented as a precursor pathway to drowning.

## Limitations

- Mechanistic review rather than direct protocol trial.
- Does not provide a self-experiment dose or acclimation schedule.

## Why it matters

It gives the mechanistic reason for making breathing control and calm entry a first-line safety instruction in cold-plunge protocols.

## Potential experiment signals

- gasp reflex
- breathing-rate spike
- coughing or aspiration
- panic
- chest tightness

## Protocol takeaway

Treat breathing control as a safety prerequisite. Do not progress time, submersion depth, or face immersion if the entry gasp and hyperventilation remain uncontrolled.
