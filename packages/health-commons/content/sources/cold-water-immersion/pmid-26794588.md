This source is included for **Emergency and thermoregulation boundary**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Clinical review (2016).
- **People studied or addressed:** Clinical heat- and cold-stress illness context.
- **Role in Murph:** clinical emergency boundary.
- **Directness to Cold Plunge:** clinical emergency-boundary context rather than direct protocol evidence.
- **Population mismatch:** High for ordinary cold-plunge dosing; this source addresses clinical illness recognition and management rather than healthy-user protocol optimization.
- **Most relevant Murph signals:** shivering severity, confusion or mental-status change, respiratory depression, cardiac rhythm symptoms, hypotension/collapse.

## Findings

- The review states that hypothermia below 35°C is a medical emergency.
- It describes severe hypothermia as potentially involving shivering, respiratory depression, cardiac dysrhythmias, impaired mental function, hypotension, and progression to cardiac arrest or coma.
- Management is framed in clinical terms—warming, hydration, and cardiovascular support—so the source belongs in emergency-stop boundaries, not in protocol dosing.

## Limitations

- Clinical review rather than direct plunge study.
- High mismatch with healthy-user dose setting.

## Why it matters

This is the best batch source for drawing the line between normal discomfort and a genuine cold emergency that needs prompt escalation.

## Protocol takeaway

Use this source for emergency-stop language, not for dosing. Confusion, collapse, respiratory depression, or significant cardiac symptoms are escalation signs, not adaptation signs.
