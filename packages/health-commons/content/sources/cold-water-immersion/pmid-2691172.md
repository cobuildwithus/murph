This source is included for **Cold-shock physiology**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Editorial review (1989).
- **People studied or addressed:** Humans suddenly immersed in cold water.
- **Role in Murph:** direct safety context.
- **Directness to Cold Plunge:** direct safety-boundary context for plunge entry.
- **Population mismatch:** Minimal for the entry-hazard question, although the article is a general cold-water immersion review rather than a modern home cold-plunge protocol.
- **Most relevant Murph signals:** initial gasp, hyperventilation, panic, loss of breath control, palpitations or dizziness on entry.

## Findings

- The review’s central claim is that cold-shock responses can cause serious incapacitation or death well before a person becomes hypothermic.
- It therefore shifts attention from late core-cooling narratives to the first-minute respiratory and cardiovascular hazard window.
- This is useful for safety framing, but it is not a dosing or benefit source.

## Limitations

- Review article rather than a pooled quantitative experiment.
- Predates much of the later habituation and winter-swimming literature.
- Does not by itself specify a beginner dose or home-use protocol.

## Why it matters

It keeps the protocol page focused on the highest-risk window: the initial cold-shock period before a person ever reaches true hypothermia.

## Potential experiment signals

- initial gasp
- hyperventilation
- panic
- loss of breath control
- palpitations or dizziness on entry

## Protocol takeaway

Use this to warn that the first minute can be the main danger window; beginners should prioritize controlled entry and breathing, not toughness.

## Claim use

`safety-only`. Keep this source on the safety/context boundary rather than using it as direct efficacy support for the protocol.
