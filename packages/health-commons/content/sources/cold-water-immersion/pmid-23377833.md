This source is included for **Post-exercise evening sleep study**.



## Findings

Eleven male cyclists completed three evening conditions: no exercise, exercise only, and exercise followed by cold-water immersion. After each condition they underwent full overnight polysomnography.

The accessible abstract reports no differences between conditions for total sleep time, sleep efficiency, sleep onset latency, REM latency, wake after sleep onset, or sleep-stage proportions. Core temperature fell below exercise-only and control values before bedtime after cold-water immersion, but most thermal differences disappeared during the night.

This is a high-value adjacent null-result source: even in a setting where pre-sleep body temperature changed, cold-water immersion did not translate into obvious whole-night sleep improvement.



## Why it matters

This source is often cited in sleep discussions, but its real use is to show that evening post-exercise partial-body immersion did not obviously improve whole-night sleep in trained cyclists.
