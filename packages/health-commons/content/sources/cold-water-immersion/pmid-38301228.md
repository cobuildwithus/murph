This source is included for **Sports recovery and training-adaptation boundary**.

**Findings:** Repeated short CWI reduced ventilatory responses during immersion, supporting a cold-habituation signal in this young sample.; The study concluded that CWI habituated participants to cold water but did not lead to adaptations during exercise in normobaric hypoxia.

**Why it matters:** It separates a direct habituation signal from unsupported performance-adaptation claims.

**Potential experiment signals:** cold habituation; ventilation during immersion; pulmonary responses; metabolic responses; sympathoadrenal responses; exercise in normobaric hypoxia.

**Protocol takeaway:** Can inform habituation and tolerance language; do not use as evidence that repeated cold plunges improve exercise capacity or hypoxia performance.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Population mismatch:** Healthy young exercise physiology sample; not clinical, older, cardiovascular-risk, or typical unsupervised users.

**Limitations:** Young healthy sample with only two women.; Hypoxia-exercise outcome is not a general wellness endpoint.; Short, intensive three-day exposure schedule differs from many consumer cold-plunge routines.
