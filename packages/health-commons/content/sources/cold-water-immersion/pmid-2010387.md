This source is included for **Temperature-stratified cold-shock response**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Acute laboratory crossover physiology study (1991).
- **People studied or addressed:** Eight healthy adult subjects undergoing head-out cold-water immersion (8 participants).
- **Role in Murph:** direct experimental safety evidence.
- **Directness to Cold Plunge:** direct safety-boundary evidence for plunge entry conditions.
- **Population mismatch:** Low for the entry-phase physiology question, though it used short head-out laboratory immersions rather than a broader home protocol.
- **Most relevant Murph signals:** entry symptoms by temperature, breath control on entry, pre-entry breathing behavior, panic or dizziness, respiratory distress.

## Findings

- Eight adults completed brief head-out immersions at 5°C, 10°C, and 15°C, with an added 10°C condition after voluntary hyperventilation.
- The study was designed specifically to examine how temperature and prior hyperventilation affect the hazardous initial human response to cold-water immersion.
- The available abstract confirms that these variables mattered, even though the brief search-accessible text did not provide a full quantitative table.

## Limitations

- Small sample size (n=8).
- Brief laboratory immersions rather than real-world unsupervised plunges.
- The accessible abstract snippet did not provide all effect sizes.

## Why it matters

This is direct experimental evidence that both water temperature and pre-entry breathing state matter during the first minutes of cold-water exposure.

## Potential experiment signals

- entry symptoms by temperature
- breath control on entry
- pre-entry breathing behavior
- panic or dizziness
- respiratory distress

## Protocol takeaway

Do not intentionally hyperventilate before a plunge, and do not assume that a brief dip is automatically safe just because it is short.
