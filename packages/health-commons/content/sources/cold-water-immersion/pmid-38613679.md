This source is included for **Sports recovery and training-adaptation boundary**.

**Findings:** Repeated CWI reduced CK trivially compared with control and increased HSP-72 compared with HWI; HWI produced larger CK reduction and different IL-1ra and growth-factor responses. Because the trial was a small athlete biomarker study and water immersion may reflect both hydrostatic and thermal effects, it supports individualized/periodized athlete context rather than direct general Cold Plunge efficacy.

**Why it matters:** Maps inflammatory and hormone-marker boundaries for repeated post-resistance CWI and reinforces periodized use in athletes.

**Potential experiment signals:** biomarker:creatine_kinase; biomarker:hsp_72; biomarker:il_1ra; biomarker:pdgf_bb; biomarker:igf_1; biomarker:inflammatory_cytokines.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use for mechanism/context only; biomarker shifts in male rugby players should not be converted into general health or performance claims.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness to Cold Plunge:** `adjacent_variant`.

**Population mismatch:** Highly trained male rugby players during in-season resistance training; not general cold-plunge users.

**Intervention or exposure:** Repeated post-resistance exercise CWI (15°C × 15 min, whole-body) or HWI (39°C × 15 min, whole-body) during 4-week blocks.

**Comparator or control:** No-immersion control block.

**Duration or follow-up:** Twelve-week program divided into three 4-week intervention blocks, with fasted morning blood measures every fourth week.

**Endpoints:** creatine kinase; myoglobin; interleukin-1 receptor antagonist; IL-1β; IL-6; IL-8; IL-17a; TNF-α; IL-4; IL-10; HSP-72; PDGF-BB; IGF-1.

**Effect estimates or direction:** Repeated CWI produced trivial CK reductions compared with control and increased HSP-72 compared with hot-water immersion; HWI produced larger CK reduction and shifted IL-1ra and growth-factor signals.

**Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse-event extraction available; authors note resistance-training adaptation pathways and athlete periodization as interpretation boundaries.

**Limitations:** Small all-male elite academy rugby sample. In-season resistance-training context; not general wellness. Biomarker changes do not prove clinical or performance benefit. Hot and cold water immersion both involve hydrostatic pressure, complicating temperature-specific attribution.
