This source is included for **Winter-swimming immune and hormone context**.

## Findings
- **Source kind / design:** Acute physiology study (journal_article).
- **Population:** Habitual and inexperienced healthy winter swimmers exposed to sauna plus a short ice-cold swim.
- **Participant count:** Not clearly reported in the retrieved metadata.
- **Intervention or exposure:** A single acute thermal-stress session involving sauna followed by a short swim in ice-cold water.
- **Comparator or control:** Habitual winter swimmers versus inexperienced winter swimmers.
- **Duration or follow-up:** Acute responses during a single thermal-stress session
- **Endpoints:** Plasma IL-6, leukocytes, monocytes, granulocytosis, hemoconcentration, ADH, cortisol, and stimulated cytokine release.
- **Effect estimate or direction:** Both groups showed granulocytosis and increases in ADH, cortisol, and IL-6; habitual swimmers had higher resting IL-6/leukocyte-related measures and larger cortisol changes, suggesting adaptation.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No major adverse-event signal was captured in the retrieved abstract snippets; this was an acute physiology study rather than a safety trial.
- **Limitations:** The protocol combined heat and cold, was acute rather than longitudinal, and did not test a standardized deliberate cold-plunge routine.
- **Population mismatch:** This source does not cleanly match a default self-directed deliberate cold-plunge protocol.
- **Directness to Cold Plunge:** adjacent_variant.

## Why it matters
Shows that repeated winter-swimming exposure may alter acute endocrine and immune responses, but the modality is too mixed to use as direct plunge evidence.
