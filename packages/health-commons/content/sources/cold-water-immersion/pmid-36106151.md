This source is included for **Adjacent and measurement context**.

**Findings:** The review defines habituation as a reduced response to repeated cold stimulus and states that short-duration mild cold exposures can reduce cold-defense mechanisms, increase skin temperature, attenuate shivering, and reduce cold sensations before hypermetabolic or insulative adaptations from prolonged/severe exposure. The review supports ramping/tolerance and modifier context, but it does not establish health-outcome efficacy for a cold-plunge protocol.

**Why it matters:** Clarifies what habituation means and helps separate acute tolerance/ramping signals from claims of durable health benefit.

**Potential experiment signals:** cold sensation rating; shivering; skin temperature; time-to-comfort; first-session vs habituated-session context.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use for mechanism and safety/ramping context; do not use as direct evidence that cold plunge improves health outcomes.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Population mismatch:** Reviewed cold-habituation contexts vary widely and may not match consumer cold-plunge temperature, duration, or screening.

**Limitations:** Narrative review, not a direct cold-plunge RCT.; Includes diverse cold exposure types and severities.; Does not establish wellness or disease-outcome efficacy..
