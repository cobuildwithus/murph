This source is included for **Adjacent and measurement context**.

**Findings:** Accessible summaries report slight post-exposure ACTH and cortisol increases, no melatonin change, increased post-exposure norepinephrine (p < 0.05), and epinephrine nearly doubling (p < 0.05) in the sauna-plus-ice-water condition. Because the experiment used mixed sauna, shower, room-temperature, and ice-water conditions, it should be treated as adjacent endocrine context rather than isolated cold-plunge efficacy evidence.

**Why it matters:** Provides acute endocrine stress-response context for catecholamines and stress hormones during ice-water-related exposure, while highlighting sauna-combination confounding.

**Potential experiment signals:** ACTH; cortisol; melatonin; norepinephrine; epinephrine; sauna co-exposure flag.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as adjacent endocrine mechanism context only; do not infer cold-plunge benefits from sauna-plus-ice-water endocrine changes.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Population mismatch:** Experienced male winter swimmers exposed to sauna/cold sequences differ from typical screened cold-plunge users and from isolated cold-plunge protocols.

**Limitations:** Very small male winter-swimmer sample.; Most conditions combine heat and cold or compare non-plunge exposures, limiting directness.; Accessible extraction did not provide exact effect sizes beyond summarized direction and p-values.; Not a wellness outcome or long-term protocol trial..
