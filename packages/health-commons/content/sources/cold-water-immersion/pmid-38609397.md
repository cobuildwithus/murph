This source is included for **Safety, adverse events, and cold-shock boundaries**.

**Findings:** In 13 healthy young men, a single cold-water immersion below 4°C increased blood protein concentrations and worsened erythrocyte deformability. This is an extreme-dose mechanistic safety signal and should not be used as evidence of cold-plunge benefit.

**Why it matters:** Provides a safety/mechanistic boundary for very cold exposures below typical recommended ranges.

**Potential experiment signals:** blood viscosity/haemorheology proxies, symptoms after extreme cold, vascular risk context.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use to caution against extrapolating safety from very brief extreme-cold immersion; no efficacy claim.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

## Extraction notes

- Directness to Cold Plunge: `same_mechanism`.
- Population mismatch: Healthy young men under study conditions differ from general users, women, older adults, or people with vascular/cardiac disease.
- Limitations: Small sample of healthy men only.; Extreme <4°C exposure is not a standard wellness cold-plunge dose.; Mechanistic blood changes do not establish clinical outcomes.
