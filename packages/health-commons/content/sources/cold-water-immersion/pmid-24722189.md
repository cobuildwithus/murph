This source is included for **Direct cold-plunge dose and habituation evidence**.

**Findings:** The severe acclimation program shifted thermoregulation and reduced cold-stress/discomfort signals, but the exposure could last up to 170 minutes and is not a standard cold-plunge dose. Because sessions continued until rectal temperature reached 35.5 °C or 170 minutes, findings should be treated as safety/dose-boundary context.

**Why it matters:** Important acclimation boundary source, but exposure is much more severe than standard cold plunges.

**Potential experiment signals:** thermoregulation; cold stress markers; psychological discomfort; cold sensation; immune activity; specific immunity.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use for severe-acclimation context only; do not promote as a routine protocol model.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Population mismatch:** Severe cold-acclimation program in healthy males; substantial mismatch to short cold-plunge users.

**Limitations:** Healthy male-only sample.; Severe prolonged exposure outside ordinary cold-plunge protocols.; No randomized wellness outcome.
