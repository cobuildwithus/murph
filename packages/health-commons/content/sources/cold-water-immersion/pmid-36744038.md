This source is included for **Sports recovery and training-adaptation boundary**.

**Findings:** Across 20 included studies, CWI reduced immediate DOMS and RPE, lowered CK at 24 h, and lowered lactate at 24 and 48 h. The meta-analysis found immediate CMJ reduction and no clear evidence that CWI changed CRP or IL-6 during a 48-hour recovery period.

**Why it matters:** Separates subjective and biochemical recovery signals from immediate explosive-performance suppression and null inflammatory-marker findings.

**Potential experiment signals:** self_report:doms; self_report:rpe; performance:countermovement_jump; biomarker:creatine_kinase; biomarker:lactate; biomarker:c_reactive_protein; biomarker:interleukin_6.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use for adjacent post-exercise recovery endpoints and for the caution that cold exposure immediately after exercise may reduce explosive performance.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness to Cold Plunge:** `adjacent_variant`.

**Population mismatch:** Healthy post-exercise samples, not general cold-plunge users and not Murph participants.

**Intervention or exposure:** Cold-water immersion after high-intensity or exhaustive exercise; included studies defined CWI as water at or below 15°C.

**Comparator or control:** Control/passive or no-CWI recovery conditions in included randomized and crossover studies.

**Duration or follow-up:** Immediate, 24-hour, and 48-hour post-exercise recovery windows.

**Endpoints:** delayed-onset muscle soreness; ratings of perceived exertion; countermovement jump; creatine kinase; lactate/lactate dehydrogenase; C-reactive protein; interleukin-6.

**Effect estimates or direction:** Pooled data showed immediate reductions in DOMS and RPE, reduced CMJ at 0 h, lower CK at 24 h, lower lactate at 24 and 48 h, and no clear effect on CRP or IL-6 over 48 h.

**Adverse events or safety notes:** Immediate CMJ reduction is a performance/safety-of-use boundary for returning to explosive activity soon after CWI; no adverse-event ledger was otherwise available.

**Limitations:** Included articles were limited to English-language studies and older search coverage. The authors note that subgroup analysis by sites and water temperatures did not clarify all heterogeneity. Immediate performance impairment conflicts with some subjective recovery improvements. Healthy exercise-recovery context does not establish non-exercise wellness benefits.
