## Extraction summary

This source is included for **Immune, inflammation, and illness context**.

**Findings:** The review described evidence, mainly from small mammals, suggesting acute severe chilling can suppress several immune-response components; this is a mechanistic caution, not cold-plunge efficacy evidence.; Because much of the severe-chilling signal comes from animal or non-protocol-specific contexts, it should not be generalized into a human cold-plunge benefit or harm claim without direct evidence.

**Why it matters:** It provides a safety/biology boundary for public immunity claims by showing that cold stress effects can be directionally mixed and exposure-dependent.

**Potential experiment signals:** immune function; lymphocyte proliferation; natural killer cell activity; infection susceptibility.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use only as historical mechanism and caution context; do not cite it as evidence that cold plunges improve immunity.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
