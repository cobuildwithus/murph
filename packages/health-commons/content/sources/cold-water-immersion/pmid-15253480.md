This source is included for **Winter-swimming wellbeing study**.

## Findings
- **Source kind / design:** Prospective observational comparison (journal_article).
- **Population:** Adults participating in winter swimming and non-swimming controls.
- **Participant count:** Not clearly reported in the retrieved metadata.
- **Intervention or exposure:** Regular winter swimming across a 4-month season in natural cold water.
- **Comparator or control:** Non-swimming controls.
- **Duration or follow-up:** 4-month winter swimming season
- **Endpoints:** Profile of Mood States / subjective well-being domains including vigor, energy, tension, fatigue, mood, and self-reported symptoms.
- **Effect estimate or direction:** Swimmers felt more energetic, active, and brisk than controls after the winter season; adaptation was also associated with lower tension and fatigue in retrieved summaries.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** Safety was not the main endpoint. Baseline health status differed, with swimmers reportedly having more physician-diagnosed diseases, which complicates interpretation.
- **Limitations:** Outdoor winter swimming is an adjacent variant that can bundle exercise, social contact, anticipation, and environmental exposure. Retrieved metadata did not clearly report sample size.
- **Population mismatch:** This source does not cleanly match a default self-directed deliberate cold-plunge protocol.
- **Directness to Cold Plunge:** adjacent_variant.

## Why it matters
Suggests mood and vigor may be worth tracking, while showing why winter-swimming evidence must stay separate from deliberate plunge claims.

Adjacent winter-swimming context only; do not promote into direct cold-plunge efficacy claims.
