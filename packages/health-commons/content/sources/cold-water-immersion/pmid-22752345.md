This source is included for **Repeated-use athlete recovery study**.



## Findings

In a crossover design, 11 cyclists completed two separate 3-day training blocks and recovered after each session either by standing in 10°C water or at room temperature for 5 minutes.

Cold-water immersion better maintained mean sprint power, cadence, and mean exercise heart rate than passive recovery, and ln rMSSD immediately after immersion was higher. However, there was no clear difference in time-trial performance or waking ln rMSSD.

The discussion also notes no obvious effect on sleep onset latency and no higher nocturnal parasympathetic activity after cold-water immersion. That mixed pattern is useful because it shows immediate autonomic changes do not necessarily translate into overnight recovery advantages.



## Why it matters

Important adjacent source for repeated-use recovery behavior, because it looks at consecutive days rather than a single immersion. It still remains specific to trained cyclists.

## Protocol takeaway

Use as adjacent athlete-recovery context only. It should not be used to claim that repeated cold plunges improve next-day performance or sleep in ordinary users.



## Claim use

Context only. Appropriate for repeated-use athlete-recovery context, not for direct cold-plunge efficacy claims in broader populations.
