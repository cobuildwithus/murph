This source is included for **Parasympathetic recovery study**.



## Findings

Ten male cyclists completed two repeated supramaximal cycling exercises separated by a 20-minute recovery period that included either 5 minutes of 14°C cold-water immersion or a heated control condition.

Cold-water immersion had no effect on rectal temperature, exercise performance, or HR recovery tau, but vagal-related HRV indices decreased after exercise only in the control condition. Relative to control, cold-water immersion increased HRV indices before the second exercise and increased rMSSD30s after it.

No adverse events were reported in the accessible abstract, although the paper notes that cold-water immersion can raise arrhythmia concerns in some settings. For Murph, that makes this a mechanistic adjacent source plus a reminder that autonomic responses are not automatically benign for every user.



## Why it matters

A classic adjacent physiology study for post-exercise vagal rebound. It is useful for mechanistic/autonomic language but still belongs outside direct everyday cold-plunge claims.

Use only as adjacent post-exercise autonomic context. Do not generalize its HRV findings to ordinary cold-plunge sessions that are not embedded in supramaximal exercise recovery.
