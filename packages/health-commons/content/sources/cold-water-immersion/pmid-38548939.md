This source is included for **Sports recovery and training-adaptation boundary**.

**Findings:** The randomized crossover trial found no clear performance or perceptive-recovery advantage for CWI over thermoneutral immersion or passive recovery, although athletes preferred water-immersion options to passive recovery.

**Why it matters:** Preserves a null/mixed athlete finding: repeated CWI was preferred but did not outperform thermoneutral or passive recovery for measured performance.

**Potential experiment signals:** performance:swim_100m_freestyle; performance:upper_body_power; performance:lower_body_power; measure:flexibility; self_report:pain; self_report:tiredness; self_report:preference.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as a negative/does-not-confirm sports-recovery source, especially when discussing perceived preference versus measured performance.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness to Cold Plunge:** `adjacent_variant`.

**Population mismatch:** Competitive adolescent swimmers using post-exercise recovery between training sessions; not a general cold-plunge protocol.

**Intervention or exposure:** Cold-water immersion at 14 ± 1°C, three times during a training week after land-based resistance training and before swim training.

**Comparator or control:** Thermoneutral water immersion at 27 ± 1°C as placebo and passive recovery.

**Duration or follow-up:** Three interventions per week between land-based resistance training and swim training; randomized order with 1-week washout.

**Endpoints:** 100 m freestyle performance; flexibility; upper body power; lower body power; shoulder proprioception; pain; tiredness; well-being; sleep-related perceptions; preference.

**Effect estimates or direction:** CWI did not improve swimming or functional performance outcomes compared with thermoneutral or passive recovery; pain/tiredness showed intervention effects without clear post hoc separation, and athletes preferred water-immersion options over passive recovery.

**Adverse events or safety notes:** No explicit adverse-event signal extracted; water immersion preference was higher than passive recovery.

**Limitations:** Small adolescent swimmer sample. Sport-specific training-week design may not generalize to adult/general cold-plunge users. Crossover placebo condition complicates expectation effects. No clear advantage on performance or perceptive outcomes.
