Cold-water immersion has a broad literature, but only a small part of it is direct evidence for a simple, unsupervised home cold-plunge experiment.

This bibliography separates five jobs:

1. **Direct healthy-adult plunge evidence:** the review backbone and the small protocol-like studies that support cautious claims about delayed stress/affect and limited repeated-use cardiovascular signals.
2. **Safety boundaries:** cold-shock, cardiorespiratory conflict, arrhythmia plausibility, drowning-risk framing, and acclimation papers that shape screening and stop conditions more than efficacy.
3. **Dose and mechanism context:** temperature, duration, and acute biomarker papers that help explain physiological load without proving that colder or longer is better.
4. **Athlete recovery and training timing:** post-exercise CWI and contrast-water literatures that can matter for soreness, performance timing, sleep, or strength adaptation, but should stay separate from stand-alone daily-use claims.
5. **Adjacent variants and public claims:** winter swimming, cold showers, cryotherapy, clinical supervised protocols, and external named protocol pages that help map boundaries and expectations rather than support Murph’s default assertions.

## Corpus snapshot

- 55 curated records total.
- A small direct healthy-adult subset underpins the default protocol; most remaining records are safety-boundary, mechanistic, athlete-recovery, or adjacent-variant context.
- Coverage years: 1989–2025.
