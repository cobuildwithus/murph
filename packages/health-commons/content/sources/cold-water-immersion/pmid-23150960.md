This source is included for **Safety, adverse events, and cold-shock boundaries**.

**Findings:** The review frames accidental hypothermia as a clinical safety condition requiring recognition, staging, and appropriate rewarming/triage rather than self-experiment escalation. Emergency hypothermia evidence is useful for safety boundaries but should not be promoted as direct evidence for routine cold-plunge benefits.

**Why it matters:** Provides clinical safety context for why duration, supervision, rewarming, and stop rules matter.

**Potential experiment signals:** core temperature if available; shivering status; confusion; rewarming needs; arrhythmia symptoms.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use for safety-only hypothermia boundaries and do not cite as efficacy evidence.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

**Population mismatch:** Accidental hypothermia patients may be injured, intoxicated, exposed for long durations, or otherwise unlike voluntary cold-plunge users.

**Limitations:** Clinical review, not a cold-plunge trial.; Emergency populations differ greatly from screened wellness users.; Does not estimate cold-plunge benefits.
