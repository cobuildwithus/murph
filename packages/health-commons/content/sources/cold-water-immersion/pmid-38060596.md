This source is included for **Safety, adverse events, and cold-shock boundaries**.

**Findings:** In 15 matched pairs of children/adolescents with LQT1 and controls, water-activity testing found no significant ventricular arrhythmias in LQT1 participants but did show a lower heart-rate decrease than controls during facial immersion and whole-body submersion tasks. This is a cardiac-risk boundary, not reassurance for unsupervised cold plunging.

**Why it matters:** Helps keep inherited arrhythmia conditions separate from routine cold plunge claims and supports medical-clearance boundaries.

**Potential experiment signals:** known LQT/arrhythmia diagnosis, beta-blocker use, heart-rate response, syncope history.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as cardiac-risk screening context: known channelopathy/arrhythmia conditions require clinician guidance before cold-water exposure.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.
