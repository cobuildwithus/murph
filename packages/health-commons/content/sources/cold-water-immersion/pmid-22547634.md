This source is included for **Arrhythmia risk mechanism**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Topical review / mechanistic hypothesis review (2012).
- **People studied or addressed:** Cold-water submersion physiology with healthy-volunteer arrhythmia observations.
- **Role in Murph:** direct arrhythmia-mechanism boundary.
- **Directness to Cold Plunge:** direct safety-boundary evidence for arrhythmia risk mechanisms during plunges.
- **Population mismatch:** Low for the mechanism question, although this is a mechanistic review and not a consumer plunge trial.
- **Most relevant Murph signals:** palpitations, ectopy symptoms, chest pain or pressure, dizziness, face immersion or breath-holding behavior.

## Findings

- The review proposes that cold water can simultaneously trigger sympathetic cold-shock tachycardia and parasympathetic diving-response bradycardia.
- That simultaneous autonomic drive—'autonomic conflict'—is proposed as a mechanism for ectopy and potentially more serious arrhythmias.
- The review also notes that arrhythmias have been observed in healthy volunteers during cold-water submersion.

## Limitations

- Topical mechanistic review rather than a direct randomized safety trial.
- Does not itself define a safe home dose.

## Why it matters

This is the key mechanistic source for why cold plunges need explicit arrhythmia, palpitations, and cardiac-history warnings rather than only hypothermia warnings.

## Potential experiment signals

- palpitations
- ectopy symptoms
- chest pain or pressure
- dizziness
- face immersion or breath-holding behavior

## Protocol takeaway

Do not combine abrupt cold entry with deliberate breath holds or face immersion, and treat cardiac symptoms or a significant cardiac history as a reason for extra caution or clinician input.

`safety-only`. Keep this source on the safety/context boundary rather than using it as direct efficacy support for the protocol.
