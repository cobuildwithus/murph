This source is included for **Safety, adverse events, and cold-shock boundaries**.

**Findings:** The review proposes autonomic conflict as a plausible way cold-water exposure can become arrhythmogenic when sympathetic cold-shock responses and parasympathetic diving responses are co-activated. The source supports treating breath-holding and face/underwater submersion as higher-risk variants rather than interchangeable with a head-out cold-plunge protocol.

**Why it matters:** Provides a mechanistic safety boundary for why breath-holding, face submersion, and panic should not be treated as benign variations of cold plunge.

**Potential experiment signals:** heart rate; arrhythmia symptoms; breath-hold/face-submersion context; panic or gasp response.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as safety-only evidence: avoid breath-holding/underwater submersion and flag arrhythmia-prone contexts.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

**Population mismatch:** Mechanistic and submersion contexts differ from screened, supervised, head-out cold plunge.

**Limitations:** Review/mechanistic source, not a cold-plunge efficacy study.; Includes submersion and breath-hold contexts that may exceed a head-out cold-plunge protocol.; Does not quantify benefit outcomes.
