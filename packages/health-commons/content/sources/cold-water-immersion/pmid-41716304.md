This source is included for **Sports recovery and training-adaptation boundary**.

**Findings:** The meta-analysis found CWI reduced CK (g -0.24) and DOMS (g -0.40) compared with passive rest, while CMJ (g -0.02) and MVIC (g 0.08) did not show significant recovery benefits. Partial CWI significantly inhibited immediate CMJ performance (g -0.94), so the source cautions against use when short intervals require continuous explosive power output.

**Why it matters:** Clarifies that whole-body immersion may not be necessary for acute muscle-damage recovery and preserves immediate explosive-power caution.

**Potential experiment signals:** biomarker:creatine_kinase; self_report:doms; performance:countermovement_jump; performance:mvic; protocol:immersion_depth.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as adjacent evidence for muscle-damage recovery and body-region dose context; include the immediate explosive-power caution.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness to Cold Plunge:** `adjacent_variant`.

**Population mismatch:** Post-exercise muscle-damage studies in healthy/athlete samples; not direct wellness Cold Plunge evidence.

**Intervention or exposure:** Single acute CWI, categorized as lower-body/partial or whole-body immersion.

**Comparator or control:** Seated passive rest control.

**Duration or follow-up:** Acute CWI with outcomes measured from immediately post-intervention through 72 h after exercise.

**Endpoints:** creatine kinase; delayed-onset muscle soreness; countermovement jump; maximal voluntary isometric contraction.

**Effect estimates or direction:** CWI reduced CK (g -0.24) and DOMS (g -0.40) versus control, but did not significantly improve CMJ or MVIC; partial CWI immediately inhibited CMJ (g -0.94), and benefits concentrated around 24 h.

**Adverse events or safety notes:** Immediate explosive-power inhibition with partial CWI is a caution for short intervals requiring repeated explosive output.

**Limitations:** Potential publication bias for CK. Female participants were underrepresented. Acute muscle-damage recovery only; not long-term adaptation evidence. Blinding infeasibility creates performance-bias concerns.
