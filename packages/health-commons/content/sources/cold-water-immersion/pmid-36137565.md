This source is included for **Claim calibration and uncertainty review**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Review article / literature review (2022).
- **People studied or addressed:** Voluntary cold-water exposure literature excluding accidental immersion, wetsuit use, and >20°C water (104 included studies).
- **Role in Murph:** same-mechanism uncertainty and safety review.
- **Directness to Cold Plunge:** same-mechanism review with strong claim-calibration and safety value.
- **Population mismatch:** Moderate: broad voluntary cold-water exposure literature is wider than a brief cold-plunge protocol and includes heterogeneous settings and habits.
- **Most relevant Murph signals:** claim tracking vs actual outcomes, confounders, cardiopulmonary symptoms, temperature and exposure heterogeneity, social/activity confounding.

## Findings

- After filtering, the review regarded 104 studies as relevant.
- Its main conclusion is that the health effects of voluntary cold-water exposure remain debated and difficult to separate from confounding factors such as activity level, social interaction, and generally healthier lifestyles.
- The review also keeps risks explicit, including hypothermia and heart/lung problems related to cold shock.

## Limitations

- Broad heterogeneous literature review rather than one defined protocol.
- Many included studies were small, single-sex, or differed in water temperature and salinity.
- Benefit claims remain vulnerable to healthy-user and lifestyle confounding.

## Why it matters

This is the best source in the batch for preventing overclaiming when users try to generalize broad cold-water hype into a specific cold-plunge protocol.

## Potential experiment signals

- claim tracking vs actual outcomes
- confounders
- cardiopulmonary symptoms
- temperature and exposure heterogeneity
- social/activity confounding

## Protocol takeaway

Do not oversell benefits from this literature. Keep uncertainty, confounding, and cardiopulmonary risk explicit.

## Claim use

`safety-only`. Keep this source on the safety/context boundary rather than using it as direct efficacy support for the protocol.
