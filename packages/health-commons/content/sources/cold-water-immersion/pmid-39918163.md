This source is included for **Sports recovery and training-adaptation boundary**.

**Findings:** The systematic review reported that all 12 included articles observed parasympathetic reactivation with CWI after physical exertion, supporting HRV as an adjacent recovery signal rather than a direct wellness outcome.

**Why it matters:** Useful for HRV endpoint selection and autonomic-recovery framing while avoiding direct wellbeing claims from a biomarker.

**Potential experiment signals:** biomarker:hrv; biomarker:parasympathetic_reactivation; measure:post_exercise_recovery.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as measurement/mechanistic context for HRV and parasympathetic reactivation, not as direct Cold Plunge efficacy evidence.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness to Cold Plunge:** `measurement_context`.

**Population mismatch:** Athlete/post-exercise HRV context rather than direct general cold-plunge protocol evidence.

**Intervention or exposure:** Cold-water immersion after physical exertion.

**Comparator or control:** Passive recovery or other recovery conditions in included HRV studies.

**Duration or follow-up:** Acute post-exercise HRV recovery windows; timing varied by included study.

**Endpoints:** heart rate variability; parasympathetic reactivation; post-exercise recovery.

**Effect estimates or direction:** The review reported that all included studies found parasympathetic reactivation after CWI, with some statistically significant comparisons against passive recovery and moderate-to-large effect sizes in several studies.

**Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse-event extraction available from accessible abstract-level data.

**Limitations:** HRV is a recovery/physiology marker, not a direct health-outcome endpoint. Included study protocols and HRV metrics varied. Post-exercise athlete context does not establish general wellness benefit. Accessible extraction relied on abstract/metadata rather than a full text PDF in Git.
