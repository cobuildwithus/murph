This source is included for **Winter-swimming acclimation and cardiovascular context**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Literature review (2019).
- **People studied or addressed:** Winter swimmers and whole-body cold-water exposure context.
- **Role in Murph:** adjacent acclimation/cardiovascular context.
- **Directness to Cold Plunge:** adjacent winter-swimming context rather than direct plunge evidence.
- **Population mismatch:** High: winter swimmers experience a different exposure pattern and task demand than a short stationary plunge.
- **Most relevant Murph signals:** adaptation history, entry symptoms, palpitations, time in cold water, whole-body stress tolerance.

## Findings

- The review describes winter swimming as a stressful whole-body cold exposure to which swimmers may acquire variable degrees of adaptation.
- It also notes that harmful effects remain possible and recommends a stepwise strategy to initiate and build up the activity.
- That makes it adjacent acclimation context, not a direct cold-plunge outcome source.

## Limitations

- Adjacent winter-swimming variant rather than stationary cold plunges.
- Review article rather than a defined protocol trial.

## Why it matters

This source is useful for acclimation and cardiovascular-context language, but it should not be promoted into direct evidence for a home plunge protocol.

## Potential experiment signals

- adaptation history
- entry symptoms
- palpitations
- time in cold water
- whole-body stress tolerance

## Protocol takeaway

Use this as adjacent acclimation context only: gradual build-up makes sense, but winter-swimming protection claims should stay separate from brief plunges.

## Claim use

`safety-only`. Keep this source on the safety/context boundary rather than using it as direct efficacy support for the protocol.
