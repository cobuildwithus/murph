This source is included for **Athlete sleep and recovery study**.



## Findings

Thirty recreationally trained men completed five days of heat training and were randomized to 90 minutes of cycling with passive recovery, the same training plus 15 minutes of cold-water immersion, or a shorter/higher-intensity heat-training condition with passive recovery.

Compared with baseline, heat training reduced total time asleep and perceived sleep quality and increased awake duration and wake after sleep onset. The abstract reports shorter sleep latency in the shorter-training arm versus the longer passive-recovery arm, but no differences between interventions for the other sleep variables.

The authors specifically note that adding cold-water immersion had minimal effect on sleep quantity or quality. That makes this a useful boundary source against overextending athlete-recovery sleep claims.



## Why it matters

Important for guarding sleep claims. This is a primary cold-water recovery sleep study, but the context is consecutive-day heat training in recreationally trained men, not ordinary cold-plunge use.

## Protocol takeaway

Use as athlete-only sleep context. It does not support a general claim that adding cold-water immersion improves sleep quantity or quality.



## Claim use

Context only. Appropriate for athlete heat-training sleep context, not for direct protocol claims that a cold plunge improves sleep.
