This source is included for **Adjacent and measurement context**.

**Findings:** Mean systolic blood pressure in winter swimmers decreased from 134 ± 12 to 128 ± 12 mmHg (p < 0.05), but controls also showed a slight non-significant drop; plasma norepinephrine decreased from autumn to spring without a statistically significant between-group difference; serotonin decreased in both groups, and beta-endorphin/HVA were not clearly changed in accessible summaries. Because several changes were also observed in controls, the source supports preserving a seasonal/control attribution boundary rather than treating winter swimming as the sole causal explanation.

**Why it matters:** Provides human seasonal biomarker context for catecholamines, serotonin-related measures, beta-endorphin, and blood pressure while showing why seasonal/control effects must be preserved.

**Potential experiment signals:** systolic blood pressure; norepinephrine; epinephrine; serotonin; beta-endorphin; seasonal timing/context.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use only as adjacent mechanistic context; do not claim winter swimming caused biomarker changes because controls showed similar seasonal directions.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Population mismatch:** Habitual Finnish winter swimmers and controls across a winter season differ from a short, head-out, consumer cold-plunge protocol in screened adults.

**Limitations:** Small observational/seasonal comparison rather than randomized intervention.; Winter swimming is an adjacent variant that may include swimming, outdoor context, and repeated seasonal exposure rather than a standardized cold plunge.; Similar biomarker directions in controls limit attribution to winter swimming.; Accessible extraction relied on abstract/indexed metadata and secondary snippets; no PDF was stored..
