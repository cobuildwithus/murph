This source is included for **Sports recovery and training-adaptation boundary**.

**Findings:** Neither CWI nor percussive massage significantly improved performance recovery over 72 h compared with passive rest, and CWI showed immediate lower jump performance after the intervention. The study provides a useful recovery endpoint set—soreness, stiffness, skin temperature, leg volume, CK, and three jump tests—but its findings do not support a direct CWI performance-recovery claim.

**Why it matters:** Preserves a direct post-exercise RCT with no clear recovery advantage and an immediate performance-decline signal.

**Potential experiment signals:** self_report:muscle_soreness; self_report:stiffness; biomarker:creatine_kinase; performance:squat_jump; performance:countermovement_jump; performance:drop_jump.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as a does-not-confirm adjacent recovery trial and as a caution against immediate post-plunge explosive-performance claims.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness to Cold Plunge:** `adjacent_variant`.

**Population mismatch:** Physically active adults after induced eccentric fatigue; not general cold-plunge use.

**Intervention or exposure:** Twelve minutes of lower-extremity CWI at 11 ± 0.5°C up to hip height after eccentric exercise.

**Comparator or control:** Twelve minutes of percussive massage at 40 Hz or passive supine rest.

**Duration or follow-up:** Measurements at baseline, immediately after exercise, immediately after intervention, and 24, 48, and 72 h after exercise.

**Endpoints:** muscle soreness; perceived stiffness; skin temperature; leg volume; creatine kinase; squat jump; countermovement jump; drop jump reactive strength index.

**Effect estimates or direction:** Neither CWI nor percussive massage improved 72-hour performance recovery versus passive rest; CWI caused immediate lower jump performance after the intervention, and soreness showed no group-by-time advantage.

**Adverse events or safety notes:** Immediate jump-performance reduction after CWI is a near-term performance/safety boundary.

**Limitations:** Small RCT in physically active adults. Single lower-limb CWI exposure after a specific eccentric protocol. Not a long-term adaptation or general wellness study. No significant performance-recovery improvement versus passive rest.
