This source is included for **Autonomic recovery comparison**.



## Findings

Twenty male subjects completed exhaustive tests at maximal aerobic speed on three occasions with randomized 6-minute recovery strategies: cold-water immersion, active recovery, or passive recovery.

Both active recovery and cold-water immersion reduced lactate relative to passive recovery late in recovery. For autonomic outcomes, the study reported a time-by-recovery interaction for SDNN and lnLF, with cold-water immersion showing higher SDNN than passive recovery at 15 minutes and higher SDNN than active recovery during recovery and again at 30 and 75 minutes.

The authors described the autonomic benefit as limited. That wording matters because the source is often cited for HRV recovery, but it does not establish broad cold-plunge efficacy outside strenuous exercise.



## Why it matters

Useful for comparing cold water immersion with active and passive recovery after strenuous exercise, but still clearly belongs to adjacent sports-recovery physiology.
