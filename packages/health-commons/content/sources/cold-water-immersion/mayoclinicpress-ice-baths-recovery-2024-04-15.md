This source is included for **External protocol/public-claims context**.

**Findings:** The source states that the science behind ice-bath benefits is still thin and that much research is too weak for strong claims. The source reports that a 15-day Wim Hof Method study did not show positive changes in blood pressure, heart rate, heart function, or mood; because the method is bundled, this is not direct cold-plunge-only evidence. The source reports fewer sick days in a cold-shower study while noting no immune-marker support, making this adjacent context rather than direct cold-plunge immune evidence. The source warns about cold shock, drowning, blood-pressure and heart-rate stress, hypothermia, and frostbite, and recommends starting with brief home exposures rather than extreme settings.

**Why it matters:** Useful public synthesis because it explicitly counters overclaiming while preserving practical safety and dose boundaries.

**Potential experiment signals:** soreness/fatigue; mood; sick days; immune markers; heart rate; blood pressure; cold shock.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use for evidence-boundary and safety language; do not treat cited adjacent studies as direct proof for Cold Plunge.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Population mismatch:** General public article; not a Murph trial and not solely cold-plunge exposure.

**Limitations:** Public explainer, not a systematic evidence extraction. Includes adjacent cold-shower and Wim Hof bundled-protocol evidence. No source-owned effect estimates beyond cited study summaries.
