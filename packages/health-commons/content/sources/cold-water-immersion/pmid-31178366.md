This source is included for **Fatality mechanism context**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Review article (2019).
- **People studied or addressed:** Whitewater recreation and cold-water fatality context.
- **Role in Murph:** adjacent fatality mechanism context.
- **Directness to Cold Plunge:** adjacent variant fatality context.
- **Population mismatch:** High: whitewater recreation includes current, rescue, impact, and swimming demands that differ sharply from a controlled plunge.
- **Most relevant Murph signals:** gasp, tachypnea, panic, palpitations, current/swimming demands vs stationary use.

## Findings

- The review proposes that some otherwise poorly explained cold-water 'flush drowning' cases in whitewater are manifestations of cold-water immersion syndrome.
- It notes that the initial cold-shock response begins in water below roughly 21–25°C and that the respiratory component includes an inhalational gasp followed by tachypnea that may exceed 60 breaths per minute.
- It also connects autonomic conflict with ectopic beats or arrhythmias and suggests that preparation, conditioning, and equipment may modify some responses.

## Limitations

- Adjacent whitewater fatality context rather than voluntary stationary plunges.
- Review article rather than direct cold-plunge trial.

## Why it matters

This source helps prevent a false sense of security about 'not-that-cold' water and reinforces that respiratory cold shock can be a primary fatality pathway.

Use this as fatality-mechanism context only. It supports threshold and respiratory-hazard warnings, but it should not be treated as direct evidence about a stationary cold plunge protocol.
