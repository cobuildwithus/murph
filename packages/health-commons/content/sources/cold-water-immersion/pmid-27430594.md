This source is included for **Temperature comparison recovery trial**.



## Findings

Sixty healthy male participants were randomized to passive recovery, 15 minutes of cold-water immersion at 9°C, or 15 minutes at 14°C after an eccentric exercise protocol.

Markers included creatine kinase, muscle soreness, pain threshold, perceived recovery, and maximal voluntary isometric contraction up to 96 hours. The abstract reports large time effects for all outcomes, earlier immediate soreness recovery in the cold-water groups, and earlier maximal-force recovery in the 14°C group compared with 9°C and control, but no significant overall group or group-by-time effects.

The authors reported no evidence of a clear dose-response relationship and no deleterious effects on performance. That makes this a useful adjacent implementation source, but not strong support for a single best cold-plunge temperature.



## Why it matters

Directly compares two cold-water temperatures after exercise, which is useful for adjacent implementation nuance, but the population and outcome set are specific to sports recovery.

## Protocol takeaway

Use as adjacent recovery-temperature context only. It does not show a robust dose-response relationship that should be converted into a general cold-plunge prescription.



## Claim use

Context only. This trial can calibrate adjacent recovery-temperature claims, but it should not be promoted as direct support for general cold-plunge efficacy or a universally optimal water temperature.
