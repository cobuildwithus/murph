This source is included for **Cardiovascular/autonomic physiology context**.

**Findings:** Cool head-out immersion increased middle cerebral artery blood velocity and mean arterial pressure over time, providing cardiovascular/cerebrovascular context. The pilot study did not find a clear alteration in cerebrovascular reactivity to hypercapnia despite elevated middle cerebral artery blood velocity.

**Why it matters:** Useful for candidate cerebrovascular and blood-pressure endpoints while preserving temperature/duration mismatch.

**Potential experiment signals:** middle cerebral artery velocity; mean arterial pressure; cerebrovascular reactivity; water temperature; immersion duration.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as measurement/context evidence, not support for a cold-plunge benefit claim.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Population mismatch:** 25 °C cool head-out immersion for 45 minutes differs from short colder cold plunge.

**Limitations:** Pilot sample of 10 healthy young adults.; Cool 25 °C water differs from colder cold-plunge protocols.; Physiology endpoints only; no wellness outcome.
