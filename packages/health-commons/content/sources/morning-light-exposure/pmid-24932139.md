This source is included for **indoor_workplace_classroom_home_daylight**.

**Findings:** Window/daylight office workers showed better subjective sleep and health scores and longer actigraphy-estimated sleep duration than windowless-office workers. Several actigraphy metrics were not different, and the architecture/workstation assignment was not randomized.

**Why it matters:** It is a key boundary source: daylight through workplace windows may matter for sleep and quality of life, but this is not the same as intentionally going outdoors after waking.

**Potential experiment signals:** PSQI global score and sleep disturbance, SF-36 vitality and role limitations, actigraphy sleep duration, physical activity, and workday light exposure.

**Protocol takeaway:** Window/daylight access is relevant context and may be a fallback when outdoor exposure is impractical, but findings should be labeled observational and indoor.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
