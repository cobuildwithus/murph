This source is included for **care_home_dementia_older_adult_indoor_light**.

**Findings:** This natural-light availability study compared institutionalized elderly residents exposed to different waking and sleeping light environments. Reported results linked higher waking light and lower sleeping-time light with better circadian rhythm and sleep-quality measures.

**Why it matters:** Supports the mechanistic boundary that both daytime light and nighttime darkness can shape circadian outcomes in institutional settings.

**Potential experiment signals:** waking light exposure, nighttime light exposure, circadian rhythm timing, sleep quality, nocturnal disturbances

**Protocol takeaway:** Use only for same-mechanism context about light/dark contrast; do not translate it into a healthy-adult outdoor dose claim.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
