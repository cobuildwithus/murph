This source is included for **clinical_light_therapy_device_boundaries**.

**Findings:** Population: Adults with acute bipolar depression. Intervention/exposure: Bright light therapy as adjunctive treatment, one hour every morning for two weeks. Comparator/control: Dim red light therapy control. Duration/follow-up: 2 weeks Endpoints: HAMD-17 reduction response, QIDS-SR16 onset day, switch into hypomania/mania, adverse events. Effect/direction: Seventy-four participants were randomized and 63 completed. Response rates were 78.19% for BLT versus 43.33% for control (p<0.01), and median onset day in the BLT group was 4.33 days. Safety/adverse events: No participants experienced BLT-emergent hypomania; no serious adverse events were reported in the accessible extract.

**Why it matters:** Core bipolar safety and clinical boundary source.

**Potential experiment signals:** HAMD-17 response, QIDS onset, hypomania/mania switch, adverse events

**Protocol takeaway:** Short-term supervised bipolar treatment results should not be generalized to self-directed morning light in bipolar-spectrum users.

**Directness and boundaries:** Directness is `clinical_supervised` with protocol-evidence scope `clinical_supervised`. Population mismatch: Acute bipolar depression, not general outdoor protocol users.

**Limitations and uncertainty:** Short duration; single-blind design; acute bipolar depression population; device-based adjunctive clinical treatment.
