This source is included for **indoor_workplace_classroom_home_daylight**.

**Findings:** LED task lighting was associated with better global sleep quality, subjective sleep assessments, and reduced daytime dysfunction compared with fluorescent task lighting. Sleep duration did not significantly differ, though a positive trend was noted in the accessible abstract.

**Why it matters:** It adds modern windowless-office evidence that spectral/task-light differences can affect subjective sleep quality, while preserving the protocol boundary.

**Potential experiment signals:** PSQI global and component scores, daily sleep diary, daytime dysfunction, sleep duration, and task-lighting condition.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as indoor artificial-light context for windowless offices, not as outdoor morning-light evidence.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
