This source is included for **clinical_sleep_insomnia_dementia_light_therapy_boundaries**.

**Findings:** This Cochrane review sought randomized controlled trials of bright light therapy for primary sleep problems in adults aged 60 and older, with dementia and/or depression screened out. Reviewers found no eligible trials and therefore no conclusions could be drawn about effectiveness. The review describes typical bright light treatment as sitting in front of a light box emitting about 10,000 lux for around two hours daily, with timing depending on sleep-pattern irregularity, but that dose was not evaluated in eligible included trials.

**Why it matters:** This preserves an older null/inconclusive boundary and prevents overstatement of older-adult bright-light evidence.

**Potential experiment signals:** trial availability, sleep quality, sleep timing, older-adult inclusion criteria, safety evidence gap

**Protocol takeaway:** Do not cite this source as efficacy evidence; cite it only for the absence of eligible older-adult RCT evidence in that review.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
