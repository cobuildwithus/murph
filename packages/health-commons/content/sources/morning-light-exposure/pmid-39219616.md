This source is included for **direct_outdoor_daylight_protocol**.

**Findings:** Seventeen professional male Australian Football athletes were monitored for 14 days. Higher morning light exposure was associated with greater total sleep time (r = 0.31, p < 0.001). Higher daytime light was associated with higher subjective sleep quality (r = 0.48, p < 0.05). Higher evening light was associated with higher Athlete Sleep Screening Questionnaire global scores (r = 0.52, p < 0.05), indicating worse sleep behavior. Other tested relationships were not significant in accessible summaries.

**Why it matters:** Separates morning/daytime light timing from sleep indices in a real-world athlete setting, while clearly mismatched to a general adult protocol.

**Potential experiment signals:** total sleep time, subjective sleep quality, athlete sleep behavior questionnaire, morning mEDI lux, daytime mEDI lux, evening mEDI lux

**Protocol takeaway:** In male professional athletes, higher morning/daytime light was associated with positive sleep indices, while higher evening light related to worse sleep behavior.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
