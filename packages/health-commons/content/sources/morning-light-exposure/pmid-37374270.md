This source is included for **clinical_sleep_insomnia_dementia_light_therapy_boundaries**.

**Findings:** Fourteen adults with insomnia were randomized 1:1. The light-therapy group used a 6000 K, 380 lux, 480 nm device for at least 25 minutes before 09:00 for two weeks. Within the light-therapy group, ESS, ISI, and PSQI improved, but between groups only ESS change was significant after adjustment (control -0.14 vs light therapy -1.43, p = 0.021). Actigraphy sleep measures, serum cortisol, and clock-gene expression were not significant. One person reported mild glare; no serious adverse events were reported.

**Why it matters:** This pilot is highly relevant to morning timing, but it is small, open-label, indoor/device-based, and mixed across endpoints.

**Potential experiment signals:** Epworth Sleepiness Scale, Insomnia Severity Index, Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index, actigraphy sleep efficiency, total sleep time, wake after sleep onset, cortisol, clock-gene expression, glare

**Protocol takeaway:** Morning light-device therapy may improve daytime sleepiness in adults with insomnia, but objective sleep and biomarker results were not significant; keep as context-only.
