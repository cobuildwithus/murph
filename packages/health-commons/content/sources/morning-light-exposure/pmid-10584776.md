This source is included for **safety_boundaries**.

**Source key:** `source_artifact:pmid-10584776`.

**Findings:** Mild treatment-emergent symptoms included jumpiness/jitteriness (8.8%), headache (8.4%), and nausea (15.9%); overactive/excited/elated symptoms emerged more under morning light and remitted more under evening light.

**Why it matters:** Bright-light treatment produced mostly mild side effects, including jitteriness, headache, nausea, and timing-related activation signals.

**Potential experiment signals:** treatment-emergent symptoms, jumpiness/jitteriness, headache, nausea, overactive/excited/elated symptoms.

**Protocol takeaway:** Morning outdoor-light protocols should ask users to watch for headache, nausea, jitteriness, sleep disruption, and elevated or activated mood, especially if light exposure is intensified.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

**Extraction notes:**

- **Participant count:** 83 (reported).
- **Population:** Adults with winter seasonal affective disorder receiving bright light therapy.
- **Intervention or exposure:** 10,000-lux bright light therapy for 30 minutes daily in morning or evening.
- **Comparator or control:** Baseline symptom ratings and morning-versus-evening timing comparisons; no outdoor-light control.
- **Duration or follow-up:** 10 to 14 days.
- **Endpoints:** treatment-emergent symptoms; jumpiness/jitteriness; headache; nausea; overactive/excited/elated symptoms
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Mild treatment-emergent symptoms included jumpiness/jitteriness (8.8%), headache (8.4%), and nausea (15.9%); overactive/excited/elated symptoms emerged more under morning light and remitted more under evening light.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** Mostly mild side effects; this review/article is most useful as a symptom-spectrum and activation boundary rather than as outdoor sunlight evidence.
- **Population mismatch:** Seasonal affective disorder patients, not general morning-routine users.
- **Directness to Morning Outdoor Light Exposure:** Safety boundary. This source does not establish direct efficacy for the protocol variant.

**Directness and boundaries:** The exposure was supervised 10,000-lux artificial light for SAD, not unsupervised outdoor morning light.

**Limitations and uncertainty:**

- Clinical bright-light box exposure, not outdoor morning light.
- SAD treatment population; not healthy self-experimenters.
- Short treatment window.
