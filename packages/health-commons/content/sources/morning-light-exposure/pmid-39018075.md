**Findings:** In 6,639 U.S. adults from NHANES 2011–2014, time above a 1000-lux threshold was associated with lower PHQ-9 depression symptoms (β = -0.19; 95% CI -0.34 to -0.04) and more regular sleep (β = 1.60; 95% CI 0.95–2.25). After adjusting for sleep regularity index, the association between bright-light duration and depression score was no longer significant (β = -0.11; 95% CI -0.26 to 0.04; P = .13), suggesting sleep regularity partly explained the association. This is not a morning or outdoor-only protocol.

**Why it matters:** Recent high-visibility measured-light study with sleep-regularity relevance, while not morning-specific or outdoor-only.

**Potential experiment signals:** time above 1000 lux, sleep regularity index, PHQ-9 depression score, vitamin D

**Protocol takeaway:** More environmental bright light was associated with more regular sleep and lower depression symptoms, but bright light was not outdoor-only or morning-specific.
