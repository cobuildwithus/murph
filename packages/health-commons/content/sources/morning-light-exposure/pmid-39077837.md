This source is included for **direct_outdoor_daylight_protocol**.

**Findings:** In a daily diary sample of 103 U.S. adults followed for up to 70 days, timing of sunlight exposure predicted next-night sleep quality. Morning sunlight exposure, relative to no sunlight, predicted better sleep quality on the brief Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index. Duration of sunlight exposure was generally not associated with sleep quality. The study is useful for timing hypotheses but not causal protocol claims.

**Why it matters:** Directly tests day-level sunlight timing and next-night sleep quality, including a morning sunlight comparison.

**Potential experiment signals:** next-night subjective sleep quality, sunlight timing, sunlight duration, daily diary adherence

**Protocol takeaway:** Morning sunlight timing may be more informative than reported sunlight duration for next-night subjective sleep quality, but the study is observational and self-reported.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
