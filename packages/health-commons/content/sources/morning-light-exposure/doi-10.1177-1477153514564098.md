This source is included for **indoor_workplace_classroom_home_daylight**.

**Findings:** Accessible records report significantly more light exposure in summer than winter, with sleep quantity and sleep quality higher in summer. Mood findings were less clear and not strong enough for a direct mood claim.

**Why it matters:** It reinforces that season and ambient light environment can alter sleep outcomes and should be captured as contextual modifiers in morning-light experiments.

**Potential experiment signals:** Personal light exposure, circadian stimulus, sleep duration/quantity, sleep quality, sleep latency, mood/depression scores, and season.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context for season/light-dose modifiers; do not use as direct evidence for outdoor morning light.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
