This source is included for **free_living_observational_measurement**.

**Findings:** PD participants had lower daytime mean light than controls (201.1 vs 337.7 lux, p<0.001), higher nighttime light (2.0 vs 0.7 lux, p<0.001), shorter duration ≥1000 lux (24.7 vs 50.5 min, p<0.001), and more nighttime duration ≥10 lux (12.6 vs 5.5 min, p<0.001). Among PD patients, the highest quartile of daytime ≥1000 lux versus lowest was associated with +8.0% sleep efficiency and -36.9 min WASO. Highest nighttime mean light versus lowest was associated with -6.8% sleep efficiency, +24.1 min WASO, longer sleep-onset latency, and higher fragmentation index.

**Why it matters:** It preserves both the daytime-light positive signal and nighttime-light risk boundary in a measured-light clinical cohort.

**Potential experiment signals:** minutes ≥1000 lux, nighttime mean light, minutes ≥10 lux at night, sleep efficiency, WASO, SOL, fragmentation index.

**Population mismatch / directness:** Parkinson's disease outpatients; disease, medication, and motor symptoms may affect sleep and actigraphy. Directness: `adjacent_variant`.

**Limitations:** cross-sectional clinical study; PD population; control light measured for fewer days; night-light meters differed and may not be fully validated; actigraphy limitations in PD; not a morning outdoor-light intervention.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as adjacent clinical context: bright daytime exposure and low nighttime exposure may be separable exposure targets.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
