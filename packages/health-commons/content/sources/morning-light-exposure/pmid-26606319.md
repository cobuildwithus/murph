This source is included for **clinical_sleep_insomnia_dementia_light_therapy_boundaries**.

**Findings:** The review included 53 studies and 1154 participants. Light therapy was reported effective for sleep problems overall (Hedges g = 0.39), circadian rhythm sleep disorders (g = 0.41), insomnia (g = 0.47), and Alzheimer's disease/dementia-related sleep problems (g = 0.30). The authors noted smaller effects for randomized trials in circadian rhythm sleep disorders, intensity-related moderation in insomnia, and an indication of publication bias.

**Why it matters:** This backbone review helps prevent overgeneralizing device-based bright-light findings across populations and sleep disorders.

**Potential experiment signals:** overall sleep-problem effect size, circadian rhythm sleep disorder effect size, insomnia effect size, dementia-related sleep effect size, light intensity, publication bias

**Protocol takeaway:** Light-therapy effects are plausible but modest and heterogeneous; use as context-only clinical-boundary evidence.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
