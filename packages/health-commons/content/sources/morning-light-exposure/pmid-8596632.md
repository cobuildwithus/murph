This source is included for **timing_dose_circadian_metrics**.

**Findings:**
- The study directly examines dose-response relationships for circadian resetting by light.
- It supports the idea that light dose, not merely exposure occurrence, matters.
- No outdoor-morning dose threshold is extracted from this batch.

**Intervention or exposure:** Different light intensities/doses

**Comparator or control:** Lower or alternative light-dose conditions

**Duration or follow-up:** Acute controlled light exposure

**Endpoints:** Circadian clock resetting

**Effect estimates or direction:** Direction: light dose was related to circadian resetting; exact effect estimates not extracted.

**Safety/adverse events:** No adverse events extracted.

**Limitations:**
- Controlled lab setting.
- Sample size/effect estimates not extracted here.
- Illuminance-response may not map directly to outdoor behavior.

**Population mismatch:** Lab physiology participants rather than general outdoor-light users.

**Directness to Morning Outdoor Light Exposure:** `same_mechanism`.

**Why it matters:** It is a core source for the dose-response concept behind morning-light prescriptions.

**Potential experiment signals:** light intensity, circadian phase shift, dose-response.

**Protocol takeaway:** Protocol instructions should distinguish dim indoor light from brighter outdoor light, while avoiding unverified dose thresholds.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
