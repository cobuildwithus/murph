This source is included for **free_living_observational_measurement**.

**Findings:** In 6,441 students from multiple cities, later MSFsc was associated with being farther from the equator, later sunset, more reported time outdoors, and waking after sunrise. The extracted record notes no correlation with daily light intensity. The authors interpreted the student exposure pattern as possibly involving little morning light and more later light, which could contribute to phase delay.

**Why it matters:** It keeps a large-sample, real-world chronotype context while explicitly preserving null/mixed elements.

**Potential experiment signals:** MSFsc, social jetlag, time outdoors, sunrise/sunset timing, chronotype.

**Population mismatch / directness:** Student sample and ecological/self-reported light exposure; not adult wearable-measured morning outdoor light. Directness: `adjacent_variant`.

**Limitations:** self-reported sleep and time outdoors; environmental rather than personal measured light; student population; cross-sectional; no intervention.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context for why time outdoors and waking relative to sunrise may influence chronotype; avoid direct protocol claims.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
