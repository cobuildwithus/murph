This source is included for **clinical_light_therapy_device_boundaries**.

**Findings:** Population: Depressed inpatients in a specialized affective disorders unit who stayed in one type of hospital room. Intervention/exposure: Southeast-facing rooms with more daylight exposure. Comparator/control: Northwest-facing rooms with less daylight exposure. Duration/follow-up: Length of inpatient admission in a 1-year naturalistic follow-up Endpoints: length of stay, depression severity reduction. Effect/direction: Length of stay was 29.2±26.8 days in southeast rooms versus 58.8±42.0 days in northwest rooms (p=0.01); depression severity reduction was 52.2% versus 42.2% and was not statistically significant (p=0.42). Safety/adverse events: No adverse events were extracted.

**Why it matters:** Preserves a mixed daylight-room boundary: possible operational outcome, weak symptom outcome.

**Potential experiment signals:** length of stay, depression severity percent reduction

**Protocol takeaway:** Do not infer outdoor-light antidepressant causality from non-random room placement.

**Directness and boundaries:** Directness is `clinical_supervised` with protocol-evidence scope `clinical_supervised`. Population mismatch: Affective-disorder inpatients rather than routine wellness users.

**Limitations and uncertainty:** Small analyzed sample; non-randomized; not blinded; curtain use and actual personal light exposure were not controlled; room lighting specifications incompletely measured.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
