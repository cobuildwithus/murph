This source is included for **direct_outdoor_daylight_protocol**.

**Findings:** Older nursing-home residents were assigned to a sunlight-exposure test group and control group in accessible summaries. Direct sunlight between 8 AM and 10 AM for 5 days was reported to improve the global sleep-quality score (P < .001). Safety extraction is limited: sun allergy and sleeping-pill use were exclusion criteria, and the accessible abstract did not report adverse events.

**Why it matters:** Rare direct outdoor morning-sunlight intervention close to the default Morning Outdoor Light Exposure habit, although it is limited to an institutional older-adult population.

**Potential experiment signals:** subjective sleep quality, PSQI global score, sleep problems, morning light adherence, sun exposure tolerance

**Protocol takeaway:** Morning outdoor sunlight may improve subjective sleep quality in older nursing-home residents with poor sleep, but the short duration and population limit general adult claims.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.
