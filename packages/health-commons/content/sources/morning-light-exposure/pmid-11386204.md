This source is included for **care_home_dementia_older_adult_indoor_light**.

**Findings:** In 66 institutionalized elderly nursing-home patients, median daytime illumination was low and time above bright-light thresholds was brief. Higher light exposure predicted fewer nighttime awakenings, while severe dementia was linked with more daytime sleep and lower activity.

**Why it matters:** Provides foundational same-mechanism context that institutional indoor light exposure can be very low and may relate to sleep consolidation and activity timing in frail older adults.

**Potential experiment signals:** nighttime awakenings, daytime activity, daytime sleep, activity acrophase, ambient light exposure

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as contextual support for why morning/daytime light dose matters, not as direct evidence that healthy adults benefit from outdoor morning light.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
