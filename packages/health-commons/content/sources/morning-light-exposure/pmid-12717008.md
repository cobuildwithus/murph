This source is included for **timing_dose_circadian_metrics**.

**Findings:**
- The source provides a human phase response curve to timed bright-light exposure.
- It supports the central timing principle that light can phase-shift differently depending on circadian timing.
- It should not be used as proof of a specific outdoor protocol benefit.

**Intervention or exposure:** Timed bright-light pulses

**Comparator or control:** Different circadian timing of light pulses

**Duration or follow-up:** Single bright-light pulse protocol

**Endpoints:** Phase response curve and circadian phase shifts

**Effect estimates or direction:** Direction: phase response depends on timing; exact effect estimates not extracted for this batch.

**Safety/adverse events:** No adverse events extracted.

**Limitations:**
- Laboratory bright-light pulses.
- Participant count/effect sizes not extracted.
- Does not test outdoor light.

**Population mismatch:** Lab subjects rather than everyday users.

**Directness to Morning Outdoor Light Exposure:** `same_mechanism`.

**Why it matters:** It is canonical timing evidence for phase-response language.

**Potential experiment signals:** circadian time, phase advance, phase delay, melatonin phase.

**Protocol takeaway:** Morning-light guidance should avoid implying that all light is beneficial at all times; timing is fundamental.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
