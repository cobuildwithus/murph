This source is included for **direct_outdoor_daylight_protocol**.

**Findings:** In 877 community-dwelling older men, wrist actigraphy and light monitoring over about one week linked reduced daytime bright-light exposure with increased sleep-wake fragmentation. Time above >1000 lux was the strongest light-exposure correlate. The study reported that morning and evening >1000-lux exposure did not distinguish low versus high fragmentation as well as afternoon light; an optimal timing point was described around 6.7 hours after habitual sleep offset. This is therefore mixed for a morning-specific claim but useful direct context for outdoor/daytime bright light.

**Why it matters:** Exceptionally relevant because it studies timing of outdoor/bright light exposure in free-living older adults, while also warning against overclaiming morning-specific effects.

**Potential experiment signals:** sleep-wake fragmentation, sleep-wake consolidation, time above 1000 lux, outdoor gardening/walking time, habitual sleep offset

**Protocol takeaway:** Bright outdoor/daytime light exposure relates to better sleep-wake consolidation in older men, but this study did not find morning light to be the best timing discriminator.
