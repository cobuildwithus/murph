This source is included for **indoor_workplace_classroom_home_daylight**.

**Findings:** The optimized daylight/views condition was associated with longer sleep duration and higher cognitive simulation performance than the traditional blinds condition. The effect may have been larger among participants with shorter baseline sleep, but the intervention blended daylight dose and view access.

**Why it matters:** It suggests that daylight dose and views can affect sleep and performance in real office settings, useful for context and fallback design.

**Potential experiment signals:** Actigraphy sleep duration, equivalent melanopic lux, office daylight/view condition, cognitive simulation score, and baseline sleep duration subgroup.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as indoor daylight context; do not cite as direct evidence that outdoor morning light causes the same outcomes.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
