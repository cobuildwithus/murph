This source is included for **direct_outdoor_daylight_protocol**.

**Findings:** The study surveyed 2,709 urban Indian women: 1,316 office-going women and 1,393 homemakers aged 18–79 years. It combined questionnaire survey, actigraphy/light, and urinary melatonin assay components. Objective light/activity data indicated homemakers had greater exposure to brighter morning light intensities than office-going women and better light-activity coupling on weekdays; office-going women showed delayed sleep onset in young and old age groups. Urinary melatonin levels did not differ among groups in accessible summaries. This is an adjacent cross-sectional occupation/population source, not a protocol trial.

**Why it matters:** Useful population-specific feasibility/context source on daily outdoor light, occupation, age, and morning light intensity, but not an intervention.

**Potential experiment signals:** sleep onset timing, morning light intensity, light-activity coupling, intraday activity variability, urinary melatonin

**Protocol takeaway:** Homemakers had greater brighter morning light exposure and better light-activity coupling than office-going women, while urinary melatonin did not differ.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
