This source is included for **clinical_light_therapy_device_boundaries**.

**Findings:** Population: Patients with bipolar depression in randomized controlled light-therapy trials. Intervention/exposure: Adjunctive light therapy in bipolar depression. Comparator/control: Control light or other control conditions in included RCTs. Duration/follow-up: Varied by RCT Endpoints: depressive symptoms, clinical response, remission, acceptability, affective switches. Effect/direction: Seven trials representing 259 patients were identified. The review concluded evidence was positive but nonconclusive that adjunctive light therapy reduces bipolar depressive symptoms and increases clinical response. Safety/adverse events: No difference in affective switches was found between active light and control conditions (OR 1.30; 95% CI 0.38 to 4.44; p=0.67); the treatment was described as well tolerated.

**Why it matters:** High-priority safety boundary for bipolar-spectrum users.

**Potential experiment signals:** depression scores, response, remission, affective switch

**Protocol takeaway:** Clinical supervision is central; do not translate to self-directed outdoor light in bipolar disorder.

**Directness and boundaries:** Directness is `clinical_supervised` with protocol-evidence scope `clinical_supervised`. Population mismatch: Bipolar depression clinical trials, not general outdoor-light users.

**Limitations and uncertainty:** Small samples; different light parameters; short treatment durations; variable trial quality; bipolar safety concerns.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
