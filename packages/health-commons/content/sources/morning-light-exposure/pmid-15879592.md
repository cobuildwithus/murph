This source is included for **care_home_dementia_older_adult_indoor_light**.

**Findings:** In a small pilot study of 11 nursing-home patients with dementia, two hours per day of morning bright-light treatment for two weeks significantly reduced actigraphic daytime sleep during part of the day. The reported effect was not sustained at later follow-up.

**Why it matters:** Provides a concrete small-sample signal on daytime sleep and illustrates short-lived effects in frail dementia populations.

**Potential experiment signals:** daytime sleep, actigraphy, carryover after intervention, adherence

**Protocol takeaway:** Use for adjacent same-mechanism context about daytime sleep and intervention persistence; not direct default-protocol evidence.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
