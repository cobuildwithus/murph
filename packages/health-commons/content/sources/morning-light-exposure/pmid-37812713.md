This source is included for **direct_outdoor_daylight_protocol**.

**Findings:** The study used an open-source wrist-worn SpectraWear light logger and smartphone data collection over seven days in 59 UK adults. Prior light exposure over several hours was associated with lower subjective sleepiness; brighter light in the late sleep episode and after wake was linked to reduced early morning sleepiness/sleep inertia. Higher pre-bedtime light exposure was associated with longer sleep onset latency. Earlier sleep timing correlated with more reproducible daily light patterns and higher daytime/lower night-time light exposure.

**Why it matters:** High-quality real-world measured-light study for timing and mechanism, but it is not outdoor-only or an intervention.

**Potential experiment signals:** melanopic EDI, sleepiness, sleep inertia, sleep onset latency, sleep timing regularity, daytime versus night-time light contrast

**Protocol takeaway:** Measured everyday light patterns relate to sleepiness and sleep timing; pre-bedtime light is a boundary to monitor alongside morning exposure.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
