This source is included for **timing_dose_circadian_metrics**.

**Findings:**
- The 1-hour bright white light PRC produced maximum melatonin-rhythm phase shifts reported as about -2.02 hours for delay and +1.20 hours for advance.
- The authors reported that the 1-hour exposure generated roughly 40% of the response of a longer 6.7-hour PRC exposure.
- This is quantitative same-mechanism evidence, not a direct protocol test.

**Intervention or exposure:** Bright white light pulse

**Comparator or control:** Circadian timing of the pulse and comparison with longer-pulse PRC context

**Duration or follow-up:** Single 1-hour bright white light pulse

**Endpoints:** Melatonin rhythm phase shifts and phase response curve

**Effect estimates or direction:** Maximum reported phase delay about -2.02 h and phase advance about +1.20 h for a 1-hour pulse; approximately 40% of longer 6.7-hour PRC response.

**Safety/adverse events:** No adverse events extracted.

**Limitations:**
- Controlled laboratory bright-light pulse.
- Participant count not extracted.
- Outdoor daylight, behavior, and adherence are not tested.

**Population mismatch:** Laboratory physiology participants rather than general users.

**Directness to Morning Outdoor Light Exposure:** `same_mechanism`.

**Why it matters:** It gives a rare extracted quantitative effect estimate for duration and timing language.

**Potential experiment signals:** 1-hour bright light, phase advance, phase delay, melatonin rhythm.

**Protocol takeaway:** A 1-hour bright-light exposure can shift circadian phase under controlled conditions; translation to brief outdoor exposure must be caveated.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
