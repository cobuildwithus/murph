This source is included for **direct_outdoor_daylight_protocol**.

**Findings:** Twenty healthy participants underwent ambulatory polysomnography and wrist-actigraphy/light assessment, with laboratory salivary DLMO. Later first exposure to >10 lux was associated with more awakenings during subsequent sleep. Later light exposure was associated with shorter latency to first REM episode; less light exposure was associated with higher REM percentage; slow-wave sleep accumulation was larger after high maximum intensity and early first light exposure. The authors explicitly framed these as real-life observations requiring confirmation by interventional studies.

**Why it matters:** Higher-fidelity sleep phenotyping links real-life light exposure timing/intensity with subsequent sleep architecture, but not outdoor morning protocol efficacy.

**Potential experiment signals:** awakenings, REM latency, REM percentage, slow-wave sleep accumulation, first light >10 lux timing, maximum light intensity

**Protocol takeaway:** Earlier first light exposure and high maximum intensity related to sleep-architecture signals, but the evidence is small and mechanistic/observational.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
