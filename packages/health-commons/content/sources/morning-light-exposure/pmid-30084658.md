This source is included for **timing_dose_circadian_metrics**.

**Findings:**
- The title-level result states that late bedtimes prevented circadian phase advances to morning bright light in adolescents.
- This indicates that morning light may not overcome an incompatible sleep schedule.
- It is a boundary-condition source, not an efficacy source.

**Intervention or exposure:** Morning bright light with differing bedtime context

**Comparator or control:** Late bedtimes versus earlier/controlled bedtime context

**Duration or follow-up:** Morning bright-light phase-advance protocol; exact duration not extracted

**Endpoints:** Circadian phase advance

**Effect estimates or direction:** Direction: late bedtimes blunted/prevented phase advances to morning bright light.

**Safety/adverse events:** No adverse events extracted.

**Limitations:**
- Adolescent population.
- Morning bright light rather than outdoor daylight.
- Effect size and sample size not extracted.

**Population mismatch:** Adolescents and bright-light lab context, not general adult outdoor users.

**Directness to Morning Outdoor Light Exposure:** `same_mechanism`.

**Why it matters:** It prevents overclaiming that morning light works regardless of bedtime.

**Potential experiment signals:** bedtime, morning bright light, phase advance, adolescent sleep.

**Protocol takeaway:** Protocol guidance should mention that late nights and irregular schedules can reduce the expected timing effect.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
