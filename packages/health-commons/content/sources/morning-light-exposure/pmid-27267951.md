This source is included for **clinical_light_therapy_device_boundaries**.

**Findings:** Population: Patients with current nonseasonal major depressive episodes in bipolar or unipolar disorder; accessible extract: 50 bipolar and 45 unipolar. Intervention/exposure: Morning bright light therapy as adjunctive treatment. Comparator/control: Placebo/sham light condition. Duration/follow-up: 14 days treatment in accessible extract Endpoints: depression improvement, safety, dropout. Effect/direction: Ninety-five patients enrolled; 52 were randomized to BLT and 43 to placebo in the ITT population; 83 completed. After 14 days, significant improvement occurred in all groups (p<0.001). Accessible extraction did not identify a clear between-group advantage. Safety/adverse events: The study aimed to examine safety, but specific adverse-event results were not extracted from accessible abstract-level text.

**Why it matters:** Preserves mixed/null clinical-device boundary evidence outside SAD.

**Potential experiment signals:** depression improvement, dropout, safety

**Protocol takeaway:** Do not use within-group improvement as proof of bright-light efficacy or outdoor-light benefit.

**Directness and boundaries:** Directness is `clinical_supervised` with protocol-evidence scope `clinical_supervised`. Population mismatch: Nonseasonal major depressive episodes in clinical care, not general outdoor-light users.

**Limitations and uncertainty:** Mixed bipolar/unipolar clinical population; adjunctive device therapy; short duration; no clear between-group effect extracted.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
