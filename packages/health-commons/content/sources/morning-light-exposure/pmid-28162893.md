This source is included for **timing_dose_circadian_metrics**.

**Findings:**
- The study extends natural light-dark entrainment observations across seasonal and weekend contexts.
- It supports the concept that reduced daytime light and increased nighttime electric light can be associated with later circadian and sleep timing.
- Seasonal light availability is a relevant protocol-context variable.

**Intervention or exposure:** Natural light-dark exposure across seasons and weekend conditions

**Comparator or control:** Modern electric-light schedule and/or seasonal contrasts

**Duration or follow-up:** Seasonal and weekend natural-light observation/exposure windows

**Endpoints:** Circadian timing and sleep timing

**Effect estimates or direction:** Direction: natural daylight exposure patterns were linked to circadian/sleep timing alignment; no extracted single-dose estimate.

**Safety/adverse events:** No adverse events extracted.

**Limitations:**
- Naturalistic exposure bundle.
- Participant count not extracted.
- Seasonal context limits generalizability.

**Population mismatch:** Healthy adult seasonal field context; not a clinical or occupational protocol trial.

**Directness to Morning Outdoor Light Exposure:** `same_mechanism`.

**Why it matters:** It captures real-world variability in daylight exposure that affects implementation.

**Potential experiment signals:** season, outdoor daylight, sleep timing, circadian phase, weekend schedule.

**Protocol takeaway:** Protocol language should account for season and latitude rather than implying all mornings provide equivalent light dose.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
