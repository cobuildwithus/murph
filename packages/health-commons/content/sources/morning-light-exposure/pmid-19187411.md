This source is included for **clinical_sleep_insomnia_dementia_light_therapy_boundaries**.

**Findings:** Fifty-one community adults with primary insomnia were randomized in a factorial design to bright or dim light and morning or evening administration. Bright light around 4000 lux for 45 minutes daily for 12 weeks did not produce subjective or objective sleep changes that were generally different from scheduled dim light around 65 lux, although scheduled light shifted circadian phase predictably.

**Why it matters:** This older adult insomnia RCT is important because it reports a null/no-clear-advantage result despite scheduled bright light and predictable circadian phase shifts.

**Potential experiment signals:** subjective sleep logs, sleep questionnaires, actigraphy, polysomnography, melatonin/circadian phase, CLOCK polymorphism

**Protocol takeaway:** Scheduled clinical bright light can shift phase without clearly improving insomnia symptoms versus dim light; do not use as direct support for outdoor-morning-exposure efficacy.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
