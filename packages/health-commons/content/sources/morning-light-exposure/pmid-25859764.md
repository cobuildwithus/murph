This source is included for **clinical_light_therapy_device_boundaries**.

**Findings:** Population: Adults with seasonal affective disorder during an acute episode. Intervention/exposure: Cognitive-behavioral therapy adapted for SAD (CBT-SAD). Comparator/control: Light therapy, randomized group n=89 in accessible extract; CBT-SAD n=88. Duration/follow-up: Acute treatment episode; commonly reported trial treatment course was 6 weeks, but exact schedule should be checked in full text before protocol synthesis. Endpoints: depressive symptom improvement, acute response/remission, treatment option comparison. Effect/direction: CBT-SAD and light therapy were comparably effective during an acute SAD episode according to the article conclusion. Safety/adverse events: No adverse-event detail was extracted from accessible abstract-level text.

**Why it matters:** Positions device light therapy as one formal SAD treatment option among nonpharmacologic therapies.

**Potential experiment signals:** acute depressive symptoms, response, remission

**Protocol takeaway:** This is clinical SAD treatment evidence and should not be used as outdoor morning-light efficacy evidence.

**Directness and boundaries:** Directness is `clinical_supervised` with protocol-evidence scope `clinical_supervised`. Population mismatch: Adults with SAD, not general outdoor-light users.

**Limitations and uncertainty:** SAD treatment population; active psychotherapy comparator; device-based light therapy rather than outdoor exposure; acute outcomes only in this source page.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
