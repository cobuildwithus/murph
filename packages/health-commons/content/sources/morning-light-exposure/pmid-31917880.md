This source is included for **safety_boundaries**.

**Source key:** `source_artifact:pmid-31917880`.

**Findings:** Six RCTs evaluated bright-light therapy for bipolar depression. The meta-analysis found no significant difference in manic switching versus placebo/control (RR 1.00, 95% CI 0.28 to 3.59) and reported no severe adverse events in the accessible abstract-level extraction.

**Why it matters:** Bipolar RCT meta-analysis found no significant increase in manic switching and no severe adverse events in the extracted evidence.

**Potential experiment signals:** manic switching, severe adverse events, depressive symptoms, remission.

**Protocol takeaway:** Light-based protocols should still include mania monitoring because confidence around rare switch events is limited by small trial counts.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

**Extraction notes:**

- **Participant count:** 6 (rcts_in_meta_analysis).
- **Population:** Patients with bipolar disorder in randomized trials of bright-light therapy.
- **Intervention or exposure:** Bright light therapy for bipolar depressive or manic symptoms.
- **Comparator or control:** Placebo or control conditions in included RCTs.
- **Duration or follow-up:** Literature through June 2019; trial durations varied.
- **Endpoints:** manic switching; severe adverse events; depressive symptoms; remission
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Six RCTs evaluated bright-light therapy for bipolar depression. The meta-analysis found no significant difference in manic switching versus placebo/control (RR 1.00, 95% CI 0.28 to 3.59) and reported no severe adverse events in the accessible abstract-level extraction.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** Aggregate RCT safety data are reassuring but imprecise; manic switch remains monitored.
- **Population mismatch:** Bipolar clinical population; relevant as a safety screen, not a direct outdoor protocol population.
- **Directness to Morning Outdoor Light Exposure:** Safety boundary. This source does not establish direct efficacy for the protocol variant.

**Directness and boundaries:** The source is safety-only here; efficacy findings are not promoted to the outdoor protocol.

**Limitations and uncertainty:**

- Few RCTs and imprecision around switch risk.
- No RCT evidence for recurrence prevention in euthymia or improving manic-state symptoms was extracted.
- Clinical bright-light therapy, not outdoor daylight.
