This source is included for **clinical_light_therapy_device_boundaries**.

**Findings:** Population: Depressed inpatients: 415 with unipolar depression and 187 with bipolar depression. Intervention/exposure: Hospital rooms with eastern windows receiving more morning sunlight. Comparator/control: Hospital rooms with western windows. Duration/follow-up: Length of index hospitalization Endpoints: length of hospitalization. Effect/direction: Bipolar depressed inpatients in eastern rooms had a mean hospital stay 3.67 days shorter than those in western rooms; no comparable effect was found among unipolar depressed inpatients. Safety/adverse events: No adverse-event data were extracted; this was an observational room-exposure study.

**Why it matters:** Useful daylight-room boundary evidence and a bipolar-population caution point.

**Potential experiment signals:** hospital length of stay, diagnostic subgroup

**Protocol takeaway:** Associational inpatient room evidence cannot establish causal benefit of outdoor morning light.

**Directness and boundaries:** Directness is `clinical_supervised` with protocol-evidence scope `clinical_supervised`. Population mismatch: Severe inpatient bipolar/unipolar depression, not healthy outdoor-light users.

**Limitations and uncertainty:** Retrospective naturalistic design; room assignment not randomized; no individual outdoor exposure or light dose measurement; clinical inpatient setting.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
