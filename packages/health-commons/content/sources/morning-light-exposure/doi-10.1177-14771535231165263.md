This source is included for **indoor_workplace_classroom_home_daylight**.

**Findings:** High light levels significantly improved acute alertness during exposure hours versus standard lighting, but no significant overall sleep or mood difference was found. Compared with evening high light, morning high light was associated with longer deep sleep, easier falling asleep, and lower negative mood.

**Why it matters:** It preserves both positive alertness and null sleep/mood findings while adding a timing caution about evening bright light.

**Potential experiment signals:** Acute alertness, sleep wristband outcomes, deep sleep duration, difficulty falling asleep, mood/negative mood, vertical illuminance, melanopic lux, and timing of high light.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as adjacent youth/classroom evidence and timing context only; do not convert to adult outdoor protocol efficacy.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
