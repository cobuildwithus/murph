This source is included for **indoor_workplace_classroom_home_daylight**.

**Findings:** After five school days with short-wavelength light removed by orange glasses, students’ DLMO was delayed by about 30 minutes. This supports the circadian importance of short-wavelength daytime light but does not test outdoor exposure.

**Why it matters:** It is a clear mechanistic classroom source that preserves the circadian phase pathway while flagging youth and intervention mismatch.

**Potential experiment signals:** DLMO timing, short-wavelength light filtering, school-day light exposure, and circadian phase.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as mechanistic context only: short-wavelength daytime light can shift circadian timing, but this is not direct outdoor exposure evidence.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
