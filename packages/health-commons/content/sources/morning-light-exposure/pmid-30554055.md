This source is included for **free_living_observational_measurement**.

**Findings:** In 1,108 elderly HEIJO-KYO participants, PAL was defined as the 2-hour average bedroom light intensity before rise time. Sleep disturbance prevalence increased with PAL exposure. In adjusted models, the highest PAL quartile versus lowest had OR 1.65 for sleep disturbances (95% CI 1.16 to 2.34). Actigraphic sleep efficiency was 2.6% lower, wake after sleep onset 7.5 minutes longer, and sleep-onset latency 0.2 log-minutes longer in Q4 versus Q1.

**Why it matters:** It is timing-specific and unusually close to the morning window, but the exposure occurred before waking and was associated with worse rather than better sleep outcomes.

**Potential experiment signals:** 2-hour pre-rise light average, PSQI sleep disturbance, sleep efficiency, WASO, sleep-onset latency, chronotype.

**Population mismatch / directness:** Older adults and pre-awake bedroom light rather than adult post-wake outdoor light; association is adverse and observational. Directness: `same_mechanism`.

**Limitations:** cross-sectional; older-adult cohort; pre-awake exposure not post-wake outdoor exposure; possible chronotype and bedroom-window confounding; no causality.

**Protocol takeaway:** Treat as cautionary context: pre-awake bedroom light is not the same as deliberate morning outdoor light after wake-up.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
