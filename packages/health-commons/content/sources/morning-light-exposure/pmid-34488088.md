This source is included for **direct_outdoor_daylight_protocol**.

**Findings:** In UK Biobank adults, median outdoor daylight time was reported around 2.5 hours/day. Each additional hour outdoors during the day was associated with sleep/circadian-related outcomes including greater ease getting up, less tiredness, fewer insomnia symptoms, and earlier chronotype; mood-related associations were also reported. The source is large and useful for endpoint context, but exposure was self-reported outdoor time and not a prescribed morning protocol.

**Why it matters:** Very large real-world context source on outdoor daylight time and sleep/circadian-related outcomes, while remaining self-reported and non-morning-specific.

**Potential experiment signals:** time outdoors in daylight, insomnia symptoms, tiredness, ease getting up, chronotype, mood symptoms

**Protocol takeaway:** Greater habitual outdoor daylight time is associated with better sleep/circadian and mood-related outcomes, but the study cannot establish that a morning protocol causes those outcomes.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
