This source is included for **safety_boundaries**.

**Source key:** `source_artifact:pmid-9444494`.

**Findings:** Newly emergent side effects were generally low; unfavourable effects compared with trimipramine alone included aggravated sedation, persisting restlessness, emerging sleep disturbance, decreased appetite, and worsening vertigo.

**Why it matters:** Adjunct bright-light therapy in medicated major-depression patients showed generally low but clinically relevant side-effect signals.

**Potential experiment signals:** newly emergent side effects, sedation, restlessness, sleep disturbance, appetite, vertigo.

**Protocol takeaway:** Protocol cautions should include medication context and ask users to stop or adjust if restlessness, sedation, vertigo, appetite change, or sleep disruption emerges.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

**Extraction notes:**

- **Participant count:** 28 (reported).
- **Population:** Patients with major depression receiving trimipramine with or without adjunct bright-light therapy.
- **Intervention or exposure:** Adjunct bright-light therapy plus trimipramine.
- **Comparator or control:** Trimipramine monotherapy.
- **Duration or follow-up:** Treatment duration not fully extracted in this batch.
- **Endpoints:** newly emergent side effects; sedation; restlessness; sleep disturbance; appetite; vertigo
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Newly emergent side effects were generally low; unfavourable effects compared with trimipramine alone included aggravated sedation, persisting restlessness, emerging sleep disturbance, decreased appetite, and worsening vertigo.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** Adjunct light therapy can interact with medication and symptom context to produce non-ocular adverse effects.
- **Population mismatch:** Major-depression pharmacotherapy patients, not general morning-routine users.
- **Directness to Morning Outdoor Light Exposure:** Safety boundary. This source does not establish direct efficacy for the protocol variant.

**Directness and boundaries:** Trimipramine co-treatment and major-depression clinical care limit directness.

**Limitations and uncertainty:**

- Small older clinical sample.
- Trimipramine co-treatment confounds attribution.
- Not an outdoor-light study.
