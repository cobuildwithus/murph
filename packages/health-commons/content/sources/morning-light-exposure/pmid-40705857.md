This source is included for **safety_boundaries**.

**Source key:** `source_artifact:pmid-40705857`.

**Findings:** ISBD recommends anti-manic prophylaxis, especially for bipolar I disorder, and clinical monitoring at initiation and during ongoing light treatment; daily morning or midday light is preferred, with 30 minutes/day as a target and 15-minute starts with weekly 15-minute increments for patients wary of hypomania or partial responders.

**Why it matters:** ISBD clinical recommendations emphasize anti-manic prophylaxis, conservative titration, and monitoring when using light therapy in bipolar disorder.

**Potential experiment signals:** mania/hypomania monitoring, depression symptoms, side effects, timing, dose escalation.

**Protocol takeaway:** Morning outdoor-light instructions should warn people with bipolar disorder, recent mania/hypomania, mixed symptoms, or rapid cycling to seek clinician guidance before deliberate light-dosing changes.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

- **Participant count:** not applicable.
- **Population:** People with bipolar disorder being considered for bright-light therapy.
- **Intervention or exposure:** Bright light therapy for bipolar disorder, preferably morning or midday, with dose titration and monitoring.

- **Adverse events or safety notes:** Recent mania/hypomania, mixed symptoms, rapid cycling, retinal disease, or photosensitizing medications require caution or specialist review in clinical light-therapy contexts.
