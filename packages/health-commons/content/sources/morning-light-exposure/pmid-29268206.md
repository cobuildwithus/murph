This source is included for **clinical_light_therapy_device_boundaries**.

**Findings:** Population: Outpatients with bipolar depression. Intervention/exposure: Morning bright light therapy at 10,000 lux for 30 minutes daily for two weeks, as add-on treatment. Comparator/control: Dim light below 500 lux. Duration/follow-up: 2 weeks Endpoints: Hamilton Rating Scale for Depression, MADRS, response, remission, side effects. Effect/direction: Response rates were 81% with BLT versus 19% with dim light; remission rates were 44% versus 12.5%. Depression scores were significantly reduced in BLT compared with dim light on assessed scales. Safety/adverse events: Side effects were similar in both groups; headache was the most common side effect in accessible extract.

**Why it matters:** Efficacy context plus bipolar safety boundary for bright light therapy.

**Potential experiment signals:** HAMD, MADRS, response, remission, side effects

**Protocol takeaway:** Bipolar populations require clinical supervision and findings should not be generalized to outdoor routines.

**Directness and boundaries:** Directness is `clinical_supervised` with protocol-evidence scope `clinical_supervised`. Population mismatch: Bipolar depression outpatients, not routine wellness users.

**Limitations and uncertainty:** Small sample; bipolar depression requires clinical supervision; device-based high-lux light; short duration.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
