This source is included for **clinical_light_therapy_device_boundaries**.

**Findings:** Population: Patients with seasonal affective disorder/winter depression. Intervention/exposure: Daily 1-hour morning walk outdoors in natural light. Comparator/control: Low-dose artificial light, 0.5 hour at 2800 lux, under double-blind conditions and treated as a placebo-like comparison. Duration/follow-up: 1 week treatment; withdrawal follow-up noted in secondary descriptions Endpoints: depression self-ratings, Hamilton depression score, salivary melatonin onset/offset, morning cortisol. Effect/direction: Natural morning light improved all depression self-ratings; Hamilton remission was reported as 50% after the walk versus 25% after low-dose artificial light. Morning walk phase-advanced salivary melatonin onset and/or offset and decreased morning cortisol; low-dose artificial light did not modify melatonin or cortisol patterns. Safety/adverse events: Adverse events were not extracted from accessible abstract-level text.

**Why it matters:** This is a direct bridge to outdoor morning light but remains clinical SAD evidence and an exercise-bundled variant.

**Potential experiment signals:** depression self-ratings, Hamilton remission, salivary melatonin timing, morning cortisol

**Protocol takeaway:** Do not treat this as unconfounded outdoor-light evidence because the intervention included walking and a SAD treatment population.

**Directness and boundaries:** Directness is `clinical_supervised` with protocol-evidence scope `clinical_supervised`. Population mismatch: Adults with winter SAD, not a general wellness population.

**Limitations and uncertainty:** SAD clinical treatment population; morning walk bundles light exposure with exercise and outdoor context; short treatment duration; sample size not extracted from accessible text.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
