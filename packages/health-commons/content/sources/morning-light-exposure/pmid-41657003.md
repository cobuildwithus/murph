This source is included for **free_living_observational_measurement**.

**Findings:** Accessible records identify a longitudinal study in adults with stable heart failure. Extracted related-summary details indicate time above 500 lux was associated with stronger circadian quotient in males and better interdaily stability; higher time above 100 lux and greater circadian quotient were associated with improved sleep efficiency; higher time above 100 lux was associated with fewer PVT lapses. Exact analytic denominators and final-model details require full-text verification.

**Why it matters:** It captures a recent clinical population where real-world light-sleep associations may be mixed or modified by disease burden.

**Potential experiment signals:** time above 100 lux, time above 500 lux, sleep efficiency, inter-daily stability, PVT lapses, alertness.

**Population mismatch / directness:** Stable heart-failure clinical cohort; not a healthy-adult morning outdoor-light intervention. Directness: `adjacent_variant`.

**Limitations:** clinical population; full-text access limited in batch; observational/secondary analysis; possible disease and medication confounding; exact follow-up interval uncertain.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use only as clinical population context; do not generalize to healthy adult morning outdoor-light effects.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
