This source is included for **free_living_observational_measurement**.

**Findings:** Among 133 healthy adults monitored for 7 days, all zeitgebers except evening physical activity correlated with at least five of ten sleep/rest-activity outcomes. Adjusted analyses linked later first and last indoor light and eating with later bedtimes and L5/M10 timing. Later first outdoor light was associated with longer sleep duration and lower MESOR, while higher morning physical activity predicted earlier in-bed/out-of-bed times, acrophase, and L5/M10 timing.

**Why it matters:** The paper anchors a practical measurement point: light is one of several daily zeitgebers, and timing may be as important as amount.

**Potential experiment signals:** first outdoor light time, last indoor light time, bedtime, sleep duration, inter-daily stability, L5/M10 timing.

**Population mismatch / directness:** Healthy adults are relevant, but the exposure was self-reported zeitgeber timing rather than prescribed outdoor morning light. Directness: `same_mechanism`.

**Limitations:** observational; self-reported light timing; multiple zeitgebers bundled; no randomized light assignment.

**Protocol takeaway:** Track first outdoor-light timing and other zeitgebers as context variables, but avoid claiming protocol efficacy from these associations.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
