This source is included for **free_living_observational_measurement**.

**Findings:** The review included 25 papers on personal daily light exposure in healthy adults. It concluded that the included studies were generally low quality and that available evidence was only a first exploration. Evidence was limited for a positive relationship between amount/timing of light exposure and rest-activity rhythm and some sleep architecture estimates, while evidence for circadian phase, sleep estimates, sleep quality, and mood was conflicting. The review called for intervention studies to establish causal insight.

**Why it matters:** It is the batch's key adult general-population evidence-quality anchor.

**Potential experiment signals:** personal light exposure, rest-activity rhythm, sleep architecture, sleep quality, circadian phase, mood.

**Population mismatch / directness:** Review covers healthy adults but not specifically outdoor morning-light protocols. Directness: `background`.

**Limitations:** low-quality included studies; heterogeneous exposure metrics; mixed/conflicting outcomes; not protocol-specific; review cannot establish causality.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as background to state that real-world personal-light evidence is promising but not definitive; causal claims require intervention evidence.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
