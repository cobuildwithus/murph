This source is included for **free_living_observational_measurement**.

**Findings:** In 57 high-school students, afternoon-shift students had longer sleep duration, lower sleepiness, and lower overall light exposure than morning-shift students. Evening types had greater sleepiness. Morning-type students on the morning schedule had higher morning light exposure from 08:00-12:00 and lower evening/night light from 21:00-03:00 than evening types. Academic achievement, psychological distress, social rhythm, school satisfaction, and alcohol-use measures did not differ by schedule in the extracted summary.

**Why it matters:** The study gives timing-specific context around morning light, evening/night light, and adolescent sleep constraints.

**Potential experiment signals:** morning light 08:00-12:00, evening/night light 21:00-03:00, sleep duration, sleepiness, chronotype.

**Population mismatch / directness:** Adolescents under imposed school schedules; not an adult voluntary outdoor-light intervention. Directness: `adjacent_variant`.

**Limitations:** small adolescent sample; school-shift confounding; observational; mixed outcomes; not direct protocol evidence.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use only as adolescent boundary evidence; do not generalize school-shift effects to adult outdoor morning-light routines.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
