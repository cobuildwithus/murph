This source is included for **direct_outdoor_daylight_protocol**.

**Findings:** Adults with depression reported outdoor daylight exposure on workdays and free days. Accessible summaries report median exposure around 1 hour on workdays and 3 hours on free days. More daylight exposure was associated with lower depressive and insomnia symptoms after covariate adjustment, but also with higher hypo-manic-like symptoms; psychotic-like symptoms and daytime sleepiness were largely unrelated, and age-stratified analyses found no symptom dimensions associated with daylight in 18–24-year-olds. This is not a morning-specific protocol or causal study.

**Why it matters:** Large clinically relevant outdoor daylight exposure source for sleep and mood correlates, but not morning-specific and not interventional.

**Potential experiment signals:** outdoor daylight hours on workdays, outdoor daylight hours on free days, insomnia symptoms, depressive symptoms, somatic symptoms

**Protocol takeaway:** More self-reported outdoor daylight exposure was associated with lower insomnia and depressive symptoms in adults with depression, but this remains context-only.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
