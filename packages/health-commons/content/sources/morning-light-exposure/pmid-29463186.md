This source is included for **free_living_observational_measurement**.

**Findings:** Delayed-schedule participants had a smaller light-dark amplitude than controls, attributed to lower daytime and higher nighttime exposure. Total blue light was lower in the delayed group, likely reflecting less blue-rich outdoor exposure; lower daily blue light was associated with later DLMO. Higher white/blue light after DLMO and evening device use before bed were also associated with later DLMO in extracted notes.

**Why it matters:** It helps distinguish morning/daytime light insufficiency from evening/night light excess in delayed sleepers.

**Potential experiment signals:** blue-light daily total, daytime light, nighttime light, DLMO, device use before bed.

**Population mismatch / directness:** Young adults with delayed schedules; not a general healthy-adult outdoor-light intervention. Directness: `adjacent_variant`.

**Limitations:** very small sample; clinical/delayed-schedule complaint; case-control design; light sensors may not capture retinal dose; no causal inference.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as adjacent delayed-schedule context, particularly for tracking evening device use and daytime outdoor light separately.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
