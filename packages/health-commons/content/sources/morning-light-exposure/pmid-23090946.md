This source is included for **timing_dose_circadian_metrics**.

**Findings:**
- The study estimates a human phase response curve to a long short-wavelength light pulse.
- It supports the concept that spectral composition influences circadian effects.
- It is mechanism evidence, not a practical outdoor-light protocol.

**Intervention or exposure:** Short-wavelength light

**Comparator or control:** Circadian timing of exposure and phase-response modeling

**Duration or follow-up:** Single 6.5-hour light pulse

**Endpoints:** Circadian phase response curve

**Effect estimates or direction:** Direction: short-wavelength light produced timing-dependent phase responses; exact estimates not extracted.

**Safety/adverse events:** No adverse events extracted.

**Limitations:**
- Long controlled light pulse.
- Participant count/effect size not extracted.
- No outdoor natural-light intervention.

**Population mismatch:** Lab physiology setting rather than daily outdoor behavior.

**Directness to Morning Outdoor Light Exposure:** `same_mechanism`.

**Why it matters:** It helps connect spectrum, melanopic metrics, and phase-response language.

**Potential experiment signals:** short-wavelength light, spectrum, phase response curve, circadian phase.

**Protocol takeaway:** Outdoor light should be described not only by lux but also by biologically relevant spectral metrics when possible.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
