**Source key:** `source_artifact:pmid-30888626`.

**Findings:** Photosensitive drug eruptions are cutaneous adverse events from medication plus UV or visible radiation and are classified as phototoxic or photoallergic; diagnosis relies on drug history and eruptions affecting sun-exposed areas, with phototesting or photopatch testing in selected cases.

**Why it matters:** Management review translates drug-induced photosensitivity into practical diagnosis, prevention, and management concerns.

**Potential experiment signals:** photoallergic eruptions, phototoxic eruptions, diagnosis, prevention, management.

**Protocol takeaway:** Protocol pages should include a photosensitizing-medication caution and avoid giving individualized medication advice.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

- **Intervention or exposure:** Prevention and management of drug-induced photosensitivity.
- **Comparator or control:** Narrative management review.

- **Effect estimates or direction:** Photosensitive drug eruptions are cutaneous adverse events from medication plus UV or visible radiation and are classified as phototoxic or photoallergic; diagnosis relies on drug history and eruptions affecting sun-exposed areas, with phototesting or photopatch testing in selected cases.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** People taking photosensitizing medications may need sun avoidance, protective clothing, sunscreen, or medication review before outdoor light exposure.
