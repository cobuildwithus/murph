This source is included for **free_living_observational_measurement**.

**Findings:** In 99 adolescents with evening circadian preference, lower average light intensity across the preceding 24 hours was associated with later sleep onset (p<0.01) and later next-day sleep offset (p<0.05). Later last exposure above 10 lux was associated with later sleep onset, shorter objective total sleep time, later bedtime, and shorter subjective total sleep time (all p<0.001 in extracted notes). Exploratory analyses linked lower early-morning light from 04:00-09:00 with later sleep onset, later next-day sleep offset, and later waketime.

**Why it matters:** It provides timing and threshold variables that may be useful for future self-tracking: early morning light and last exposure above 10 lux.

**Potential experiment signals:** 24-hour average light intensity, last exposure >10 lux, early morning light 04:00-09:00, sleep onset, sleep offset, total sleep time.

**Population mismatch / directness:** Adolescents with evening preference; not adult or outdoor-specific, and observational only. Directness: `adjacent_variant`.

**Limitations:** adolescent evening-preference sample; observational; threshold timing not an intervention; not outdoor-specific; multiple exploratory tests.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as adjacent adolescent context for timing metrics; do not generalize to adult outdoor morning-light efficacy.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
