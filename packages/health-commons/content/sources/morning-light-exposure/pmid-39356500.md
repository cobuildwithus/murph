This source is included for **clinical_light_therapy_device_boundaries**.

**Findings:** Population: Patients with nonseasonal depressive disorders in randomized clinical trials published from 2000 through March 2024. Intervention/exposure: Bright light therapy as adjunctive treatment. Comparator/control: Dim red light or antidepressant monotherapy alone/control conditions. Duration/follow-up: Subgrouped as less than 4 weeks and greater than 4 weeks follow-up Endpoints: remission, response, depression rating scales. Effect/direction: Eleven unique trials with 858 patients were included. Remission was 40.7% with BLT versus 23.5% control (OR 2.42; 95% CI 1.50 to 3.91; p<.001; I²=21%), and response was 60.4% versus 38.6% (OR 2.34; 95% CI 1.46 to 3.75; p<.001; I²=41%). Subgroup analyses below and above 4 weeks also favored BLT. Safety/adverse events: The accessible extraction focused on efficacy outcomes; adverse-event synthesis was not extracted.

**Why it matters:** Current high-profile clinical boundary evidence for bright light outside SAD.

**Potential experiment signals:** remission, response, depression rating scales, follow-up subgroup

**Protocol takeaway:** This supports supervised bright-light therapy context, not default outdoor morning-light efficacy.

**Directness and boundaries:** Directness is `clinical_supervised` with protocol-evidence scope `clinical_supervised`. Population mismatch: Clinical depressive-disorder patients, not general outdoor-light users.

**Limitations and uncertainty:** Clinical nonseasonal depressive disorders; adjunctive device/light therapy; outdoor daylight not isolated; trial heterogeneity.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
