**Findings:**
- The source tests morning bright light as part of a broader phase-advance package.
- Because afternoon melatonin and gradually shifted sleep are included, causal attribution to light alone is limited.
- It is adjacent-variant context for reducing bright-light duration.

**Intervention or exposure:** Morning bright light combined with afternoon melatonin and gradually shifted sleep

**Comparator or control:** Different morning bright-light duration within a multi-component protocol

**Duration or follow-up:** Multi-component phase-advance schedule; exact follow-up not extracted

**Endpoints:** Circadian phase advance

**Effect estimates or direction:** Direction: phase advance targeted with combined intervention; light-only effect not isolated in extracted materials.

**Safety/adverse events:** No adverse events extracted from this batch; melatonin co-intervention means safety boundaries may differ from light-only protocols.

**Limitations:**
- Multi-component intervention.
- Not outdoor daylight.
- Participant count/effect estimates not extracted.

**Population mismatch:** Combined protocol participants rather than general users doing outdoor morning light only.

**Directness to Morning Outdoor Light Exposure:** `adjacent_variant`.

**Why it matters:** It highlights that dose reduction may depend on other circadian interventions.

**Potential experiment signals:** morning bright light duration, melatonin timing, shifted sleep, phase advance.

**Protocol takeaway:** Do not use this as evidence that a short outdoor session alone is sufficient for phase advance.
