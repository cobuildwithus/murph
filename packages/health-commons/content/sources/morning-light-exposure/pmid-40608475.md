This source is included for **safety_boundaries**.

**Source key:** `source_artifact:pmid-40608475`.

**Findings:** Both timing groups reached a 45-minute starting dose without reaching maximum tolerated or toxic dose. Two patients (6%) had hypomanic switch at 45 minutes and five had subsyndromic hypomania; hypomanic symptoms improved within three days after dose reduction.

**Why it matters:** The LuBi trial found hypomanic and subsyndromic hypomanic events at 45 minutes/day that improved quickly after dose reduction.

**Potential experiment signals:** dose-limiting toxicity, hypomanic switch, subsyndromic hypomania, depressive symptoms, acceptability.

**Protocol takeaway:** Outdoor morning-light guidance should avoid aggressive dose escalation in bipolar-risk users and recommend stopping/reducing exposure and seeking care if hypomanic symptoms occur.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

**Extraction notes:**

- **Participant count:** 38 (enrolled).
- **Population:** Adults with nonseasonal bipolar I or II depression on mood stabilizers.
- **Intervention or exposure:** Morning or midday bright-light therapy with dose escalation from 7.5 to 45 minutes/day.
- **Comparator or control:** Morning versus midday timing in a dose-escalation randomized double-blind trial.
- **Duration or follow-up:** One-week placebo phase followed by dose-escalation light treatment with weekly safety assessment.
- **Endpoints:** dose-limiting toxicity; hypomanic switch; subsyndromic hypomania; depressive symptoms; acceptability
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Both timing groups reached a 45-minute starting dose without reaching maximum tolerated or toxic dose. Two patients (6%) had hypomanic switch at 45 minutes and five had subsyndromic hypomania; hypomanic symptoms improved within three days after dose reduction.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** Hypomanic or subsyndromic symptoms can emerge at higher dose and should trigger dose reduction and clinical monitoring.
- **Population mismatch:** Clinically supervised bipolar depression patients, not general outdoor-light users.
- **Directness to Morning Outdoor Light Exposure:** Safety boundary. This source does not establish direct efficacy for the protocol variant.

**Directness and boundaries:** Controlled bright-light therapy under mood-stabilizer treatment is not the same as outdoor sunlight.

**Limitations and uncertainty:**

- Phase 1/2 dose-escalation trial, not a general wellness protocol.
- Small sample.
- Participants were on mood stabilizers and clinically monitored.
