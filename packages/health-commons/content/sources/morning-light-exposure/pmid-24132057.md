This source is included for **clinical_light_therapy_device_boundaries**.

**Findings:** Population: Adolescents and/or young adults with delayed sleep phase disorder/delayed sleep-wake phase disorder. Intervention/exposure: Bright light, melatonin, their combination, and gradually advanced rise times; longer-term combined bright light plus melatonin in one phase. Comparator/control: Dim light/placebo and no-treatment conditions across short- and longer-term phases. Duration/follow-up: 2-week short-term treatment and 3-month longer-term follow-up phase Endpoints: subjective daytime sleepiness, objective sleepiness, fatigue, cognitive function. Effect/direction: Short-term conditions improved subjective sleepiness, fatigue, and cognitive function similarly; objective sleepiness did not improve. Longer-term combined bright light plus melatonin maintained subjective improvements, while the no-treatment group returned toward baseline. Safety/adverse events: Fifteen participants reported side effects in accessible extract: headache, nausea, eye discomfort, and skin irritation; no serious or long-lasting side effects were reported.

**Why it matters:** Good boundary evidence for bundled phase-shifting protocols and safety notes.

**Potential experiment signals:** subjective sleepiness, objective sleepiness, fatigue, cognition, side effects

**Protocol takeaway:** Do not map combined light-plus-melatonin findings directly to outdoor morning light.

**Directness and boundaries:** Directness is `clinical_supervised` with protocol-evidence scope `clinical_supervised`. Population mismatch: DSPD/DSWPD patients, not healthy protocol users.

**Limitations and uncertainty:** Bright light combined with melatonin and advanced rise-time instructions; clinical delayed sleep phase population; sample count not extracted; objective sleepiness did not improve.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
