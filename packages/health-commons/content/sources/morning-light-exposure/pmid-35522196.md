This source is included for **clinical_light_therapy_device_boundaries**.

**Findings:** Population: Participants with seasonal or non-seasonal major depressive disorder in blue-light therapy trials. Intervention/exposure: Blue-light or blue-enhanced light therapy. Comparator/control: Inactive light conditions or active light conditions. Duration/follow-up: Varied by RCT Endpoints: depression symptoms, response, remission, side effects. Effect/direction: Nine RCTs (n=347) were included. The primary meta-analysis found no evidence that blue-light conditions were more efficacious than inactive controls (MD 2.43; 95% CI -1.28 to 6.14; p=0.20), and no difference versus active light conditions (MD -0.11; 95% CI -2.38 to 2.16; p=0.93). Response and remission rates were not significantly different. Safety/adverse events: Blue-light therapy was overall well tolerated, and no significant side-effect difference between blue-light and control/active light conditions was extracted.

**Why it matters:** Prevents overgeneralizing standard bright-light evidence to all light-device wavelengths.

**Potential experiment signals:** depression mean difference, response, remission, side effects

**Protocol takeaway:** Do not equate blue-light device findings with outdoor daylight or standard bright-light boxes.

**Directness and boundaries:** Directness is `clinical_supervised` with protocol-evidence scope `clinical_supervised`. Population mismatch: Depression treatment populations in device-light trials, not general outdoor-light users.

**Limitations and uncertainty:** Wavelength-specific device variant; small total sample; short and heterogeneous protocols; efficacy remains unproven; not outdoor sunlight.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
