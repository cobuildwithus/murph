This source is included for **indoor_workplace_classroom_home_daylight**.

**Findings:** In a small crossover residential trial, improved indoor daylight exposure was associated with stable DLMO while the blinds condition delayed DLMO, sleep onset occurred earlier, sleep regularity improved, and positive affect/vitality measures improved. Sleep duration, sleep latency, and sleep efficiency did not show the same clear advantage.

**Why it matters:** Shows that the circadian system can respond to real-world daylight delivered indoors through building design, which helps explain why outdoor morning light may matter while preserving the indoor/outdoor boundary.

**Potential experiment signals:** DLMO timing, sleep onset, sleep regularity, sleep efficiency, sleep duration, daily sleep disturbance or impairment prompts, anxiety, positive affect, vitality, and personal light exposure.

**Protocol takeaway:** Indoor daylight may improve circadian alignment and selected sleep outcomes, but this paper should not be used as direct evidence that going outdoors in the morning works.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
