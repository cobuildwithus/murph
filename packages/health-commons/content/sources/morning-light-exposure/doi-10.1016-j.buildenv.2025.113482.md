This source is included for **timing_dose_circadian_metrics**.

**Findings:**
- The review evaluates wearable technologies for monitoring nonvisual effects of light on health and well-being.
- It emphasizes personal light dose dimensions including timing, intensity, duration, and spectrum.
- It identifies reliability, accuracy, applicability, and standardization gaps for real-world monitoring.

**Intervention or exposure:** Wearable monitoring of personal light dose, timing, intensity, duration, and spectrum

**Comparator or control:** Different wearable devices, measurement methods, and study designs

**Duration or follow-up:** Varied across reviewed wearable-monitoring studies

**Endpoints:** Accuracy, reliability, applicability, and integration of environmental, physiological, and subjective data

**Effect estimates or direction:** No efficacy effect; measurement review highlights gaps and framework needs.

**Safety/adverse events:** No adverse events extracted; not a safety source.

**Limitations:**
- Systematic review of measurement methods, not efficacy.
- No direct outdoor morning protocol tested.
- Heterogeneity across devices and protocols.

**Population mismatch:** Wearable-monitoring studies rather than Murph protocol users.

**Directness to Morning Outdoor Light Exposure:** `background`.

**Why it matters:** It is a current measurement-method backbone for wearable light exposure endpoints.

**Potential experiment signals:** wearable light monitoring, personal light dose, timing, intensity, duration, spectrum.

**Protocol takeaway:** Protocol measurement language should distinguish user-facing adherence from research-grade wearable light dosimetry.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
