This source is included for **free_living_observational_measurement**.

**Findings:** In 1,933 MESA participants, every 5-lux increase in light exposure during sleep was associated with 7.8 minutes greater sleep-onset timing SD (β=0.13 h; 95% CI 0.09 to 0.17) and 32% greater odds of irregular sleep onset (OR 1.32; 95% CI 1.17 to 1.50). Within-individual models suggested previous-night LEDS predicted 2.2 minutes greater next sleep-onset deviation, and a 1-hour sleep deviation predicted 0.35 lux higher future LEDS.

**Why it matters:** It is a large modern cohort preserving the safety boundary around sleep-period light and sleep regularity.

**Potential experiment signals:** light exposure during sleep, sleep onset SD, irregular sleep onset, night-to-night sleep deviation.

**Population mismatch / directness:** Older multi-ethnic cohort and sleep-period light exposure; safety boundary, not morning protocol evidence. Directness: `safety_boundary`.

**Limitations:** observational; bidirectionality limits causal interpretation; wrist light may not equal retinal or bedroom light; sleep-period rather than morning exposure.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as safety/timing boundary: a morning-light experiment should also protect darkness during sleep and monitor irregular timing.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.
