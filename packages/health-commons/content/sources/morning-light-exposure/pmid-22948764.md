**Findings:** In 192 elderly individuals, ambulatory daylight exposure was measured in two 48-hour sessions and overnight urinary 6-sulfatoxymelatonin was measured on the second night of each session. Median duration of daylight exposure ≥1000 lux was 72 minutes (IQR 37–124). Log duration of daylight ≥1000 lux was significantly associated with log urinary 6-sulfatoxymelatonin (regression coefficient 0.101; 95% CI 0.003–0.199; P = 0.043). Increasing daylight duration from 37 to 124 minutes was associated with a 13.0% increase in urinary 6-sulfatoxymelatonin.

**Why it matters:** Mechanistically relevant home-setting daylight source linking bright daylight duration with nocturnal melatonin metabolite output in older adults.

**Potential experiment signals:** duration daylight ≥1000 lux, urinary 6-sulfatoxymelatonin, melatonin rhythm proxy, daytime physical activity

**Protocol takeaway:** More daylight ≥1000 lux was associated with higher nocturnal melatonin metabolite excretion, but sleep outcomes were not primary and causality cannot be inferred.
