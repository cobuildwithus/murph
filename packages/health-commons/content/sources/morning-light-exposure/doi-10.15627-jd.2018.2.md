This source is included for **direct_outdoor_daylight_protocol**.

**Findings:** Fourteen healthy older adults wore an actigraph/light monitor for five consecutive days in summer and winter. They were exposed to high illumination levels (>1000 lux) longer in summer than winter. Sleep-quality measures did not differ significantly between seasons. In summer, daytime exposure duration to high daylight illuminance showed a significant positive correlation with sleep efficiency the following night; there was also a tendency toward less frequent napping with longer light exposure.

**Why it matters:** Directly measures natural light and sleep in a free-living older-adult setting, while preserving a non-interventional boundary.

**Potential experiment signals:** sleep efficiency, actigraphy sleep, daytime high-illuminance exposure >1000 lux, seasonal light exposure, napping frequency

**Protocol takeaway:** More daytime high-illuminance daylight may relate to better next-night sleep efficiency in summer, but the study does not show intervention efficacy.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
