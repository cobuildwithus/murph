This source is included for **clinical_light_therapy_device_boundaries**.

**Findings:** Population: Participants with seasonal affective disorder in randomized treatment trials. Intervention/exposure: Phototherapy/bright light therapy and other SAD treatment measures compared in a network meta-analysis. Comparator/control: Other intervention groups or control therapies in included trials. Duration/follow-up: Varied by included RCT Endpoints: depression score. Effect/direction: Twenty-one randomized trials involving 1037 participants were included. The accessible publisher abstract reports phototherapy was significantly more effective than other intervention groups or control therapies, with effect size 4.64 (95% CI 2.38 to 7.03), and concludes bright light therapy is a promising first-line non-pharmacologic SAD treatment. Safety/adverse events: The accessible extraction described bright light therapy as well tolerated but did not provide adverse-event counts.

**Why it matters:** Current comparative treatment context for SAD device-light therapy.

**Potential experiment signals:** network meta-analysis depression score, treatment ranking, tolerability

**Protocol takeaway:** Do not use treatment ranking for SAD to claim outdoor morning-light efficacy.

**Directness and boundaries:** Directness is `clinical_supervised` with protocol-evidence scope `clinical_supervised`. Population mismatch: SAD clinical trial participants, not general wellness users.

**Limitations and uncertainty:** Network meta-analysis combines different SAD interventions; effect-size scale requires full-text verification before protocol synthesis; clinical SAD treatment; device/light-therapy context rather than outdoor exposure.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
