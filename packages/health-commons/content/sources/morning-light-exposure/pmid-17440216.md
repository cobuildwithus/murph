This source is included for **direct_outdoor_daylight_protocol**.

**Findings:** Morning-type and evening-type individuals were monitored for 24-hour light exposure across 7 consecutive days, followed by laboratory estimates of circadian phase using dim-light melatonin onset and core body temperature minimum. Morning types had earlier sleep schedules, earlier circadian phases, more minutes of daily bright light exposure (>1000 lux), and more morning light exposure than evening types; evening types had more late-evening light. When profiles were analyzed relative to circadian phase, average differences were reduced, suggesting entrainment relationships rather than a protocol effect.

**Why it matters:** Mechanistic timing source showing that morning types receive more morning bright light and evening types receive more late-evening light.

**Potential experiment signals:** chronotype, morning bright-light minutes >1000 lux, late-evening light exposure, DLMO, circadian phase

**Protocol takeaway:** Morning-versus-evening light timing differs by chronotype; the study supports timing rationale but not protocol efficacy.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
