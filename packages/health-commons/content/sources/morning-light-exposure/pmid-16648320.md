This source is included for **clinical_light_therapy_device_boundaries**.

**Findings:** Population: Adults with winter seasonal affective disorder across Canadian centers. Intervention/exposure: Morning fluorescent white-light box treatment for 30 minutes/day plus placebo capsule. Comparator/control: Fluoxetine 20 mg/day plus placebo light box using neutral-density filters. Duration/follow-up: Acute winter SAD treatment period; exact duration not extracted from PubMed snippet Endpoints: depression response, remission, time to response, adverse events. Effect/direction: Intent-to-treat analysis showed overall improvement over time with no significant differences between light and fluoxetine. Response was 67% in both groups; remission was 50% for light and 54% for fluoxetine. Light showed earlier response onset and fewer rates of some adverse events relative to fluoxetine. Safety/adverse events: Light therapy had a lower rate of some adverse events than fluoxetine in accessible summary; specific adverse-event counts were not extracted.

**Why it matters:** Clinical device-treatment comparator, not outdoor protocol support.

**Potential experiment signals:** response, remission, onset of response, adverse events

**Protocol takeaway:** Do not translate light-box treatment equivalence with fluoxetine into outdoor-light claims.

**Directness and boundaries:** Directness is `clinical_supervised` with protocol-evidence scope `clinical_supervised`. Population mismatch: Winter SAD treatment population, not general wellness users.

**Limitations and uncertainty:** SAD clinical population; light-box device treatment; not outdoor exposure; no double-placebo condition noted in accessible summary.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
