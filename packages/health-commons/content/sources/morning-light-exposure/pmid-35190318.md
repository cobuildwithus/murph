This source is included for **free_living_observational_measurement**.

**Findings:** In 580 older adults, short-wavelength LAN power was measured at cornea level over two nights. Sleep disturbance prevalence was 34.7%. PSQI score and disturbance prevalence increased across tertiles; adjusted OR for sleep disturbances was 1.90 in the highest versus lowest tertile (95% CI 1.20 to 3.00; p=0.006). Urinary 6-sulfatoxymelatonin excretion was lower in the highest tertile (adjusted mean difference -0.14 log µg; 95% CI -0.28 to -0.007; p=0.039).

**Why it matters:** It ties real-world spectral nighttime light to both sleep and a physiological melatonin endpoint.

**Potential experiment signals:** short-wavelength LAN power, PSQI, sleep disturbance prevalence, urinary 6-sulfatoxymelatonin excretion.

**Population mismatch / directness:** Older adults and nighttime short-wavelength exposure; safety boundary rather than morning protocol evidence. Directness: `safety_boundary`.

**Limitations:** cross-sectional; older-adult cohort; nighttime exposure; not outdoor/morning; residual confounding.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as safety-only evidence for keeping nights dim and low in short-wavelength light.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.
