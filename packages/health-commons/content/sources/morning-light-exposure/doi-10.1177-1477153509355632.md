This source is included for **indoor_workplace_classroom_home_daylight**.

**Findings:** Higher daytime ocular light exposure appeared to have a positive association with sleep quality on the following night. Pleasure and arousal mood ratings were not significantly associated with the measured light exposure.

**Why it matters:** It helps identify sleep quality as a plausible signal for everyday light exposure while preserving the distinction between observational indoor light and outdoor morning exposure.

**Potential experiment signals:** Daily luminous exposure, blue spectral irradiance, office illuminance, sleep quality, pleasure, and arousal.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as same-mechanism office-light context; do not imply causality or equivalence with outdoor exposure.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
