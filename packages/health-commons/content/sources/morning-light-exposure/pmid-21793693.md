This source is included for **direct_outdoor_daylight_protocol**.

**Findings:** Eighty-eight undergraduate students aged 18–23 years in Murcia, Spain recorded wrist temperature, light exposure, and sleep for one week. Higher interdaily stability, relative amplitude, mean morning light, and light quality index correlated with higher circadian stability/relative amplitude and phase advance in sleep and wrist-temperature rhythms. Higher fragmentation of the light-exposure rhythm was associated with more fragmented sleep. This is same-mechanism timing evidence, not an outdoor protocol trial.

**Why it matters:** Mechanistic naturalistic evidence that morning light and regular high-contrast light patterns relate to circadian stability and sleep timing.

**Potential experiment signals:** mean morning light, light quality index, interdaily stability, relative amplitude, sleep fragmentation, wrist-temperature phase

**Protocol takeaway:** Regular morning/daytime light patterns may support circadian stability, but this young-student observational study is not direct protocol evidence.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
