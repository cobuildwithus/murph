This source is included for **indoor_workplace_classroom_home_daylight**.

**Findings:** The intervention produced significantly higher measured circadian stimulus at work and was associated with reduced sleepiness and higher vitality, energy, and alertness. Sleep outcomes were not the main efficacy claim in the accessible abstract.

**Why it matters:** It connects light dose metrics such as circadian stimulus to real-world alertness endpoints relevant to morning-light experiments.

**Potential experiment signals:** Circadian stimulus, sleepiness, vitality, energy, alertness, stress/sleep questionnaire context, and personal light exposure.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as same-mechanism alertness context; do not treat as proof that outdoor morning light improves sleep.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
