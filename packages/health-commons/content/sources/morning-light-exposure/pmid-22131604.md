This source is included for **clinical_light_therapy_device_boundaries**.

**Findings:** Population: Adolescents with delayed sleep phase disorder; mean age about 14.6 years in accessible extract. Intervention/exposure: Six-session cognitive-behavior therapy plus morning bright light therapy. Comparator/control: Wait-list control. Duration/follow-up: Six sessions with follow-up reported to 6 months for the intervention group Endpoints: sleep onset latency, sleep timing, daytime impairment, long-term follow-up sleep/function. Effect/direction: CBT plus bright light improved multiple sleep and daytime-impairment outcomes immediately and at longer follow-up; the component-specific effect of bright light was not isolated. Safety/adverse events: No adverse-event signal was extracted from accessible abstract-level text.

**Why it matters:** Adjacent clinical evidence for morning-timed light plus behavioral scheduling.

**Potential experiment signals:** sleep onset latency, wake time, daytime function, follow-up maintenance

**Protocol takeaway:** Do not attribute all effects to light or outdoor exposure.

**Directness and boundaries:** Directness is `clinical_supervised` with protocol-evidence scope `clinical_supervised`. Population mismatch: Adolescents with DSPD, not general adult outdoor-light users.

**Limitations and uncertainty:** Bundled behavioral therapy plus light; adolescent clinical population; wait-list design; component contribution not separable.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
