This source is included for **indoor_workplace_classroom_home_daylight**.

**Findings:** Dynamic scenarios had lower visual comfort than constant lighting and did not significantly affect task performance or actual sleep. Sleepiness was lowest when brighter light occurred around noon, and personal illuminance variation/timing related to sleep onset and duration.

**Why it matters:** It is an important null/mixed indoor-light source that prevents overgeneralizing from positive workplace lighting trials.

**Potential experiment signals:** Visual comfort, subjective sleepiness, cognitive task performance, sleep onset, sleep duration, personal illuminance level, timing, and variability.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context-only and mixed evidence: indoor dynamic-light design does not automatically improve sleep or performance.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
