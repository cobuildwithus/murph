This source is included for **safety_boundaries**.

**Source key:** `source_artifact:pmid-29348073`.

**Findings:** Across 41 studies describing 799 bipolar-disorder patients treated with light therapy, 7 (0.9%) switched into mania and 11 (1.4%) switched into hypomania; switch detection varied by assessment method, and the rapid-cycling subgroup had a higher reported switch rate.

**Why it matters:** The historical review estimated low aggregate mania/hypomania switch rates after light therapy but identified rapid cycling and assessment method as important caveats.

**Potential experiment signals:** switch into mania, switch into hypomania, rapid-cycling subgroup, assessment-method sensitivity.

**Protocol takeaway:** Morning outdoor-light guidance should include a bipolar/mania screening boundary and monitoring for elevated mood, reduced need for sleep, agitation, or mixed symptoms.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

**Extraction notes:**

- **Participant count:** 799 (patients_in_reviewed_studies).
- **Population:** People with bipolar disorder treated with antidepressant light therapy across 41 studies.
- **Intervention or exposure:** Light therapy for bipolar depression, including morning light therapy.
- **Comparator or control:** Historical rates and study-level synthesis; not a single controlled comparator.
- **Duration or follow-up:** Historical literature review; treatment durations varied.
- **Endpoints:** switch into mania; switch into hypomania; rapid-cycling subgroup; assessment-method sensitivity
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Across 41 studies describing 799 bipolar-disorder patients treated with light therapy, 7 (0.9%) switched into mania and 11 (1.4%) switched into hypomania; switch detection varied by assessment method, and the rapid-cycling subgroup had a higher reported switch rate.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** Manic or hypomanic switch is a key safety boundary even if aggregate rates were low; rapid-cycling bipolar disorder is a particular caution signal.
- **Population mismatch:** Bipolar-depression treatment population; cannot be generalized to all users, but it is directly relevant to bipolar safety screening.
- **Directness to Morning Outdoor Light Exposure:** Safety boundary. This source does not establish direct efficacy for the protocol variant.

**Directness and boundaries:** The review addresses clinical light therapy for bipolar depression, not outdoor-morning-light wellness use.

**Limitations and uncertainty:**

- Historical review of heterogeneous studies.
- Detection depended on how treatment-emergent symptoms were assessed.
- Clinical light-therapy setting, not a general outdoor-light routine.
