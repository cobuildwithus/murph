This source is included for **safety_boundaries**.

**Source key:** `source_artifact:pmid-28969438`.

**Findings:** No mood polarity switches were observed in the extracted abstract-level data; participants were monitored weekly with depression, mania, and sleep measures.

**Why it matters:** A bipolar depression RCT used midday bright light with antimanic medication and observed no mood polarity switches in the extracted report.

**Potential experiment signals:** mood polarity switch, mania rating, depression symptoms, sleep quality.

**Protocol takeaway:** The trial supports safety-screening logic: bipolar users should not infer that unsupervised morning outdoor light is risk-free.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

**Extraction notes:**

- **Participant count:** 46 (randomized).
- **Population:** Depressed adults with bipolar I or II disorder on stable antimanic medication, excluding hypomania/mania, mixed symptoms, or rapid cycling.
- **Intervention or exposure:** Adjunctive 7,000-lux bright white light at midday.
- **Comparator or control:** 50-lux dim red placebo light.
- **Duration or follow-up:** 6 weeks.
- **Endpoints:** mood polarity switch; mania rating; depression symptoms; sleep quality
- **Effect estimates or direction:** No mood polarity switches were observed in the extracted abstract-level data; participants were monitored weekly with depression, mania, and sleep measures.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** The study design used midday light and antimanic medication stability to manage switch risk.
- **Population mismatch:** Bipolar-depression clinical trial population under antimanic medication.
- **Directness to Morning Outdoor Light Exposure:** Safety boundary. This source does not establish direct efficacy for the protocol variant.

**Directness and boundaries:** Midday supervised light therapy is an adjacent variant, not direct morning outdoor exposure.

**Limitations and uncertainty:**

- Clinical supervised midday light, not outdoor morning light.
- Excluded recent hypomania/mania, mixed symptoms, and rapid cycling.
- Safety findings may not apply to unscreened users.
