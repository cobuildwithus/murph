This source is included for **free_living_observational_measurement**.

**Findings:** In 35 adolescents, less daytime light was associated with later sleep onset and shorter sleep duration. A 100-lux increase in average daytime light was associated with sleep onset 8.08 minutes earlier and sleep offset 7.16 minutes earlier; associations persisted after chronotype adjustment in the extracted summary.

**Why it matters:** It provides a recent real-world wearable-light estimate linking daytime light with sleep timing behavior.

**Potential experiment signals:** average daytime lux, minutes above 500 lux, sleep onset, sleep offset, sleep duration, Sleep Regularity Index.

**Population mismatch / directness:** Small adolescent high-school sample; not adult, outdoor-specific, or experimental. Directness: `adjacent_variant`.

**Limitations:** small sample; adolescent population; observational; school-day/free-day context; not morning-specific.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as adjacent context that daytime light may track with sleep timing; avoid causal protocol claims.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
