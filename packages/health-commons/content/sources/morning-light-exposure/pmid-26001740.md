This source is included for **clinical_light_therapy_device_boundaries**.

**Findings:** Population: Depressive inpatients treated in two hospital environments with different daylight availability. Intervention/exposure: Higher daylight-availability hospital environment with approximately three-fold greater accumulated light availability. Comparator/control: Lower daylight-availability hospital environment. Duration/follow-up: Index hospitalization Endpoints: length of hospitalization. Effect/direction: Median hospitalization fell from 14 days (IQR 8-19; n=101) in the lower-daylight hospital to 11 days (IQR 6-15; n=106) in the higher-daylight hospital; significance was reported for the whole group, while subgroup analyses were not significant. Safety/adverse events: No adverse events were extracted.

**Why it matters:** Clinical daylight-availability context supports separating architectural/daylight evidence from outdoor-habit evidence.

**Potential experiment signals:** hospital length of stay, facility daylight availability

**Protocol takeaway:** Hospital-level observational exposure cannot support causal outdoor morning-light claims.

**Directness and boundaries:** Directness is `clinical_supervised` with protocol-evidence scope `clinical_supervised`. Population mismatch: Clinical inpatient depression rather than healthy users.

**Limitations and uncertainty:** Retrospective hospital-level comparison; no individual light-dose measurement; subgroups not statistically significant; not outdoor exposure.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
