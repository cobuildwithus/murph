This source is included for **timing_dose_circadian_metrics**.

**Findings:**
- The paper examines interactions between lighting environments and individual differences in circadian light sensitivity.
- It frames bright-light need as dependent on both the person and the lighting environment.
- It should be used for personalization and boundary language, not default-efficacy claims.

**Intervention or exposure:** Lighting environments differing in brightness and timing

**Comparator or control:** Different modeled light sensitivities and lighting patterns

**Duration or follow-up:** Modeled lighting environments and timing scenarios

**Endpoints:** Predicted circadian timing stability and timing shifts

**Effect estimates or direction:** Direction: modeled circadian timing depends jointly on light sensitivity and environment; no clinical effect estimate.

**Safety/adverse events:** No adverse events extracted.

**Limitations:**
- Model-based inference.
- No extracted participant count or clinical outcome.
- Requires empirical validation for protocol dosing.

**Population mismatch:** Modeled sensitivity profiles, not an outdoor morning cohort.

**Directness to Morning Outdoor Light Exposure:** `same_mechanism`.

**Why it matters:** It directly addresses variability in dose needs, which is central to implementation language.

**Potential experiment signals:** individual light sensitivity, lighting environment, circadian timing, medication/age/mood modifiers.

**Protocol takeaway:** Protocol dose instructions should include a range and should encourage tracking response rather than assuming one dose fits all.
