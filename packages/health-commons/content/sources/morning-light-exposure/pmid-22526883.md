This source is included for **timing_dose_circadian_metrics**.

**Findings:**
- The study examines human responses to bright light across different durations.
- It provides duration-response context for light dosing.
- It should be cited as mechanism/dose evidence, not protocol-efficacy evidence.

**Intervention or exposure:** Bright light exposures of different durations

**Comparator or control:** Shorter versus longer bright-light exposure durations

**Duration or follow-up:** Multiple bright-light exposure durations

**Endpoints:** Human circadian and nonvisual responses to bright light

**Effect estimates or direction:** Direction: human responses were assessed across duration gradients; exact effect estimates not extracted.

**Safety/adverse events:** No adverse events extracted.

**Limitations:**
- Controlled bright-light lab context.
- Exact participant count/effect estimates not extracted.
- Not a natural outdoor exposure study.

**Population mismatch:** Lab participants and bright-light devices differ from everyday outdoor daylight.

**Directness to Morning Outdoor Light Exposure:** `same_mechanism`.

**Why it matters:** It helps support practical duration instructions while maintaining uncertainty about outdoor translation.

**Potential experiment signals:** duration, bright-light exposure, circadian response.

**Protocol takeaway:** Do not assume a few seconds of outdoor light is equivalent to a longer exposure period.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
