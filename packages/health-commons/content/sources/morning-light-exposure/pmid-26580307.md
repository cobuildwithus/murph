This source is included for **clinical_light_therapy_device_boundaries**.

**Findings:** Population: Adults aged 19-60 years with nonseasonal major depressive disorder of at least moderate severity. Intervention/exposure: Bright light treatment alone or combined with fluoxetine. Comparator/control: Fluoxetine alone and placebo/sham-placebo condition. Duration/follow-up: 8 weeks Endpoints: MADRS response, remission, treatment-emergent adverse events. Effect/direction: At endpoint, response/remission were 33.3%/30.0% for placebo, 29.0%/19.4% for fluoxetine, 50.0%/43.8% for light, and 75.9%/58.6% for combination. Combination therapy was superior to placebo for MADRS response (p=.005; NNT 2.4) and remission (p=.02; NNT 3.5). Safety/adverse events: All treatments were generally well tolerated with few significant differences in treatment-emergent adverse events in accessible extraction.

**Why it matters:** Landmark clinical-device evidence outside SAD boundaries.

**Potential experiment signals:** MADRS response, remission, NNT, treatment-emergent adverse events

**Protocol takeaway:** Clinical bright-light treatment and combination pharmacotherapy are not equivalent to an outdoor morning-light habit.

**Directness and boundaries:** Directness is `clinical_supervised` with protocol-evidence scope `clinical_supervised`. Population mismatch: Moderate/severe nonseasonal MDD under trial treatment, not general outdoor-light users.

**Limitations and uncertainty:** Nonseasonal MDD clinical sample; device-based bright light; single SSRI comparator; trial duration relatively short; not outdoor daylight.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
