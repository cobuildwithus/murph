This source is included for **timing_dose_circadian_metrics**.

**Findings:**
- Light can affect the circadian system and acute alertness/mood through non-image-forming visual pathways.
- Timing, intensity, duration, and spectrum are treated as relevant dimensions of light exposure.
- The source is useful background and not a direct test of morning outdoor light.

**Intervention or exposure:** Light exposure varying by timing, intensity, spectrum, and duration

**Comparator or control:** Varied by reviewed study or mechanism

**Duration or follow-up:** Varied across reviewed studies

**Endpoints:** Circadian phase, sleep, mood, alertness, and related physiology

**Effect estimates or direction:** Directionally supports light as a circadian and alerting stimulus; no protocol effect estimate extracted.

**Safety/adverse events:** No source-specific adverse events extracted; treat as mechanism/context only.

**Limitations:**
- Narrative rather than systematic review.
- Contains broader artificial and clinical-light evidence.
- No extracted adverse-event data for this batch.

**Population mismatch:** Mixed human contexts rather than a defined healthy adult outdoor-morning cohort.

**Directness to Morning Outdoor Light Exposure:** `background`.

**Why it matters:** It offers readable mechanistic framing for protocol rationale while preserving an evidence boundary.

**Potential experiment signals:** circadian timing, sleep timing, mood, alertness, light intensity and spectrum.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use to explain the biological plausibility of morning light, not to claim that the protocol improves sleep or mood.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
