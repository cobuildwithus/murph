This source is included for **timing_dose_circadian_metrics**.

**Findings:**
- The study directly compares duration and intensity as determinants of light-induced circadian change.
- Its title-level conclusion indicates duration can be more effective than intensity increases.
- This informs minute-based dosing language while remaining same-mechanism evidence.

**Intervention or exposure:** Light exposure varying by duration and intensity

**Comparator or control:** Alternative duration/intensity conditions

**Duration or follow-up:** Different light-exposure durations and intensities

**Endpoints:** Light-induced circadian clock changes

**Effect estimates or direction:** Direction: increasing duration was reported as more effective than increasing light intensity for circadian change.

**Safety/adverse events:** No adverse events extracted.

**Limitations:**
- Controlled lab exposure.
- Exact sample/effect estimates not extracted.
- Not outdoor daylight.

**Population mismatch:** Lab participants rather than users in real weather and daylight.

**Directness to Morning Outdoor Light Exposure:** `same_mechanism`.

**Why it matters:** It is directly relevant to duration-versus-intensity tradeoffs in morning-light prescriptions.

**Potential experiment signals:** duration, intensity, circadian phase shift.

**Protocol takeaway:** Protocol language can emphasize staying outside long enough to accumulate dose, with a caveat that evidence is laboratory based.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
