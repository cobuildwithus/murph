This source is included for **indoor_workplace_classroom_home_daylight**.

**Findings:** Higher daytime light exposure was significantly related to greater vitality after accounting for several person, time, activity, and sleep covariates. Associations with tension and positive or negative affect were not significant in the accessible report.

**Why it matters:** It identifies vitality and alertness as potential user-facing experiment signals and highlights that morning light effects may be stronger when baseline vitality is low.

**Potential experiment signals:** Momentary vitality, tension, positive affect, negative affect, photopic light exposure in prior windows, circadian light exposure, sleep diary covariates.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context for vitality/alertness outcomes, not as proof that outdoor morning light causes vitality improvements.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
