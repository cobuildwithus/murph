This source is included for **indoor_workplace_classroom_home_daylight**.

**Findings:** Higher morning light was associated with reduced sleep latency, especially in winter, and higher all-day light was associated with improved sleep quality and reduced depression. These are associations from office-worker field monitoring rather than randomized outdoor exposure.

**Why it matters:** This source helps explain why light dose, timing, and spectral composition during the day may change sleep and mood endpoints even in indoor work settings.

**Potential experiment signals:** Sleep quality, sleep latency, depression or mood ratings, circadian-effective light dose, and circadian entrainment/phasor metrics.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context that daytime light exposure may matter; do not cite as direct evidence for outdoor morning exposure.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
