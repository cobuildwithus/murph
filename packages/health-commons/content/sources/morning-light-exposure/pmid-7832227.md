This source is included for **safety_boundaries**.

**Source key:** `source_artifact:pmid-7832227`.

**Findings:** No ocular changes were detected after short-term treatment; long-term treatment in 17 patients with cumulative exposure durations of 60 to 1,250 hours also resulted in no ocular abnormalities.

**Why it matters:** A monitored ophthalmologic study found no ocular abnormalities after short- or long-term bright-light therapy but recommended eye monitoring in ocular-abnormality or photosensitizing-drug contexts.

**Potential experiment signals:** ocular changes, visual acuity, intraocular pressure, slit-lamp exam, ophthalmoscopy, visual fields, fundus photography, macular stress testing.

**Protocol takeaway:** Outdoor protocols should advise eye-care review for ocular disease or photosensitizing medication and should avoid direct sun-gazing.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

**Extraction notes:**

- **Participant count:** 50 (reported).
- **Population:** Patients with seasonal affective disorder receiving bright-light therapy; 17 had long-term follow-up.
- **Intervention or exposure:** Daily artificial light exposure for 30 minutes at 10,000 lux, morning or evening.
- **Comparator or control:** Ophthalmologic examinations before and after treatment; long-term follow-up in a subset.
- **Duration or follow-up:** Short-term treatment for 2 to 8 weeks; long-term treatment for 3 to 6 years during fall/winter months in 17 patients.
- **Endpoints:** ocular changes; visual acuity; intraocular pressure; slit-lamp exam; ophthalmoscopy; visual fields; fundus photography; macular stress testing
- **Effect estimates or direction:** No ocular changes were detected after short-term treatment; long-term treatment in 17 patients with cumulative exposure durations of 60 to 1,250 hours also resulted in no ocular abnormalities.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** Authors recommended periodic ophthalmologic examination for patients with preexisting ocular abnormalities or those using photosensitizing drugs.
- **Population mismatch:** SAD patients under eye examination, not general outdoor routine users.
- **Directness to Morning Outdoor Light Exposure:** Safety boundary. This source does not establish direct efficacy for the protocol variant.

**Directness and boundaries:** UV-filtered artificial therapy is not equivalent to outdoor sunlight.

**Limitations and uncertainty:**

- Artificial light therapy with ophthalmologic monitoring, not outdoor sunlight.
- Long-term follow-up subset was 17 patients.
- Current UV/outdoor exposure concerns require additional sources.
