This source is included for **free_living_observational_measurement**.

**Findings:** In 857 older adults, bedroom light intensity was measured and sleep quality was assessed subjectively and objectively. Compared with the lowest quartile of LAN intensity, the highest quartile had significantly higher odds of subjective insomnia in an adjusted model; accessible extracted text reported adjusted OR 1.61, with the confidence interval truncated in the accessible snippet. The authors concluded that LAN in home settings was associated with both subjective and objective sleep quality.

**Why it matters:** It is a key real-world safety-boundary source for distinguishing helpful daytime light from potentially harmful night light.

**Potential experiment signals:** bedroom light at night, subjective insomnia, PSQI, actigraphic sleep quality, urinary 6-sulfatoxymelatonin.

**Population mismatch / directness:** Older Japanese adults and night exposure; safety boundary only. Directness: `safety_boundary`.

**Limitations:** cross-sectional; older-adult cohort; nighttime exposure not morning light; some effect-estimate text truncated in accessible record; residual confounding.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as safety-only support for keeping sleep-period light low while pursuing morning/daytime light exposure.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.
