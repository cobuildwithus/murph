This source is included for **clinical_light_therapy_device_boundaries**.

**Findings:** Population: Adolescents and young adults aged 13-24 years with delayed sleep-wake phase disorder. Intervention/exposure: Three weeks of post-awakening green bright light with sedentary activity or with scheduled morning activity. Comparator/control: Red light with sedentary activity or red light with morning activity. Duration/follow-up: 3-week intervention with 1- and 3-month follow-up Endpoints: sleep onset, wake time, sleep onset latency, total sleep time, daytime functioning. Effect/direction: No significant differences were found between treatment groups; interaction effects were non-significant. Sleep timing, sleep onset latency, and daytime function improved over time, but long-term relapse was common. Safety/adverse events: No adverse events were extracted from accessible abstract-level text.

**Why it matters:** Important null/mixed adjacent evidence for light-plus-activity protocols.

**Potential experiment signals:** sleep onset, wake time, sleep onset latency, daytime function, relapse

**Protocol takeaway:** Within-group improvement should not be interpreted as causal light efficacy.

**Directness and boundaries:** Directness is `clinical_supervised` with protocol-evidence scope `clinical_supervised`. Population mismatch: DSWPD clinical population, not general morning outdoor-light users.

**Limitations and uncertainty:** No clear between-group advantage; active-light and activity conditions confounded; adolescent/young adult DSWPD population; device-based light rather than outdoor light.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
