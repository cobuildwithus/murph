This source is included for **indoor_workplace_classroom_home_daylight**.

**Findings:** Participants had later sleep onset and offset on days without social constraints. More morning/afternoon light was seen on workdays, but light-sleep associations were stronger on unconstrained days; higher afternoon light related to better sleep efficiency and morning/evening timing relationships were complex. The authors noted that a clothing-mounted sensor could miss outdoor daylight.

**Why it matters:** It emphasizes that social constraints can mask or modify light effects in real life, a key caveat for interpreting user experiments.

**Potential experiment signals:** Morning/afternoon/evening median illuminance, sleep onset, sleep offset, sleep efficiency, social constraints, workday/free-day status, and sensor limitations.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context for social timing and monitoring caveats; do not infer direct protocol efficacy.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
