This source is included for **clinical_light_therapy_device_boundaries**.

**Findings:** Population: Patients with mood disorders including seasonal affective disorder and nonseasonal depression. Intervention/exposure: Bright light therapy and dawn simulation in mood-disorder trials. Comparator/control: Control/sham conditions and adjunctive pharmacotherapy controls in included trials. Duration/follow-up: Varied by included trial Endpoints: depression severity, remission, adjunctive response. Effect/direction: Twenty-three RCTs were reviewed and 20 studies (n=408) entered meta-analysis. Bright light improved SAD depression scores (ES 0.84; 95% CI 0.60 to 1.08), dawn simulation improved SAD (ES 0.73; 95% CI 0.37 to 1.08), bright light improved nonseasonal depression in three studies (ES 0.53; 95% CI 0.18 to 0.89), but adjunctive bright light with antidepressant pharmacotherapy for nonseasonal depression was not effective in five studies (ES -0.01; 95% CI -0.36 to 0.34). Safety/adverse events: Adverse-event synthesis was not extracted from accessible summary.

**Why it matters:** Backbone clinical boundary evidence that also preserves mixed/null findings.

**Potential experiment signals:** depression effect size, remission odds, adjunctive subgroup

**Protocol takeaway:** Clinical light-box and dawn-simulation effects should not be generalized to outdoor morning exposure.

**Directness and boundaries:** Directness is `clinical_supervised` with protocol-evidence scope `clinical_supervised`. Population mismatch: Mood-disorder treatment populations, not general wellness users.

**Limitations and uncertainty:** Older evidence base; many reports not based on rigorous study designs; device therapies rather than outdoor exposure; heterogeneous disorders and protocols.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
