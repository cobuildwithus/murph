This source is included for **timing_dose_circadian_metrics**.

**Findings:**
- The study assessed light exposure and sleep timing in college students across late autumn and late spring.
- It reports that sleep timing associated with light exposure rather than sun time alone.
- This supports measuring user light exposure rather than assuming outdoor conditions from time of day.

**Intervention or exposure:** Measured light exposure patterns

**Comparator or control:** Sun time and seasonal timing contrasts

**Duration or follow-up:** Late autumn and late spring assessment periods

**Endpoints:** Sleep timing

**Effect estimates or direction:** Direction: sleep timing associated with measured light exposure; no extracted causal estimate.

**Safety/adverse events:** No adverse events extracted.

**Limitations:**
- Observational design.
- College-student population.
- No extracted intervention dose or causal effect size.

**Population mismatch:** College students with variable schedules may not match general working adults.

**Directness to Morning Outdoor Light Exposure:** `same_mechanism`.

**Why it matters:** It helps separate actual light dose from clock time and sunrise estimates.

**Potential experiment signals:** wearable or measured light, sleep onset timing, season, student schedule.

**Protocol takeaway:** Morning outdoor light instructions should prioritize actual outdoor exposure and visible brightness, not only 'after sunrise'.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
