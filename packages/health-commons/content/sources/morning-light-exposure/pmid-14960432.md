This source is included for **free_living_observational_measurement**.

**Findings:** In 33 healthy older adults, physical activity and ambient light variables were reported as significant contributors to sleep quality. The accessible abstract-level record notes a critical bright-light exposure threshold of about 3000 lux and a factor-analysis result suggesting independent contributions from activity and light. Exact effect sizes beyond these directions were not available in the batch materials.

**Why it matters:** It is an early free-living light-and-sleep paper, giving historical context that measured ambient light and activity can contribute independently to sleep quality in older adults.

**Potential experiment signals:** daily bright-light minutes, sleep quality rating, activity level, sleep latency.

**Population mismatch / directness:** Older adults rather than general adult protocol users; not an outdoor morning-light intervention. Directness: `same_mechanism`.

**Limitations:** small sample; observational design; older-adult population; no causal inference; limited extractable timing/dose detail.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use only as context that naturalistic light exposure may matter for sleep in older adults; do not cite as direct efficacy evidence for a morning outdoor-light routine.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
