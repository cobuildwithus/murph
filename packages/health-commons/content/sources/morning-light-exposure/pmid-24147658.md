This source is included for **free_living_observational_measurement**.

**Findings:** In 192 older adults measured for 4 days at home, median evening light exposure was 27.3 lux and median sleep-onset latency was 17 minutes. In multivariable mixed models, log-transformed average evening light exposure was associated with log-transformed subsequent sleep-onset latency (regression coefficient 0.133; 95% CI 0.020 to 0.247; p=0.021). Day length and nighttime light were also associated with sleep-onset latency.

**Why it matters:** It provides a concrete counterexample that light timing matters and that later light can worsen sleep initiation.

**Potential experiment signals:** evening lux, nighttime lux, sleep-onset latency, in-bed time, day length.

**Population mismatch / directness:** Older adults and evening light; the relevance is a safety/timing boundary rather than protocol efficacy. Directness: `safety_boundary`.

**Limitations:** observational repeated-measures; older-adult population; evening not morning exposure; residual confounding; no intervention.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as safety/timing boundary: do not blur morning light with evening light; avoid recommending bright light late in the day.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.
