This source is included for **timing_dose_circadian_metrics**.

**Findings:**
- The review explains how light measurement should account for melanopsin/ipRGC-influenced responses.
- It supports specifying biologically relevant light metrics for circadian protocols.
- It does not test an outdoor morning-light intervention.

**Intervention or exposure:** Light measurement using photopic and melanopsin/ipRGC-relevant quantities

**Comparator or control:** Conventional photopic-only measurement approaches

**Duration or follow-up:** Not applicable

**Endpoints:** Measurement validity for non-image-forming light responses

**Effect estimates or direction:** Direction: measurement framework improves characterization of nonvisual light responses; no efficacy effect.

**Safety/adverse events:** No adverse events extracted.

**Limitations:**
- Review/metrology focus.
- No participant intervention.
- No direct outcome estimates.

**Population mismatch:** Metrology source, not user outcome evidence.

**Directness to Morning Outdoor Light Exposure:** `background`.

**Why it matters:** It is a foundational metrology paper for translating daylight exposure into meaningful biological signals.

**Potential experiment signals:** melanopsin, ipRGC, α-opic metrics, light measurement.

**Protocol takeaway:** Protocol pages can state that lux is a practical proxy but melanopic metrics are preferred where available.
