This source is included for **timing_dose_circadian_metrics**.

**Findings:**
- The paper links everyday light exposure patterns with circadian phase and period constructs.
- It supports the idea that cumulative light timing across the day may matter for morning-light response.
- It is measurement/mechanism evidence, not efficacy evidence.

**Intervention or exposure:** Ambulatory daily light exposure patterns

**Comparator or control:** Modeled or observed variation in daily light patterns

**Duration or follow-up:** Daily-life light-pattern assessment

**Endpoints:** Circadian phase and period estimation

**Effect estimates or direction:** Direction: daily light patterns were informative for circadian phase/period; no treatment effect estimate extracted.

**Safety/adverse events:** No adverse events extracted.

**Limitations:**
- Mechanistic/measurement orientation.
- Participant count not extracted.
- No direct health outcome effect estimate.

**Population mismatch:** Measurement cohort rather than protocol participants.

**Directness to Morning Outdoor Light Exposure:** `same_mechanism`.

**Why it matters:** It helps justify protocol fields that record timing patterns across the day.

**Potential experiment signals:** daily light pattern, circadian phase, circadian period, wearable light data.

**Protocol takeaway:** Morning-light effects should be interpreted in the context of the participant's full-day light exposure.
