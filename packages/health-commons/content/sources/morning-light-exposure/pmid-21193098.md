This source is included for **indoor_workplace_classroom_home_daylight**.

**Findings:** No-light-exposure workers had higher rates of nonrestorative sleep, insomnia, severe insomnia, and daytime somnolence than workers with partial or complete light exposure. Multivariable results still associated no light exposure with insomnia and hypersomnia, but the study remains cross-sectional.

**Why it matters:** It highlights that low daytime light can be associated with poor sleep in real-world work settings, useful as context for users in windowless or low-light jobs.

**Potential experiment signals:** Insomnia, severe insomnia, hypersomnia, nonrestorative sleep, daytime somnolence, work schedule, and workplace light category.

**Protocol takeaway:** Treat as large observational context about light underexposure; do not infer that outdoor morning light prevents insomnia from this design.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
