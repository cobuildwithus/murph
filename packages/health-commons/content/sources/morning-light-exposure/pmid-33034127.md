This source is included for **clinical_light_therapy_device_boundaries**.

**Findings:** Population: Adults with bipolar depression in randomized adjunctive bright-light trials. Intervention/exposure: Adjunctive bright light therapy. Comparator/control: Control conditions in included RCTs. Duration/follow-up: Varied by RCT Endpoints: response, remission, manic switch, serious adverse effects. Effect/direction: Four trials with 190 participants were included. Response favored adjunctive bright light therapy (RR 1.78; 95% CI 1.24 to 2.56; p=0.002; I²=17%), while remission was not statistically significant (RR 2.03; 95% CI 0.48 to 8.59; p=0.34; I²=67%). Safety/adverse events: No serious adverse effects were reported; manic switch was 1.1% in bright light versus 1.2% in control in accessible extraction.

**Why it matters:** Preserves both efficacy and safety uncertainty in bipolar light therapy.

**Potential experiment signals:** response RR, remission RR, manic switch, serious adverse effects

**Protocol takeaway:** Response benefit should not be taken as unsupervised outdoor-light guidance for bipolar users.

**Directness and boundaries:** Directness is `clinical_supervised` with protocol-evidence scope `clinical_supervised`. Population mismatch: Bipolar depression clinical trials, not healthy outdoor-light users.

**Limitations and uncertainty:** Few small RCTs; heterogeneity for remission; short clinical trials; bipolar disorder safety context; device light not outdoor exposure.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
