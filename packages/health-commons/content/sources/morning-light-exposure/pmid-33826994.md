This source is included for **free_living_observational_measurement**.

**Findings:** In 115 adolescents, the less urbanized region had greater daytime light exposure, a higher proportion of morning types, and less excessive daytime sleepiness. More urbanized adolescents had trends toward shorter weekday sleep, more irregular weekday/weekend sleep duration, and poorer objective sleep-quality markers, while showing better attentional performance in the extracted summary. Early morning school start time remained a challenge across contexts.

**Why it matters:** It adds environmental-confounding context for light behavior and sleep outcomes.

**Potential experiment signals:** daytime light exposure, chronotype, daytime sleepiness, sleep efficiency, WASO, attention task performance.

**Population mismatch / directness:** Adolescents and urbanization comparison; not adult protocol users or a randomized light exposure. Directness: `adjacent_variant`.

**Limitations:** adolescent sample; urban-context confounding; observational; mixed sleep/attention findings; school schedule effects.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as boundary/context evidence that environment and schedule affect light exposure and sleep; do not treat as direct evidence.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
