This source is included for **safety_boundaries**.

**Source key:** `source_artifact:pmid-18076544`.

**Findings:** Three of four women treated with morning light developed mixed states; after switching the protocol to midday light, two of five women achieved full response and two showed early improvement but needed dose increase; the authors advised starting with brief 15-minute midday light to reduce mixed-state risk.

**Why it matters:** A bipolar case series reported mixed states in three of four women initially treated with morning light.

**Potential experiment signals:** mixed states, mania rating, depression symptoms, timing tolerability.

**Protocol takeaway:** This is a high-salience caution for any protocol that might encourage deliberate morning-light intensification in bipolar-spectrum users.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

**Extraction notes:**

- **Participant count:** 9 (case_series).
- **Population:** Women with bipolar I or II disorder in the depressed phase.
- **Intervention or exposure:** 7,000-lux light therapy after a red-light phase, titrated through 15-, 30-, and 45-minute daily epochs; initial cases used morning light, later cases used midday light.
- **Comparator or control:** Within-case timing/dose changes; no randomized comparator.
- **Duration or follow-up:** Two-week red-light phase followed by two-week dose epochs.
- **Endpoints:** mixed states; mania rating; depression symptoms; timing tolerability
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Three of four women treated with morning light developed mixed states; after switching the protocol to midday light, two of five women achieved full response and two showed early improvement but needed dose increase; the authors advised starting with brief 15-minute midday light to reduce mixed-state risk.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** Morning bright light can trigger mixed-state symptoms in bipolar illness, even with antimanic drugs, in this case series.
- **Population mismatch:** Women with bipolar disorder under clinical monitoring.
- **Directness to Morning Outdoor Light Exposure:** Safety boundary. This source does not establish direct efficacy for the protocol variant.

**Directness and boundaries:** Case-series signal, not a rate estimate for general populations.

**Limitations and uncertainty:**

- Case series only, all women.
- Small sample and protocol changes over time.
- Artificial bright light, not outdoor morning sunlight.
