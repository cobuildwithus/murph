This source is included for **indoor_workplace_classroom_home_daylight**.

**Findings:** Morning bright electric light was associated with better sleep efficiency, lower fragmentation, earlier sleep onset, shorter sleep latency, and lower next-morning sleepiness compared with regular light. Mood, fatigue, perceived effort, and some daytime self-ratings did not show the same clear benefit.

**Why it matters:** This is an adjacent timing/dose source showing that morning light can affect sleep and alertness even outside a laboratory, but it should not be generalized to the outdoor protocol without caveats.

**Potential experiment signals:** Actigraphy and diary sleep efficiency, fragmentation, sleep onset, sleep latency, time in bed, next-morning sleepiness, daytime sleepiness, mood, fatigue, and perceived effort.

**Protocol takeaway:** Morning bright-light timing is biologically relevant, but this source is an indoor electric-light student study and should remain context-only.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
