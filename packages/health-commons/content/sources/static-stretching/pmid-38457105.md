This source is included for **adjacent_variant_boundaries**.

**Findings:** The review included 300 trials and 7080 athletes. Most studies examined acute warm-up stretching, while chronic effects appeared in less than 20% of trials; chronic trials averaged 7.4 ± 5.1 weeks and did not exceed 6 months. Dose reporting was heterogeneous and incomplete, and only five trials investigated injury-related outcomes.

**Why it matters:** Prevents overconfidence by documenting which stretching questions remain underpowered, short-term, and poorly reported in athlete literature.

**Potential experiment signals:** dose-reporting completeness, athlete population fit, injury-outcome gap, sport-specific outcomes gap, performance-outcome context.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as evidence-landscape context only, not as an efficacy source for at-home static stretching.

**Limitations and mismatch:** Scoping/gap-map design, not a pooled efficacy estimate; athlete-specific inclusion; underrepresentation of elite, female, master, disability, injured, and extreme-ROM athlete populations. Population mismatch: Healthy competitive athletes rather than general at-home users; most studies were acute warm-up studies, not chronic home flexibility training.

**Claim use:** `context-only`. Context-only evidence-landscape and uncertainty source.
