This source is included for **adjacent_variant_boundaries**.

**Findings:** The review included 41 studies and 1178 participants. Chronic static stretching produced moderate-to-large flexibility gains (SMD = 0.96, 95% CI 0.70 to 1.22, p < 0.001) and trivial-to-small positive effects on strength (SMD = 0.21) and power (SMD = 0.19). Sedentary participants showed larger strength gains than recreationally active participants; trained participants were unclear.

**Why it matters:** This is direct chronic static-stretching evidence for flexibility outcomes and a boundary against overselling strength or power outcomes.

**Potential experiment signals:** flexibility / ROM, muscle strength, muscle power, stretching dose and adherence.

**Protocol takeaway:** Supports flexibility as the primary claim; strength and power effects are secondary, small, and population-dependent.

**Limitations and mismatch:** Healthy participants; heterogeneous stretch types and doses; studies with higher quality yielded smaller strength effects. Strength/power findings should not replace flexibility as the main outcome. Population mismatch: Healthy-study populations; not specific to unsupervised at-home users, symptomatic people, or one standardized stretch menu.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`. Supports protocol for flexibility/ROM; strength and power are secondary and bounded.
