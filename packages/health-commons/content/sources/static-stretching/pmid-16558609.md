This source is included for **hamstring_sit_reach**.

**Findings:**
Four 30-second static hamstring stretches can improve acute ROM, but the immediate carryover may be very brief. Endpoint: Active knee-extension range of motion. Effect/direction: Static stretching enhanced hamstring flexibility, but the retained effect lasted only about 3 minutes after cessation.

**Why it matters:** Shows that immediate gains from a static-stretch bout may fade quickly, which is important for how home outcomes are timed and interpreted.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- Active knee-extension ROM
- Immediate versus delayed toe-touch
- Perceived retained flexibility

**Protocol takeaway:** Do not interpret a single immediate post-stretch improvement as durable flexibility adaptation; repeated-session outcomes should be tracked separately.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

**Extraction notes:**
- Study design: Randomized acute protocol comparing static hamstring stretching with a control condition after active warm-up movements.
- Population: 30 male participants with limited right-hamstring flexibility.
- Intervention/exposure: Four consecutive 30-second static hamstring stretches separated by 15-second rests after active warm-up knee extensions.
- Comparator/control: Control condition without the static-stretch intervention.
- Duration/follow-up: Single session; short same-day follow-up.
- Endpoints: Active knee-extension range of motion
- Adverse events or safety notes: Adverse events were not reported in accessible abstract/metadata.
- Limitations: Acute male-only sample; same-day retention only; not a repeated home program.
- Population mismatch: Direct to acute hamstring stretching, but not a multi-week home protocol.
