This source is included for **region_specific_remote_measurement**.

**Findings:** Accessible summaries report that 120 seconds of gravitational stretching acutely increased hip ROM by about 4.9 degrees on average and decreased knee ROM by about 2.1 degrees.

**Why it matters:** It helps distinguish immediate biomechanical change from long-term flexibility improvement.

**Potential experiment signals:** Hip ROM, Knee ROM, Modified Thomas test position, Acute gravitational stretching.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use only for acute-effect context; do not infer durable at-home protocol benefit.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Extraction boundaries:**

- Population: Young physically active persons, reported as male in secondary accessible summaries
- Intervention/exposure: 120 seconds of gravitational stretching in the modified Thomas test position
- Comparator/control: Pre-post ROM measurements
- Duration/follow-up: Immediate acute pre-post assessment
- Adverse events or safety notes: No adverse events were extracted; knee ROM decreased as a measured immediate outcome.
- Population mismatch: Young physically active sample and lab-style MTT stretch; not general at-home multiweek protocol.
- Directness: Adjacent acute stretching evidence; useful for acute dose/outcome boundaries only.

**Limitations:**

- Conference proceeding; rights status unclear.
- Acute pre-post design without long-term follow-up.
- Specific MTT-position gravitational stretch and young physically active sample.
