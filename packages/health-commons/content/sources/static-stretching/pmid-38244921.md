**Findings:** The meta-analysis found no significant superiority of foam rolling or stretching over other warm-up interventions for acute ROM, stiffness, or passive peak torque improvements. Source key: `source_artifact:pmid-38244921`.

**Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse-event synthesis was extracted; the relevant boundary is lack of superiority for acute warm-up flexibility and stiffness.

**Limitations and population mismatch:** Acute warm-up context, not direct at-home static-stretch training. Limitations: Acute warm-up studies do not establish chronic at-home flexibility gains.; Foam rolling and stretching were compared with other warm-up approaches, not no-treatment home routines..

**Why it matters:** It keeps acute warm-up claims modest and separate from home flexibility training claims.

**Potential experiment signals:** range of motion, stiffness, passive peak torque.

**Protocol takeaway:** Do not claim static stretching is uniquely superior to other warm-up methods for immediate ROM or stiffness changes.

**Claim use:** `context-only`. Boundary: Use only as context; do not downgrade chronic home static stretching based solely on acute warm-up comparisons.
