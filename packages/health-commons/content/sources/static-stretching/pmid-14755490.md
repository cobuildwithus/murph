This source is included for **mechanisms_upper_body_general_primary**.

**Findings:** Thirty sessions of plantar-flexor static stretch training increased maximal ankle dorsiflexion and reduced passive stiffness, with reported reductions in soleus reflex responses. Maximal voluntary torque was not reported to improve.

**Why it matters:** This is direct static-stretching evidence for flexibility and passive-stiffness mechanisms, but it uses a supervised lower-leg protocol rather than an at-home whole-body program.

**Potential experiment signals:**

- ankle dorsiflexion range of motion
- passive stiffness
- H-reflex and tendon-reflex amplitude
- maximal voluntary torque

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context that repeated static stretching can change range of motion and passive stiffness, while avoiding claims that every home routine produces the same neural adaptations.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
