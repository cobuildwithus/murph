This source is included for **shoulder_thoracic_upper_body**.

**Findings:**
- **Population/dose:** 14 competitive swimmers completed aerobic control, static stretching, and static stretching plus resistance-band potentiation sessions in crossover order; static stretching involved 2 x 30-second holds for latissimus dorsi and pectoralis major.
- **ROM:** Shoulder extension ROM increased after static stretching and after static stretching plus potentiation; the combined protocol produced the largest acute effect.
- **Null/mixed mechanism-performance result:** Pectoralis major and latissimus dorsi stiffness did not show significant effects; static stretching alone did not provide the same performance benefit pattern as the combined protocol.
- **Limits:** Very small athlete sample, dryland lab measures rather than swimming performance, and only a 10-minute post-protocol window.

**Why it matters:** It is current upper-body stretch evidence with ROM, stiffness, and performance endpoints, but it is an acute swimmer warm-up rather than home flexibility training.

**Potential experiment signals:** shoulder extension ROM, pectoralis/latissimus stretch tolerance, stiffness-mechanism uncertainty, post-stretch exercise timing.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context that static upper-body stretching can acutely increase shoulder extension ROM without measurable pectoralis/latissimus stiffness changes; do not promote the PAPE add-on into the static-stretching protocol.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
