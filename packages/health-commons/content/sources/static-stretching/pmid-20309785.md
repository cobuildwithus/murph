This source is included for **hamstring_sit_reach**.

**Findings:**
Repeated hamstring stretching for 12 weeks improved flexibility, with total daily stretch time appearing more important than whether 15-, 30-, or 45-second holds were used. Endpoint: Passive straight-leg-raise hamstring range of motion. Effect/direction: Stretching groups improved passive ROM versus control; no significant technique or hold-duration differences were reported when total daily dose was held constant.

**Why it matters:** Strong direct dose evidence for repeated hamstring stretching over a realistic multi-week window.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- Passive straight-leg-raise ROM
- Weekly sit-and-reach trend
- Dose adherence

**Protocol takeaway:** Supports a repeated multi-week stretching plan with total dose as a key variable; 15-, 30-, and 45-second holds can be framed as acceptable when total stretch time is matched.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

**Extraction notes:**
- Study design: Randomized 7-group trial testing active/passive stretching and different hold durations with total daily stretch duration held at 180 seconds.
- Population: 173 recreationally active university students.
- Intervention/exposure: Hamstring stretching programs totaling 180 seconds per day, 3 days per week, using active or passive stretches with 15-, 30-, or 45-second holds.
- Comparator/control: No-stretch control group and alternative stretch-duration/technique groups.
- Duration/follow-up: 12 weeks with assessments at baseline and during follow-up points through week 12.
- Endpoints: Bilateral straight-leg-raise passive range of motion
- Adverse events or safety notes: Adverse events were not reported in accessible abstract/metadata.
- Limitations: University-student sample; hamstring-specific endpoint; supervised/programmed setting rather than pure home self-management.
- Population mismatch: Direct protocol evidence for hamstring static/passive stretching dose, but not a complete home protocol.
