This source is included for **region_specific_remote_measurement**.

**Findings:** Accessible abstract reports fair correlations in the whole sample and stronger associations for fingertip-to-floor in radicular low-back-pain subgroups; straight-leg raise was less consistently useful.

**Why it matters:** Fingertip-to-floor is a simple home measure, but clinical validity findings should not be generalized casually.

**Potential experiment signals:** Fingertip-to-floor, Straight-leg raise, Low-back-pain disability, Flexion ROM.

**Protocol takeaway:** If fingertip-to-floor is used, frame it as a personal mobility signal and avoid interpreting it as a disability or hamstring-only measure.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Extraction boundaries:**

- Population: Patients with acute or subacute low back pain, with and without radicular pain
- Intervention/exposure: Fingertip-to-floor test and straight-leg raise test
- Comparator/control: Roland-Morris Disability Questionnaire and subgroup comparisons by sex and radicular pain
- Duration/follow-up: Cross-sectional validity study
- Adverse events or safety notes: No adverse events were extracted.
- Population mismatch: Acute/subacute low-back-pain patients, not general healthy home stretchers.
- Directness: Adjacent clinical measurement context; not direct protocol evidence.

**Limitations:**

- Clinical low-back-pain population with disability comparator.
- Validity for disability does not equal validity as a static-stretching flexibility endpoint.
- Subgroup results vary by sex and radicular pain status.
