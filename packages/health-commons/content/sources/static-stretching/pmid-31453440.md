This source is included for **shoulder_thoracic_upper_body**.

**Findings:**
- **Population/dose:** 36 adolescent female volleyball players were allocated to cyclic stretching, static stretching, or control; the static protocol was reported as two 45-second stretch sets.
- **Outcomes:** Shoulder flexibility increased in both cyclic and static stretching groups.
- **Mixed/performance result:** Static stretching was associated with a significant reduction in scapular-plane abduction strength, and the study could not declare a volleyball-performance effect.
- **Limits/safety:** Adolescent sport warm-up study, immediate outcomes only; no injury adverse events were identified in accessible text.

**Why it matters:** It separates acute flexibility gains from strength and sport-performance tradeoffs in an adolescent athlete warm-up context.

**Potential experiment signals:** acute shoulder flexibility, rotator-cuff strength, post-stretch performance readiness, warm-up timing.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context that static stretching can acutely increase shoulder flexibility but may not improve sport performance and may transiently reduce some strength measures.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
