This source is included for **region_specific_remote_measurement**.

**Findings:** Pilot reliability was high, but concurrent validity among common hamstring flexibility tests was reported as poor to fair, with correlations roughly in the r=0.45-0.65 range in accessible summaries.

**Why it matters:** The protocol may use simple hamstring endpoints; this paper warns against treating one test as a universal hamstring flexibility truth.

**Potential experiment signals:** Hamstring flexibility, Sit-and-reach score, Straight-leg raise, Knee-extension angle.

**Protocol takeaway:** Track one hamstring measure consistently rather than comparing sit-and-reach, straight-leg raise, and knee-extension scores as if they were equivalent.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Extraction boundaries:**

- Population: Apparently healthy adults; pilot reliability subgroup was reported separately in the source
- Intervention/exposure: Four clinical hamstring flexibility tests: active knee extension, passive straight-leg raise, sit-and-reach, and standing toe-touch style measures
- Comparator/control: Clinical hamstring tests compared against each other and by repeated measures
- Duration/follow-up: Single assessment design with pilot reliability testing; exact retest interval not extracted
- Adverse events or safety notes: No adverse events were extracted.
- Population mismatch: Healthy adults; not a protocol trial and not a self-measurement study.
- Directness: Directly relevant to choosing home hamstring-flexibility endpoints, but not direct intervention evidence.

**Limitations:**

- Measurement validation, not a stretching intervention.
- Clinical tests may reflect different anatomy or movement strategies rather than a single hamstring-flexibility construct.
- The main sample was healthy and not a remote/home population.
