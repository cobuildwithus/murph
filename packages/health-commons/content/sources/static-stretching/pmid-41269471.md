This source is included for **direct_dose_synthesis**.

**Findings:** Thirteen studies with 556 participants were included. Stretch training produced large ROM gains from pre- to post-intervention (ES=0.93; 95% CI 0.54 to 1.31; p<0.001). After 2–6 weeks of detraining, ROM remained above baseline (ES=0.55; 95% CI 0.33 to 0.77; p<0.001) but decreased from post-intervention levels (ES=-0.41; 95% CI -0.73 to -0.09; p=0.016).

**Why it matters:** It fills the maintenance gap: flexibility gains should not be framed as permanent after the routine stops.

**Potential experiment signals:** ROM maintenance, post-training ROM, detraining ROM, stretching adherence

**Protocol takeaway:** To maintain flexibility gains, users likely need some ongoing flexibility-enhancing practice after the active protocol period.

**Directness and population fit:** Direct for the maintenance question after stretching, but not specific to the static-only at-home protocol and includes children/adolescents as well as adults.

**Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse-event result was extracted; this is a maintenance/detraining source, not a safety source.

**Limitations:** Low confidence in cumulative maintenance evidence, broad age range, any stretching type, heterogeneous ROM tests, and limited ability to define exactly when gains return to baseline.

**Claim use:** `context-only`. Protocol claims proposed from this source should cite `source_artifact:pmid-41269471` and preserve the listed caveats.
