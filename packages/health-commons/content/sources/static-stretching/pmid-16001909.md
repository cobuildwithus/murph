This source is included for **direct_dose_synthesis**.

**Findings:** Across 28 studies with 1,338 healthy participants, hamstring stretching was associated with ROM gains across multiple techniques, positions, and durations. The review could not identify a single most effective method because the evidence base was generally poor quality.

**Why it matters:** It supports the broad claim that hamstring stretching can increase ROM while preventing overconfident claims about the single best hold duration, position, or technique.

**Potential experiment signals:** hamstring ROM, passive knee extension, straight-leg-raise style ROM, stretching dose parameters

**Protocol takeaway:** Hamstring stretching can be used as a direct protocol component, but the page should avoid naming one older hamstring protocol as uniquely optimal.

**Directness and population fit:** Direct for hamstring flexibility and common home-stretch targets, but mixed static/PNF/ballistic/active techniques mean static-only claims should be phrased cautiously.

**Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse-event result was extracted from the accessible review summary; do not infer absence of harms.

**Limitations:** Generally poor methodological quality, heterogeneous protocols, English/publication restrictions, and limited ability to compare duration or position.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`. Protocol claims proposed from this source should cite `source_artifact:pmid-16001909` and preserve the listed caveats.
