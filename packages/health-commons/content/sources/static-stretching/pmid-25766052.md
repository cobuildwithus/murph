This source is included for **hamstring_sit_reach**.

**Findings:**
A 7-week static hamstring program improved flexibility in soccer players, but athletic population and incomplete sprint details keep it context-only. Endpoint: Sit-and-reach flexibility and 30-meter sprint performance. Effect/direction: Accessible reporting indicates significant flexibility improvement in the experimental group; sprint-performance effect was not fully available in accessible snippets.

**Why it matters:** Adds sit-and-reach evidence for a high-frequency static hamstring program, but athletic context limits direct application.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- Sit-and-reach distance
- 30-meter sprint time
- Training-session adherence

**Protocol takeaway:** Use for athlete-context outcome evidence; do not claim sprint performance effects unless full source extraction confirms them.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Extraction notes:**
- Study design: Controlled study of static hamstring stretching plus usual training versus control in young soccer players.
- Population: 103 healthy soccer players in accessible reporting.
- Intervention/exposure: Four static hamstring stretching exercises at the end of training, 6 days/week; each stretch held 30 seconds and repeated twice with 15-second rest.
- Comparator/control: Control group.
- Duration/follow-up: 7 weeks.
- Endpoints: Sit-and-reach flexibility, 30-meter sprint performance
- Adverse events or safety notes: Adverse events were not reported in accessible abstract/metadata.
- Limitations: Young soccer-player population; training-setting intervention; sprint effects not fully extracted from accessible snippets.
- Population mismatch: Adjacent because athletic youth setting differs from general at-home users.
