This source is included for **home_field_test_measurement**.

**Findings:** Across 34 studies, sit-and-reach tests had moderate mean validity for hamstring extensibility (reported pooled correlations about 0.46 to 0.67) and low validity for lumbar extensibility (about 0.16 to 0.35).

**Why it matters:** This is a key boundary source: sit-and-reach can be useful for hamstring-related field tracking but should not be framed as a strong lumbar flexibility measure.

**Potential experiment signals:** Sit-and-reach centimeters, Hamstring-extensibility proxy, Lumbar validity caveat.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use sit-and-reach as a practical posterior-chain/hamstring-leaning field endpoint; avoid claiming it precisely measures lumbar flexibility.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Participants from sit-and-reach criterion-validity studies.
- **Comparator/control:** Criterion measures of hamstring and lumbar extensibility.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Meta-analysis of cross-sectional validity coefficients.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse events; measurement validity review only.
- **Limitations:** Validity varies by sit-and-reach variant and criterion measure.; Not intervention efficacy evidence.; Lumbar extensibility validity is low.
- **Population mismatch:** Measurement review, not at-home intervention efficacy.
