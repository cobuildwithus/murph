This source is included for **adjacent_variant_boundaries**.

**Findings:** Six randomized clinical trials met inclusion criteria. Because muscles and outcome measures differed, the review did not pool effects; it reported consistent strong evidence across all six trials and three muscle groups that eccentric training can improve lower-limb flexibility measured by joint ROM or muscle fascicle length.

**Why it matters:** Provides historical boundary evidence that eccentric training can improve flexibility but was not static stretching.

**Potential experiment signals:** lower-limb joint ROM, muscle fascicle length, eccentric-training sibling signal.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context only; do not promote eccentric-training findings into static-stretching claims.

**Limitations and mismatch:** Only six trials, no pooled effect estimate, mixed muscles and outcomes, and participant count was not available from accessible abstract/search records. Adjacent modality only. Population mismatch: Lower-limb eccentric training; no direct at-home static-stretching protocol.

**Claim use:** `context-only`. Context-only boundary for eccentric-training flexibility claims.
