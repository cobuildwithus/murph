This source is included for **hip_flexor_hip_extension**.

**Findings:**
- Forty healthy older volunteers were randomized to intervention (n=20) or control (n=20).
- The intervention was an 8-week home hip-and-ankle flexibility program with weekly investigator visits.
- The intervention group reportedly improved combined hip/knee motion, ankle motion, and freely chosen gait speed.
- Because the intervention combined hip and ankle stretching, effects cannot be assigned to hip-flexor static stretching alone.

**Intervention/exposure:** Home hip-and-ankle flexibility training with weekly investigator visits.

**Comparator/control:** Control group.

**Duration/follow-up:** 8 weeks.

**Effect estimates or direction:** Combined hip/knee motion, ankle motion, and freely chosen gait speed reportedly improved; hip-flexor-specific attribution is not possible.

**Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse-event data were extracted from available records.

**Why it matters:** It adds older-adult gait context but is adjacent because hip and ankle stretching were bundled.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- Combined hip/knee motion
- Ankle motion
- Freely chosen gait speed
- Stride length

**Protocol takeaway:** Use only as adjacent context that multi-joint flexibility training may improve gait function in older adults; do not attribute effects solely to hip-flexor stretching.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness:** `adjacent_variant`. Context-only; do not use for direct hip-flexor dose or mechanism claims.

**Population mismatch:** Older adults rather than general home users; combined joint intervention rather than isolated hip flexor stretching.

**Limitations:**
- Older-adult sample only.
- Hip and ankle stretching were combined.
- Paywalled full text.
- Low priority for static-stretch protocol claims because intervention is multi-joint.
