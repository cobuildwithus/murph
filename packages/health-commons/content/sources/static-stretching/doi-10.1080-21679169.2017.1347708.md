This source is included for **hamstring_sit_reach**.

**Findings:**
Static stretching and PNF appear broadly comparable in the accessible synthesis, but heterogeneity limits direct protocol claims. Endpoint: Hamstring flexibility, including straight-leg-raise measures. Effect/direction: Accessible reporting indicates no clear overall advantage between static stretching and PNF on straight-leg-raise analysis, with heterogeneity across individual studies.

**Why it matters:** Keeps comparator evidence balanced: PNF is a common alternative, and the literature does not uniformly show a clear advantage over static stretching.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- Straight-leg-raise ROM
- Technique preference
- Need for partner/therapist

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context only: static stretching remains plausible, but PNF comparisons should not be converted into direct static-protocol claims.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Extraction notes:**
- Study design: Systematic review and meta-analysis of trials comparing static stretching and PNF for hamstring flexibility.
- Population: Included trial populations varied; accessible reporting indicates children, elderly people, and disease populations were excluded.
- Intervention/exposure: Static hamstring stretching.
- Comparator/control: Proprioceptive neuromuscular facilitation stretching.
- Duration/follow-up: Varied by included study.
- Endpoints: Straight-leg-raise hamstring flexibility, Other hamstring flexibility measures
- Adverse events or safety notes: Adverse events were not reported in accessible abstract/metadata.
- Limitations: Comparator synthesis of heterogeneous trials; not a direct home static-stretching protocol; exact pooled effect estimates not extracted from open abstract snippets.
- Population mismatch: Adjacent because it compares static stretching with PNF rather than testing the target home protocol.
