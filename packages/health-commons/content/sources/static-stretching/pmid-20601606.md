This source is included for **safety_guidelines_special_populations**.

**Findings:** In contact sport participants, generalized joint hypermobility was associated with increased knee injury risk in the accessible summary, while ankle injury risk was not clearly increased.

**Why it matters:** Supports caution for hypermobile users, especially where joint injury risk is relevant.

**Potential experiment signals:** lower limb injury, knee injury, ankle injury, contact sport risk.

**Safety notes:** This is not a home-stretching study, but it reinforces that hypermobility is a risk context rather than a flexibility target.

**Limitations:** Sports-injury exposure context; injury mechanisms and training loads differ from at-home static stretching.

**Population mismatch:** Sport/contact-sport populations, not home flexibility users.

**Protocol takeaway:** Do not use hypermobility as a success marker; treat it as a caution marker.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.
