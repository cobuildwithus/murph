This source is included for **direct_dose_synthesis**.

**Findings:** The acute stretching synthesis reported a small ROM improvement versus control (ES=-0.555; 95% CI -0.677 to -0.434; p<0.001; I²=33.32). Subgroup signals were significant for sit-and-reach, hamstrings, and triceps surae, while the hip adductor subgroup was not significant.

**Why it matters:** It separates immediate ROM changes from chronic flexibility training and helps set short-term expectations.

**Potential experiment signals:** acute range of motion, sit-and-reach, hamstring ROM, triceps surae ROM, hip adductor ROM

**Protocol takeaway:** A single stretching session can temporarily improve ROM, but acute effects should not be used as proof of durable flexibility gains.

**Directness and population fit:** Direct for immediate effects of stretching but not for chronic dose or durable flexibility; static-only extraction should be separated from broader technique findings.

**Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse-event result was extracted from accessible abstract/search material.

**Limitations:** Acute-only evidence, technique heterogeneity, and not a home-only or static-only protocol synthesis.
