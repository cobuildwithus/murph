This source is included for **home_field_test_measurement**.

**Findings:** The back-saver sit-and-reach was described as reasonably accurate and stable, and as a safe and acceptable alternative to traditional and chair formats in young women.

**Why it matters:** It helps select a sit-and-reach format and flags that different variants should not be mixed without care.

**Potential experiment signals:** Back-saver sit-and-reach centimeters, Chair sit-and-reach centimeters, Straight-leg raise angle if available.

**Protocol takeaway:** Back-saver or chair variants may be practical alternatives, but Murph logs should keep the variant fixed for within-person trends.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Female university students.
- **Comparator/control:** Passive straight-leg raise criterion measure and alternate sit-and-reach formats.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Three trials of each test on the same day.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** Back-saver sit-and-reach was described as a safe and acceptable alternative in this young female sample; no adverse-event counts were extracted.
- **Limitations:** Female university-student sample limits generalizability.; Same-day testing does not capture long-term home repeatability.
- **Population mismatch:** Young women only; not a broad adult or intervention population.
- **Artifact rights:** open_access; no copyrighted PDF is included in Git by this draft.
