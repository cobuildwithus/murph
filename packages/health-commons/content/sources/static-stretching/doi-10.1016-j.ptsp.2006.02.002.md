This source is included for **ankle_calf_dorsiflexion**.

**Findings:** In 19 volunteers, 3 weeks of twice-daily 5 x 30-second gastrocnemius stretching increased passive ankle dorsiflexion compared with control, while gait ankle dorsiflexion and time-to-heel-off did not differ between groups.

**Why it matters:** This is direct calf-stretching evidence for ankle dorsiflexion ROM and a useful boundary that ROM gains may not automatically translate into gait timing outcomes.

**Potential experiment signals:** Passive ankle dorsiflexion goniometry; Gait kinematics; Heel-off timing.

**Protocol takeaway:** Static gastrocnemius stretching can be logged as a plausible calf/ankle ROM intervention, but the protocol should not promise gait-timing changes from this source.

**Limitations:** Small sample size. Participants had restricted dorsiflexion and prior lower-extremity overuse history rather than a general home-flexibility population. Functional gait outcomes were not improved despite ROM gains.

**Population mismatch:** Some mismatch: volunteers had restricted passive dorsiflexion and overuse-injury history.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
