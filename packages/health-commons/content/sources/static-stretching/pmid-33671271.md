This source is included for **mechanisms_syntheses**.

**Findings:** source_artifact:pmid-33671271 included eight studies with 165 participants; shorter acute hip-flexor stretching durations were reported as having no effect or positive effects on performance, while 270-480 seconds showed a significant but trivial impairment.

**Why it matters:** Adds a safety and dose-boundary reminder that very long acute hip-flexor stretching bouts can have performance tradeoffs even when flexibility is the target.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- Log unusually long holds separately.
- Separate flexibility goals from immediate performance testing after stretching.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as adjacent dose-boundary context: short hip-flexor stretches were not clearly performance-impairing, while longer acute doses showed trivial impairment in pooled performance outcomes.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness and mismatch:** same_mechanism. Performance outcomes and hip-flexor-only studies differ from whole-routine flexibility outcomes in at-home users.

**Safety/adverse events:** No adverse events were extracted; the relevant safety-adjacent signal is short-term performance impairment after longer acute stretching doses.

**Limitations:**
- Small evidence base of eight studies.
- Performance outcomes are not direct flexibility outcomes.
- Acute performance tradeoff is not the same as adverse-event evidence.
