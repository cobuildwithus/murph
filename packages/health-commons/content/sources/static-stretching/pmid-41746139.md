This source is included for **home_low_burden_trials**.

**Findings:** A 2026 RCT compared home-based stretching frequencies for shoulder protraction in office workers and found small posture/function improvements with limited clinical-meaningfulness signals. Available extraction notes report within-group posture/function improvements in both groups; high-frequency stretching had additional improvements in some left-side posture/strength outcomes, but most changes were small or not clinically meaningful except some trapezius-strength changes.

**Why it matters:** It fills an at-home upper-body frequency gap and preserves the caveat that measurable changes may be small.

**Potential experiment signals:** shoulder posture, neck disability, shoulder function, periscapular strength, adherence.

**Protocol takeaway:** Home upper-body stretching frequency can be studied and may help posture-related endpoints, but claims should stay endpoint-specific and clinically cautious.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.
