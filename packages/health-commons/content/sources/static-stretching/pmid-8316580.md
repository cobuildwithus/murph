This source is included for **hip_flexor_hip_extension**.

**Findings:**
- Twenty-five healthy athletic male college students were assigned to control (n=7), hip-extension stretching (n=9), or trunk-flexor exercise (n=9).
- The hip-stretch group completed six passive hip-extension stretching sessions over 3 weeks.
- Right hip-extension ROM improved from -20.4° to -8.3° and left hip-extension ROM from -16.8° to -7.0° in the stretching group.
- Walking and running economy did not significantly change.

**Intervention/exposure:** Six passive hip-extension stretching sessions over 3 weeks.

**Comparator/control:** Control group and trunk-flexor exercise group.

**Duration/follow-up:** 3 weeks.

**Effect estimates or direction:** Right hip extension improved from -20.4° to -8.3° and left from -16.8° to -7.0°; walking/running economy did not significantly change.

**Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse-event data were extracted.

**Why it matters:** It is directly relevant to hip-extension ROM but old, small, male-only, and not clearly at-home static stretching; it also preserves a null performance/economy finding.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- Right hip-extension ROM
- Left hip-extension ROM
- Walking economy
- Running economy
- Trunk muscle performance

**Protocol takeaway:** Use cautiously as direct ROM context: passive hip-extension stretching improved ROM over 3 weeks, but gait economy did not improve.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness:** `direct_protocol`. Context-only despite direct ROM endpoint; use primarily to separate ROM gains from gait-economy claims.

**Population mismatch:** Healthy athletic male college students; limited generalizability.

**Limitations:**
- Small sample and male-only athletic college population.
- Old methods and permission-required access.
- Passive stretching sessions may not match self-guided home static stretching.
- No significant gait-economy changes.
