This source is included for **mechanisms_syntheses**.

**Findings:** source_artifact:pmid-37231582 reported a moderate decrease in muscle stiffness after long-term static stretching versus control in 10 included papers, with no extracted moderator effect for sex, stiffness-assessment type, or total stretching duration.

**Why it matters:** Provides quantitative context that long-term static stretching can reduce muscle stiffness, balancing tolerance-only explanations.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- Multi-week duration matters when interpreting stiffness-related mechanisms.
- ROM change may coexist with stiffness change but should still be measured separately.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as mechanism context only in this batch: stiffness reduction is plausible after multi-week static stretching, but this is not the direct at-home outcome synthesis.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness and mismatch:** direct_protocol. Laboratory static-stretching training studies are not identical to an unsupervised at-home flexibility routine.

**Safety/adverse events:** No adverse-event data were extracted from the accessible summary.

**Limitations:**
- Only 10 included papers in the extracted summary.
- Aggregate participant count was not extracted.
- No adverse-event or adherence data for at-home practice were extracted.
