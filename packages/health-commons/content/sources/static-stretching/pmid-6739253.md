This source is included for **hamstring_sit_reach**.

**Findings:**
Static stretching improved flexibility in this older comparator study, but the source is historical context rather than direct protocol evidence. Endpoint: Flexibility. Effect/direction: All three stretching methods significantly improved flexibility from pre- to post-test in accessible reporting.

**Why it matters:** Older foundational comparator evidence that static stretching can be one effective flexibility method, while not isolating modern home protocol details.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- General flexibility measures
- Technique comparison

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as historical context only; rely on newer hamstring-specific evidence for protocol dose and claims.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Extraction notes:**
- Study design: Comparative study of static, dynamic, and PNF stretching techniques.
- Population: 63 college women.
- Intervention/exposure: Static stretching technique.
- Comparator/control: Dynamic stretching and PNF stretching techniques.
- Duration/follow-up: 7 weeks, 3 days/week, with pre/mid/post testing in accessible reporting.
- Endpoints: Flexibility
- Adverse events or safety notes: Adverse events were not reported in accessible abstract/metadata.
- Limitations: Older study; broad flexibility endpoint details limited; college women only; not a home protocol.
- Population mismatch: Adjacent because population and endpoint details are limited and comparator-focused.
