This source is included for **adjacent_variant_boundaries**.

**Findings:** Thirty-two sedentary young women were randomized to traditional Pilates with stretching (n=16) or nontraditional Pilates without stretching (n=16). After 8 weeks, traditional Pilates was superior for sit-and-reach (d = 0.87; p = 0.035), with no between-group differences for strength, vertical jump, or endurance tests. Fifty-five adverse events were reported; nonstretch Pilates had more pain/discomfort events, but the odds-ratio estimate was imprecise (OR = 4.20, 95% CI 0.69 to 25.26).

**Why it matters:** Useful boundary trial because the stretching component improved flexibility inside a Pilates program without improving strength, jump, or endurance beyond nonstretch Pilates.

**Potential experiment signals:** sit-and-reach, 10-RM knee extensors, vertical jump, handgrip, 1-min sit-ups, Sorensen endurance, exercise pain/discomfort.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as adjacent Pilates context only; it supports flexibility specificity but not static stretching alone.

**Limitations and mismatch:** Small sample, eight losses handled by ITT, women only, equipment Pilates, not powered for adverse-event differences, and not a direct at-home static-stretching intervention. Population mismatch: Young women using supervised Pilates equipment; stretching was embedded in a Pilates strengthening program and was not a stand-alone static-stretching routine.

**Claim use:** `context-only`. Context-only boundary for Pilates/stretch-strength sequencing claims.
