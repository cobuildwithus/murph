This source is included for **hamstring_sit_reach**.

**Findings:**
A brief static hamstring-stretch session can create measurable hamstring-length improvements that attenuate but persist across a 24-hour follow-up window. Endpoint: Active knee-extension hamstring length. Effect/direction: Static stretch groups improved hamstring length across post-stretch time points, with gains declining after the immediate post-test; static stretch alone and warm-up plus static stretch were not clearly different.

**Why it matters:** Provides directly relevant evidence that a short static-stretch bout can measurably change hamstring length over a same-day to next-day window.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- Active knee-extension angle
- Same-day toe-touch distance
- 24-hour carryover

**Protocol takeaway:** A home protocol can reasonably ask users to measure flexibility at consistent times because acute gains may fade across hours while still showing 24-hour carryover.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

**Extraction notes:**
- Study design: Four-group randomized study of warm-up plus static stretch, static stretch only, warm-up only, and control.
- Population: 56 participants with limited bilateral hamstring length.
- Intervention/exposure: Static hamstring stretching; accessible abstract describes three 30-second passive static stretches, with or without warm-up.
- Comparator/control: Warm-up only and control groups.
- Duration/follow-up: Single session; outcomes measured immediately, 15 minutes, 60 minutes, 4 hours, and 24 hours.
- Endpoints: Active knee-extension hamstring length
- Adverse events or safety notes: Adverse events were not reported in accessible abstract/metadata.
- Limitations: Acute study; endpoint is hamstring-specific; supervised protocol may not reflect home adherence.
- Population mismatch: Not a full-body home stretching protocol; limited-hamstring adult sample.
