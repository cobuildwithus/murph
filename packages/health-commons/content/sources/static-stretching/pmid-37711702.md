This source is included for **mechanisms_syntheses**.

**Findings:** source_artifact:pmid-37711702 included 17 papers; acute static stretching significantly decreased MTU stiffness, while the long-term MTU stiffness effect was not statistically significant. Total stretching duration related to acute but not long-term effects in extracted meta-regression results.

**Why it matters:** Highlights a mixed mechanism picture: static stretching acutely lowers MTU stiffness, while long-term MTU stiffness evidence was not statistically significant in the extracted results.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- Separate immediate post-stretch ROM/stiffness checks from multi-week outcomes.
- Record total acute stretch duration when interpreting stiffness response.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as mechanism context only: acute stiffness changes should not be assumed to equal durable MTU adaptation from a home program.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness and mismatch:** direct_protocol. Young healthy laboratory samples and stiffness endpoints may not represent all at-home users or practical outcomes.

**Safety/adverse events:** No adverse-event findings were extracted; the review is mechanistic.

**Limitations:**
- High heterogeneity in extracted results.
- Long-term MTU stiffness effect was not statistically significant in the extracted meta-analysis.
- No adverse-event extraction for at-home use.
