## Findings
- Twelve healthy men completed 60-second hamstring stretches at 100% and 120% pain-onset dose in a crossover design.
- Both intensities increased ROM and passive torque at end ROM.
- MTU stiffness decreased only in the higher-intensity condition; stretch load correlated positively with ROM change and negatively with stiffness change.

## Why it matters
Clarifies dose/intensity boundaries: more intense stretching may be needed for MTU stiffness change, while ROM/tolerance can change at both intensities.

## Protocol takeaway
Supports careful intensity language: higher discomfort/load may change stiffness acutely, but this should not be generalized as necessary or safe for all users.

## Claim use
`supports-protocol`.

## Limitations and population mismatch
- Acute laboratory study in healthy young men.
- Higher-intensity stretching produced meaningful discomfort during the stretch.
- Does not establish a multiweek at-home dose or safety profile.
