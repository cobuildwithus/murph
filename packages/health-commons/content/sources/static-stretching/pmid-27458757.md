This source is included for **direct_dose_synthesis**.

**Findings:** The pooled results favored static stretching over control for passive straight-leg raise (12.04; 95% CI 9.61 to 14.47), passive knee extension (8.58; 95% CI 6.31 to 10.84), and active knee extension (8.35; 95% CI 5.15 to 11.55).

**Why it matters:** It is one of the most direct syntheses for the common home target of hamstring flexibility using static stretching rather than mixed stretch types.

**Potential experiment signals:** passive straight-leg raise, passive knee extension, active knee extension, hamstring flexibility

**Protocol takeaway:** Static hamstring stretching is a direct evidence-supported component for improving hamstring ROM in healthy young adults.

**Directness and population fit:** Highly direct for hamstring static stretching, but narrower than the full Murph audience because the review excluded older adults, children, athletes, and clinical populations.

**Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse-event result was extracted from the abstract; do not infer adverse events were absent.

**Limitations:** Participant count and dose subgroup detail were not available from the extracted abstract; findings are limited to healthy young adults and ROM outcomes.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`. Protocol claims proposed from this source should cite `source_artifact:pmid-27458757` and preserve the listed caveats.
