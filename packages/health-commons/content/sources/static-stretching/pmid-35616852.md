This source is included for **adjacent_variants_recovery_modalities**.

**Findings:** Eleven controlled or randomized studies with 46 effect sizes and 290 participants were analyzed. Foam rolling training increased ROM overall (ES = 0.823, 95% CI 0.325 to 1.322, p = 0.001, I² = 72.76). Longer interventions (>4 weeks) were more favorable than <=4 weeks, and effects differed by muscle group; hamstrings and quadriceps improved while triceps surae/ankle dorsiflexion did not show improvement in the source summary.

**Why it matters:** Demonstrates that foam rolling has its own ROM evidence base and should be scoped as a sibling modality rather than absorbed into static stretching.

**Potential experiment signals:** joint ROM, hamstring/quadriceps ROM, foam-rolling adherence.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use only as a sibling-modality context source; it does not support static-stretching-specific claims.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
