This source is included for **hamstring_sit_reach**.

**Findings:**
Hamstring flexibility training improved dancer flexibility outcomes, but the specialized dancer population makes this context rather than primary protocol evidence. Endpoint: Active/passive straight-leg raise, sit-and-reach, and sagittal spine measures. Effect/direction: Training and retraining improved SLR and sit-and-reach outcomes versus control in accessible reporting; rest period did not show significant loss/change.

**Why it matters:** Provides sit-and-reach and straight-leg-raise evidence in a highly flexible population, but the dancer context limits general protocol claims.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- Sit-and-reach distance
- Active and passive straight-leg raise
- Retention after rest

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as population-mismatch context for retention/retraining, not as primary support for general home users.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Extraction notes:**
- Study design: Randomized controlled trial of hamstring flexibility training with rest and retraining phases.
- Population: 57 female dancers.
- Intervention/exposure: Hamstring flexibility training; accessible abstract describes four exercises, 3 sets of 20 seconds, 4 sessions per week.
- Comparator/control: Control group.
- Duration/follow-up: 7 weeks of training, 18 days of rest, and 3 weeks of retraining.
- Endpoints: Active straight-leg raise, Passive straight-leg raise, Sit-and-reach, Sagittal spine disposition
- Adverse events or safety notes: Adverse events were not reported in accessible abstract/metadata.
- Limitations: Specialized dancer population; high flexibility/training context; protocol may not represent general at-home users.
- Population mismatch: Dancer population makes it context-only despite direct hamstring static-stretch relevance.
