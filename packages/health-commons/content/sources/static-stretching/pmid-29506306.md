This source is included for **direct_dose_synthesis**.

**Findings:** Twenty-three articles were included in the quantitative synthesis. All stretching typologies improved ROM over the long term; static protocols showed significant gains compared with ballistic or PNF protocols (p<0.05). The authors reported that weekly stretching time seemed fundamental when stretches were applied for at least 5 minutes per week, while time within a single session did not seem significant; weekly frequency was positively associated with ROM.

**Why it matters:** It is an older dose scaffold for weekly time and frequency, useful when framed as broad stretching evidence and compared against the newer static-specific meta-regression.

**Potential experiment signals:** range of motion, weekly stretching time, weekly stretching frequency, stretch typology

**Protocol takeaway:** This source supports emphasizing consistent weekly practice, but newer static-specific dose evidence should be preferred for precise static-stretch dose ceilings.

**Directness and population fit:** Direct for stretching dose and ROM, but the intervention set spans multiple stretch typologies and does not isolate at-home static stretching.

**Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse-event result was extracted from the accessible abstract/search material.

**Limitations:** Broad typology mix, no extracted aggregate participant count, and dose conclusions should be superseded or cross-checked by newer static-specific meta-regression.
