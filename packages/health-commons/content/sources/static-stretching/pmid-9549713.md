This source is included for **hamstring_sit_reach**.

**Findings:**
Static hamstring stretching improved flexibility and outperformed dynamic ROM in this comparator trial. Endpoint: Knee-extension hamstring flexibility. Effect/direction: Static stretching and dynamic ROM improved flexibility; static stretching was reported as more effective than dynamic ROM after 6 weeks.

**Why it matters:** Helps contextualize static stretching against dynamic ROM for hamstring flexibility.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- Knee-extension ROM
- Stretch dose adherence

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as supportive context that static hamstring stretching can improve flexibility, but avoid overextending it beyond hamstrings or beyond the comparator setting.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Extraction notes:**
- Study design: Randomized comparison of static stretching, dynamic range-of-motion training, and control.
- Population: 58 adults aged 21-41 with limited hamstring flexibility.
- Intervention/exposure: Static hamstring stretch, reported as a 30-second daily/frequent stretch protocol.
- Comparator/control: Dynamic range-of-motion training and control group.
- Duration/follow-up: 6 weeks.
- Endpoints: Knee-extension hamstring flexibility
- Adverse events or safety notes: Adverse events were not reported in accessible abstract/metadata.
- Limitations: Adjacent comparator context; older study; hamstring-specific endpoint.
- Population mismatch: Direct anatomy and modality but comparator-focused rather than a complete home protocol.
