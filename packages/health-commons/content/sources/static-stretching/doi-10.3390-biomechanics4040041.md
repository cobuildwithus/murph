This source is included for **region_specific_remote_measurement**.

**Findings:** Accessible summaries report an average acute hip-extension ROM increase of about 6.2 degrees and an average immediate knee-flexion ROM decrease of about 5.4 degrees; no significant prolonged effect was found at 48 hours.

**Why it matters:** This helps separate acute ROM change from lasting flexibility improvement.

**Potential experiment signals:** Hip extension ROM, Knee flexion ROM, Acute flexibility change, 48-hour retention.

**Protocol takeaway:** Do not imply one static-stretching session creates durable flexibility gains; frame immediate gains as acute and transient unless longer protocols support retention.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Extraction boundaries:**

- Population: Participants in a hip-flexor stretching study; exact sample size not extracted from batch materials
- Intervention/exposure: Single 3-minute gravity stretch in the modified Thomas test position
- Comparator/control: Baseline and 48-hour follow-up measurements
- Duration/follow-up: Immediate post-stretch and 48-hour follow-up
- Adverse events or safety notes: No adverse events were extracted; knee-flexion ROM decreased acutely as a measured biomechanical outcome.
- Population mismatch: Specific lab-style hip-flexor stretch context; not a general at-home static-stretching program.
- Directness: Adjacent acute stretching evidence for hip-flexor ROM; supports measurement and dose caution, not durable efficacy.

**Limitations:**

- Single-session acute stretch, not a multi-week at-home program.
- MTT position is a specific gravity-assisted stretch, not necessarily the protocol’s standard dose.
- Participant count was not extracted from available batch materials.
