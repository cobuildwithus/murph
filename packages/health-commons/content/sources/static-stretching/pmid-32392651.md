This source is included for **ankle_calf_dorsiflexion**.

**Findings:** In 30 healthy volunteers, both techniques changed measured parameters; the inclined board showed greater triceps surae length change and ankle dorsiflexion than the wall stretch, with lower muscle activity but higher perceived stretch intensity.

**Why it matters:** This directly informs common at-home calf-stretch technique options, while also flagging that higher intensity may accompany greater board-based effects.

**Potential experiment signals:** Ankle dorsiflexion; Triceps surae length; Muscle activity; VAS/stretch intensity.

**Protocol takeaway:** An inclined board may produce a stronger immediate calf/ankle stretch signal than a wall stretch, but it is a technique variant and may feel more intense.

**Limitations:** Immediate/single-session technique comparison. Healthy volunteers only. Radiographic setup and inclined board may not match all at-home settings.

**Population mismatch:** Healthy participants; no clinical or long-term home-adherence population.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.
