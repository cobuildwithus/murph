This source is included for **adverse_event_clinical_population_mismatch**.

**Findings:** The meta-analysis reported a small maximal-strength reduction overall and a larger reduction when static-stretch bouts lasted at least 60 seconds versus passive controls; reductions were not significant for jumping, sprinting, explosive strength, or rate of force development. Source key: `source_artifact:pmid-38735533`.

**Adverse events or safety notes:** No injury adverse-event synthesis was extracted; performance decrement is the relevant safety/timing boundary.

**Limitations and population mismatch:** Acute performance literature, not direct home flexibility outcomes. Limitations: Acute isolated testing does not establish chronic training effects.; Findings apply mainly to immediate performance timing after stretching..

**Why it matters:** It informs timing guidance for users who stretch near training or competition.

**Potential experiment signals:** maximal strength, jumping, sprinting, explosive strength, rate of force development.

**Protocol takeaway:** Prefer flexibility sessions away from maximal-strength efforts when long static holds are used.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`. Boundary: Use to warn against long static holds immediately before maximal strength tasks, not to discourage flexibility practice generally.
