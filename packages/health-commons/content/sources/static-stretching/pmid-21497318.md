This source is included for **hip_flexor_hip_extension**.

**Findings:**
- Eighty-two healthy elderly participants were randomized to hip-flexor stretching (n=43) or shoulder-abductor control stretching (n=39).
- The hip-flexor group performed twice-daily stretching for 10 weeks with twice-weekly supervision by a rehabilitation clinician.
- The treatment group significantly improved passive hip-extension ROM (reported p=0.007).
- Among participants with limited baseline dynamic hip extension, treatment increased stride length and peak hip extension and decreased anterior pelvic tilt.

**Intervention/exposure:** Twice-daily hip-flexor stretching with twice-weekly clinician supervision.

**Comparator/control:** Shoulder-abductor stretching control.

**Duration/follow-up:** 10 weeks.

**Effect estimates or direction:** Passive hip-extension ROM improved (p=0.007); selected gait metrics improved in participants with limited baseline dynamic hip extension.

**Adverse events/safety notes:** No discrete adverse-event count was extracted; clinician supervision and high adherence are relevant to translation.

**Why it matters:** It suggests technique support and baseline limitation may matter for translating hip-flexor stretching into gait-relevant outcomes in older adults.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- Passive hip-extension ROM
- Peak hip extension during walking
- Anterior pelvic tilt
- Stride length
- Gait speed
- Adherence

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as adjacent older-adult evidence: supervised twice-weekly support plus twice-daily home stretching improved passive hip-extension ROM and selected gait metrics in limited-extension subgroups.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness:** `adjacent_variant`. Context-only for the general at-home protocol; stronger relevance for older adults with limited hip extension and supervised technique.

**Population mismatch:** Healthy elderly participants; not general adult or younger flexibility users.

**Limitations:**
- Older adult sample only.
- Supervised clinician contact differs from purely at-home self-guided use.
- Gait benefits were subgroup-dependent.
