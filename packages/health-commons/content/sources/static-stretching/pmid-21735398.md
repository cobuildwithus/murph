This source is included for **adverse_event_clinical_population_mismatch**.

**Findings:** The review concluded stretching before or after exercise has little or no clinically important effect on muscle soreness; extracted estimates included very small reductions on a 100-point soreness scale. Source key: `source_artifact:pmid-21735398`.

**Adverse events or safety notes:** No major adverse-event synthesis was extracted in this batch.

**Limitations and population mismatch:** Exercise recovery/soreness context, not stand-alone flexibility protocol. Limitations: Exercise soreness prevention context differs from a flexibility routine.; Soreness outcomes do not establish ROM gains..

**Why it matters:** It prevents misleading soreness-recovery claims for a flexibility protocol.

**Potential experiment signals:** delayed-onset muscle soreness, post-exercise soreness.

**Protocol takeaway:** Users may track soreness, but the protocol should not promise DOMS prevention.

**Claim use:** `context-only`. Boundary: Do not promise that static stretching prevents or meaningfully reduces soreness after exercise.
