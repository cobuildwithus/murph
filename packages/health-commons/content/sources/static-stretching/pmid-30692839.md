This source is included for **hamstring_sit_reach**.

**Findings:**
Static stretching alone was not the best-performing comparator against IASTM and PNF in this clinical trial. Endpoint: Passive and active straight-leg-raise flexibility. Effect/direction: IASTM and PNF improved hamstring flexibility better than static stretching alone in accessible reporting.

**Why it matters:** Preserves negative/mixed comparator evidence: static stretching may not be the most effective option against clinician-assisted techniques.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- Passive straight-leg raise
- Active straight-leg raise
- Access to clinician-assisted technique

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context-only; do not claim static stretching is superior to clinician-assisted/manual techniques.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Extraction notes:**
- Study design: Randomized clinical trial comparing IASTM, PNF, and static stretching alone.
- Population: 48 participants in accessible reporting.
- Intervention/exposure: Static hamstring stretching alone.
- Comparator/control: Instrument-assisted soft tissue mobilization and PNF techniques.
- Duration/follow-up: Full intervention duration not extracted from accessible snippets.
- Endpoints: Passive straight-leg raise, Active straight-leg raise
- Adverse events or safety notes: Adverse events were not reported in accessible abstract/metadata.
- Limitations: Manual therapy/PNF comparator; not self-administered home static stretching; exact effect estimates not extracted.
- Population mismatch: Adjacent because comparator interventions require clinician/technique support beyond simple home static stretching.
