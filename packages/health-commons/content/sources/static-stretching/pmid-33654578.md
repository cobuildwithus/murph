This source is included for **shoulder_thoracic_upper_body**.

**Findings:**
- **Population/dose:** 25 collegiate baseball players completed static stretching and dynamic tubing sessions on separate days; each static stretch was held for a total of 30 seconds.
- **Outcomes:** Static stretching improved internal rotation, external rotation, and total rotational ROM immediately, at 30 minutes, and at 60 minutes.
- **Comparative result:** Differences versus dynamic tubing were small or absent; the authors noted the internal-rotation difference in favor of static stretching was probably not clinically relevant and did not remove participants from GIRD classification.
- **Limits/safety:** Acute athletic sample, one-hour follow-up only, no long-term dose inference; no intervention adverse events were identified in extracted text.

**Why it matters:** It describes a practical multi-stretch shoulder sequence and shows acute ROM durability up to one hour, but the comparator and athletic warm-up context make it context rather than core chronic evidence.

**Potential experiment signals:** pre/post shoulder rotation ROM, measurement timing after stretch, GIRD threshold status, athletic warm-up context.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context for immediate shoulder-ROM changes and a no-clear-advantage comparison versus tubing; do not cite as evidence that acute gains resolve clinically meaningful GIRD.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
