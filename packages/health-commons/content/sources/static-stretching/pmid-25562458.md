This source is included for **region_specific_remote_measurement**.

**Findings:** Accessible summaries report low reliability for some goniometric measurements and high intra- and inter-rater reliability for trigonometric measurements, with trigonometric ICCs around 0.90-0.95.

**Why it matters:** This matters because home hip-flexor tracking can be misleading if users rely on poorly standardized goniometric angles.

**Potential experiment signals:** Hip-extension ROM, Modified Thomas test, Goniometric reliability, Trigonometric reliability.

**Protocol takeaway:** Prefer simple, standardized, repeatable hip-extension procedures or photo/trigonometric support rather than casual manual goniometry.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Extraction boundaries:**

- Population: Healthy participants aged 18-36 years
- Intervention/exposure: Modified Thomas test hip-extension ROM measured using goniometric and trigonometric approaches
- Comparator/control: Goniometric technique versus trigonometric technique; intra-rater and inter-rater reliability
- Duration/follow-up: Repeated measurement reliability design
- Adverse events or safety notes: No adverse events were extracted.
- Population mismatch: Healthy young adults, not general home users.
- Directness: Measurement-context evidence for hip-extension outcomes; not static-stretching efficacy.

**Limitations:**

- Measurement study only.
- Small healthy sample.
- Technique-dependent reliability means the source is also a warning about casual goniometry.
