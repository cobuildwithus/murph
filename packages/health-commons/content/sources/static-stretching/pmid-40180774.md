This source is included for **mechanisms_syntheses**.

**Findings:** source_artifact:pmid-40180774 included 65 studies with 1542 adults and reported small stiffness decreases after acute and chronic static stretching, a moderate increase in maximum tolerable passive resistive torque after chronic stretching, no significant fascicle-length effect, and associations between chronic ROM gains, stiffness decrease, and tolerance increase.

**Why it matters:** Best-fit contemporary mechanism synthesis for explaining static-stretching ROM gains through stiffness and stretch-tolerance pathways while avoiding structural overclaiming.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- Track both ROM and perceived tolerance/discomfort.
- Avoid using fascicle-length or architecture change as a default assumed mechanism.
- Intensity may influence acute stiffness responses.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context-only mechanism synthesis: chronic ROM gains were associated with decreased stiffness and increased stretch tolerance, while fascicle length was not significantly affected in the extracted results.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness and mismatch:** same_mechanism. Mostly young adult controlled-study samples; not specifically unsupervised at-home participants.

**Safety/adverse events:** No adverse-event findings were extracted; the source is mechanism-focused.

**Limitations:**
- Mechanistic endpoints do not establish at-home adherence, safety, or user-facing benefit.
- Some outcomes had imprecision in extracted GRADE-style discussion.
- Adults were studied, but population mix may not match all Murph users.
