This source is included for **ankle_calf_dorsiflexion**.

**Findings:** In 80 active adults, 6 weeks of daily 10-, 30-, or 60-minute plantar-flexor stretching improved knee-to-wall and orthosis-goniometer dorsiflexion measures; orthosis goniometry showed clearer duration-dependent differences, while knee-to-wall gains were reported across groups without significant between-group differences.

**Why it matters:** This is one of the most directly knee-to-wall-relevant sources in the batch, but its long-duration orthosis-based framing should not be collapsed into a low-burden home protocol.

**Potential experiment signals:** Knee-to-wall dorsiflexion; Goniometer dorsiflexion; Stretch duration.

**Protocol takeaway:** Knee-to-wall can be used as an ankle-dorsiflexion endpoint, and stretch duration may matter; avoid assuming very high daily doses are required for all users.

**Limitations:** Daily duration arms of 10, 30, and 60 minutes exceed the likely burden of a low-burden home protocol. A calf muscle stretching orthosis was used, so implementation differs from simple wall stretching. Knee-to-wall did not show the same between-duration separation as orthosis goniometry.

**Population mismatch:** Active adults from sports study programs/local clubs; orthosis-based high-duration dosing may mismatch low-burden users.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.
