This source is included for **mechanisms_syntheses**.

**Findings:** source_artifact:pmid-37318696 included 19 studies with 467 participants and reported trivial-to-small fascicle-length increases, no clear effects on fascicle angle or muscle thickness overall, and stronger architecture signals with high volume or high intensity.

**Why it matters:** Clarifies whether flexibility gains may involve architectural change; effects appear small/trivial and more likely at high volume or high intensity.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- Do not use architecture change as a primary user-facing endpoint.
- Track total stretching volume if investigating structural mechanisms.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as mechanism context only: typical home stretching should not promise visible muscle architecture changes unless dose and intensity match high-volume study conditions.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness and mismatch:** same_mechanism. Mostly young healthy lower-limb samples; high-volume/intensity subgroups may not match low-burden home routines.

**Safety/adverse events:** No adverse-event signal was extracted from the review summary.

**Limitations:**
- Architecture endpoints are mechanistic rather than user-facing flexibility outcomes.
- High-volume and high-intensity subgroups may not be practical or appropriate for all home users.
- No at-home adverse-event rates extracted.
