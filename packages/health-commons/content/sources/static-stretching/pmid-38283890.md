This source is included for **home_low_burden_trials**.

**Findings:** A six-week home static-stretch arm improved flexibility measures in older women, though eccentric exercise appeared stronger for physical-capacity outcomes. All exercise arms improved hamstring flexibility and passive ankle dorsiflexion in available extraction notes; eccentric exercise was reported as more beneficial for physical capacities than static or dynamic stretching.

**Why it matters:** It is recent direct home evidence and simultaneously preserves mixed comparative findings.

**Potential experiment signals:** hamstring flexibility, ankle dorsiflexion, functional performance, adherence.

**Protocol takeaway:** Home static stretching can improve flexibility in older women, but strength or gait-function claims need separate exercise evidence.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

## Extraction notes

- **Source kind:** journal_article.
- **Study design:** Six-week home-based static stretching versus dynamic stretching or eccentric exercise in older women.
- **Participant count:** 51.
- **Population:** Healthy older women.
- **Intervention or exposure:** Home-based static stretching arm performed three times weekly for six weeks..
- **Comparator or control:** Home-based dynamic closed-chain stretching and eccentric exercise arms..
- **Duration or follow-up:** Six weeks.
- **Endpoints:** hamstring flexibility, passive ankle dorsiflexion, muscle-tendon architecture, functional performance.
- **Effect estimates or direction where available:** All exercise arms improved hamstring flexibility and passive ankle dorsiflexion in available extraction notes; eccentric exercise was reported as more beneficial for physical capacities than static or dynamic stretching..
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse events were identified in the available extraction inputs..
- **Limitations:** Older women only.; Comparator arms were active exercise variants; no simple healthy adult no-treatment comparison extracted.; Functional and strength benefits were not clearly best for static stretching..
- **Population mismatch:** Older-woman sample and multiple exercise arms rather than general adult at-home flexibility alone..
- **Directness to At Home Static Stretching For Flexibility:** Direct home static-stretching evidence with flexibility endpoints, but age- and comparator-bound..
- **Claim-use boundary:** Use to support flexibility endpoints, not to claim static stretching beats eccentric exercise for strength/function..
