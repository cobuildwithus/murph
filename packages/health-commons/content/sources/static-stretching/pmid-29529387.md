**Findings:** Low-intensity stretching showed small to moderate beneficial effects on perceived soreness and function, while high-intensity stretching had likely harmful effects on some recovery markers, including eccentric torque and creatine kinase at extracted time points. Source key: `source_artifact:pmid-29529387`.

**Adverse events or safety notes:** Safety-relevant intensity signal: high-intensity stretching after eccentric exercise may worsen some muscle-damage or performance markers.

**Limitations and population mismatch:** Post-eccentric-exercise recovery context, not general flexibility practice. Limitations: Recovery from unaccustomed eccentric exercise is not the same as routine flexibility training.; Participant count was not extracted in this batch..

**Why it matters:** It supports conservative intensity and recovery cautions.

**Potential experiment signals:** perceived soreness, function, eccentric torque, creatine kinase.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use gentle-to-moderate intensity, especially after unfamiliar or high-load exercise.

**Claim use:** `context-only`. Boundary: Use to prefer low/tolerable intensity and avoid aggressive stretching after unusually hard exercise.
