This source is included for **direct_dose_synthesis**.

**Findings:** Twenty studies were included. Static stretching increased ankle dorsiflexion ROM by 5.17° (95% CI 4.39 to 5.95; I²=0%); PNF increased DFROM by 4.32° (95% CI 1.59 to 7.04; I²=46%); ballistic stretching did not show a positive result (3.77°; 95% CI -0.03 to 7.56; I²=46%).

**Why it matters:** It is a direct ankle/calf chronic-stretching synthesis and helps separate static stretching from ballistic stretching for ankle mobility.

**Potential experiment signals:** ankle dorsiflexion ROM, calf flexibility, stretching type comparison

**Protocol takeaway:** Static calf/ankle stretching can be used as a direct protocol component; ballistic stretching should not be promoted as the preferred ankle-dorsiflexion variant.

**Directness and population fit:** Direct for chronic calf/ankle stretching in healthy users, but not a clinical-injury source.

**Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse-event result was extracted from the abstract/search material.

**Limitations:** Population limited to healthy individuals; dose comparisons were protocol-heterogeneous; exact aggregate participant count was not extracted.
