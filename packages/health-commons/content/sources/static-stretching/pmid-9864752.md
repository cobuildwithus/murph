This source is included for **home_field_test_measurement**.

**Findings:** Reliability sample n=76: chair sit-and-reach test-retest reliability was r=0.92 for men and r=0.96 for women. Validity sample n=80: criterion validity with passive straight-leg raise was r=0.76 for men and r=0.81 for women, comparable to standard and back-saver sit-and-reach formats.

**Why it matters:** It supports chair sit-and-reach as a low-burden endpoint when floor testing is impractical or less acceptable.

**Potential experiment signals:** Chair sit-and-reach centimeters, Passive straight-leg raise criterion, 2- to 5-day test-retest.

**Protocol takeaway:** Chair sit-and-reach is a strong candidate endpoint for older or floor-limited users, but keep chair height and setup consistent.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Older adults; separate reliability sample mean age about 70.5 years and validity sample mean age about 74.2 years.
- **Comparator/control:** Repeated chair sit-and-reach over 2 to 5 days and passive straight-leg raise criterion; compared with standard sit-and-reach and back-saver sit-and-reach.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Reliability retest after 2 to 5 days; validity testing at one assessment.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** Chair sit-and-reach was described as a safe and socially acceptable alternative to floor sit-and-reach in older adults; no adverse-event counts were extracted.
- **Limitations:** Older adult population may not generalize to all users.; Separate reliability and validity samples.
- **Population mismatch:** Older adults, not general adult stretching intervention participants.
