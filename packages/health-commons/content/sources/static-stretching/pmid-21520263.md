## Findings
- Fifteen healthy men completed a 5-minute static stretch exposure.
- Muscle-tendon-unit and muscle stiffness decreased immediately and remained decreased 10 minutes after stretching.
- Tendon stiffness was higher immediately after stretching and fascicle length did not change.

## Why it matters
Shows that short static stretching can change passive stiffness components over the time scale relevant to immediate post-stretch flexibility measurements.

## Potential experiment signals
- passive muscle-tendon-unit stiffness

## Protocol takeaway
Supports cautious protocol language that static stretching may acutely reduce muscle/MTU stiffness, with tendon behavior not simply decreasing in parallel.
