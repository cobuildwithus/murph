## Findings
- Eight healthy young men completed acute passive dorsiflexion stretching with ultrasonographic assessment of gastrocnemius muscle-tendon behavior.
- Available records report increased dorsiflexion range of motion and reduced passive stiffness after stretching, with elongation attributed mainly to muscle rather than tendon.
- The study is acute, small, and male-only, so it is mechanistic context rather than a direct home-program trial.

## Why it matters
Helps explain how static stretching can acutely change end-range dorsiflexion and passive stiffness without proving long-term home-program outcomes.

## Potential experiment signals
- ankle dorsiflexion range of motion

## Limitations and population mismatch
- Small all-male acute laboratory sample.
- No multiweek at-home adherence, safety, or patient-centered outcomes.
- Mechanistic calf endpoint may not generalize to all lower-limb stretches.
