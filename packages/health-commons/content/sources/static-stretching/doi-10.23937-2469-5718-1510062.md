This source is included for **hip_flexor_hip_extension**.

**Findings:**
- Ten young male participants with non-specific low back pain and reduced hip extension were studied.
- The intervention was an immediate active static iliopsoas stretch protocol described as three 20-second stretches.
- Available records indicate no statistically significant immediate improvement in hip extension during gait (reported p=0.07) and possible lumbar-rotation effects.
- The small, clinical, acute design limits translation to a repeated at-home flexibility protocol.

**Intervention/exposure:** Immediate active static iliopsoas stretching, described as 3 × 20 seconds.

**Comparator/control:** Control/no-stretch condition in randomized acute trial.

**Duration/follow-up:** Immediate post-stretch gait assessment.

**Effect estimates or direction:** No clear immediate improvement in hip extension during gait in available records (p=0.07); possible lumbar-rotation concern noted in descriptions.

**Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse-event count was extracted; acute gait/lumbar-rotation interpretation is a cautionary boundary.

**Why it matters:** It is a useful null/near-null adjacent finding for acute hip-flexor stretching: a single brief exposure may be insufficient to change gait mechanics.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- Hip extension during gait
- Lumbar rotation during gait
- Pelvic kinematics
- Immediate post-stretch response

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context-only evidence that one brief iliopsoas stretching session should not be expected to immediately normalize gait mechanics, especially in low-back-pain users.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness:** `adjacent_variant`. Context-only; do not use as support for direct at-home flexibility efficacy.

**Population mismatch:** Young men with non-specific low back pain; not healthy general at-home flexibility users.

**Limitations:**
- Very small sample (n=10).
- Young male clinical low-back-pain population only.
- Immediate effects only; no repeated home protocol or follow-up.
- Blinding limitations were noted in available descriptions.
- Evidence was extracted from open web/abstract-level records, not a redistributed PDF.
