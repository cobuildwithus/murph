This source is included for **shoulder_thoracic_upper_body**.

**Findings:**
- **Population/dose:** 60 young women volleyball athletes with GIRD were assigned to 3 x 30-second horizontal-adduction stretches with or without manual scapular stabilization.
- **Outcomes:** Stabilization produced larger immediate gains in posterior shoulder tightness/horizontal adduction and passive internal rotation than the nonstabilized version.
- **Limits:** Sport-specific, asymptomatic population; immediate outcomes only; manual stabilization limits direct home translation.
- **Safety:** No intervention adverse events were identified in accessible abstracts.

**Why it matters:** It shows that technique fidelity and scapular positioning can change immediate shoulder-ROM response, but the stabilized condition requires assistance and is not a pure self-guided home stretch.

**Potential experiment signals:** shoulder internal rotation, cross-body/horizontal-adduction range, form fidelity notes.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use to justify clear coaching on cross-body shoulder-stretch setup and scapular control; do not make a general at-home claim that manual stabilization is required.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

- **Claim-use boundary:** Use as manual scapular-stabilization/form-fidelity context only; it is not proof for unsupervised home users.
