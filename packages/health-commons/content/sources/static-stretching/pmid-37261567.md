This source is included for **shoulder_thoracic_upper_body**.

**Findings:**
- **Dose/population:** 38 physically active adults were randomized to pectoralis major stretching or a lower-limb control; stretching lasted 7 weeks, 3 sessions/week, 15 minutes/session.
- **ROM/strength:** Shoulder extension ROM increased by 6% (p < .01, d = .92) and long-length MVIC increased by 11% (p = .01, d = .76).
- **Null mechanism result:** Pectoralis major stiffness did not significantly change, and short-length MVIC did not change.
- **Limits:** The program was high volume; limitations included no rotator-cuff stiffness measurement and potential control-condition/cross-over issues.

**Why it matters:** It is direct upper-body static-stretching training evidence in nonclinical adults and distinguishes ROM gains from measured pectoralis major stiffness changes.

**Potential experiment signals:** shoulder extension ROM, pectoralis stretch tolerance, long-length strength boundary, adherence burden.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as support that repeated pectoralis static stretching can increase shoulder extension ROM, while treating the 15-minute/session volume as a high-dose boundary rather than a default home prescription.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.
