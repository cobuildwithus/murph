This source is included for **hamstring_sit_reach**.

**Findings:**
Static stretching improved hamstring flexibility in high-school males, but the youth/male and eccentric-comparator context makes it adjacent evidence. Endpoint: Passive 90/90 hamstring flexibility. Effect/direction: Static stretching and eccentric training improved flexibility versus control; no meaningful difference between static stretching and eccentric training was reported in accessible data.

**Why it matters:** Confirms static hamstring stretching can improve flexibility in adolescents, but population and comparator limit direct use.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- Passive 90/90 hamstring angle
- Adolescent athlete flexibility

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context for static stretching efficacy in youth/athlete settings; do not generalize directly to all at-home adult users.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Extraction notes:**
- Study design: Randomized comparison of eccentric training, static stretching, and control.
- Population: 69 high-school males with limited hamstring flexibility.
- Intervention/exposure: Static hamstring stretching.
- Comparator/control: Eccentric hamstring training and control group.
- Duration/follow-up: 6 weeks.
- Endpoints: Passive 90/90 hamstring flexibility
- Adverse events or safety notes: Adverse events were not reported in accessible abstract/metadata.
- Limitations: Adolescent male athlete/school sample; comparator includes eccentric training; not home general adult protocol.
- Population mismatch: Adjacent due high-school male population and eccentric-training comparator.
