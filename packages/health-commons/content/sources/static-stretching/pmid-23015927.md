This source is included for **region_specific_remote_measurement**.

**Findings:** The randomized comparison tested whether adding joint mobilization changed shoulder ROM outcomes relative to stretching alone; exact effect sizes were not extracted from the available batch materials.

**Why it matters:** It is one of the few intervention sources in this batch and helps keep shoulder ROM claims region-specific.

**Potential experiment signals:** Shoulder internal rotation motion loss, Posterior shoulder tightness, Shoulder ROM.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use only as adjacent evidence that targeted shoulder stretching can be studied for ROM; do not generalize to all flexibility endpoints.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Extraction boundaries:**

- Population: College-age asymptomatic participants with posterior shoulder tightness/internal rotation motion loss
- Intervention/exposure: Cross-body/posterior shoulder stretching program
- Comparator/control: Stretching plus posterior shoulder joint mobilization
- Duration/follow-up: Intervention duration not extracted from batch materials
- Adverse events or safety notes: No adverse events were extracted from the available materials.
- Population mismatch: Young asymptomatic participants with shoulder-specific tightness, not general home stretchers.
- Directness: Adjacent intervention evidence: stretching is directly involved, but the region and comparator are shoulder-specific and partly supervised/clinical.

**Limitations:**

- Adjacent shoulder-region variant, not a general at-home whole-body static-stretching program.
- Asymptomatic college-age sample limits generalization.
- Exact dose, sample size, and effect estimates need verification before stronger claims.

- **Claim-use boundary:** Use as shoulder-specific, partly supervised/clinical adjacent evidence only; exact dose, sample, and effects need verification.
