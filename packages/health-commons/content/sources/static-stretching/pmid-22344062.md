This source is included for **hip_flexor_hip_extension**.

**Findings:**
- Twenty-four young men with hip mobility below the 50th percentile were randomized to stretching, stretching plus motor control, core endurance plus motor control, or control.
- The home exercise programs lasted 6 weeks.
- Both stretching-containing groups showed significant hip ROM improvements (reported p<0.05), with mobility reaching or exceeding the 75th percentile in available reports.
- The multi-component design limits direct attribution to static stretching alone.

**Intervention/exposure:** Six-week home exercise programs: stretching, stretching plus motor control, core endurance plus motor control, or control.

**Comparator/control:** Control and non-stretching/motor-control/core-endurance arms.

**Duration/follow-up:** 6 weeks.

**Effect estimates or direction:** Stretching-containing groups improved hip ROM (p<0.05 in available records); multi-component arms limit attribution.

**Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse-event data were extracted.

**Why it matters:** It supports the idea that home hip mobility programs can improve hip ROM, while reminding the protocol not to attribute multi-component effects solely to static stretching.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- Hip ROM percentile/degree change
- Hip rotation
- Core endurance
- Motor-control contribution

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as adjacent context that stretching-containing home programs improved hip ROM over 6 weeks; avoid direct static-stretch dose claims.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness:** `adjacent_variant`. Context-only because the protocol components are not purely at-home static stretching.

**Population mismatch:** Young men with limited hip mobility; not broad adult population.

**Limitations:**
- Young men only.
- Small group sizes.
- Intervention arms included motor-control and core-endurance components.
- Paywalled full text.
