This source is included for **home_field_test_measurement**.

**Findings:** Repeated testing produced significant pre-to-post changes in AKE and SLR measures, indicating that the tests themselves can induce a warm-up effect and may overestimate flexibility when repeated too many times.

**Why it matters:** It warns home users to keep the number of test trials consistent and not turn testing into an extra stretching dose.

**Potential experiment signals:** Number of test trials, AKE/SLR pre-post shift, Testing-induced warm-up.

**Protocol takeaway:** Standardize trial count and avoid excessive repeated testing before logging the official flexibility score.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Healthy collegiate students with hamstring tightness.
- **Comparator/control:** Pre-test, repeated warm-up/testing trials, and post-test values.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Single testing session with 10 trials per protocol.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse events extracted.
- **Limitations:** Study used AKE and SLR rather than sit-and-reach directly.; Healthy collegiate tight-hamstring sample.; Measurement artifact, not intervention efficacy.
- **Population mismatch:** Measurement-artifact study with angular tests; adjacent to home reach testing.
