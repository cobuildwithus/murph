This source is included for **adjacent_variant_boundaries**.

**Findings:** Across 55 studies and 2756 participants, resistance training increased ROM (ES = 0.73; p < 0.001). Resistance training did not differ significantly from stretch training for ROM (ES = 0.08; p = 0.79), and adding resistance training to stretching did not outperform stretching alone (ES = -0.001; p = 0.99).

**Why it matters:** Important boundary evidence showing that ROM gains can occur through resistance training, especially with external loads and full-range movement.

**Potential experiment signals:** joint range of motion, loaded mobility, strength-through-range context.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as adjacent context only; do not present resistance-training effects as static-stretching evidence.

**Limitations and mismatch:** Substantial heterogeneity and reporting-bias signal for resistance training versus controls; bodyweight-only training did not show a significant ROM improvement. The intervention is adjacent rather than direct. Population mismatch: Resistance training, including Pilates/free weights/machines/resistance bands, is not static stretching.

**Claim use:** `context-only`. Context-only boundary for loaded-mobility and strength-through-range variants.
