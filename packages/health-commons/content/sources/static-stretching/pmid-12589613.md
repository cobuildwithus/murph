This source is included for **hip_flexor_hip_extension**.

**Findings:**
- Ninety-six healthy elderly participants were randomized to hip-flexor stretching (n=47) or shoulder-abductor control stretching (n=49).
- The program was home-based and performed twice daily for 10 weeks after one-time instruction.
- Static peak hip extension improved in the treatment group from 6.1±2.5° to 7.7±3.6° (reported p=0.032), while control did not change.
- Dynamic hip-extension changes during comfortable and fast walking were reported as trends rather than clearly significant.

**Intervention/exposure:** Home hip-flexor stretching performed twice daily after one-time instruction.

**Comparator/control:** Shoulder-abductor stretching control.

**Duration/follow-up:** 10 weeks.

**Effect estimates or direction:** Static peak hip extension improved (6.1±2.5° to 7.7±3.6°, p=0.032); dynamic gait hip-extension changes were trends rather than clearly significant.

**Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse-event data were extracted from the available abstract/indexed record.

**Why it matters:** It is an older-adult home-stretching trial with a relevant hip-extension endpoint, but population and gait outcomes make it adjacent to a general flexibility protocol.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- Static peak hip extension

- Anterior pelvic tilt
- Gait variables

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as adjacent evidence that longer home hip-flexor stretching can improve static hip-extension ROM in older adults, but gait effects were mixed.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness:** `adjacent_variant`. Context-only for general protocol; support static hip-extension ROM in older adults but not definitive gait improvement.

**Population mismatch:** Healthy elderly adults rather than general adult home users or young flexibility seekers.

**Limitations:**
- Older-adult sample limits generalization.
