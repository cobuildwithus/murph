This source is included for **hamstring_sit_reach**.

**Findings:**
Schoolchildren improved and maintained hamstring extensibility, but the child/school setting is a major population and implementation mismatch. Endpoint: Hamstring extensibility. Effect/direction: Hamstring extensibility improved after the development phase and was maintained during maintenance dosing in accessible reporting; maintenance groups did not clearly differ.

**Why it matters:** Provides maintenance/detraining context for hamstring flexibility, with a major youth/school population boundary.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- Hamstring extensibility
- Maintenance adherence
- Detraining period

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context for maintenance dosing, not as direct adult home evidence.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Extraction notes:**
- Study design: Cluster-randomized school-based stretching development and maintenance program.
- Population: 150 schoolchildren were reported in accessible source text; analysis counts require full-text verification.
- Intervention/exposure: School-based hamstring stretching development program followed by different maintenance doses.
- Comparator/control: Control group and alternative maintenance-dose group.
- Duration/follow-up: 9-week development phase, 5-week detraining phase, and 11-week maintenance phase.
- Endpoints: Hamstring extensibility
- Adverse events or safety notes: Adverse events were not reported in accessible abstract/metadata.
- Limitations: Children/school setting; cluster design; not adult home stretching; analysis count details need full-text verification.
- Population mismatch: Adjacent because schoolchildren and school implementation differ from adult at-home protocol.
