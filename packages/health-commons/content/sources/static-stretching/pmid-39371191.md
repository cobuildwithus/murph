This source is included for **region_specific_remote_measurement**.

**Findings:** Accessible summaries report that the seated wall angel scoring system distinguished injured from uninjured sides and was associated with patient-reported shoulder function.

**Why it matters:** It is a practical shoulder-related screen that may appeal to home users, so claim boundaries are important.

**Potential experiment signals:** Seated wall angel, Shoulder mobility, Shoulder function, Clinical scoring.

**Protocol takeaway:** If seated wall angel appears, label it as a shoulder/thoracic movement screen and not a pure flexibility or efficacy endpoint.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Extraction boundaries:**

- Population: Shoulder-function participants; exact sample not extracted
- Intervention/exposure: Seated wall angel scored 0-3 bilaterally based on wall-contact criteria
- Comparator/control: Injured versus uninjured side and patient-reported shoulder function
- Duration/follow-up: Prospective cohort/clinical utility design; exact follow-up not extracted
- Adverse events or safety notes: No adverse events were extracted.
- Population mismatch: Shoulder clinical population/context, not general at-home flexibility users.
- Directness: Measurement/clinical utility context for shoulder mobility endpoint.

**Limitations:**

- Shoulder clinical utility source, not static-stretching efficacy.
- Exact sample size and effect estimates not extracted.
- Scored wall contact may be affected by thoracic posture and shoulder pathology.
