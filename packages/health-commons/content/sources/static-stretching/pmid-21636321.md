- **Study design:** Randomized controlled trial

- **Intervention / exposure:** Passive static hamstring and hip stretching
- **Comparator / control:** See study design; control or comparator condition where specified in findings.
- **Duration / follow-up:** 4 weeks; four hamstring/hip stretches performed 5 times per week
- **Directness:** Direct lower-limb static/passive stretching training evidence in healthy adults, but not necessarily unsupervised at-home delivery.
- **Claim-use boundary:** supports-protocol

## Findings
- Twenty-two healthy participants were randomized to 4 weeks of passive hamstring/hip stretching or non-stretching control.
- The stretching group showed increased hamstring extensibility and reduced passive stiffness.
- Accessible abstract details report no significant change in strength or visual analog stretch tolerance.

## Why it matters
Connects a multiweek static/passive stretching dose to both ROM and stiffness endpoints while preserving strength and stretch-tolerance null findings.

## Protocol takeaway
Supports using repeated passive/static stretching for hamstring extensibility while avoiding claims that every tolerance or strength measure changes.

## Limitations and population mismatch
- Small healthy-adult sample.
- Protocol included several hamstring/hip stretches and may not match a minimal at-home routine exactly.
- Adverse events were not identified in accessible abstract details.
