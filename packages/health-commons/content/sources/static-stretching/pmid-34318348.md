This source is included for **hip_flexor_hip_extension**.

**Findings:**
- Thirty women with chronic non-specific low back pain were randomized to static stretching or control groups.
- The stretching intervention lasted 8 weeks with three sessions per week and included modified Thomas-position, lunge, and prone hip-extension-oriented stretches.
- The authors reported no significant group-by-time interaction for the outcomes, while time effects and experimental-group pre/post changes were reported for pain, disability, and passive hip ROM.
- The authors identified population and design limitations including female-only sampling, uncontrolled menstrual cycle, and lack of placebo condition.

**Intervention/exposure:** Static stretching exercises for hip flexor/hip-related mobility, three sessions weekly.

**Comparator/control:** Control group in randomized trial.

**Duration/follow-up:** 8 weeks.

**Effect estimates or direction:** No significant group-by-time interactions; time effects and experimental-group pre/post changes reported for VAS, ODI, and passive hip ROM.

**Adverse events/safety notes:** Stretching positions were modified to avoid pain or discomfort; no discrete adverse-event count was extracted.

**Why it matters:** It shows a clinical hip-stretching routine that resembles home flexibility work, but the population and comparator make it adjacent rather than direct evidence for general at-home flexibility users.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- Passive hip range of motion
- Pain intensity
- Oswestry Disability Index
- Adherence/tolerability of repeated static stretching

**Protocol takeaway:** Use only as adjacent clinical context: repeated static hip stretching can be feasible and may improve within-group symptoms/ROM, but the trial did not confirm clear superiority over control on interaction tests.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness:** `adjacent_variant`. Context-only for protocol safety/adjacent outcomes; do not use as direct proof that the at-home protocol improves pain or disability in the general population.

**Population mismatch:** Clinical low-back-pain cohort with bilateral hip-flexor tightness; not healthy adults doing general flexibility training.

**Limitations:**
- Female-only sample aged 25–40.
- Chronic non-specific low back pain population, not healthy flexibility seekers.
- No placebo condition.
- Menstrual cycle was not controlled.
- Group-by-time interactions were not significant despite within-group changes.
