This source is included for **hip_flexor_hip_extension**.

**Findings:**
- Seven asymptomatic young men completed immediate pre/post testing for static stretching and a soft-tissue/PNF-style procedure.
- Static stretching was reported to significantly improve hip extension and hip flexion ROM.
- Gait economy was reported to improve at several exercise intensities after static stretching, but the tiny sample and acute design limit reliability.
- The adjacent soft-tissue/PNF procedure also improved hip ROM, making isolation of static-stretch-specific conclusions limited.

**Intervention/exposure:** Acute static stretching procedure; also compared with soft-tissue mobilization/PNF-style procedure.

**Comparator/control:** Pre-stretch baseline and alternate procedure comparison.

**Duration/follow-up:** Immediate pre/post testing.

**Effect estimates or direction:** Static stretching significantly improved hip extension and hip flexion ROM in available reports; gait-economy signals are weak due n=7.

**Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse-event data were extracted.

**Why it matters:** It is historically relevant for hip-extension ROM response but too small and acute to drive home protocol claims.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- Hip-extension ROM
- Hip-flexion ROM
- Oxygen consumption/gait economy during treadmill walking/running

**Protocol takeaway:** Use only as historical adjacent context that acute stretching can change measured hip ROM; avoid strong claims about gait economy.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness:** `adjacent_variant`. Context-only; do not use for direct dose recommendations or performance claims.

**Population mismatch:** Asymptomatic young males; not general adult home users.

**Limitations:**
- Extremely small sample (n=7).
- Male-only young adult sample.
- Acute procedure, not a repeated home program.
- One comparator used soft-tissue and PNF elements rather than static stretching alone.
- Older article with permission-required access.

**Artifact and rights status:** Permission-required/publisher-controlled article. Do not store PDF in Git.
