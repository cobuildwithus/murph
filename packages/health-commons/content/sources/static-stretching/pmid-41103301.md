This source is included for **adjacent_variants_recovery_modalities**.

**Findings:** Fifteen studies with 465 participants were included. Post-exercise stretching had statistically nonsignificant effects on soreness (SMD = -0.06, p = 0.63), strength (SMD = 0.27, p = 0.19), performance (SMD = 0.18, p = 0.22), flexibility (SMD = -0.06, p = 0.67), and pain threshold (SMD = -0.02, p = 0.93). The authors described the intervention as physiologically safe and practical but with limited effectiveness.

**Why it matters:** Current recovery synthesis reinforces that soreness, strength, performance, flexibility, and pain-threshold claims should not be made from post-exercise stretching evidence.

**Potential experiment signals:** muscle soreness, strength, performance, flexibility, pain threshold.

**Protocol takeaway:** Do not market the home flexibility protocol as a post-exercise recovery intervention.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
