This source is included for **shoulder_thoracic_upper_body**.

**Findings:**
- **Dose:** Baseball players performed 3 sets of 30-second passive sleeper stretches.
- **Outcomes:** Internal rotation increased from 43.8 ± 9.5° to 46.9 ± 9.8°, and posterior shoulder motion improved from -3.5 ± 7.7° to -1.2 ± 8.8°. External rotation did not materially change.
- **Interpretation:** Authors noted the significant acute changes might not be clinically significant.
- **Limits/safety:** Small, male athletic sample; clinician-applied stretches may not match self-stretching; no intervention adverse events were identified in the extracted text.

**Why it matters:** It provides a simple dose anchor for immediate shoulder internal-rotation response, while clearly limiting interpretation to acute, clinician-delivered stretching in male athletes.

**Potential experiment signals:** shoulder internal-rotation degrees, posterior-shoulder tightness, immediate pre/post stretch response.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as acute shoulder-ROM support for three 30-second posterior-shoulder holds; avoid claiming durable flexibility gains or clinically meaningful improvement from this source alone.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.
