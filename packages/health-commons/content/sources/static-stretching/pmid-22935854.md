This source is included for **hamstring_sit_reach**.

**Findings:**
A practical 12-week hamstring-stretching program improved flexibility outcomes in adult women workers. Endpoint: Toe-touch test, passive straight-leg raise, and sagittal posture measures. Effect/direction: Stretching increased toe-touch and straight-leg-raise flexibility; standing spinal posture was not significantly changed in accessible reporting.

**Why it matters:** Connects static hamstring stretching to practical adult flexibility endpoints that resemble common at-home tracking signals.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- Toe-touch distance
- Passive straight-leg raise
- Sagittal posture notes during toe-touch

**Protocol takeaway:** Supports using toe-touch and straight-leg-raise style measures to monitor a multi-week hamstring-stretch program.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

**Extraction notes:**
- Study design: Randomized controlled workplace stretching program versus control.
- Population: 58 adult women workers.
- Intervention/exposure: Hamstring stretching program using three exercises, 20 seconds each, 3 sessions per week.
- Comparator/control: Control group.
- Duration/follow-up: 12 weeks.
- Endpoints: Passive straight-leg raise, Toe-touch test, Sagittal spinal posture
- Adverse events or safety notes: Adverse events were not reported in accessible abstract/metadata.
- Limitations: Adult women workplace sample; supervised/workplace setting; spinal posture findings are not equivalent to general back-pain outcomes.
- Population mismatch: Direct for practical hamstring stretching outside a lab, but workplace not home and women-only.
