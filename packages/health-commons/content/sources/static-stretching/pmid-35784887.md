This source is included for **region_specific_remote_measurement**.

**Findings:** Accessible materials describe the four tests as reliable, and suggest passive knee extension may have stronger validity for predicting hamstring injury; exact SEM/MDD values were not extracted in this batch.

**Why it matters:** Hamstring flexibility is likely a core home-stretching outcome; this source helps select repeatable tests.

**Potential experiment signals:** Hamstring flexibility, Straight-leg raise, Passive knee extension, Sit-and-reach, Toe touch.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use named hamstring tests consistently and avoid mixing sit-and-reach with knee-angle measures in the same claim.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

**Extraction boundaries:**

- Population: Young healthy adults
- Intervention/exposure: Straight-leg raise, passive knee extension, sit-and-reach, and toe-touch style hamstring flexibility tests
- Comparator/control: Psychometric comparison across tests
- Duration/follow-up: Measurement study; exact retest interval not extracted
- Adverse events or safety notes: No adverse events were extracted.
- Population mismatch: Young healthy sample, not general home stretchers.
- Directness: Directly relevant to choosing hamstring endpoints for the protocol; not efficacy evidence.

**Limitations:**

- Young healthy adults only.
- Measurement psychometrics rather than stretching intervention.
- Injury-prediction implication should not be turned into a static-stretching outcome claim.
