This source is included for **mechanisms_syntheses**.

**Findings:** source_artifact:pmid-37092369 reviewed 18 studies; all 16 ROM studies reported ROM increases, eight reported greater ROM gains at higher intensity, and two of four strength studies found greater strength decreases at higher intensity.

**Why it matters:** Helps distinguish comfortable/moderate intensity from higher intensity or painful stretching when discussing dose boundaries.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- Track stretch intensity separately from duration.
- Avoid scheduling strength or power tests immediately after high-intensity stretching.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context-only dose-boundary evidence in this batch: higher intensity may increase ROM in some studies, but it is not a basis for recommending painful home stretching.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness and mismatch:** direct_protocol. Direct static-stretching topic, but included protocols and intensity definitions do not equal the Murph at-home routine.

**Safety/adverse events:** No adverse-event extraction was available; the practical caution is that painful or high-intensity stretching may create strength/performance tradeoffs.

**Limitations:**
- Only four included studies assessed strength outcomes in the extracted abstract.
- Not a pooled meta-analysis in the extracted source summary.
- Intensity above discomfort or into pain is not appropriate to generalize into a low-risk home protocol.
