This source is included for **direct_dose_synthesis**.

**Findings:** Static calf stretching increased ankle dorsiflexion versus no stretching by weighted mean differences of 2.07° for ≤15 minutes, 3.03° for >15 to 30 minutes, and 2.49° for >30 minutes of stretching. The >30-minute result became non-significant in sensitivity analysis restricted to blinded outcome assessment.

**Why it matters:** It gives a direct calf/ankle target-area estimate and a useful warning that statistically significant ROM changes can still be small.

**Potential experiment signals:** ankle dorsiflexion ROM, calf flexibility, stretching duration bands

**Protocol takeaway:** Calf stretching can support ankle dorsiflexion, but expected gains should be framed as small and not automatically clinically important.

**Directness and population fit:** Direct for at-home calf stretching and ankle dorsiflexion, especially because the review emphasized techniques used in home programs.

**Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse-event result was extracted from the DARE summary; do not infer that adverse events were absent.

**Limitations:** Only five RCTs; no allocation concealment or therapist/participant blinding; some heterogeneity; patient-centered outcomes such as pain or function were not central.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`. Protocol claims proposed from this source should cite `source_artifact:pmid-16926259` and preserve the listed caveats.
