This source is included for **hip_flexor_hip_extension**.

**Findings:**
- Sixty adults older than 45 with mild-to-moderate bilateral knee osteoarthritis were randomized.
- The stretching group received rectus femoris stretching in addition to a multicomponent program; the control group received the same program without rectus femoris stretching.
- The rectus-femoris stretching group reportedly improved pain, WOMAC, flexion ROM, step length, and gait speed more than control.
- Knee-extension ROM did not significantly differ between groups, and the trial is not direct evidence for healthy hip-extension flexibility.

**Intervention/exposure:** Rectus femoris stretching added to multicomponent knee osteoarthritis rehabilitation.

**Comparator/control:** Same rehabilitation program without rectus femoris stretching.

**Duration/follow-up:** 4 weeks.

**Effect estimates or direction:** Stretching group improved pain, WOMAC, flexion ROM, step length, and gait speed more than control; knee-extension ROM was not significantly different between groups.

**Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse-event count was extracted; clinical knee-OA population warrants separate safety interpretation.

**Why it matters:** It is adjacent rectus-femoris stretching evidence but not direct hip-extension flexibility evidence and is confounded by a broader rehab package.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- Pain
- WOMAC function
- Knee ROM
- Step length
- Gait speed
- Clinical adverse/tolerability notes if reported

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as low-priority adjacent clinical context only; do not use to support general hip-flexor flexibility claims.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness:** `adjacent_variant`. Context-only; avoid direct protocol claims except as an adjacent clinical rectus-femoris stretching example.

**Population mismatch:** Adults with knee osteoarthritis; high mismatch for at-home hip-flexor flexibility protocol.

**Limitations:**
- Knee osteoarthritis population, not hip-extension-limited healthy adults.
- Multicomponent program included multiple stretches and strengthening exercises.
- Outcome focus was knee pain/function/gait, not hip-extension flexibility.
- Between-group knee-extension ROM was not significant.
