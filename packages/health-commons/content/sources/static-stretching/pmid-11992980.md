This source is included for **direct_dose_synthesis**.

**Findings:** Thirteen RCTs with 504 participants were included. The four moderate-quality studies showed a pooled mean ROM increase of 8° (95% CI 6 to 9), while poorer-quality trials showed a pooled increase of 6° (95% CI 5 to 8). Effects were assessed at least one day after the last stretch intervention.

**Why it matters:** It shows older RCT evidence that ROM gains can last at least one day after stopping, while warning not to overstate permanence or contracture treatment effects.

**Potential experiment signals:** joint ROM, lasting ROM after cessation, stretching duration, limited extensibility

**Protocol takeaway:** Regular stretching can produce ROM increases lasting beyond the same day, but modern protocol claims should cite newer direct syntheses for dose and preserve this review’s confirmation caveat.

**Directness and population fit:** Direct for lasting ROM after repeated stretching, but older and broad across stretching delivery modes rather than static-only home stretching.

**Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse-event result was extracted from the DARE summary.

**Limitations:** Older evidence, many poor-quality trials, exclusion of non-English/unpublished material, no intensity quantification, and no clinically significant contracture populations.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`. Protocol claims proposed from this source should cite `source_artifact:pmid-11992980` and preserve the listed caveats.
