This source is included for **home_field_test_measurement**.

**Findings:** The study is reported as evaluating validity, reliability, and responsiveness of the fingertip-to-floor test, with an excellent overall conclusion; exact coefficients were not extracted here.

**Why it matters:** It supports fingertip-to-floor as a simple ruler-based field test but requires careful labeling as global forward-bend reach rather than pure lumbar flexibility.

**Potential experiment signals:** Fingertip-to-floor distance, Forward-bend reach change, Pain or symptom context if applicable.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use fingertip-to-floor as an optional global reach endpoint, not as a pure lumbar-mobility measure.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Clinical low-back-pain or lumbar-mobility assessment population.
- **Comparator/control:** Other clinical mobility measures and repeated assessment; exact comparators not fully extracted.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Clinical measurement study; follow-up not extracted.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse events extracted.
- **Limitations:** Clinical population and back-pain context are not the same as general home stretching.; Exact psychometric coefficients not extracted in this batch.; Finger-floor distance can reflect hip, hamstring, pelvic, and lumbar motion rather than lumbar mobility alone.
- **Population mismatch:** Clinical/back-pain population mismatch for general at-home flexibility tracking.
- **Artifact rights:** permission_required; no copyrighted PDF is included in Git by this draft.

- **Claim-use boundary:** Use for endpoint selection and finger-floor/toe-touch interpretation only; do not use as evidence that static stretching changes flexibility.
