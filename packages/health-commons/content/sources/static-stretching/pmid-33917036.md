This source is included for **direct_dose_synthesis**.

**Findings:** Eleven articles with 452 participants were included. The authors concluded that strength training and stretching were not different in their effects on ROM, while noting high heterogeneity across study designs, protocols, and populations.

**Why it matters:** It prevents overclaiming that static stretching is the only exercise route to ROM gains and identifies strength-through-range as an adjacent variant.

**Potential experiment signals:** range of motion, adjacent variant boundary, strength training comparator

**Protocol takeaway:** Do not use this as proof that the at-home static-stretching protocol is uniquely superior; use it to frame strength training as a separate flexibility-enhancing pathway.

**Directness and population fit:** Adjacent rather than direct: it compares another exercise modality with stretching and should not be used as a static-stretching dose source.

**Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse-event result was extracted from the accessible abstract/search material.

**Limitations:** High heterogeneity, limited dose-response data, varied joints/populations, and comparator-focused design.

**Claim use:** `context-only`. Protocol claims proposed from this source should cite `source_artifact:pmid-33917036` and preserve the listed caveats.
