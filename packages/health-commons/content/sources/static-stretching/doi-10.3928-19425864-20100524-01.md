This source is included for **home_low_burden_trials**.

**Findings:** Acute self-administered posterior-shoulder static stretching improved two shoulder ROM endpoints in collegiate baseball pitchers. Acute internal-rotation ROM increased by about 4.3 ± 5.2° and horizontal-adduction ROM by about 3.4 ± 4.0°; external rotation did not meaningfully change. No significant between-stretch differences were reported.

**Why it matters:** It shows a no-assistance static stretch can be performed in a low-burden setting and produce immediate ROM change, while also exposing the athlete-only and acute-design limits.

**Potential experiment signals:** shoulder internal rotation ROM, horizontal adduction ROM, session comfort/pain, adherence.

**Protocol takeaway:** Self-administered static stretches can plausibly improve ROM immediately, but chronic at-home claims need longer trials.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

## Extraction notes

- **Source kind:** journal_article.
- **Study design:** Acute repeated-measures comparison of three non-assisted posterior-shoulder static stretches.
- **Participant count:** 15.
- **Population:** Male collegiate baseball pitchers.
- **Intervention or exposure:** Three self-administered posterior-shoulder static stretches (horizontal cross-arm, standing sleeper stretch at 90°, standing sleeper stretch at 45°), each performed as three 30-second bouts with 30 seconds rest..
- **Comparator or control:** Within-person pre/post ROM comparison across stretch variants; no no-stretch control..
- **Duration or follow-up:** Single acute stretching session with immediate post-stretch ROM testing.
- **Endpoints:** glenohumeral internal rotation ROM, external rotation ROM, horizontal adduction ROM.
- **Effect estimates or direction where available:** Acute internal-rotation ROM increased by about 4.3 ± 5.2° and horizontal-adduction ROM by about 3.4 ± 4.0°; external rotation did not meaningfully change. No significant between-stretch differences were reported..
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse events were identified in the available extraction inputs..
- **Limitations:** Acute only; no long-term flexibility training inference.; Sport-specific male pitcher sample.; No no-stretch control and stretch force was self-selected/not monitored..
- **Population mismatch:** Athlete-only, male, shoulder-specific population rather than general adults doing full-body at-home flexibility work..
- **Directness to At Home Static Stretching For Flexibility:** Direct self-administered static-stretch evidence, but acute and shoulder/sport specific..
- **Claim-use boundary:** Use for the feasibility and immediate-ROM plausibility of self-administered static stretches; do not use as evidence of chronic flexibility gains..
