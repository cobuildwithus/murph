This source is included for **hamstring_sit_reach**.

**Findings:**
Static stretching improved acute flexibility after warm-up, but the injured-subject context makes it an adjacent source with safety-screening implications. Endpoint: Passive knee-extension hamstring flexibility. Effect/direction: Warm-up increased flexibility; static stretching further increased flexibility after warm-up, while dynamic stretching decreased flexibility after warm-up in accessible reporting.

**Why it matters:** Shows static stretching may add flexibility after warm-up even in people with prior injury, but injury status requires cautious boundaries.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- Passive knee-extension ROM
- Prior hamstring injury status
- Symptom response

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context for acute response and injury-history caution; do not treat it as evidence that injured users should self-manage without screening.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Extraction notes:**
- Study design: Crossover study comparing warm-up, static stretching, and dynamic stretching in injured and uninjured subgroups.
- Population: 36 participants, including previously injured and uninjured subjects.
- Intervention/exposure: Static hamstring stretching after warm-up.
- Comparator/control: Warm-up alone and dynamic stretching.
- Duration/follow-up: Acute testing over two days with short rest follow-up.
- Endpoints: Passive knee-extension hamstring flexibility
- Adverse events or safety notes: Adverse events were not reported in accessible abstract/metadata.
- Limitations: Previously injured subgroup context; acute design; dynamic-stretch comparator; not a repeated home protocol.
- Population mismatch: Adjacent because it includes injury-history groups and acute lab procedures.
