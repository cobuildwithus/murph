This source is included for **hip_flexor_hip_extension**.

**Findings:**
- Seventy-four frail elderly patients were randomized to hip-flexor stretching (n=33) or shoulder-abductor control stretching (n=41).
- The protocol used twice-daily stretching for 10 weeks with twice-weekly clinician supervision.
- The treatment group significantly increased passive hip extension, walking speed, and stride length.
- Peak hip extension and anterior pelvic tilt during walking did not significantly improve at comfortable or fast walking speeds.

**Intervention/exposure:** Twice-daily hip-flexor stretching with twice-weekly clinician supervision in frail elderly patients.

**Comparator/control:** Shoulder-abductor stretching control.

**Duration/follow-up:** 10 weeks.

**Effect estimates or direction:** Passive hip extension, walking speed, and stride length improved; peak hip extension and anterior pelvic tilt during walking did not significantly improve.

**Adverse events/safety notes:** No discrete adverse-event count was extracted; frailty, supervision, and attrition/removal are safety/translatability cautions.

**Why it matters:** It preserves a mixed finding in a high-mismatch population: flexibility and gait-speed measures may improve without clear dynamic hip-extension kinematic improvement.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- Passive hip-extension ROM
- Walking speed
- Stride length
- Peak hip extension during walking
- Anterior pelvic tilt
- Dropout/compliance

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as adjacent frailty-context evidence only; do not apply directly to healthy home flexibility users.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness:** `adjacent_variant`. Context-only and population-specific; do not generalize efficacy or safety to unsupervised users.

**Population mismatch:** Frail elderly patients; high mismatch for general at-home flexibility protocol.

**Limitations:**
- Frail elderly clinical population with multiple limitations.
- High attrition/removal was reported in available descriptions.
- Supervision differs from self-guided at-home use.
- Dynamic hip-extension and pelvic-tilt outcomes were not significant.
