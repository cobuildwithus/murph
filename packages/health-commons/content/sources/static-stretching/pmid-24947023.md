## Quick extraction
- **Source kind:** journal_article
- **Study design:** Controlled 3-week neuromechanical training study
- **Participants:** 21 (analyzed)
- **Population:** Healthy male participants; 22 randomized and 21 analyzed in accessible article text
- **Intervention / exposure:** Static plantar-flexor stretching
- **Comparator / control:** See study design; control or comparator condition where specified in findings.
- **Duration / follow-up:** 3 weeks; twice daily; 4 × 30-second plantar-flexor stretches
- **Directness:** Direct lower-limb static-stretch training evidence with extensive mechanism endpoints, but calf-specific and laboratory-supervised.
- **Claim-use boundary:** supports-protocol

## Findings
- After exclusions, 12 stretch participants and 9 controls were available in the accessible report.
- Three weeks of twice-daily 4 × 30-second stretching increased dorsiflexion ROM by about 19.9% and increased end-ROM passive joint moment by about 28%.
- Muscle stiffness decreased about 18%, while EMG, tendon length, fascicle length, and fascicle rotation did not show corresponding changes.

## Why it matters
High-value mechanism paper separating stretch tolerance, passive stiffness, neuromuscular activity, reflex excitability, and architecture.

## Protocol takeaway
Supports the idea that ROM gains may be accompanied by increased stretch tolerance and muscle/fascicle elongation, not necessarily tendon lengthening or altered EMG activity.
