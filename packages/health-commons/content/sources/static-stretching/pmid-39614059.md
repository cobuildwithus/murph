This source is included for **direct_dose_synthesis**.

**Findings:** Data from 189 studies representing 6,654 adults showed a moderate positive acute effect of static stretching on flexibility (Hedges g=0.63; 95% CI 0.52 to 0.75; p<0.001) and a large positive chronic effect (Hedges g=0.96; 95% CI 0.84 to 1.09; p<0.001). Flexibility improvements were maximized at 4 minutes per muscle group per session for acute effects and 10 minutes per muscle group per week for chronic effects; no additional benefit was observed beyond those volumes.

**Why it matters:** This is the primary dose anchor for an at-home static-stretching flexibility protocol because it is static-specific and reports dose plateaus.

**Potential experiment signals:** flexibility, range of motion, acute stretching dose, chronic weekly stretching dose

**Protocol takeaway:** Static stretching improves adult flexibility; a practical chronic target near 10 minutes per muscle group per week is evidence-aligned, while more may not add benefit in the meta-regression.

**Directness and population fit:** Highly direct for adult at-home static stretching dose and flexibility outcomes, though at-home supervision/adherence may differ from included trials.

**Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse-event result was extracted from the accessible abstract; do not use this as a safety-clearance source.

**Limitations:** Adult-only evidence, dose-response at group level, controlled-study conditions may not match home adherence, and individual response can vary by baseline flexibility and target muscle.
