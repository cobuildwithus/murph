This source is included for **direct_dose_synthesis**.

**Findings:** Twenty-seven RCTs with 606 participants were included. A single bout of static and dynamic stretching showed similar short-term effects on hamstring ROM/length. Across multiple bouts, static stretching was better than dynamic stretching for hamstring ROM in acute (MD -5.13; 95% CI -7.65 to -2.61), subacute (MD -5.30; 95% CI -6.33 to -4.27), and long-term categories (MD -10.40; 95% CI -10.97 to -9.83); medium-term differences were not significant. Stiffness and passive-torque findings were sparse or mixed.

**Why it matters:** It is a recent hamstring synthesis that separates static stretching from dynamic stretching and preserves stiffness as a mixed/underpowered endpoint.

**Potential experiment signals:** hamstring ROM, hamstring flexibility, hamstring stiffness, passive torque

**Protocol takeaway:** For hamstring ROM, repeated static stretching has evidence of benefit over dynamic stretching; do not claim robust stiffness changes from this source.

**Directness and population fit:** Direct for hamstring static stretching, but it is a static-vs-dynamic comparison rather than a no-treatment efficacy synthesis.

**Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse-event result was extracted from the PubMed abstract.

**Limitations:** Hamstring-only target, sparse stiffness evidence, some time-window categories with limited studies, and comparator focus on dynamic stretching.
