This source is included for **region_specific_remote_measurement**.

**Findings:** Accessible summaries report excellent inter-rater and intra-rater reliability, with ICCs at or above 0.91; virtual shoulder internal-rotation measurements were slightly lower than in-person values.

**Why it matters:** Supports photo-based remote shoulder ROM tracking as a possible Murph signal.

**Potential experiment signals:** Shoulder internal rotation behind the back, Forearm pronation/supination, Virtual measurement reliability, MDC/SEM.

**Protocol takeaway:** Shoulder ROM can be measured remotely with standardized images, but compare like-with-like rather than mixing virtual and in-person values.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Extraction boundaries:**

- Population: Adults aged 18-60 years without upper-extremity injuries in accessible summaries
- Intervention/exposure: Virtual goniometric measurements from static mobile images for shoulder internal rotation behind the back and forearm motions
- Comparator/control: In-person goniometric measurements
- Duration/follow-up: Virtual ratings within 10 days of in-person measurements
- Adverse events or safety notes: No adverse events were extracted.
- Population mismatch: Adults without upper-extremity injuries; not general home stretchers.
- Directness: Remote measurement context for shoulder ROM endpoints.

**Limitations:**

- Healthy/injury-free upper-extremity sample.
- Static-image virtual rating is more structured than casual home self-measurement.
- Slight systematic difference between virtual and in-person internal-rotation values should be considered.
