This source is included for **home_field_test_measurement**.

**Findings:** The authors concluded that absolute finger-floor distance values do not qualify as a valid parameter for lumbar mobility. Reported correlations varied by population and segment, with weak asymptomatic correlations and stronger pelvic than lumbar associations in some subgroups.

**Why it matters:** It prevents overclaiming a simple toe-touch/finger-floor measure as a direct lumbar-flexibility endpoint.

**Potential experiment signals:** Finger-floor distance, Lumbar mobility caveat, Pelvic/global reach contribution.

**Protocol takeaway:** Label finger-floor distance as global forward-bend reach; do not present it as a lumbar mobility measurement.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Adults with chronic low back pain and asymptomatic adults; matched subcohort analyses also reported.
- **Comparator/control:** Epionics-SPINE measures of pelvic and lumbar range of flexion.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Cross-sectional assessment.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse events extracted.
- **Limitations:** This is a validity-boundary source, not intervention evidence.; Findings argue against using FFD as isolated lumbar mobility.
- **Population mismatch:** Lumbar-mobility assessment population, including low-back-pain participants; adjacent to general flexibility tracking.
