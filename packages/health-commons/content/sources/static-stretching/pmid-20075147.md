This source is included for **mechanisms_syntheses**.

**Findings:** source_artifact:pmid-20075147 distinguishes transient mechanical changes during stretching from longer-lasting changes in stretch sensation or tolerance. It is useful for mechanism language, not for dose or outcome claims.

**Why it matters:** Gives the protocol vocabulary for explaining flexibility gains as stretch tolerance, sensation, and transient viscoelastic effects rather than automatic permanent tissue lengthening.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- Log perceived stretch intensity or discomfort, because tolerance may influence how far a user can move.
- Do not interpret every ROM increase as proof of structural muscle length change.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as mechanism context only: flexibility gains may reflect modified sensation or tolerance, so protocol language should not promise permanent muscle lengthening.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness and mismatch:** background. Not a direct at-home static-stretching trial and not specific to a home flexibility protocol.

**Safety/adverse events:** No adverse-event findings were extracted from this narrative mechanism review.

**Limitations:**
- Narrative review, not a pooled efficacy analysis.
- Older synthesis; later meta-analyses refine stiffness and tolerance estimates.
- No direct at-home protocol outcomes or adverse-event rates were extracted.
