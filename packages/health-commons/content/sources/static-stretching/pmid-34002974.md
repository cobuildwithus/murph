This source is included for **hamstring_sit_reach**.

**Findings:**
1. In older adults with hamstring tightness, 30-second static hamstring stretching improved ROM without the same neural-stress signal reported for 60 seconds. Endpoint: Knee range of motion. Effect/direction: Thirty- and 60-second stretches improved ROM versus control in accessible reporting; 30 seconds was presented as an optimal duration when safety findings were considered.
2. This source adds a safety boundary against assuming longer static hamstring holds are always preferable in older adults. Endpoint: Dermatomal somatosensory evoked-potential amplitudes. Effect/direction: Sixty-second stretching was associated with reduced DSSEP amplitudes interpreted by the authors as neural stress.

**Why it matters:** Adds a safety boundary to dose selection: longer holds are not automatically better in older adults.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- Knee ROM
- Stretch discomfort or neurologic symptoms
- 24-hour retention

**Protocol takeaway:** Use 30-second holds as a conservative default for older adults or people with nerve-like symptoms, and avoid pushing long hamstring holds when symptoms appear.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

**Extraction notes:**
- Study design: Randomized trial comparing sham/control and 15-, 30-, and 60-second hamstring-stretch durations.
- Population: 100 older adults aged 60-65 years with bilateral hamstring tightness.
- Intervention/exposure: Static hamstring stretching for 15, 30, or 60 seconds.
- Comparator/control: Sham/control condition.
- Duration/follow-up: Single session with immediate and 24-hour follow-up.
- Endpoints: Knee range of motion, Dermatomal somatosensory evoked potentials
- Adverse events or safety notes: No conventional adverse-event count was reported in accessible abstract; the study specifically raised a safety/physiology concern for 60-second stretches in older adults.
- Limitations: Older adult sample with tightness; acute physiology endpoints; neural-stress interpretation may not generalize to younger adults.
- Population mismatch: Direct for hamstring stretch-duration safety in older adults, but not a broad adult home protocol.
