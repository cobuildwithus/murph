This source is included for **hamstring_sit_reach**.

**Findings:**
Static stretching evidence is sensitive to setup: pelvic positioning influenced hamstring flexibility gains. Endpoint: Hamstring flexibility/range of motion. Effect/direction: Anterior pelvic tilt positioning increased flexibility, while posterior pelvic tilt did not show a significant increase in accessible reporting.

**Why it matters:** Shows that body position and pelvic cueing can change hamstring-flexibility response, which matters for at-home form instructions.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- Active or passive hamstring range of motion
- Sit-and-reach or toe-touch distance
- Form notes: pelvis/spine position

**Protocol takeaway:** Use this as direct but form-specific support: static hamstring stretches should include simple posture cues rather than treating stretch dose alone as the only active ingredient.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

**Extraction notes:**
- Study design: Controlled comparison of anterior versus posterior pelvic position while stretching hamstrings with PNF and static methods.
- Population: 20 participants; further demographic detail not available in accessible abstract/metadata.
- Intervention/exposure: Hamstring stretching sessions with pelvic position controlled; static stretching was one of the limb-level stretching methods.
- Comparator/control: Different pelvic positions and PNF stretching comparator.
- Duration/follow-up: Eight sessions.
- Endpoints: Hamstring flexibility/range of motion
- Adverse events or safety notes: Adverse events were not reported in accessible abstract/metadata.
- Limitations: Small older study; demographic and exact endpoint details were limited in accessible metadata; pelvic-position manipulation is narrower than a full home protocol.
- Population mismatch: Not explicitly home-based and not general whole-body flexibility; hamstring-specific lab/supervised protocol.
