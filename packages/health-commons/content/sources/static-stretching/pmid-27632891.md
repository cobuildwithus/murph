This source is included for **direct_dose_synthesis**.

**Findings:** The review found moderate evidence for immediate and short-term positive effects of cross-body stretch on posterior shoulder tightness and GIRD in asymptomatic young subjects. It did not find evidence that sleeper stretch was better than no intervention.

**Why it matters:** It supports a narrow shoulder ROM claim while warning against extrapolating to long-term shoulder-pain or symptomatic-athlete outcomes.

**Potential experiment signals:** posterior shoulder tightness, glenohumeral internal rotation deficit, shoulder internal rotation ROM

**Protocol takeaway:** Cross-body shoulder stretch may be reasonable for shoulder ROM, but it should be presented as shoulder-context evidence rather than a core whole-body static-stretching claim.

**Directness and population fit:** Direct for shoulder stretching but population- and region-specific; at-home general flexibility claims should not be based primarily on this source.

**Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse-event result was extracted from the accessible source material.

**Limitations:** Limited long-term evidence, asymptomatic-young-subject emphasis, heterogeneity, and uncertain applicability to symptomatic shoulder-pain users.
