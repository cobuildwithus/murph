This source is included for **mechanisms_upper_body_general_primary**.

**Findings:** End ROM and passive torque at end ROM increased after stretching, while muscle-tendon stiffness decreased immediately and recovered faster than ROM.

**Why it matters:** The study separates immediate ROM gains from short-lived viscoelastic changes, which helps bound mechanism claims.

**Potential experiment signals:**

- dorsiflexion ROM
- passive torque
- muscle-tendon unit stiffness
- post-stretch time course

**Protocol takeaway:** Do not equate post-stretch ROM improvements with lasting viscoelastic tissue changes; describe acute stiffness effects as transient.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
