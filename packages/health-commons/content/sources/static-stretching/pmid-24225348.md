This source is included for **mechanisms_syntheses**.

**Findings:** source_artifact:pmid-24225348 included 23 studies with 734 participants and reported evidence that static stretching alone increased ankle dorsiflexion in some comparisons, with standardized mean-difference ranges reported for static stretching. Treat this as joint-specific context.

**Why it matters:** Provides ankle-specific context for static stretching among other conservative dorsiflexion interventions and helps avoid generalizing ankle findings to whole-body flexibility.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- Ankle dorsiflexion can be measured separately from broader sit-and-reach or hip ROM outcomes.
- Mobility changes may be local and joint-specific.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as ankle-specific context only; static stretching effects on ankle dorsiflexion should not be promoted as whole-protocol efficacy evidence.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness and mismatch:** same_mechanism. Target joint is ankle dorsiflexion; populations are healthy/athletic rather than a broad unsupervised home population.

**Safety/adverse events:** No adverse-event signal was extracted from the abstract-level review details used here.

**Limitations:**
- Ankle-specific endpoint.
- Included multiple conservative intervention types, not static stretching alone.
- The authors noted a paucity of high-quality evidence for some interventions.
