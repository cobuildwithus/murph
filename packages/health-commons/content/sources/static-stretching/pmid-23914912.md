This source is included for **ankle_calf_dorsiflexion**.

**Findings:** The review included 9 studies and reported that static stretching as part of a home exercise program had the strongest identified effect on increasing dorsiflexion about 2 weeks after acute ankle sprain, with Cohen d reported as 1.06 and a wide confidence interval.

**Why it matters:** This preserves rehabilitation context while separating ankle-sprain treatment claims from general at-home flexibility claims.

**Potential experiment signals:** Ankle sprain dorsiflexion; Home exercise; Effect-size boundary.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as adjacent injury-rehab context only; do not claim this protocol treats ankle sprain.

**Limitations:** Clinical injury population. Review included only 9 studies with varied interventions. Static stretching was part of standardized care/home exercise rather than a pure static-stretch protocol.

**Population mismatch:** Ankle-sprain rehabilitation population.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
