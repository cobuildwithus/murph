This source is included for **direct_dose_synthesis**.

**Findings:** In RCTs of stretching for GIRD, stretching alone statistically improved shoulder internal rotation and horizontal adduction ROM, while external rotation ROM and pain intensity did not clearly improve. Adding dorsal gliding or manual stabilization/manual techniques was sometimes better than stretching alone, but those are supervised or assisted variants rather than plain home static stretching.

**Why it matters:** It preserves a shoulder-specific evidence boundary: ROM may improve in GIRD, but certainty is very low and manual-therapy/stabilization findings do not transfer cleanly to self-directed home static stretching.

**Potential experiment signals:** shoulder internal rotation ROM, horizontal adduction ROM, external rotation ROM, pain intensity

**Protocol takeaway:** Shoulder static-stretch claims should stay narrow: GIRD-focused stretching may improve internal rotation and horizontal adduction, but it should not be generalized into broad pain or external-rotation claims.

**Directness and population fit:** Direct for shoulder stretching but mismatched to a general home-flexibility protocol because the population is GIRD/athlete-heavy and several favorable comparisons involve manual therapy or stabilization.

**Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse-event result was extracted from the available batch material; do not infer that adverse events were absent.

**Limitations:** Very-low certainty, small subgroup samples, shoulder/GIRD specificity, and adjacent manual-therapy components limit claim strength.

**Claim use:** `context-only`. Protocol claims proposed from this source should cite `source_artifact:doi-10.3390/app14167166` and preserve the listed caveats.
