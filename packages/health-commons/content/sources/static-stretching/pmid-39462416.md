This source is included for **hip_flexor_hip_extension**.

**Findings:**
- Twenty-six healthy physically active participants contributed 52 hips in a randomized crossover design.
- Each stretch condition used two 30-second sets with 30 seconds of rest.
- The posterior-pelvic-tilt stretch improved the rectus-femoris hip-flexibility force measure more than the conventional hip-extension stretch (reported group-by-time p=0.034; PPTS mean difference 4.85 N·m, p=0.003; conventional stretch mean difference 0.06 N·m, p=0.969).
- No active-knee-extension differences were reported between conditions, and there were no dropouts or losses.

**Intervention/exposure:** Posterior-pelvic-tilt half-kneeling hip-extension stretch, 2 × 30 seconds.

**Comparator/control:** Conventional half-kneeling hip-extension stretch, 2 × 30 seconds, on the opposite limb/condition.

**Duration/follow-up:** Immediate acute assessment after stretching, with crossover conditions.

**Effect estimates or direction:** Posterior-pelvic-tilt stretch improved RHF force more than conventional stretch (F=4.775, p=0.034; PPTS mean difference 4.85 N·m, p=0.003); AKE showed no significant difference.

**Adverse events/safety notes:** Discomfort was monitored and participants were told to stop for sharp pain; no dropouts or losses were reported.

**Why it matters:** It is direct, recent evidence for hip-extension static-stretch technique details that can be translated into home guidance, especially cueing pelvic position.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- Rectus femoris flexibility by rectus femoris hip-flexibility test
- Active knee-extension test
- Stretch discomfort
- Technique adherence

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as direct technique evidence: a half-kneeling hip-extension stretch with posterior pelvic tilt may be more effective acutely than a conventional version for rectus-femoris/hip-flexor flexibility.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

**Directness:** `direct_protocol`. Supports acute hip-flexor/rectus-femoris flexibility and technique-cue claims, not long-term pain, gait, or performance claims.

**Population mismatch:** Healthy active adults aged 18–35; findings may not generalize to older, sedentary, painful, or mobility-limited users.

**Limitations:**
- Young healthy active sample only.
- Acute effects only; no long-term follow-up.
- Potential carryover/control concerns despite a 30-minute rest period.
- No walking or running biomechanics measured.
