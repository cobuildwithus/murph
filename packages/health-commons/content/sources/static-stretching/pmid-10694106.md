This source is included for **adverse_event_clinical_population_mismatch**.

**Findings:** 333 total injuries were reported; 158 in the stretch group and 175 in control. Hazard ratios were 0.95 for all injuries (95% CI 0.77 to 1.18), 0.83 for soft-tissue injuries (95% CI 0.63 to 1.09), and 1.22 for bone injuries (95% CI 0.86 to 1.76). Source key: `source_artifact:pmid-10694106`.

**Adverse events or safety notes:** No protocol-attributable adverse event signal was extracted, but stretching did not meaningfully reduce lower-limb injury risk in this supervised preexercise setting.

**Limitations and population mismatch:** Adjacent injury-prevention warm-up evidence in male military recruits, not direct at-home flexibility evidence. Limitations: Military recruits only; young male population and high training load differ from unsupervised at-home flexibility work.; The exposure was preexercise stretching inside warm-up, not a stand-alone flexibility routine..

**Why it matters:** It is a high-priority guardrail against overselling injury-prevention benefits for a home flexibility protocol.

**Potential experiment signals:** lower-limb injury incidence, soft-tissue injury, bone injury, fitness and age risk factors.

**Protocol takeaway:** Do not claim routine static stretching prevents lower-limb injuries; present injury prevention as unproven in adjacent warm-up trials.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`. Boundary: Use only to avoid claiming that at-home static stretching prevents lower-limb injuries.
