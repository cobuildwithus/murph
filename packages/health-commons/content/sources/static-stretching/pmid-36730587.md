This source is included for **adjacent_variant_boundaries**.

**Findings:** Meta-analysis of 22 ROM outcomes from 17 studies and 376 participants found a large increase in lower-limb passive ROM (Hedges g = 0.86, 95% CI 0.65 to 1.08). Subgroups remained positive across 4-5, 6-8, and 9-14 week programs and across dorsiflexion, knee extension, and knee flexion endpoints.

**Why it matters:** Shows a strong adjacent lower-limb ROM signal from eccentric resistance training, supporting a separate loaded-mobility boundary.

**Potential experiment signals:** lower-limb passive joint ROM, dorsiflexion ROM, knee extension/flexion ROM, eccentric-training tolerance.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context only; it cannot set static-stretching dose or claim static stretching caused eccentric-training effects.

**Limitations and mismatch:** Adjacent loaded intervention; healthy populations; lower-limb passive ROM only; mechanisms and dose optimization remain uncertain. Publisher PDF rights require permission. Population mismatch: Healthy lower-limb eccentric-training interventions, not at-home static stretching.

**Claim use:** `context-only`. Context-only boundary for eccentric resistance-training variants.
