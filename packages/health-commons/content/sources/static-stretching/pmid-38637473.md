This source is included for **adjacent_variant_boundaries**.

**Findings:** The review identified 42 studies and 1318 cumulative participants. Chronic static stretching showed small maximal-strength effects (d = 0.30, 95% CI 0.14 to 0.46, p < 0.001) and small hypertrophy effects (d = 0.20, 95% CI 0.08 to 0.32, p = 0.003). Longer durations and longer intervention periods were more favorable, while lower dosage often did not reach significance.

**Why it matters:** Draws a clear boundary: strength and hypertrophy findings are small and tied to high-volume stretching, not typical low-burden at-home flexibility routines.

**Potential experiment signals:** maximal strength, muscle size / hypertrophy, high-volume stretch burden, protocol feasibility.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context only for muscle-gain boundaries; do not promise hypertrophy from a short home flexibility routine.

**Limitations and mismatch:** Evidence certainty was low for overall strength and hypertrophy; average methodological quality was fair; adaptations were small relative to high training effort. Not a direct flexibility-protocol outcome source. Population mismatch: Targets strength/hypertrophy and often high-volume stretching, not low-burden flexibility-oriented at-home static stretching.

**Claim use:** `context-only`. Context-only boundary for high-volume static-stretching strength/hypertrophy claims.
