## Quick extraction
- **Source kind:** journal_article
- **Study design:** Acute within-subject static versus cyclic stretching comparison
- **Participants:** 20 (reported)
- **Population:** Healthy men
- **Intervention / exposure:** Static and cyclic gastrocnemius stretching
- **Comparator / control:** See study design; control or comparator condition where specified in findings.
- **Duration / follow-up:** Single session; 5-minute cycling warm-up plus 2-minute static stretching, 2-minute cyclic stretching, or control on separate days
- **Directness:** Adjacent mechanism evidence because it compares static and cyclic stretching acutely; not a home training trial.
- **Claim-use boundary:** context-only

## Findings
- Twenty healthy men completed control, static stretching, and cyclic stretching conditions.
- The static-stretch condition had significant effects for maximum ROM, muscle-tendon junction angle, and real-time elastography relative to cyclic stretching.
- Cyclic stretching produced higher normalized peak torque than static stretching in accessible article details.

## Why it matters
Separates static stretching from cyclic stretching and shows stiffness/hardness endpoints may differ by modality.

## Potential experiment signals

## Protocol takeaway
Use as mechanism context only; do not generalize cyclic-stretch strength or stiffness findings to static stretching.
