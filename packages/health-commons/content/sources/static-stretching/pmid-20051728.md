This source is included for **hamstring_sit_reach**.

**Findings:**
Static stretching showed benefit but was not the strongest comparator in this active-versus-static hamstring study. Endpoint: Active knee-extension range of motion and retention after stopping. Effect/direction: Static/passive stretching improved flexibility, but active stretching produced larger gains and better maintenance in accessible reporting.

**Why it matters:** Places static stretching in context against an active flexibility method, preserving mixed comparator evidence instead of overstating static superiority.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- Active knee-extension ROM
- Retention after stopping
- Adherence/completion

**Protocol takeaway:** Static stretching can improve hamstring flexibility, but this source should be used as context because an active technique outperformed it in the studied comparison.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Extraction notes:**
- Study design: Comparative trial of active stretching versus static/passive stretching with follow-up after cessation.
- Population: 65 enrolled; accessible abstract notes lower completion and follow-up counts.
- Intervention/exposure: Static/passive hamstring stretching.
- Comparator/control: Active stretching technique.
- Duration/follow-up: 6 weeks, with reassessment 4 weeks after training interruption.
- Endpoints: Active knee-extension range of motion
- Adverse events or safety notes: Adverse events were not reported in accessible abstract/metadata.
- Limitations: Comparator is an adjacent active-stretch technique; completion/follow-up attrition; not home-specific.
- Population mismatch: Adjacent to static home stretching because it compares static/passive stretching against active stretching rather than testing the target protocol alone.
