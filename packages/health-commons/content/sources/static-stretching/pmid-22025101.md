This source is included for **safety_guidelines_special_populations**.

**Findings:** In adults with chronic low back pain, yoga was more effective than a self-care book but not clearly more effective than stretching for function and symptoms.

**Why it matters:** Shows supervised stretching can be used clinically but should not be generalized to healthy at-home flexibility outcomes.

**Potential experiment signals:** back-related function, pain bothersomeness, adverse events, chronic low back pain symptoms.

**Safety notes:** Clinical low-back-pain users are not the default audience; stretching-related symptom worsening should be tracked and escalated if neurologic/radiating or worsening.

**Limitations:** Clinical pain population, class-based intervention, not an at-home flexibility protocol and not designed around ROM endpoints.

**Population mismatch:** Adults with chronic low back pain, not healthy users pursuing flexibility.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as clinical context only; do not cite it as proof that home static stretching improves flexibility in healthy adults.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.
