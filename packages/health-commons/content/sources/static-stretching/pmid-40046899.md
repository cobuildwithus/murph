## Quick extraction
- **Source kind:** journal_article
- **Study design:** Randomized controlled trial
- **Participants:** 44 (analyzed)
- **Population:** Asymptomatic adults aged 18–40 years in accessible article text
- **Intervention / exposure:** Seated unilateral static hamstring stretching
- **Comparator / control:** See study design; control or comparator condition where specified in findings.
- **Duration / follow-up:** 6 weeks; two sessions per week; static arm used seated unilateral hamstring stretch 2 × 30 seconds per extremity
- **Directness:** Direct lower-limb static-stretching protocol evidence with a low-burden dose, although it used asymptomatic adults and limb-level data.
- **Claim-use boundary:** supports-protocol

## Findings
- Forty-four participants remained after exclusions; the study treated 88 limbs as data points in accessible article text.
- The static-stretch group completed a seated unilateral hamstring stretch, 2 × 30 seconds per extremity, twice weekly for 6 weeks.
- Passive knee extension improved in the static-stretch and low-load eccentric groups versus control at 6 weeks; the static versus control mean difference was reported as 5.12° with effect size 0.79 and p < 0.001.
- The study design does not identify the mechanism of flexibility change.

## Why it matters
Highly relevant to at-home feasibility because the static-stretch arm used only two 30-second seated hamstring stretches twice weekly for 6 weeks.
