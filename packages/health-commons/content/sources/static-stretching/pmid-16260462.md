This source is included for **adverse_event_clinical_population_mismatch**.

**Findings:** The analysis reported exertional rhabdomyolysis after strenuous exercise was not uncommon in this setting, acute renal failure risk was low, and post-exercise stretching of involved extremities might increase risk. Source key: `source_artifact:pmid-16260462`.

**Adverse events or safety notes:** Exertional rhabdomyolysis is a serious safety context; stretching was one adjacent exposure and causality should not be overclaimed.

**Limitations and population mismatch:** Strenuous-exercise/rhabdomyolysis context, not routine home flexibility. Limitations: High-intensity school exercise/outbreak context.; Stretching was not a randomized stretching intervention..

**Why it matters:** It supports not stretching aggressively after unusually hard exercise or when systemic red flags appear.

**Potential experiment signals:** exertional rhabdomyolysis, acute renal failure risk, post-exercise stretching exposure.

**Protocol takeaway:** Do not use stretching to push through severe post-exercise pain or systemic symptoms.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`. Boundary: Use for red flags after unusually strenuous exercise: severe pain, swelling, weakness, dark urine, or systemic symptoms.
