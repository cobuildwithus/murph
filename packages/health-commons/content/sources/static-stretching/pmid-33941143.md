This source is included for **shoulder_thoracic_upper_body**.

**Findings:**
- **Dose/population:** 42 overhead athletes with GIRD were randomized to a novel stretch or modified sleeper stretch; the dose was 3 sessions/week for 4 weeks, 3 repetitions of 30-second holds with 30-second rests.
- **ROM:** Both groups improved shoulder internal-rotation ROM. The combined mean rose from 49.4 ± 14.1° at baseline to 63.1 ± 13.2° immediately post-test and 70.0 ± 12.7° at week 4. No group-by-time difference was reported for ROM.
- **Pain:** In the symptomatic subgroup, pain reduction was larger in the novel-stretch group than the modified-sleeper group, but the pain sample was small.
- **Safety/limits:** The paper states no reported harm or adverse effects. Limitations include heterogeneous sports, no no-treatment control, no long-term follow-up, and a small pain subgroup.

**Why it matters:** It provides detailed home/gym-feasible dosing, adherence support, and explicit no-harm reporting for shoulder static stretching in an athletic deficit population.

**Potential experiment signals:** internal-rotation ROM, pain rating, adherence log, adverse-effect check-in.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as supportive adjacent evidence for 3 x 30-second shoulder stretches, 3 times weekly for 4 weeks, with the caveat that no no-treatment control was included and athletes had GIRD.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

- **Claim-use boundary:** Use only as posterior-shoulder/GIRD, overhead-athlete, self-assisted stretching context; no-harm reporting in this small coached study is not general safety evidence.
