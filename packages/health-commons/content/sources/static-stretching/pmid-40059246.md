This source is included for **adjacent_variant_boundaries**.

**Findings:** The review screened 797 records after duplicates and included six controlled or randomized studies with 658 total participants. Interventions lasted 4 weeks to 6 months and used static or dynamic stretching. Five of six studies reported decreased pain scores or reduced pain prevalence/severity, but one lower-back-pain study found no added effect compared with other therapies/controls, and control groups often improved too.

**Why it matters:** Keeps pain-treatment claims separate from a general flexibility protocol while preserving evidence that stretching may affect pain in symptomatic contexts.

**Potential experiment signals:** pain score, pain prevalence/severity, stretch tolerance, range of motion, muscle stiffness.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context only; a general at-home flexibility protocol should not claim to treat pain without matching population, safety, and clinical guidance.

**Limitations and mismatch:** Only six studies; no meta-analysis because outcomes used heterogeneous scales/questionnaires; mixed static/dynamic stretching and symptomatic populations. Mechanisms remain uncertain. Population mismatch: Symptomatic pain populations and pain-prevalence studies; mixed static/dynamic stretching; not general healthy at-home flexibility training.

**Claim use:** `context-only`. Context-only boundary for pain-treatment claims and safety tracking.
