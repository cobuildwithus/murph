This source is included for **safety_guidelines_special_populations**.

**Findings:** Accessible summaries report support for therapeutic exercise and motor function training to improve function, well-being, and quality of life in G-HSD/hEDS, with additional research needed on effectiveness and dosage.

**Why it matters:** Reinforces that G-HSD/hEDS care involves therapeutic exercise and motor function training, not generic flexibility range chasing.

**Potential experiment signals:** function, well-being, quality of life, therapeutic exercise, motor function training, adaptive equipment, dose uncertainty.

**Safety notes:** Hypermobile users need condition-specific physical therapy framing and dose uncertainty acknowledgment.

**Limitations:** Scoping review; evidence and intervention dosage remain uncertain and not directly static-stretching focused.

**Population mismatch:** G-HSD/hEDS clinical population.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use for safety/referral boundaries, not for direct claims that static stretching improves flexibility in healthy users.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.
