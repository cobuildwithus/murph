This source is included for **region_specific_remote_measurement**.

**Findings:** Most online versus face-to-face tests did not differ significantly; excellent agreement was reported for chair sit-and-reach, while some tests such as arm curl and 8-foot up-and-go showed lower agreement.

**Why it matters:** This helps distinguish remote feasibility from unsupervised home validity.

**Potential experiment signals:** Chair sit-and-reach, Back scratch, Functional fitness tests, Balance tests, Online measurement validity.

**Protocol takeaway:** Remote measurement can work for some flexibility field tests, but protocol pages should flag supervision and clinical-population caveats.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Extraction boundaries:**

- Population: People with low back pain, mean age about 58 years in accessible summaries
- Intervention/exposure: Online supervised Senior Fitness Test and balance assessments
- Comparator/control: Face-to-face testing
- Duration/follow-up: Cross-sectional comparison of online and in-person assessments
- Adverse events or safety notes: No adverse events were extracted.
- Population mismatch: People with low back pain and supervised online testing; not healthy unsupervised home stretchers.
- Directness: Remote-measurement context with clinical population mismatch.

**Limitations:**

- Clinical low-back-pain population.
- Online supervised testing differs from unsupervised home measurement.
- Fitness battery includes flexibility but is not a stretching intervention.
