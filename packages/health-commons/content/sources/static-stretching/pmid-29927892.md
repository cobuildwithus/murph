This source is included for **hip_flexor_hip_extension**.

**Findings:**
- Twenty healthy active adults with limited passive hip extension completed a 3-week home-based static hip-flexor stretching program.
- Passive hip extension increased significantly after the stretching program (reported p<0.001).
- Active hip extension, anterior pelvic tilt, and lumbar extension during running did not improve clearly (reported p≥0.05).
- The study reported no relationship between passive hip-extension change and changes in running kinematics.

**Intervention/exposure:** Three-week home-based static hip-flexor stretching program.

**Comparator/control:** Pre-intervention baseline; running-kinematic relationships examined before/after.

**Duration/follow-up:** 3 weeks.

**Effect estimates or direction:** Passive hip extension increased (p<0.001 in available records); running hip extension, anterior pelvic tilt, and lumbar extension did not clearly improve (p≥0.05).

**Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse-event data were available in the extracted abstract/indexed record.

**Why it matters:** This is directly relevant to an at-home flexibility protocol because the intervention was a home-based hip-flexor static-stretching program and the endpoint was passive hip extension.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- Passive hip-extension range of motion
- Active hip extension during running
- Anterior pelvic tilt during running
- Lumbar extension during running

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as direct evidence that a short home hip-flexor static-stretching block can improve passive hip-extension mobility, while avoiding claims that passive gains automatically change running kinematics.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

**Directness:** `direct_protocol`. Supports passive hip-extension ROM claims only; dynamic running-kinematic effects should be treated as null/mixed.

**Population mismatch:** Active adults/runners with limited passive hip extension; not a general adult population and not a clinical pain cohort.

**Limitations:**
- Small sample (n=20).
- Evidence was identified from abstract/indexed records rather than fully extracted full text.
- Healthy active adults/runners do not represent all home users.
- Running-kinematics outcomes may require training or motor-control work beyond stretching.
