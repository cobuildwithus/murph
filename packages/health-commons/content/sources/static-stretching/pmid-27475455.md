This source is included for **shoulder_thoracic_upper_body**.

**Findings:**
- **Population/dose:** 24 college baseball players with posterior shoulder tightness were randomized to modified cross-body or modified sleeper stretching for 4 weeks; the reported dose was 3 daily repetitions of 30-second holds.
- **Outcomes:** Both groups significantly increased shoulder internal-rotation and horizontal-adduction ROM.
- **Mechanism/stiffness:** Teres minor stiffness decreased in the modified cross-body group, while infraspinatus stiffness decreased in the modified sleeper group.
- **Limits/safety:** Small athletic sample, no no-stretch control, and one nonthrowing-shoulder injury during baseball practice was an exclusion event rather than an attributed stretch adverse event.

**Why it matters:** It extends posterior-shoulder static stretching beyond immediate response into a four-week dose while adding muscle-stiffness endpoints.

**Potential experiment signals:** shoulder internal rotation, horizontal adduction/cross-body reach, soreness or injury notes, weekly ROM trend.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as supportive evidence for repeated 30-second posterior-shoulder stretching over several weeks, while noting that both tested variants improved ROM and each was linked to different stiffness changes.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.
