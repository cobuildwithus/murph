This source is included for **hamstring_sit_reach**.

**Findings:**
Standing and supine static hamstring stretches appear similarly effective for improving hamstring flexibility over 3 weeks. Endpoint: Hamstring flexibility. Effect/direction: Both positions significantly improved flexibility; no significant difference between standing and supine stretch positions.

**Why it matters:** Supports offering a home-friendly choice of standing or supine hamstring stretches when the goal is improved flexibility.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- Hamstring range of motion
- Ease of stretch setup
- Adherence by preferred position

**Protocol takeaway:** Standing and supine options can both be included; position choice may be guided by comfort, balance, and safety rather than presumed superiority.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

**Extraction notes:**
- Study design: Randomized trial comparing standing versus supine hamstring stretch positions, with limbs assigned to positions.
- Population: 29 healthy participants with limited bilateral hamstring flexibility.
- Intervention/exposure: Standing static hamstring stretch; accessible reporting indicates 3 sets of 30 seconds, 3 days/week for 3 weeks.
- Comparator/control: Supine static hamstring stretch.
- Duration/follow-up: Three weeks.
- Endpoints: Hamstring flexibility
- Adverse events or safety notes: Adverse events were not reported in accessible abstract/metadata.
- Limitations: Small sample; healthy limited-flexibility participants; position comparison rather than no-stretch efficacy trial.
- Population mismatch: Direct to home static hamstring stretching but not a full-body protocol.
