This source is included for **shoulder_thoracic_upper_body**.

**Findings:**
- **Population/intervention:** 28 participants with short latissimus dorsi muscle were randomized to two latissimus dorsi stretching positions.
- **Outcomes:** The accessible abstract reports significant within-group increases in shoulder flexion angle and middle thoracic mobility and decreases in latissimus dorsi tone/stiffness in both groups.
- **Comparative result:** No between-group difference was reported for the two stretching positions.
- **Limits/safety:** Dose details and adverse-event reporting were not extractable from the accessible abstract; population is selected for short latissimus dorsi rather than general home users.

**Why it matters:** This is one of the more protocol-adjacent upper-body static stretching trials because it targets shoulder flexion and thoracic mobility, not only sport-specific internal-rotation deficits.

**Potential experiment signals:** shoulder-flexion angle, overhead-reach comfort, thoracic-rotation or thoracic-mobility tape measure, perceived stretch tolerance.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as supportive but cautious direct evidence that upper-body static stretching can improve shoulder-flexion and thoracic-mobility measures in a selected short-latissimus population; do not claim one tested position is superior.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.
