This source is included for **safety_guidelines_special_populations**.

**Findings:** Physical therapy is described as central to JHS/hEDS management, with attention to diagnosis, presentation, and best treatment approaches rather than generic flexibility stretching.

**Why it matters:** Important because diagnosed hypermobility should not be treated as simply needing more range of motion.

**Potential experiment signals:** clinical assessment, individualization, joint control, stability, pain, function.

**Safety notes:** Hypermobility programs should prioritize assessment, stability, control, and individualized care; end-range lax-joint stretching may be inappropriate.

**Limitations:** Expert/guideline review; not a direct at-home healthy-adult static stretching trial.

**Population mismatch:** Diagnosed JHS/hEDS clinical population, not general flexibility users.

**Protocol takeaway:** For diagnosed or suspected hypermobility syndromes, route the user toward clinician-guided stability work rather than generic static stretching.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.
