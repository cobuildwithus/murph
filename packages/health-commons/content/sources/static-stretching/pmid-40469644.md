This source is included for **hip_flexor_hip_extension**.

**Findings:**
- Twenty-three analyzed participants contributed 46 limbs after exclusions/outliers.
- The intervention used five 30-second lunge-and-reach stretches per leg daily for 6 weeks, with reported compliance around 79%.
- Modified Thomas Test change was greater in the intervention group than control (5.92±3.73° versus 2.59±4.62°; reported p=0.01; mean difference 3.33°, 95% CI 0.84 to 5.81).
- Single-leg broad-jump change favored the intervention, but prone hip-extension strength and single-leg bridge endurance did not significantly differ.

**Intervention/exposure:** Daily bilateral lunge-and-reach hip-flexor stretching: five 30-second stretches per leg.

**Comparator/control:** Control group.

**Duration/follow-up:** 6 weeks.

**Effect estimates or direction:** Modified Thomas Test change favored intervention (5.92±3.73° vs 2.59±4.62°, p=0.01; mean difference 3.33°, 95% CI 0.84–5.81); broad jump improved but hip-extension strength and bridge endurance did not significantly differ.

**Adverse events/safety notes:** No discrete adverse-event count was extracted; compliance was reported around 79%.

**Why it matters:** It provides directly translatable home-stretching dose information: daily bilateral lunge-and-reach stretches, five 30-second holds per leg, over 6 weeks.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- Modified Thomas Test hip-extension angle
- Single-leg broad-jump distance
- Prone hip-extension strength
- Single-leg bridge endurance
- Self-reported compliance

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as direct evidence that a daily lunge-and-reach hip-flexor stretch can improve Modified Thomas Test flexibility over 6 weeks in young healthy adults; do not overstate glute strength/endurance effects.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

**Directness:** `direct_protocol`. Supports flexibility changes from a specific daily lunge-and-reach routine; performance and strength outcomes are mixed.

**Population mismatch:** Healthy college-aged adults; not older adults, clinical pain populations, or general sedentary users.

**Limitations:**
- Healthy college-aged population only.
- Control participants’ activity routines were not monitored.
- Strength testing method changed during the study.
- Endurance test was not isolated to gluteus maximus.
- The article reports limb-level analyses, so participant and limb counts should not be conflated.
