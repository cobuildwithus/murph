This source is included for **direct protocol and dose evidence**.

**Findings:** After 48-hour starvation, whole-body glucose disposal during clamp decreased from 39.8 to 24.1 µmol/kg/min, glucose oxidation decreased from 21.8 to 3.9 µmol/kg/min, forearm glucose uptake fell, and the net thermogenic response was diminished.

**Why it matters:** Foundational direct 48-hour evidence for acute insulin-resistance and thermogenesis boundaries.

**Potential experiment signals:** whole-body glucose disposal, glucose oxidation, forearm glucose uptake, thermogenic response, ketone/protein-adjusted gas exchange.

**Protocol takeaway:** A 48-hour fast can reduce insulin-stimulated glucose disposal and thermogenic response; this is a physiologic adaptation, not an efficacy endpoint.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

**Study design and directness:** Small within-person acute physiology study using hyperinsulinemic clamp.. Directness to Prolonged Fasting (24–72 Hours): `direct_protocol`.

**Comparator/control:** Fed-state clamp condition in the same participants

**Duration/follow-up:** 48-hour starvation with clamp/thermogenesis assessment

**Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse-event table extracted; insulin resistance and reduced thermogenic response are safety-relevant interpretation boundaries.

**Limitations:** Small male-only mechanistic sample; older methods; no clinical outcomes.

**Population mismatch:** Non-diabetic normal-weight men only; not directly generalizable to women, obesity, diabetes, or medication-treated populations.
