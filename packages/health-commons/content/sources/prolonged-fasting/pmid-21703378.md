This source is included for **eating-disorder and restriction-risk boundary**.

**Findings:** In Project EAT, dieting and disordered-eating behaviors tracked from adolescence into young adulthood over 10 years.

**Why it matters:** Longitudinal adolescence-to-young-adulthood evidence on dieting/disordered eating persistence; supports conservative boundaries for youth and young adults.

**Potential experiment signals:** disordered eating behaviors, restrictive behavior, binge/restrict cycles.

**Protocol takeaway:** Avoid normalizing fasting experiments for adolescents or people with persistent dieting/ED behavior histories.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness and caveat:** Dieting and weight-control behaviors broadly, not 24–72-hour prolonged fasting. Broad dieting behavior evidence, not a fasting intervention.
