This source is included for **FMD/VLCD and modified-fasting boundary**.

**Findings:** The two-day zero-calorie condition increased fat oxidation more than the VLCD condition; both restriction conditions increased hunger/prospective food consumption, with no detected inhibitory-control effect and lower vigor in the zero-calorie condition.

**Why it matters:** This is one of the few batch records with a true two-day zero-calorie arm, but the source was extracted in the VLCD boundary batch because its central question is calorie-restriction comparison.

**Potential experiment signals:** biomarker:fat-oxidation, symptom:hunger, mood:vigor, cognitive:inhibitory-control.

**Protocol takeaway:** Treat as partially direct mechanistic/boundary evidence for a 48-hour zero-calorie condition, not as an outcomes trial proving clinical benefit of the 24–72 hour protocol.

**Directness and limitations:** Directness is `adjacent_variant`. Small young-adult crossover study; short-term substrate and subjective endpoints; no longer clinical follow-up. Population mismatch: Overweight/obese young adults under a comparison protocol, not a broad unsupervised community fast.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
