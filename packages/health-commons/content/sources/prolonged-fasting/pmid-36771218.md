This source is included for **direct protocol and dose evidence**.

**Findings:** After 36 hours versus 12 hours of fasting, the study reported improved insulin sensitivity with higher glucose variation and reduced early insulin response in non-obese people; obesity and type 2 diabetes groups showed attenuated or different metabolic responses.

**Why it matters:** Direct 36-hour fasting evidence with important population-mismatch and phenotype-difference information.

**Potential experiment signals:** OGTT glucose, OGTT insulin, IVGTT/metabolomics, insulin sensitivity, glucose variability, metabolic response by phenotype.

**Protocol takeaway:** 36-hour fasting can improve insulin-sensitivity measures but may increase glucose variation and reduce early insulin response in non-obese participants; obesity/T2D responses were attenuated/different.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

**Study design and directness:** Prospective cohort trial with metabolic testing at 12h and 36h fasting states.. Directness to Prolonged Fasting (24–72 Hours): `direct_protocol`.

**Comparator/control:** 12-hour fasting condition and group comparisons across obesity/T2D/non-obese cohorts

**Duration/follow-up:** 36-hour versus 12-hour fasting comparison

**Adverse events or safety notes:** Population includes type 2 diabetes; protocol use should flag medication/hypoglycemia risk even though adverse-event counts were not extracted.

**Limitations:** Cohort trial and phenotype comparison; not a healthy-only RCT; exact clinical safety outcomes not extracted.

**Population mismatch:** Includes obesity and type 2 diabetes, which are clinically important but mismatch a general healthy-adult protocol.
