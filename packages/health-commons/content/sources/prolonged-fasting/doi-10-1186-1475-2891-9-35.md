This source is included for **FMD/VLCD and modified-fasting boundary**.

**Findings:** In 16 obese completers, modified alternate-day fasting produced weight loss and maintained physical activity; fast-day intake was about one-quarter of energy needs rather than zero calories.

**Why it matters:** Shows how a low-calorie “fast day” differs operationally from a water-only 24–72 hour fast and why appetite/adherence logging matters.

**Potential experiment signals:** biomarker:body-weight, behavior:fast-day-adherence, behavior:physical-activity, symptom:hunger.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as adjacent modified-fasting context only; do not cite as proof that a zero-calorie 24–72 hour fast causes the same weight, appetite, or activity effects.

**Directness and limitations:** Directness is `adjacent_variant`. Small pilot, completer sample, self-reported intake and physical activity, and not a zero-calorie prolonged fast. Population mismatch: Adults with obesity in a repeated alternate-day fasting program, not a single 24–72 hour water-only fast.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
