This source is included for **snowball/gap-fill candidates**.

**Findings:**
- `finding:pmid-12748412-modified-fasting-sleep-pilot` — The pilot reported significant decreases in arousals and periodic limb movements, a non-significant REM increase, improved subjective ratings of sleep quality, daytime concentration, vigour, and emotional balance, and weight decrease from 66.5 ± 11.7 kg to 63.0 ± 11.9 kg among completers.
- `finding:pmid-12748412-magnesium-melatonin-decrease` — The study reported a serum magnesium decrease and a moderate decrease in urinary melatonin excretion, making this an adjacent safety/measurement signal rather than a simple positive sleep claim.

**Why it matters:** This is a small adjacent-variant study that suggests sleep and daytime-function signals may be worth tracking during longer modified fasting, while also flagging magnesium/melatonin changes.

**Potential experiment signals:** sleep arousals, periodic limb movements, REM sleep, subjective sleep quality, daytime concentration, serum magnesium, urinary melatonin, body weight.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as adjacent evidence for sleep-signal selection, not as direct evidence for water-only 24–72 hour fasting or for efficacy.

**Directness to Prolonged Fasting (24–72 Hours):** `adjacent_variant`.

**Population mismatch:** Non-obese healthy volunteers on a one-week modified fast are adjacent but not the same as 24–72 hour prolonged fasting.

**Limitations:** Small open pilot, no randomized control, modified-fast exposure longer than 72 hours, mostly female completers, and home-based conditions may differ from Murph target.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
