This source is included for **eating-disorder and restriction-risk boundary**.

**Findings:** In 122 participants monitored for 96 hours, a 24-hour fast did not significantly increase pathological eating behaviors, while baseline disinhibition predicted eating episodes around the fast.

**Why it matters:** Important mixed/null acute-fasting source that can prevent overclaiming while still informing caution around short-term fasts and eating pathology.

**Potential experiment signals:** binge/restrict cycles, pathological eating patterns, restrictive behavior.

**Protocol takeaway:** Do not overstate risk for every participant; use baseline disinhibition/binge risk to gate participation.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

**Directness and caveat:** Single 24-hour experimental fast is protocol-adjacent but not a full 24–72-hour repeated or unsupervised prolonged-fasting protocol. Short-term study; not evidence that 24–72-hour prolonged fasting is safe for people with ED risk.
