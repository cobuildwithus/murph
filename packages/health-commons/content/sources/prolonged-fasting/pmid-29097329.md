This source is included for **direct protocol and dose evidence**.

**Findings:** The randomized crossover study in overweight women reported that two-day fasting evoked stress but did not affect mood, brain activity, cognitive, psychomotor, or motor performance in the tested setting.

**Why it matters:** Direct two-day human fasting tolerability/performance source with a mixed signal: stress response without broad performance impairment.

**Potential experiment signals:** stress, mood, brain activity, cognitive performance, psychomotor performance, motor performance.

**Protocol takeaway:** A 48-hour fast may increase stress while not necessarily impairing tested cognitive or motor performance in this narrow sample.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

**Study design and directness:** Randomized crossover behavioral/physiology study.. Directness to Prolonged Fasting (24–72 Hours): `direct_protocol`.

**Comparator/control:** Usual-diet/control condition in randomized crossover design

**Duration/follow-up:** Two-day fast

**Adverse events or safety notes:** Stress response is safety/tolerability-relevant; detailed adverse-event counts were not extracted.

**Limitations:** Overweight women only; exact fasting implementation and effect estimates should be checked from the article before synthesis.

**Population mismatch:** Overweight female sample; may not generalize to men, normal-weight adults, diabetes, athletes, or high-risk settings.
