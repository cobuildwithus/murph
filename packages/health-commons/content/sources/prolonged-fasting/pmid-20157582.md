This source is included for **Clinical/residential supervised fasting boundary**.

**Findings:** In a 10-patient case series, fasting around chemotherapy was reported as tolerable and associated with reduced self-reported side effects in some cycles; no controlled efficacy estimate is available.

**Why it matters:** Early human oncology fasting evidence often cited; extraction keeps it as case-series safety/tolerability context.

**Potential experiment signals:** fatigue, GI symptoms, weakness, adverse events.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use only as oncology case-series context; not direct protocol evidence.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Cancer patients receiving chemotherapy.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Fasting 48–140 hours before and/or 5–56 hours after chemotherapy.
- **Comparator/control:** Within-person comparison to chemotherapy cycles without fasting in some cases.
- **Duration/follow-up:** 48–140 hours before chemotherapy and/or 5–56 hours after chemotherapy.
- **Endpoints:** chemotherapy side effects, fatigue, weakness, gastrointestinal symptoms, treatment efficacy signals
- **Effect estimate or direction:** Case reports described tolerability and reduced self-reported side effects in some fasting cycles; no evidence of reduced chemotherapy efficacy was reported.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** Fasting was reported as well tolerated in the case series.
- **Limitations:** Case series, self-selection, oncology co-intervention, no controlled efficacy estimate.
- **Population mismatch:** Cancer/chemotherapy population; not healthy prolonged fasting.
