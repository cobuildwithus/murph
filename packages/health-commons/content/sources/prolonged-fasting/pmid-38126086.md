This source is included for **direct protocol and dose evidence**.

**Findings:** The study concluded that acute 24-hour fasting reduces tolerance to progressive central hypovolemia by blunting increases in peripheral resistance compared with a 3-hour postprandial state.

**Why it matters:** Direct safety-physiology source for presyncope, dehydration, blood donation, heat, and strenuous-activity cautions during fasting.

**Potential experiment signals:** central hypovolemia tolerance, lower-body negative pressure tolerance, blood pressure, peripheral resistance, presyncope/orthostatic symptoms, ketones.

**Protocol takeaway:** A 24-hour fast may reduce tolerance to blood-volume stress; avoid combining fasting with dehydration, blood donation, hot environments, or orthostatic stress without caution.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

**Study design and directness:** Acute human safety-physiology study.. Directness to Prolonged Fasting (24–72 Hours): `direct_protocol`.

**Comparator/control:** 3-hour postprandial/fed condition

**Duration/follow-up:** 24-hour fast before acute hypovolemia challenge

**Adverse events or safety notes:** Safety-relevant physiologic intolerance to central hypovolemia/presyncope risk; not an adverse-event rate source for typical fasting settings.

**Limitations:** Laboratory lower-body-negative-pressure challenge; healthy young adults; not a field study of fasting adverse events.

**Population mismatch:** Healthy young adults; hypovolemia challenge is an induced stressor, not ordinary fasting, but relevant to dehydration/orthostatic boundaries.
