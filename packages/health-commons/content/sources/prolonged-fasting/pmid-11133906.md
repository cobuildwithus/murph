This source is included for **direct protocol and dose evidence**.

**Findings:** After a 72-hour fast versus a 13-hour fast, postprandial carbohydrate oxidation was significantly reduced and fat oxidation increased, while glucose and insulin excursions were significantly greater, indicating decreased glucose tolerance after the prolonged fast.

**Why it matters:** Direct upper-bound protocol evidence for refeeding/metabolic-response claims; importantly, it preserves a mixed/negative glucose-tolerance signal.

**Potential experiment signals:** postprandial carbohydrate oxidation, postprandial fat oxidation, glucose and insulin excursions, free fatty acids, whole-room indirect calorimetry.

**Protocol takeaway:** A 72-hour fast can produce large substrate shifts but may transiently impair mixed-meal glucose tolerance during refeeding.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

**Study design and directness:** Acute human physiology study comparing 72-hour versus overnight fast before standardized meal.. Directness to Prolonged Fasting (24–72 Hours): `direct_protocol`.

**Comparator/control:** 13-hour overnight fast followed by the same mixed meal

**Duration/follow-up:** 72-hour fast with 12-hour post-meal observation

**Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse-event table was extracted; the key safety-relevant finding is transiently worse glucose tolerance at refeeding.

**Limitations:** Small male-only sample and laboratory calorimetry setting; upper-edge 72-hour fast rather than 24–48 hours.

**Population mismatch:** Nonobese men only; may not generalize to women, older adults, obesity, diabetes, medication use, or unsupervised fasting.
