This source is included for **eating-disorder and restriction-risk boundary**.

**Findings:** A meta-analysis of 32 studies (N=63,181) estimated that 22.36% of children/adolescents screened positive for disordered eating by SCOFF, with higher prevalence among girls, older adolescents, and higher BMI.

**Why it matters:** Provides background prevalence context showing disordered eating is common enough in youth to warrant conservative exclusion language.

**Potential experiment signals:** adolescent safety boundaries, disordered eating prevalence, screening and contraindications.

**Protocol takeaway:** Adolescent participation needs a conservative exclusion/referral boundary.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness and caveat:** General youth prevalence context, not a fasting intervention. Prevalence context, not fasting-specific evidence.
