This source is included for **safety and adverse events**.

**Findings:** The meta-analysis suggested IF was not associated with a greater overall risk of adverse events in adults with overweight or obesity. A numerically higher dizziness risk was noted in a subgroup, but the result did not clearly establish a higher risk.

**Why it matters:** Current AE-focused synthesis preserving null and dizziness-signal uncertainty.

**Potential experiment signals:** fatigue, headache, dizziness, dropout, adverse events

**Protocol takeaway:** Use for adjacent IF safety context only; preserve no-clear-increase result and dizziness caveat.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

**Directness to Prolonged Fasting (24–72 Hours):** adjacent_variant.

**Population and exposure:** Adults with overweight or obesity in randomized controlled trials of intermittent fasting. Exposure/intervention: Intermittent fasting variants compared with control diets or other active comparators. Comparator/control: Control groups in included RCTs. Duration/follow-up: Varied trial durations and follow-up..

**Effect direction or estimates:** The meta-analysis concluded IF was not associated with a greater risk of adverse events overall, while noting a numerically higher dizziness risk in a subgroup.

**Adverse events or safety notes:** Fatigue, headache, dizziness, and dropout were extracted; dizziness signal was numerically higher in one subgroup.

**Limitations:** Adjacent IF/TRE variants, overweight/obesity populations, heterogeneous regimens; not water-only 24-72 hour fasting.

**Population mismatch:** Adjacent intermittent fasting variants, often time-restricted eating or modified regimens, not the target protocol.
