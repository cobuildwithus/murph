This source is included for **eating-disorder and restriction-risk boundary**.

**Findings:** Among low-carbohydrate dieters, the study linked restrictive carbohydrate dieting and associated IF with cognitive restraint and food craving signals.

**Why it matters:** Useful for combined-restriction contexts where users pair fasting with carbohydrate avoidance, potentially increasing restrictive or craving-driven cycles.

**Potential experiment signals:** food craving, restrictive behavior, binge/restrict cycles, eating disorder symptomatology.

**Protocol takeaway:** Discourage stacking prolonged fasting with other restrictive diet rules in self-experiments.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

**Directness and caveat:** Low-carbohydrate dieting and associated IF, not stand-alone 24–72-hour prolonged fasting. This is a combined restriction context, not direct prolonged-fasting evidence.
