This source is included for **Clinical/residential supervised fasting boundary**.

**Findings:** In 1610 participants fasting 4–41 days, observational data reported BP decreases, especially in hypertensive subgroups, with frequent antihypertensive medication changes; this is supervised long-duration evidence.

**Why it matters:** Large clinical-program BP source that strongly reinforces medication/supervision caveats.

**Potential experiment signals:** morning blood pressure, medication reduction/cessation, weight, adverse events.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use for supervised BP-treatment boundary and medication caveats only.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Adults with and without hypertension, including medicated and non-medicated hypertensive subgroups.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Long-term Buchinger-style fasting program around 250 kcal/day.
- **Comparator/control:** Within-person before/after and subgroup comparisons.
- **Duration/follow-up:** 4–41 fasting days, mean about 10 days; exceeds 72 hours.
- **Endpoints:** blood pressure, antihypertensive medication use, body weight, safety
- **Effect estimate or direction:** Reported blood-pressure decreases overall and larger decreases in hypertensive subgroups; low-BP subgroup increased toward normal.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** Medication changes were common; safety requires clinical context/monitoring.
- **Limitations:** Observational, long duration, medically supervised clinical program, possible medication confounding.
- **Population mismatch:** Long clinical fasting program, not 24–72 hour self-directed fasting.
- **Directness to Prolonged Fasting (24–72 Hours):** clinical_supervised
