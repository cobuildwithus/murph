This source is included for **safety and adverse events**.

**Findings:** The guidance states that starvation-related metabolic changes may lead to severe, irreversible, or fatal complications. The guidance highlights refeeding syndrome as a potentially fatal risk that should not be trivialized.

**Why it matters:** Boundary guidance for monitoring, capacity, hydration, and refeeding risk.

**Potential experiment signals:** dehydration, electrolytes, refeeding syndrome, capacity, monitoring

**Protocol takeaway:** Use only as safety-boundary guidance; not as evidence for wellness fasting benefits.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

**Directness to Prolonged Fasting (24–72 Hours):** general_guideline.

**Population and exposure:** Detainees or prisoners undertaking hunger strikes, and clinicians providing care. Exposure/intervention: Clinical monitoring and management of total food refusal/hunger strike, including refeeding. Comparator/control: Not applicable. Duration/follow-up: Primarily prolonged food refusal beyond short-term fasting..

**Effect direction or estimates:** Guidance emphasizes that starvation can cause severe, irreversible, or fatal complications and that refeeding syndrome can be fatal.

**Adverse events or safety notes:** Starvation complications, dehydration and electrolyte risks, and refeeding syndrome.

**Limitations:** Guideline/expert practice source; detainee hunger-strike context differs from voluntary wellness fasting.

**Population mismatch:** High contextual mismatch: detention, coercion, legal/ethical constraints, and often longer fasts.
