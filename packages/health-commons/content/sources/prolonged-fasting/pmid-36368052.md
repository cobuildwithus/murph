This source is included for **eating-disorder and restriction-risk boundary**.

**Findings:** In a national Canadian adolescent/young-adult survey (N=2,762), self-reported intermittent fasting was associated with eating-disorder psychopathology, with behavior-specific associations varying by gender.

**Why it matters:** Large youth/young-adult observational record directly linking intermittent fasting engagement with eating-disorder behaviors and psychopathology; useful for onboarding screens and referral language, not efficacy.

**Potential experiment signals:** restrictive behavior, binge/restrict cycles, eating disorder psychopathology, screening and contraindications.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as a behavioral screening and monitoring boundary before any fasting experiment.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

**Directness and caveat:** Intermittent fasting in Canadian adolescents and young adults, not a supervised 24–72-hour prolonged-fast intervention. Association does not establish causality and the exposure was intermittent fasting rather than 24–72-hour prolonged fasting.
