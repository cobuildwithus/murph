This source is included for **metabolic timecourse and mechanisms**.

**Findings:** Accessible abstract text reported resting energy expenditure rising from 3.97 +/- 0.9 to 4.53 +/- 0.9 kJ/min by day 3, norepinephrine rising from 1716 +/- 574 to 3728 +/- 1636 pmol/L by day 4, glucose declining from 4.9 +/- 0.5 to 3.5 +/- 0.5 mmol/L, and no significant insulin change.

**Why it matters:** Shows that energy expenditure and catecholamines can rise during the 48-72 hour window despite falling glucose.

**Potential experiment signals:** resting energy expenditure, norepinephrine, glucose, insulin

**Protocol takeaway:** Metabolic rate need not fall immediately during short-term starvation; catecholamine response can confound perceived energy and stress signals.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

**Extraction boundaries:** Directness: `partly_direct_protocol`. Population mismatch: Healthy lean adults may not generalize to older adults, obesity, diabetes, or medication users. Limitations: Small study; 84-hour fast exceeds the 72-hour protocol upper bound; physiologic surrogate outcomes only. Safety notes: No adverse-event signal extracted from the available abstract text.
