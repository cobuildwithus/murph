This source is included for **metabolic timecourse and mechanisms**.

**Findings:** Accessible article/search text reported no grade 3 toxicities attributed to fasting, fasting-related toxicities limited to <= grade 2 and most commonly fatigue, headache, and dizziness, and feasibility/safety up to 72 hours around chemotherapy; correlative signals were preliminary and uncontrolled.

**Why it matters:** The duration overlaps the protocol but the clinical context makes it a safety-boundary source, not an efficacy source.

**Potential experiment signals:** fatigue, headache, dizziness, weight regain, beta-hydroxybutyrate, IGF-1, neutropenia

**Protocol takeaway:** Use only as supervised oncology safety context; do not generalize to healthy self-experimentation efficacy.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

**Extraction boundaries:** Directness: `clinical_supervised_boundary`. Population mismatch: Oncology patients receiving chemotherapy are a supervised clinical population; findings do not establish safety for unsupervised general fasting or for all medical conditions. Limitations: Cancer/chemotherapy setting, small nonrandomized dose-escalation design, no usual-diet control, and confounding by chemotherapy regimens. Safety notes: Fasting-related toxicities were reported as <= grade 2, most commonly fatigue, headache, and dizziness; one subject failed to regain fasting-associated weight before the next chemotherapy cycle in accessible article text.
