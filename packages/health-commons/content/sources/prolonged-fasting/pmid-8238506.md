This source is included for **direct protocol and dose evidence**.

**Findings:** In young adult men, short-term fasting progressively altered lipid and glucose metabolism, with adipose tissue triglyceride mobilization increasing markedly between 18 and 24 hours.

**Why it matters:** Direct time-course source for when the protocol begins crossing from overnight fasting into prolonged fasting physiology.

**Potential experiment signals:** glucose and insulin, free fatty acids, lipolysis, substrate oxidation, adipose tissue triglyceride mobilization, time course.

**Protocol takeaway:** The 18–24-hour transition is metabolically meaningful, with increasing adipose triglyceride mobilization and substrate-shift dynamics.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

**Study design and directness:** Acute human metabolism time-course study.. Directness to Prolonged Fasting (24–72 Hours): `direct_protocol`.

**Comparator/control:** Serial time points during fasting

**Duration/follow-up:** Short-term fasting with serial lipid and glucose metabolism measurements; extracted candidate emphasizes transition around and beyond 24 hours

**Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse-event data extracted.

**Limitations:** Older male-only physiology time-course; no clinical outcomes or long-duration safety data.

**Population mismatch:** Young adult men only; results may differ in women, older adults, obesity, diabetes, or athletes.
