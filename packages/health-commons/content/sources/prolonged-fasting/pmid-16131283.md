This source is included for **Clinical/residential supervised fasting boundary**.

**Findings:** In the fasting subgroup, 743 patients (78%) reported improved health; fasting was described as well tolerated with no serious fasting-related adverse effects reported.

**Why it matters:** Provides a large clinical-program safety/tolerability signal and emphasizes medical-ward context.

**Potential experiment signals:** adverse events, quality of life, symptom improvement, lifestyle adherence.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use to support supervised-program safety boundary and patient-reported outcomes only.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Inpatients in an integrative medicine ward who underwent fasting therapy.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Fasting therapy incorporated into an inpatient integrative medicine program.
- **Comparator/control:** Nonfasting ward patients or internal comparison, depending on endpoint.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Ward-based fasting therapy with 3- and 6-month follow-up for lifestyle adherence outcomes.
- **Endpoints:** patient-reported health improvement, quality of life, complaint improvement, lifestyle adherence, adverse events
- **Effect estimate or direction:** Fasting was reported as well tolerated; 743 fasting patients (78%) reported health improvement; no serious fasting-related adverse effects were reported.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No serious fasting-related adverse effects reported in the abstract-level extraction.
- **Limitations:** Observational clinical ward context; total fasting subgroup count not encoded here because only the responder count/percentage was extracted.
- **Population mismatch:** Inpatient integrative medicine patients, not a healthy 24–72 hour community protocol.
- **Directness to Prolonged Fasting (24–72 Hours):** clinical_supervised
