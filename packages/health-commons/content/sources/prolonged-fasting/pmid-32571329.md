This source is included for **Clinical/residential supervised fasting boundary**.

**Findings:** In a 30-patient cross-over pilot, modified short-term fasting around chemotherapy reported toxicity/tolerance signals; fasting discomfort included hunger, dizziness, weakness, and headache, generally described as low grade.

**Why it matters:** Adds oncology toxicity/tolerability evidence and explicitly captures fasting discomfort as safety signal.

**Potential experiment signals:** toxicity grading, hunger, dizziness, weakness, headache.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as oncology safety boundary; not direct protocol support.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Gynecologic cancer patients receiving chemotherapy.
- **Intervention/exposure:** 96-hour modified short-term fasting, sometimes preceded by a ketogenic diet.
- **Comparator/control:** Normocaloric diet chemotherapy cycles and ketogenic-diet support comparison.
- **Duration/follow-up:** 96-hour modified fasting around chemotherapy; exceeds 72 hours.
- **Endpoints:** chemotherapy-induced toxicities, fasting discomfort, compliance, body composition, blood markers
- **Effect estimate or direction:** Pilot reported reduced chemotherapy-induced toxicities/tolerance signals; ketogenic support did not clearly improve fasting discomfort/compliance.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** Fasting-related discomfort included hunger, dizziness, weakness, and headache in extracted summaries; side effects were low grade.
- **Limitations:** Pilot oncology cross-over; modified fasting not water-only; duration exceeds protocol.
- **Population mismatch:** Cancer/chemotherapy population.
