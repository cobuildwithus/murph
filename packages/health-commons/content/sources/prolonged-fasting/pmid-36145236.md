This source is included for **safety and adverse events**.

**Findings:** The study reported a glucose-to-ketone substrate switch and stated that adults could tolerate the 10-day complete fast under supervision. Extracted text reports a slight decrease in kidney function and significant sodium/chloride decreases during the 10-day complete fast.

**Why it matters:** Adjacent supervised longer fast with electrolyte, kidney, and homeostasis markers.

**Potential experiment signals:** metabolic homeostasis, ketones, lipids, liver function, kidney function, sodium, chloride, tolerability

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as longer-fast physiology/safety boundary, especially electrolyte and kidney markers.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

**Directness to Prolonged Fasting (24–72 Hours):** adjacent_variant.

**Population and exposure:** Male adults in a controlled health research building under medical supervision. Exposure/intervention: 10-day complete fasting with baseline, calorie restriction, and full-recovery periods. Comparator/control: Within-person baseline and recovery periods. Duration/follow-up: 3-day baseline, 10-day complete fast, 4-day calorie restriction, 5-day full recovery..

**Effect direction or estimates:** Extracted text reports a glucose-to-ketone substrate switch by days 3-6, no liver-function influence, slight kidney-function decrease, significant sodium/chloride decreases, and tolerability of 10-day complete fasting.

**Adverse events or safety notes:** Slight kidney-function decrease and sodium/chloride decreases are safety-relevant.

**Limitations:** Small male-only supervised cohort; duration exceeds 72 hours; no parallel control.

**Population mismatch:** Adjacent longer supervised complete fast, not 24-72 hours.
