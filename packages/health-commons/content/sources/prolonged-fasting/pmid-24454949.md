This source is included for **measurement and behavioral context**.

**Findings:** Accessible article text reported increased appetite scores, liking, snack reward responses, and ad libitum energy intake after the 24-hour fast compared with fed condition.

**Why it matters:** Provides direct evidence that a 24-hour fast can increase food reward and intake signals, relevant to adherence and refeed behavior.

**Potential experiment signals:** hunger, food reward, ad libitum intake, cravings, refeed behavior

**Protocol takeaway:** A 24-hour fast may heighten food reward and intake after the fast; this supports guidance around refeeding planning, not efficacy.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Extraction boundaries:** Directness: `direct_protocol`. Population mismatch: Nonclinical adults; not a binge-eating, eating-disorder, or metabolic-disease sample. Limitations: Small short study; behavioral endpoints; may not predict longer fast adherence in all populations. Safety notes: No serious adverse-event signal extracted; behavioral appetite/reward response is a protocol adherence/safety context signal.
