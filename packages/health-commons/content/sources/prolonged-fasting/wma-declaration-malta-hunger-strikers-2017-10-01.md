This source is included for **safety and adverse events**.

**Findings:** The WMA declaration states that prolonged fasting risks death or permanent damage, while short-term refusals rarely raise the same ethical problems. The declaration recommends early medical history, baseline examination, regular monitoring when consented, daily communication, respect for competent refusal, and use of up-to-date refeeding guidance when oral feeding begins.

**Why it matters:** Authoritative ethical/medical boundary source for prolonged food refusal and refeeding.

**Potential experiment signals:** monitoring, capacity, autonomy, dehydration, thiamine/fluid intake, refeeding, forced feeding

**Protocol takeaway:** Use only for safety/ethical boundaries; not for efficacy claims or routine wellness guidance.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

**Directness to Prolonged Fasting (24–72 Hours):** general_guideline.

**Population and exposure:** Hunger strikers, especially detained persons, and clinicians managing voluntary refusal of food. Exposure/intervention: Ethical and medical guidance for hunger strikes and prolonged food refusal. Comparator/control: Not applicable. Duration/follow-up: Prolonged food refusal; short-term refusals noted as usually less ethically problematic..

**Effect direction or estimates:** The declaration states that prolonged fasting risks death or permanent damage; it emphasizes autonomy, capacity assessment, regular examination, daily communication, minimizing harm, and cautious refeeding.

**Adverse events or safety notes:** Death/permanent damage risk, coercion/forced-feeding harms, and need for up-to-date refeeding guidance.

**Limitations:** Ethical policy for hunger strikes, not a wellness fasting clinical protocol.

**Population mismatch:** High contextual mismatch due to detention, coercion, and political hunger-strike context.
