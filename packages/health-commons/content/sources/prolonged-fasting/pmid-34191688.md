This source is included for **eating-disorder and restriction-risk boundary**.

**Findings:** Among 64 intermittent fasters, most reported ~16-hour daily fasting for weight loss, and a substantial subgroup had EDE-Q scores at or above a clinical cutoff.

**Why it matters:** Describes how intermittent fasting is implemented among men and women and its association with eating-disorder symptomatology; supports caution around user motivation and behavior screening.

**Potential experiment signals:** restrictive behavior, eating disorder symptomatology, screening and contraindications.

**Protocol takeaway:** Screen motivation, loss-of-control eating, and rule-bound restriction before protocol participation.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

**Directness and caveat:** Intermittent fasting in self-selected adults, not direct 24–72-hour prolonged fasting. Small cross-sectional convenience sample; no causal inference.
