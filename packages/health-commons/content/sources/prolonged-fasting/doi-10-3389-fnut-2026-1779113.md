This source is included for **metabolic timecourse and mechanisms**.

**Findings:** The abstract reported lower mean 24-hour glucose and lower postprandial glucose AUC for the afternoon-start condition; the morning start was reported as optimal for AUC above baseline after the fast; beta-hydroxybutyrate reached the ketosis threshold after afternoon/evening starts but not morning; insulin and glucagon differences were not significant.

**Why it matters:** Directly addresses how the start time of a 24-hour fast can change glucose and ketone readouts.

**Potential experiment signals:** mean 24-hour glucose, postprandial glucose AUC, OGTT glucose AUC above baseline, beta-hydroxybutyrate, insulin, glucagon

**Protocol takeaway:** A 24-hour fast is not one uniform exposure; start time can alter glycemic and ketone signals.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

**Extraction boundaries:** Directness: `direct_protocol`. Population mismatch: Adults with overweight/obesity, not necessarily healthy lean or metabolically diseased populations. Limitations: Short 24-hour intervention; overweight/obesity sample; immediate post-fast OGTT may not represent normal refeeding; start-time effects may not generalize to multi-day fasts. Safety notes: No fasting-specific adverse-event signal extracted from the available article text.
