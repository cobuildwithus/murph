This source is included for **direct protocol and dose evidence**.

**Findings:** The study reported lower body weight, fat mass and muscle mass, lower blood glucose, higher free fatty acids and other fasting metabolites, unchanged hand-grip strength and perception-reaction time, and progressively reduced PWC170/work capacity by 72 hours.

**Why it matters:** This is direct 72-hour human evidence for an upper-bound protocol dose, but its most relevant use is exercise/performance safety rather than efficacy.

**Potential experiment signals:** body weight, body composition, blood glucose, free fatty acids, exercise tolerance, hand-grip strength, reaction time, perceived exertion.

**Protocol takeaway:** A 3-day fast in trained young men lowered physical work capacity, so strenuous exercise during 60–72 hour fasts should be treated as a safety/performance boundary rather than an expected benefit.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.
