This source is included for **safety and adverse events**.

**Findings:** Fasting headache is described as a common secondary headache pattern, often tension-type-like, with likelihood increasing as fasting duration increases. The review implicates hypoglycemia and caffeine withdrawal as possible contributors but explicitly leaves causal mechanisms unresolved.

**Why it matters:** Focused source for one common fast-related symptom and for stop-condition nuance.

**Potential experiment signals:** headache, hypoglycemia, caffeine withdrawal, dehydration

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context for headache as a plausible fasting symptom whose likelihood may rise with duration; do not use for incidence or efficacy claims.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness to Prolonged Fasting (24–72 Hours):** same_mechanism.

**Population and exposure:** People experiencing fasting-associated headache across clinical and observational literature. Exposure/intervention: Fasting as a headache trigger, including religious and voluntary fasts. Comparator/control: None; narrative review. Duration/follow-up: Not a single intervention duration; fasting duration considered as a trigger gradient..

**Effect direction or estimates:** Most fasting headaches resemble tension-type headache, and the probability of onset increases as fasting duration increases; hypoglycemia and caffeine withdrawal are discussed but unresolved.

**Adverse events or safety notes:** Fasting-associated headache is the primary safety symptom discussed.

**Limitations:** Narrative review; no pooled incidence estimate; mechanisms remain uncertain.

**Population mismatch:** Symptom-focused literature and mixed fasting contexts, not a 24-72 hour wellness protocol trial.
