This source is included for **Clinical/residential supervised fasting boundary**.

**Findings:** In a 34-patient randomized cross-over pilot, short-term fasting around chemotherapy was reported as well tolerated and appeared to improve quality-of-life/fatigue measures; oncology context limits directness.

**Why it matters:** Captures human tolerability/QOL evidence under oncology supervision while preserving directness limits.

**Potential experiment signals:** quality of life, fatigue, adverse events.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as oncology tolerability boundary only.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Patients with breast or ovarian cancer receiving chemotherapy.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Short-term fasting around chemotherapy cycles.
- **Comparator/control:** Cross-over normocaloric/usual diet chemotherapy cycles.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Short-term fasting around chemotherapy; exact fasting schedule varies by study protocol.
- **Endpoints:** quality of life, fatigue, chemotherapy tolerance, adverse events
- **Effect estimate or direction:** Pilot reported short-term fasting was well tolerated and appeared to improve quality-of-life/fatigue outcomes.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No serious adverse effects were reported in the extracted source summary; drop-outs were not attributed to fasting.
- **Limitations:** Pilot cross-over design, oncology population, chemotherapy co-intervention.
- **Population mismatch:** Cancer treatment context, not healthy fasting.
- **Directness to Prolonged Fasting (24–72 Hours):** adjacent_variant
