This source is included for **metabolic timecourse and mechanisms**.

**Findings:** Accessible abstract/search text reported that 36-hour versus 12-hour fasting was associated with lower fasting plasma glucose and insulin, slightly reduced first-phase insulin secretion, and opposite-directed changes in hepatic versus whole-body insulin action/disposition indices.

**Why it matters:** Direct 36-hour fasting study for insulin secretion/action, squarely inside the target protocol window.

**Potential experiment signals:** fasting glucose, fasting insulin, first-phase insulin secretion, hepatic insulin action, whole-body insulin action

**Protocol takeaway:** A 36-hour fast can lower fasting glucose/insulin while producing nuanced organ-specific insulin-action changes.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

**Extraction boundaries:** Directness: `direct_protocol`. Population mismatch: Healthy young males only; does not generalize to women, older adults, diabetes, or medication users. Limitations: Small male-only healthy sample; specialized insulin-action modeling; not a clinical outcome trial. Safety notes: No adverse-event signal extracted.
