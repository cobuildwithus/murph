This source is included for **eating-disorder and restriction-risk boundary**.

**Findings:** This study evaluated whether impulse-control difficulties moderate the relationship between intermittent fasting and disordered eating behaviors.

**Why it matters:** Adds a psychological-risk moderator to fasting/disordered-eating screening; useful as a candidate for future extraction on behavioral vulnerability flags.

**Potential experiment signals:** binge/restrict cycles, eating disorder symptomatology, screening and contraindications.

**Protocol takeaway:** Do not rely on diet-label screening alone; include binge/purge and impulse-control red flags.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

**Directness and caveat:** Intermittent fasting and disordered-eating measures, not a prolonged-fasting intervention. Participant count and quantitative effect estimates were not extracted from the accessible record.
