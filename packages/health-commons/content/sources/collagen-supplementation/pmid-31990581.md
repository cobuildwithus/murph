This source is included for **joint-osteoarthritis**.

**Findings:**

- **dose — Dose and duration:** Bongers 2020 tests a common 10 g/day dose in self-reported knee pain. Effect/direction: 10 g/day Peptan B2000 collagen peptide for 12 weeks.
- **null_result — VAS knee pain:** Collagen peptides did not outperform placebo for knee pain. Effect/direction: VAS change −1.6 ± 2.4 with collagen vs −1.9 ± 2.6 with placebo; p=0.42.
- **null_result — KOOS, Lysholm, inflammatory/cartilage/bone biomarkers:** Within-group improvements should not be attributed to collagen-specific effects. Effect/direction: Function scores improved in both groups but not between groups; biomarkers were not impacted.
- **population — Population mismatch:** Null result is most applicable to non-OA self-reported knee pain. Effect/direction: Healthy active middle-aged/older adults with self-reported knee pain, not diagnosed OA.

**Why it matters:** This negative RCT protects the protocol from overclaiming symptom effects in non-OA knee pain.

**Potential experiment signals:** VAS knee pain, Lysholm questionnaire, KOOS, inflammatory biomarkers, cartilage biomarkers, bone biomarkers.

**Protocol takeaway:** Collagen peptides are not consistently superior to placebo in self-reported knee pain over 12 weeks.

**Murph takeaway:** Use a control period or repeated standardized ratings to distinguish placebo/time effects from supplement effects.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

**Directness and boundary:** `direct_protocol`. Healthy active adults with self-reported knee pain, not diagnosed OA.

**Safety notes:** No detailed adverse-event extraction available from accessible summaries.

**Limitations:**

- Self-reported knee pain rather than diagnosed OA.
- Null between-group result despite within-group improvements.
