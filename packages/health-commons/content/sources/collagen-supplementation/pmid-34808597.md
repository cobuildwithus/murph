This source is included for **exercise-performance-recovery**.

**Findings:** Accessible abstract/reporting indicates maximal rate of force development and several countermovement-jump characteristics improved more with hydrolyzed collagen plus vitamin C, while maximal force and squat-jump outcomes did not show clear between-group differences.

**Why it matters:** Performance-adjacent trial with a co-intervention.

**Potential experiment signals:** rate of force development, countermovement jump, maximal isometric squat.

**Protocol takeaway:** Collagen plus vitamin C before training may affect lower-limb force-development measures, but this should remain adjacent-variant context.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness and boundary:** `adjacent_variant`. Vitamin C co-administration prevents direct attribution to collagen peptides alone.

**Safety notes:** No adverse-event signal extracted from accessible abstract snippets.

**Limitations:** Co-administered vitamin C; not isolated HCP.; Male athletes only.; Short duration.; Performance endpoint rather than body-composition or injury outcome.

**Population mismatch:** Young male athletes and vitamin-C co-intervention; adjacent variant only.
