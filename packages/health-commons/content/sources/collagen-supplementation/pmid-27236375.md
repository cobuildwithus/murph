This source is included for **safety-quality-contaminants**.

**Findings:** Population/exposure: Sera from patients with fish allergy; skin-prick testing reported for at least one patient Intervention or exposure: Fish collagen/protein extracts from 11 bony and cartilaginous fish species exposed to heating conditions. Comparator/control: Different fish species and heating conditions; cartilaginous versus bony fish extracts. Duration/follow-up: In vitro and skin-prick testing; no clinical follow-up duration Endpoints: IgE reactivity, collagen thermostability, skin-prick response. Direction/effect: Collagen retained IgE reactivity after heating; substantial degradation required high heating burden, and bony fish extracts showed stronger reactivity than cartilaginous fish in the accessible report. Safety notes: Supports an allergy caution for fish-derived collagen because heat processing does not reliably eliminate IgE reactivity. Limitations: Accessible abstract did not provide the full patient/sera count.; Mechanistic allergy evidence, not a supplement tolerability trial.; Does not estimate incidence of reactions in general collagen supplement users.. Population mismatch: Fish-allergic patients and laboratory extracts, not general HCP users.

**Why it matters:** Explains why marine/fish-derived collagen products remain relevant to fish-allergy screening and labeling cautions.
