This source is included for **tendon-loading-ligament**.

**Findings:** Achilles tendon cross-sectional area and muscle thickness increased more with collagen peptides; tendon stiffness and strength increased in both groups without clear between-group differences.

**Population and intervention:** 40 healthy male volunteers, mean age about 26 years. Intervention/exposure: 5 g/day specific collagen peptides combined with 14 weeks of high-load resistance training. Comparator/control: 5 g/day placebo combined with the same resistance-training program. Duration/follow-up: 14 weeks of high-load resistance training.

**Endpoints:** Achilles tendon cross-sectional area and stiffness, plantar-flexor strength, and muscle thickness.

**Safety notes:** Adverse events were not reported in the extracted record.

**Limitations:** Healthy young men, not a tendinopathy population.; Structural/mechanical endpoints rather than pain or functional recovery endpoints.; Resistance training co-intervention is required for interpretation.

**Population mismatch:** Healthy male training adaptation study; not direct evidence for injured tendons or women.

**Why it matters:** This is a direct supplement-plus-loading trial for Achilles tendon properties, useful for differentiating structural adaptation from clinical pain outcomes.

**Potential experiment signals:** Achilles tendon CSA; Achilles tendon stiffness; plantar-flexor strength; calf muscle thickness

**Protocol takeaway:** For healthy training contexts, collagen peptides may support tendon morphology when combined with high-load resistance training, but pain or injury claims need separate evidence.
