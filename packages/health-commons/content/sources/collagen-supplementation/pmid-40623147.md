This source is included for **tendon-loading-ligament**.

**Findings:** Collagen peptide supplementation increased muscle-tendon stiffness and normalized rate of torque development; no extracted change in CSA or maximal voluntary contraction.

**Population and intervention:** 50 healthy young sedentary males; collagen peptide n=25 and placebo n=25. Intervention/exposure: 10 g/day collagen peptide beverage for 16 weeks. Comparator/control: Placebo beverage for 16 weeks. Duration/follow-up: 16 weeks.

**Endpoints:** Medial gastrocnemius stiffness, Achilles tendon stiffness, normalized rate of torque development, muscle and tendon CSA, maximum voluntary contraction.

**Safety notes:** Adverse events were not reported in the extracted record.

**Limitations:** Healthy sedentary young men.; No structured training/loading program was identified in the extracted record.; Surrogate stiffness/explosive strength endpoints rather than clinical tendon symptoms.

**Population mismatch:** Sedentary male physiology/performance study; not tendon rehabilitation or active-athlete training evidence.

**Why it matters:** This recent RCT broadens direct evidence beyond supervised training, but it should be kept separate from tendon-loading rehabilitation trials.

**Potential experiment signals:** Achilles tendon stiffness; medial gastrocnemius stiffness; rate of torque development; maximal voluntary contraction

**Protocol takeaway:** Collagen peptides may affect muscle-tendon mechanical properties even outside a clear training protocol, but the finding is not clinical tendinopathy evidence.
