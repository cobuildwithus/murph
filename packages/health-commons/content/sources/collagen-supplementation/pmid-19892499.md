This source is included for **bone-density-turnover**.

**Findings:**

- **Population:** Postmenopausal women with osteopenia/low bone mineral density; mean age 57.3 ± 4.8 years; BMI 27.4 ± 4.5 kg/m² in accessible abstract.
- **Intervention/exposure:** 10 g/day collagen hydrolysates for 24 weeks.
- **Comparator/control:** Placebo for 24 weeks.
- **Duration/follow-up:** 24 weeks with markers at baseline, 12 weeks, and 24 weeks.
- **Endpoints:** CTX, osteocalcin, bone-specific alkaline phosphatase, bone metabolism biomarkers.
- **Effect/direction:** No significant between-group differences in CTX, osteocalcin, or bone-specific alkaline phosphatase; authors concluded collagen hydrolysate did not affect biochemical indices of bone remodeling.
- **Adverse events/safety:** Adverse events were not described in the accessible abstract.
- **Limitations:** BMD change was not assessed in the accessible abstract.; Calcium intake was inadequate in many participants and participants had excess body weight, both noted by authors as context.; Older small RCT; product specificity and adherence details require full text.
- **Population mismatch/directness:** Direct target population for postmenopausal low BMD, but only biomarker outcomes over 24 weeks.

**Why it matters:** This is a direct HCP-alone RCT in postmenopausal low-BMD women and preserves a null comparator against later positive studies.

**Potential experiment signals:** CTX, osteocalcin, bone-specific alkaline phosphatase, calcium intake context.

**Protocol takeaway:** Do not claim collagen hydrolysate consistently improves bone turnover; this RCT found no significant marker advantage at 10 g/day over 24 weeks.
