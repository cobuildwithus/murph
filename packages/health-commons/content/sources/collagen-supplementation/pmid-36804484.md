This source is included for **bone-density-turnover**.

**Findings:**

- **Population:** Healthy young men aged 24 ± 4 years; population mismatch for postmenopausal osteopenia/osteoporosis.
- **Intervention/exposure:** 20 g hydrolysed collagen 1 hour before short high-impact jumping exercise, once or twice daily across 3-day intervention periods.
- **Comparator/control:** Placebo before comparable jumping exercise.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Three 3-day intervention periods separated by 10-day washout.
- **Endpoints:** P1NP, CTX-I, fasted and postprandial bone turnover.
- **Effect/direction:** Daily jumping increased fasted P1NP over days, but collagen supplementation did not further increase P1NP and CTX-I was not affected by collagen.
- **Adverse events/safety:** Adverse events not extracted.
- **Limitations:** Healthy young male population.; Acute 3-day exercise intervention; no BMD outcomes.; Collagen timing/dose around exercise cannot be generalized to long-term supplementation.
- **Population mismatch/directness:** Same-mechanism acute exercise context with major population mismatch.

**Why it matters:** It is a well-defined null marker study that prevents overclaiming collagen-plus-exercise bone effects.

**Potential experiment signals:** fasted P1NP, postprandial P1NP, CTX-I, exercise frequency.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context-only null evidence for acute bone-turnover modulation; do not generalize to BMD outcomes.
