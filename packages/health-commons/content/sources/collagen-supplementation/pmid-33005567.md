This source is included for **safety-quality-contaminants**.

**Findings:** Population/exposure: Protein powder supplement product records from Consumer Reports and Clean Label Project datasets; no human participants Intervention or exposure: Protein powder supplement consumption modeled at one or three servings per day. Comparator/control: Reference doses and adult lead biokinetic model thresholds. Duration/follow-up: Modeled daily intake at recommended serving assumptions Endpoints: arsenic exposure, cadmium exposure, mercury exposure, lead exposure, hazard quotient, hazard index, modeled blood lead. Direction/effect: Most modeled non-carcinogenic hazard indices were below 1; modeled adult blood lead levels were below 5 micrograms/dL under the authors' assumptions. Safety notes: Contaminants were detectable in public datasets; risk depended on product and serving assumptions. Limitations: Protein powders broadly, not collagen-specific.; Relied on public CR/CLP datasets with blinded or incomplete product data.; Assumed serving size for some CLP products.. Population mismatch: Modeled supplement exposure, not measured collagen users.

**Why it matters:** Provides peer-reviewed context for interpreting consumer heavy-metal testing without overgeneralizing to all collagen powders.
