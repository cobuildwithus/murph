This source is included for **skin-aging**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Randomized, triple-blind, placebo-controlled parallel trial.
- **People studied or addressed:** Women aged 45–60 years.
- **Intervention/exposure:** 10 g/day hydrolyzed freshwater marine collagen.
- **Comparator/control:** Placebo.
- **Duration/follow-up:** 12 weeks.
- **Role in Murph:** direct protocol evidence for Hydrolyzed Collagen Peptides.

**Findings:** Wrinkle score was reported to decline significantly from baseline after 12 weeks, with a larger reduction than placebo on the right side of the face. Elasticity improvements were clearest in the 45–54 subgroup. Self-reported appearance measures improved versus placebo, but these subjective outcomes should not be treated as device-measured endpoints.

**Adverse events or safety:** Accessible summaries describe the product as safe and well tolerated.

**Why it matters:** Direct open full-text RCT for fish-derived hydrolyzed collagen with wrinkle and elasticity endpoints.

**Potential experiment signals:**

- VISIA wrinkle score
- skin elasticity by cutometer
- self-reported skin appearance including hydration, radiance, firmness, wrinkles, and elasticity

**Protocol takeaway:** Supports wrinkle and some elasticity signals, but subgroup and self-report boundaries should be preserved.

**Limitations and population fit:**

- Participant count was not confirmed from the accessible abstract/source snippets.
- Some elasticity signals were subgroup-specific.
- Self-reported outcomes are less objective than device measures.
- Freshwater marine/pangasius collagen product may not generalize to all collagen peptides.

**Population mismatch:** Women aged 45–60 years may not match all Murph users; do not generalize to different ages, sexes, baseline skin status, or collagen product types without caveats.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.
