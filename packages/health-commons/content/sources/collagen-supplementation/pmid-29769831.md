This source is included for **tendon-loading-ligament**.

**Findings:** CAIT and FAAM-G improved more with collagen peptides than placebo; arthrometer mechanical stiffness endpoints did not show significant between-group improvement; follow-up questionnaire favored fewer ankle injuries and better perceived stability.

**Population and intervention:** 50 athletes with chronic ankle instability; not a clinical tendinopathy cohort. Intervention/exposure: 5 g/day specific collagen peptides for 6 months. Comparator/control: 5 g/day maltodextrin placebo. Duration/follow-up: 6 months supplementation plus 3-month follow-up.

**Endpoints:** Cumberland Ankle Instability Tool (CAIT), FAAM ADL and sport subscales, ankle arthrometer measures, self-reported ankle injuries and stability at follow-up.

**Safety notes:** Adverse events were not clearly reported in the extracted record.

**Limitations:** Chronic ankle instability is ligament/joint-function evidence, not direct tendon-pain evidence.; Baseline functional scores were lower in the collagen group, which can affect interpretation of change scores.; Follow-up injury and stability outcomes were questionnaire-based.

**Population mismatch:** Athletic chronic ankle instability population; not general adults and not Achilles/patellar tendinopathy.

**Why it matters:** This is a direct collagen-peptide RCT in an athletic ligament-adjacent condition, useful for the boundary between tendon-loading and ankle-instability claims.

**Potential experiment signals:** CAIT score; FAAM ADL and sport scores; self-reported ankle sprains or instability episodes; training exposure and re-injury reporting

**Protocol takeaway:** Timed or daily collagen peptide supplementation may be relevant to ligament-adjacent athletic function, but this source should not be treated as direct Achilles or patellar tendinopathy efficacy evidence.
