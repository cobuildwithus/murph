This source is included for **tendon-loading-ligament**.

**Findings:** Patellar tendon cross-sectional area increased more with collagen peptides at 60% and 70% tendon length; tendon stiffness, muscle CSA, and strength improved in both groups without extracted between-group advantage.

**Population and intervention:** 50 healthy moderately active male participants. Intervention/exposure: 5 g/day specific collagen peptides with high-load lower-body resistance training. Comparator/control: 5 g/day placebo with the same resistance-training program. Duration/follow-up: 14 weeks of high-load resistance training.

**Endpoints:** Patellar tendon cross-sectional area at multiple tendon lengths, tendon stiffness, muscle cross-sectional area, strength.

**Safety notes:** Adverse events were not reported in the extracted record.

**Limitations:** Healthy male sample.; Surrogate morphology outcomes rather than pain/function.; The supplement effect is inseparable from resistance training.

**Population mismatch:** Healthy training population rather than tendinopathy or symptomatic users.

**Why it matters:** This trial adds patellar tendon morphology evidence to Achilles tendon data and helps define the loading-dependent evidence boundary.

**Potential experiment signals:** patellar tendon CSA at standardized tendon lengths; patellar tendon stiffness; muscle CSA; lower-body strength

**Protocol takeaway:** Collagen peptide supplementation may enhance some patellar tendon morphology adaptations during heavy training, but clinical symptom effects require separate evidence.
