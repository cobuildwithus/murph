This source is included for **tendon-loading-ligament**.

**Findings:** No significant collagen-versus-placebo advantage was extracted for tendon or aponeurosis adaptations; both groups adapted to training.

**Population and intervention:** 39 healthy recreationally active young men completed the intervention; collagen peptide group n=19 and placebo n=20. Intervention/exposure: 15 g/day specific bioactive collagen peptides during lower-body resistance training 3 times per week. Comparator/control: Placebo during the same lower-body resistance-training program. Duration/follow-up: 15 weeks of lower-body resistance training.

**Endpoints:** Patellar tendon and vastus lateralis aponeurosis morphology, tendon stiffness, Young’s modulus, strength adaptations.

**Safety notes:** Adverse events were not reported in the extracted record.

**Limitations:** Healthy young men, not a clinical tendon-pain cohort.; Null/mixed tendon remodeling result despite direct collagen peptide exposure.; Resistance training co-intervention can mask or dominate supplement effects.

**Population mismatch:** Healthy male training study; not tendinopathy treatment evidence.

**Why it matters:** This source prevents overstatement: not all direct collagen-peptide plus training trials show tendon remodeling advantages.

**Potential experiment signals:** tendon stiffness; Young’s modulus; patellar tendon CSA; vastus lateralis aponeurosis area; training load adherence

**Protocol takeaway:** Hydrolyzed/specific collagen peptides have mixed tendon-remodeling evidence; include null results when discussing tendon structure outcomes.
