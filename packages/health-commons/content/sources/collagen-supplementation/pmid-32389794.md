This source is included for **safety-quality-contaminants**.

**Findings:** Population/exposure: 101 fish-allergic patients evaluated for collagen and parvalbumin sensitization Intervention or exposure: Acid-soluble type I collagen from Atlantic salmon, barramundi, and yellowfin tuna used in diagnostic assays. Comparator/control: Parvalbumin-specific IgE and fish species comparisons. Duration/follow-up: Cross-sectional laboratory and functional testing Endpoints: collagen-specific IgE, parvalbumin-specific IgE, RBL assay, basophil activation test. Direction/effect: 21 of 101 fish-allergic patients were reported to be sensitized to collagen; functional assays showed IgE cross-linking capacity in a subset. Safety notes: Mechanistic evidence that fish collagen can be a clinically relevant allergen. Limitations: Fish-allergic diagnostic cohort, not a supplement trial.; Functional assays were performed in subsets.; Does not quantify risk for people without fish allergy.. Population mismatch: Known fish-allergic patients rather than general HCP users.

**Why it matters:** Strengthens the biological plausibility of fish-collagen reactions and source-specific allergy screening.
