This source is included for **skin-aging**.

**Findings:** Collagen-based supplements improved skin hydration and elasticity, but weather, sex distribution, heterogeneity, and bias assessment limit interpretation. Use as current direct skin endpoint evidence with practical limitations.

**Why it matters:** It is a current hydration/elasticity-focused review with explicit dose range and limitation language.

**Potential experiment signals:** skin hydration, skin elasticity, adverse events, bias.

**Protocol takeaway:** Supports hydration/elasticity tracking, but users should not expect climate-independent or universal effects.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

## Extraction details

- **Source type:** Systematic review and meta-analysis (2025).
- **People studied or addressed:** Participants in randomized controlled trials of collagen-based supplements for skin hydration and elasticity; studies were mostly female.
- **Intervention or exposure:** Collagen-based supplements, 1-10 g/day across included studies; median dose 3.5 g/day and most common dose 4 g/day reported by the review.
- **Comparator or control:** Placebo/control.
- **Duration or follow-up:** Varied across included RCTs.
- **Most relevant endpoints:** skin hydration, skin elasticity, adverse events, bias.

## Limitations and mismatch

Mostly female participants, weather not controlled, moderate heterogeneity, unclear bias, and publication bias could not be assessed. Population mismatch: Mostly female skin studies; climate/weather effects not controlled.
