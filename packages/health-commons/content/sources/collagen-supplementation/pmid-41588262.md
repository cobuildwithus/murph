This source is included for **skin-aging**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Randomized controlled dose-response trial with non-supplemented control.
- **People studied or addressed:** Healthy sedentary women aged 35–55 years.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Bioactive collagen peptides at 2.5 g/day or 10 g/day.
- **Comparator/control:** Non-supplemented control group; no matched placebo was identified in accessible summaries.
- **Duration/follow-up:** 12 weeks.
- **Role in Murph:** direct protocol evidence for Hydrolyzed Collagen Peptides.

**Findings:** The 10 g/day group reduced wrinkle number and length in accessible abstract data. Both 2.5 g/day and 10 g/day groups improved elasticity and hydration versus control in accessible abstract data. TGF-beta and Klotho increased in both collagen groups; these are mechanism/biomarker findings, not direct proof of skin-causal mechanism.

**Adverse events or safety:** Adverse-event details were not extracted from accessible abstract-level materials.

**Why it matters:** Recent direct dose-response skin-health trial in middle-aged women with wrinkle, hydration, elasticity, and biomarker endpoints.

**Potential experiment signals:**

- wrinkle number and length
- skin elasticity
- skin hydration
- TGF-beta
- Klotho
- dose response

**Protocol takeaway:** Supports dose-response skin-health signals but should be used cautiously because the accessible summary indicates a non-placebo control.

**Limitations and population fit:**

- Comparator was a non-supplemented control rather than a matched placebo in accessible summaries.
- Recent 2026 source requires final metadata/right-status verification before repository PDF handling.
- Mechanistic immune/aging biomarkers should not be overpromoted as user-visible outcomes.

**Population mismatch:** Healthy sedentary women aged 35–55 years may not match all Murph users; do not generalize to different ages, sexes, baseline skin status, or collagen product types without caveats.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.
