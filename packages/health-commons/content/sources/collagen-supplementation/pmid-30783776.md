This source is included for **bone-density-turnover**.

**Findings:**

- **Population:** Recreationally active young adult males; major population mismatch for osteopenia/osteoporosis.
- **Intervention/exposure:** 20 g/day collagen peptides starting 7 days before and continuing 2 days after a damaging exercise protocol of 150 drop jumps.
- **Comparator/control:** Placebo/control supplement.
- **Duration/follow-up:** 9-day supplementation window around an acute exercise challenge.
- **Endpoints:** P1NP, β-CTX, muscle soreness, countermovement jump, inflammation/muscle damage markers.
- **Effect/direction:** Accessible abstract reports no significant effect on β-CTX or P1NP; soreness and jump-recovery findings were not bone-density outcomes.
- **Adverse events/safety:** No adverse-event signal extracted from accessible abstract.
- **Limitations:** Acute exercise model in young men, not osteopenia/osteoporosis.; Short time window cannot evaluate BMD or fracture outcomes.; Bone-turnover markers after exercise are sensitive to timing and physiology.
- **Population mismatch/directness:** Young male exercise recovery population; use only for acute bone-turnover and recovery context.

**Why it matters:** It prevents overclaiming that collagen reliably modulates bone turnover after acute loading.

**Potential experiment signals:** P1NP, β-CTX, soreness, countermovement jump recovery.

**Protocol takeaway:** Do not use this trial to support BMD claims; cite only as acute exercise-bone-turnover context showing no marker advantage.
