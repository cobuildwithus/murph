## Quick read

- **Source type:** Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled clinical study comparing collagen hydrolysate peptide profiles.
- **People studied or addressed:** Chinese women aged 35–55 years.
- **Intervention/exposure:** 5 g/day bioactive collagen hydrolysate with high or low content of Pro-Hyp and Hyp-Gly peptides.
- **Comparator/control:** Placebo.
- **Duration/follow-up:** 8 weeks.
- **Role in Murph:** direct protocol evidence for Hydrolyzed Collagen Peptides.

**Findings:** The high-Pro-Hyp/Hyp-Gly collagen hydrolysate was reported to improve facial moisture and elasticity versus placebo. Facial aging signs, including wrinkle and roughness measures, improved more with the high-bioactive-peptide preparation. The study suggests peptide composition may matter, not just total collagen-hydrolysate dose.

**Adverse events or safety:** Blood tests reportedly showed no safety concerns and no adverse events were reported in accessible summaries.

**Why it matters:** Direct skin RCT that highlights dose and peptide-profile boundaries.

**Potential experiment signals:**

- facial skin moisture
- skin elasticity
- wrinkle number/depth
- skin roughness
- blood safety tests

**Protocol takeaway:** Supports collagen hydrolysate benefits for facial moisture, elasticity, and aging signs, with an important caveat that peptide composition appears central.

**Limitations and population fit:**

- Paywalled primary article limits extraction of full endpoint tables.
- Chinese women only.
- Product-composition comparison makes the result product-specific.
- The low-bioactive-peptide arm may not generalize to all hydrolyzed collagen products.

**Population mismatch:** Chinese women aged 35–55 years may not match all Murph users; do not generalize to different ages, sexes, baseline skin status, or collagen product types without caveats.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.
