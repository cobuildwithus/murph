This source is included for **joint-osteoarthritis**.

**Findings:**

- **dose — Dose and duration:** Clark 2008 supports a 10 g/day, six-month activity-related joint-pain protocol window. Effect/direction: 10 g/day collagen hydrolysate for 24 weeks.
- **outcome — Activity-related joint pain VAS and mobility/inflammation parameters:** Athletes with activity-related joint pain had more favorable pain/function responses with collagen hydrolysate than placebo. Effect/direction: Positive direction overall; several VAS parameters favored collagen hydrolysate, with stronger signal in knee arthralgia subgroup.
- **population — Population mismatch:** Use for activity-related discomfort claims, not direct OA symptom claims. Effect/direction: Healthy athletes with activity-related pain, not diagnosed OA.
- **limitation — Attrition/analyzability:** Attrition limits confidence in effect magnitude. Effect/direction: Only 97 of 147 randomized participants were analyzable in available abstract.

**Why it matters:** This is a foundational direct oral collagen hydrolysate RCT for activity-related joint pain.

**Potential experiment signals:** VAS joint pain during activity and rest, joint mobility, inflammation, knee arthralgia subgroup.

**Protocol takeaway:** 10 g/day for six months has athlete joint-pain support, but OA-specific claims need OA trials.

**Murph takeaway:** Best matched to activity-triggered pain ratings during sport or daily load, not structural OA endpoints.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

**Directness and boundary:** `direct_protocol`. Activity-related athlete joint pain, not clinical knee osteoarthritis.

**Safety notes:** The study is frequently cited as tolerability-relevant; detailed adverse-event counts were not extractable from the available abstract snippets.

**Limitations:**

- Large attrition/analyzability gap: 147 randomized but 97 analyzable in available abstract.
- Athletic activity-related pain is not diagnosed OA.
- Subjective pain outcomes and industry-linked collagen product context.
