This source is included for **safety-quality-contaminants**.

**Findings:** Population/exposure: Organic chicken broth preparations and tap-water control; no human participants Intervention or exposure: Bone broth or related chicken broth preparations. Comparator/control: Tap-water control and broth variants prepared without bone. Duration/follow-up: Single laboratory testing episode Endpoints: lead concentration in broth. Direction/effect: Reported lead concentrations were higher in several broth preparations than in tap-water control, including bone broth and skin/cartilage preparations. Safety notes: No human adverse events were measured; this is a contaminant exposure warning for a food source sometimes treated as a collagen source. Limitations: Very small pilot-style sample set.; Bone broth is an adjacent food-source variant, not a hydrolyzed collagen peptide supplement.; Does not measure commercial collagen peptide powders or human blood lead levels.. Population mismatch: Food samples rather than people using hydrolyzed collagen peptides.

**Why it matters:** Keeps bone broth contaminant concerns separate from hydrolyzed collagen peptide supplement efficacy claims.

**Potential experiment signals:** safety:lead-exposure, safety:bone-broth-boundary, safety:contaminants.
