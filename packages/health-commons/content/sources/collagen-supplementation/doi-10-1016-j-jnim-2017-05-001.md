This source is included for **clinical-supervised-wound-nutrition**.

**Findings:**

- Patients were randomized to usual care, a collagen peptide-containing drink, or an arginine-containing drink for 4 weeks; 51 participants were included in the reported analysis. `[source_artifact:doi-10.1016-j.jnim.2017.05.001]`
- The collagen peptide drink group had lower DESIGN-R total scores than usual care at 2 weeks and at final assessment, while the arginine drink was not significantly different from control in the accessible abstract. `[source_artifact:doi-10.1016-j.jnim.2017.05.001]`
- The accessible abstract reported no significant differences in nutritional-status measures among groups, which limits interpretation of wound effects as general nutrition repletion. `[source_artifact:doi-10.1016-j.jnim.2017.05.001]`

**Why it matters:** Shows collagen peptide-containing oral nutrition has been tested in medically supervised pressure-ulcer care, which defines a boundary for the wellness protocol rather than a direct claim. `[source_artifact:doi-10.1016-j.jnim.2017.05.001]`

**Potential experiment signals:**

  - "DESIGN-R total score"
  - "pressure ulcer healing trajectory"
  - "nutritional status"
  - "clinical wound-care escalation"

**Protocol takeaway:** Context-only: exclude from direct Hydrolyzed Collagen Peptides protocol efficacy claims because the population and endpoint are clinical pressure-ulcer treatment. `[source_artifact:doi-10.1016-j.jnim.2017.05.001]`

**Population mismatch:** Patients with pressure ulcers receiving clinical wound care; 66 randomized and 51 analyzed in the accessible abstract.

**Limitations:**

- Clinical pressure-ulcer treatment population.
- Supplement was a collagen peptide-containing drink, not necessarily a single-ingredient HCP product.
- Analyzed sample was smaller than randomized sample.
- Adverse events were not extracted from the accessible abstract.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
