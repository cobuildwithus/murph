This source is included for **tendon-loading-ligament**.

**Findings:** PINP AUC was greater with 30 g than 15 g and 0 g; 15 g did not clearly differ from 0 g in the extracted young-men study. β-CTX decreased over time without between-dose differences.

**Population and intervention:** 10 resistance-trained young men, about 26 years old. Intervention/exposure: 0 g, 15 g, or 30 g hydrolyzed collagen plus 50 mg vitamin C taken 1 hour before 4 × 10RM barbell back squat. Comparator/control: Within-participant comparison across 0 g, 15 g, and 30 g doses. Duration/follow-up: Acute 6-hour recovery after resistance exercise in crossover design.

**Endpoints:** Serum PINP area under the curve, β-CTX, plasma amino acid availability.

**Safety notes:** Adverse events were not reported in the extracted record.

**Limitations:** Acute biomarker study, not clinical or structural tendon outcome.; Small male sample.; Vitamin C coadministered.; Whole-body biomarker does not localize collagen synthesis to tendon.

**Population mismatch:** Resistance-trained young men; not tendinopathy patients or women.

**Why it matters:** It helps interpret why some tendon-loading trials use larger collagen doses near exercise sessions.

**Potential experiment signals:** serum PINP AUC; β-CTX; plasma glycine/proline/hydroxyproline; dose timing relative to loading

**Protocol takeaway:** Dose-response biomarker evidence supports mechanistic plausibility for timed HCP dosing, but outcome claims must come from longer trials.
