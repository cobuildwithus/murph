This source is included for **joint-osteoarthritis**.

**Findings:**

- **dose — Dose and duration:** Bruyère 2012 tests a substantially lower daily dose than many 5-10 g collagen peptide trials. Effect/direction: 1200 mg/day collagen hydrolysate for 6 months.
- **mixed_result — VAS pain responder rate:** Low-dose collagen hydrolysate had delayed responder signal but no early advantage. Effect/direction: Responder advantage at 6 months (51.6% vs 36.5%, p<0.05) but not at 3 months (44.1% vs 39.6%, p=0.53).
- **safety — Safety/tolerability:** Safety signal is reassuring, but event counts were not extracted. Effect/direction: No significant safety/tolerability differences between groups in accessible extract.
- **population — Population mismatch:** Knee-specific claims require caution. Effect/direction: Articular pain sites included lower limbs, upper limbs, and lumbar spine rather than knee OA alone.

**Why it matters:** It adds dose diversity and safety/tolerability context to direct oral collagen hydrolysate evidence.

**Potential experiment signals:** VAS pain responder status, articular pain, tolerability.

**Protocol takeaway:** Low-dose collagen hydrolysate may require long follow-up; use cautiously for knee-specific claims.

**Murph takeaway:** Look for delayed responder patterns rather than expecting early-week changes at low doses.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

**Directness and boundary:** `direct_protocol`. Articular pain population includes non-knee and non-OA pain sites.

**Safety notes:** Available extract reports no significant safety/tolerability differences between groups; detailed AE counts not extracted.

**Limitations:**

- Mixed articular-pain sites rather than knee OA only.
- Lower dose than most collagen-peptide protocols; positive response emerged only by 6 months.
