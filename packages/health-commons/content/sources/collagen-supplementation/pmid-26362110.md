This source is included for **skin-aging**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Ex vivo model plus two randomized placebo-controlled clinical trials.
- **People studied or addressed:** Women with dry or aging-skin features in two clinical trials.
- **Intervention/exposure:** 10 g/day oral collagen peptides in a drink.
- **Comparator/control:** 10 g/day placebo drink.
- **Duration/follow-up:** 8 weeks in hydration trial; 4 and 12 week assessments in dermal-network trial.
- **Role in Murph:** direct protocol evidence for Hydrolyzed Collagen Peptides.

**Findings:** Skin hydration was reported to improve after 8 weeks in the clinical hydration trial. Dermal collagen density increased and collagen fragmentation decreased in the dermal-network trial, with changes reported by 4 weeks and persisting through 12 weeks. Ex vivo work supported collagen and glycosaminoglycan production mechanisms but is not direct clinical efficacy evidence.

**Adverse events or safety:** Adverse-event details were not extracted from accessible materials.

**Why it matters:** Links direct skin-moisture changes with dermal-network structure measures.

**Potential experiment signals:**

- skin hydration/moisture
- dermal collagen density
- dermal collagen fragmentation
- ex vivo collagen and glycosaminoglycan production

**Protocol takeaway:** Supports hydration and dermal-structure endpoints for collagen peptides, but mechanism/ex vivo findings should not be promoted as user-visible outcomes.

**Limitations and population fit:**

- Combines two clinical trials and ex vivo work; mechanism should be separated from clinical outcomes.
- Product-specific 10 g/day collagen-peptide drink.
- Full trial-level AE tables were not extracted.

**Population mismatch:** Women with dry or aging-skin features in two clinical trials may not match all Murph users; do not generalize to different ages, sexes, baseline skin status, or collagen product types without caveats.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.
