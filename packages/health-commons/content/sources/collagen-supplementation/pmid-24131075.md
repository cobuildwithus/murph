This source is included for **skin-aging**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Prospective randomized controlled study with no-supplement and vitamin C comparison arms.
- **People studied or addressed:** Healthy volunteers.
- **Intervention/exposure:** 3 g/day collagen tripeptide, with or without 500 mg/day vitamin C in one arm.
- **Comparator/control:** No supplement control and vitamin C-only comparison arm.
- **Duration/follow-up:** 12 weeks.
- **Role in Murph:** direct protocol evidence for Hydrolyzed Collagen Peptides.

**Findings:** Corneometer hydration changes differed significantly between no supplement and collagen tripeptide groups. Cutometer elasticity changes differed significantly between no supplement and collagen tripeptide groups. Adding vitamin C did not show a clear enhancement over collagen tripeptide alone in the accessible summary.

**Adverse events or safety:** Adverse-event details were not extracted from accessible materials.

**Why it matters:** Direct collagen-tripeptide trial with hydration, TEWL, and elasticity endpoints, useful for dose and coingredient boundaries.

**Potential experiment signals:**

- skin hydration by corneometer
- transepidermal water loss
- skin elasticity by cutometer

**Protocol takeaway:** Supports 3 g/day collagen tripeptide as a direct skin-property intervention signal, but placebo-free design and vitamin C coadministration require cautious use.

**Limitations and population fit:**

- Small sample.
- No-supplement comparator rather than matched placebo.
- One active arm combined collagen tripeptide with vitamin C, creating an adjacent-variant boundary.
- Full adverse-event and adherence details were not extracted.

**Population mismatch:** Healthy volunteers may not match all Murph users; do not generalize to different ages, sexes, baseline skin status, or collagen product types without caveats.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.
