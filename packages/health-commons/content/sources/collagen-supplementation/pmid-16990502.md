This source is included for **tendon-loading-ligament**.

**Findings:** Women had lower tendon collagen synthesis rates than men at rest and after exercise in the extracted record; exercise did not increase women’s tendon FSR at 72 hours, while prior male data showed an increase.

**Population and intervention:** 15 healthy young women; early follicular n=8 and late luteal n=7. Intervention/exposure: Exercise physiology exposure: 60 minutes of one-legged kicking exercise. Comparator/control: Rested leg and historical/previous male comparison data. Duration/follow-up: Tendon biopsies 72 hours after one-legged kicking exercise.

**Endpoints:** Patellar tendon collagen fractional synthesis rate using labeled proline.

**Safety notes:** No supplement safety data; not applicable.

**Limitations:** No supplement intervention.; Background physiology only.; Male comparison relied on previously obtained data.

**Population mismatch:** Healthy women physiology; not collagen supplementation.

**Why it matters:** It contextualizes why tendon collagen synthesis can vary by sex and may not respond uniformly to loading alone.

**Potential experiment signals:** tendon fractional synthesis rate; sex-specific response; timing of biopsy after exercise

**Protocol takeaway:** Do not use this as supplement evidence; use only to interpret tendon-collagen biomarkers and population variability.
