This source is included for **exercise-performance-recovery**.

**Findings:** Whey increased acute and longer-term MPS more than collagen. Acute MPS was greater with whey than collagen in both rest and exercise legs (P=0.02); longer-term MPS was greater with whey than collagen in both rest and exercise (P<0.001). Collagen did not significantly elevate longer-term MPS above baseline.

**Why it matters:** Important negative comparator for protein-quality and muscle-synthesis claims.

**Potential experiment signals:** acute MPS, longer-term MPS, muscle collagen protein synthesis, plasma leucine.

**Protocol takeaway:** Do not position collagen peptides as a whey-like muscle-building protein source based on MPS physiology.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness and boundary:** `adjacent_variant`. Mechanistic MPS endpoints, not long-term training outcomes.

**Safety notes:** No adverse-event signal extracted from accessible abstract text.

**Limitations:** Comparator/protein-quality mechanistic trial, not an HCP efficacy trial.; Healthy older women only.; Short duration and synthesis endpoints, not long-term body composition.

**Population mismatch:** Older women; comparator is whey protein rather than placebo.
