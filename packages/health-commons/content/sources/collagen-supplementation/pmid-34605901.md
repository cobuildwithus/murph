This source is included for **tendon-loading-ligament**.

**Findings:** The review emphasizes mechanistic rationale but notes limited direct human evidence that collagen-derived peptides stimulate connective-tissue synthesis, crosslinking, or breakdown in vivo.

**Population and intervention:** Narrative review; no single participant population. Intervention/exposure: Collagen protein, gelatin, hydrolyzed collagen, or related collagen-derived protein exposures reviewed. Comparator/control: Varied comparators across reviewed studies. Duration/follow-up: Not applicable.

**Endpoints:** Connective-tissue remodeling, collagen synthesis, peptide/amino acid availability, tendon/ligament/cartilage/bone context.

**Safety notes:** No specific safety finding was extracted for this batch.

**Limitations:** Narrative review, not a systematic quantitative review.; Predates some newer tendon-adaptation trials.; Mechanistic uncertainty should be preserved.

**Population mismatch:** Mechanistic review and not a single direct clinical HCP trial.

**Why it matters:** It provides a cautionary framework for not overclaiming collagen peptide mechanisms from amino acid or peptide availability alone.

**Potential experiment signals:** collagen synthesis markers; connective-tissue remodeling endpoints; peptide bioavailability; tendon and ligament adaptation

**Protocol takeaway:** Mechanistic plausibility exists, but protocol claims should rely on direct trials and explicitly preserve uncertainty about mechanisms.
