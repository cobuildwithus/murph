This source is included for **tendon-loading-ligament**.

**Findings:** Patellar tendon CSA and peak rate of force development increased more with collagen; maximal voluntary force and vastus lateralis thickness improved without group interaction; countermovement jump did not change.

**Population and intervention:** 22 premenopausal international female Master field hockey athletes, about 37 years old; collagen n=10 and placebo n=12. Intervention/exposure: 30 g hydrolyzed collagen plus 500 mg vitamin C before eccentric resistance exercise sessions 3 times per week. Comparator/control: 30 g maltodextrin placebo plus 500 mg vitamin C before the same exercise sessions. Duration/follow-up: 8 weeks of eccentric resistance exercise plus regular hockey training.

**Endpoints:** Patellar tendon cross-sectional area, peak rate of force development, maximal voluntary force, vastus lateralis thickness, countermovement jump.

**Safety notes:** Adverse events were not reported in the extracted record.

**Limitations:** Small elite athlete sample.; Healthy/performance context rather than clinical pain.; Rights-restricted article; extracted details should be verified against full text before final synthesis.

**Population mismatch:** Elite female Master athletes, not general users or tendinopathy patients.

**Why it matters:** This source adds older female athlete evidence and a performance-adjacent rate-of-force-development endpoint.

**Potential experiment signals:** patellar tendon CSA; peak rate of force development; maximal voluntary force; jump performance

**Protocol takeaway:** Collagen may augment tendon size and explosive-force adaptations in trained female athletes, but this is not tendinopathy treatment evidence.
