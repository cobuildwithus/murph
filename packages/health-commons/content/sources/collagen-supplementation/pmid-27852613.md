This source is included for **tendon-loading-ligament**.

**Findings:** 15 g gelatin before activity increased amino acid availability and approximately doubled serum PINP versus placebo; engineered ligament outcomes improved when exposed to post-gelatin serum.

**Population and intervention:** 8 healthy active males aged 18-40 years. Intervention/exposure: 5 g or 15 g vitamin C-enriched gelatin consumed 1 hour before 6 minutes of rope skipping; repeated phase dosed three times daily for three days. Comparator/control: Isocaloric maltodextrin placebo with vitamin C beverage. Duration/follow-up: Acute crossover with repeated 3-day mechanistic phase.

**Endpoints:** Plasma amino acids, serum PINP, engineered ligament collagen content and mechanics.

**Safety notes:** No adverse-event results were extracted; registry eligibility excluded gelatin/fruit-drink allergy.

**Limitations:** Gelatin plus vitamin C is an adjacent mechanism, not hydrolyzed collagen peptide protocol evidence.; Only 8 healthy men.; Acute biomarkers and engineered ligament model, not clinical outcomes.

**Population mismatch:** Healthy active men and ex vivo engineered ligament context, not symptomatic tendon users.

**Why it matters:** This study is often cited for collagen/gelatin timing before tendon-loading activities and should be separated from HCP clinical evidence.

**Potential experiment signals:** serum PINP; plasma glycine/proline/hydroxyproline; engineered ligament collagen content; engineered ligament mechanics

**Protocol takeaway:** Mechanistically, collagen-derived amino acids around loading may influence collagen synthesis markers, but gelatin data should not be used as direct HCP outcome evidence.
