This source is included for **skin-aging**.

**Findings:** Oral collagen studies reported dermatologic benefits across skin-aging and related applications, but formulations and indications varied. Use as a source-finding review rather than a single pooled effect estimate.

**Why it matters:** It is a foundational dermatology review that captures early RCTs and product-form variation.

**Potential experiment signals:** skin hydration, skin elasticity, wrinkles, wound healing, adverse events.

**Protocol takeaway:** Supports cautious skin-outcome interest, but product and population differences must be explicit.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

## Extraction details

- **Source type:** Systematic review (2019).
- **People studied or addressed:** Participants in dermatology studies of oral collagen for pressure ulcers, xerosis, skin aging, cellulite, and related skin outcomes.
- **Intervention or exposure:** Oral collagen supplements, including collagen hydrolysate, collagen tripeptide, and collagen dipeptide products.
- **Comparator or control:** Placebo/control or study-specific comparators.
- **Duration or follow-up:** Collagen hydrolysate studies ranged from 8 to 24 weeks; tripeptide studies ranged from 4 to 12 weeks in accessible abstract summary.
- **Most relevant endpoints:** skin hydration, skin elasticity, wrinkles, wound healing, adverse events.

## Safety notes

Safety details were extracted only where the accessible source text or abstract-level record included them. Otherwise, this page intentionally leaves adverse-event counts unresolved rather than inventing them.

## Limitations and mismatch

Dermatologic indications varied; products included hydrolysate, tripeptide, and dipeptide forms. Population mismatch: Includes pressure ulcers, xerosis, cellulite, and skin aging rather than only healthy skin-aging self-experimenters.
