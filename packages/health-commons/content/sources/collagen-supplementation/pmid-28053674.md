This source is included for **tendon-loading-ligament**.

**Findings:** All groups improved. Primary VISA-A did not show a between-group effect; pain-at-rest reduction favored passive stretching plus supplement versus eccentric training in extracted secondary/subgroup analyses.

**Population and intervention:** 58 randomized adults aged 18-70 years with painful noninsertional midportion Achilles tendinopathy of at least 3 months; intention-to-treat n=55. Intervention/exposure: Eccentric training plus a supplement containing mucopolysaccharides, collagen type I, and vitamin C, or passive stretching plus the same supplement. Comparator/control: Eccentric training alone. Duration/follow-up: 12 weeks.

**Endpoints:** VISA-A, weekly VAS pain, ultrasound tendon thickness, safety.

**Safety notes:** Safety profile was assessed and described favorably, but detailed adverse-event counts were not extracted.

**Limitations:** Multi-ingredient product prevents isolating collagen.; No passive-stretching-only arm.; Limited blinding of exercise therapy.; Subgroups were underpowered.; Industry funding/conflicts were reported.

**Population mismatch:** Clinical Achilles tendinopathy population but adjacent product, not HCP-only.

**Why it matters:** It provides clinical tendon pain context while highlighting the risk of over-interpreting multi-ingredient products.

**Potential experiment signals:** VISA-A; VAS pain at rest and activity; ultrasound tendon thickness; adverse events

**Protocol takeaway:** Adjacent multi-ingredient tendinopathy evidence is mixed and should not be used as direct HCP efficacy evidence.
