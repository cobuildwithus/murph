This source is included for **tendon-loading-ligament**.

**Findings:** Patellar tendon CSA, stiffness, and Young’s modulus increased more with collagen; strength and torque outcomes improved with training but without clear group-by-time effects.

**Population and intervention:** 20 recreationally active middle-aged men, about 47 years old; collagen n=9 and placebo n=11. Intervention/exposure: 30 g hydrolyzed collagen plus 50 mg vitamin C after lower-limb resistance exercise twice weekly. Comparator/control: 30.5 g maltodextrin placebo plus 50 mg vitamin C after the same resistance exercise. Duration/follow-up: 12 weeks of progressive lower-limb resistance exercise.

**Endpoints:** Patellar tendon CSA, tendon stiffness, Young’s modulus, maximal voluntary torque, rate of torque development, 10-RM strength, vastus lateralis thickness.

**Safety notes:** Adverse events were not reported in the extracted record.

**Limitations:** Small sample.; Middle-aged men only.; Healthy training adaptation study rather than tendinopathy treatment.; Supplement given after resistance exercise, which differs from pre-loading protocols.

**Population mismatch:** Middle-aged healthy male sample; not female, older frail, or symptomatic tendon population.

**Why it matters:** This trial addresses middle-aged tendon adaptation, a useful bridge between young athlete data and older users.

**Potential experiment signals:** patellar tendon CSA; patellar tendon stiffness; Young’s modulus; rate of torque development; lower-body 10-RM

**Protocol takeaway:** In middle-aged men, hydrolyzed collagen may enhance tendon structural/mechanical adaptations during progressive resistance training, while strength gains may primarily reflect training.
