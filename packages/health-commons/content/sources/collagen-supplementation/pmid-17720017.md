This source is included for **bone-density-turnover**.

**Findings:** Calcium or calcium plus vitamin D reduced fracture risk and bone loss in older adults, which matters as a co-intervention boundary in collagen bone trials. When collagen trials add calcium/vitamin D, do not attribute all BMD or fracture-related effects to HCP.

**Why it matters:** It anchors the calcium/vitamin D comparator and adjunct issue in collagen bone-health studies.

**Potential experiment signals:** fracture risk, bone mineral density.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use only to flag co-intervention confounding in bone-health claims.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Source type:** Systematic review and meta-analysis (2007).
- **People studied or addressed:** Adults aged 50 years and older in fracture and bone-loss prevention randomized trials.
- **Intervention or exposure:** Calcium alone or calcium plus vitamin D supplementation.
- **Comparator or control:** Placebo or no treatment.
- **Duration or follow-up:** Varied across included trials; review analyzed fracture and bone-loss outcomes.
- **Most relevant endpoints:** fracture risk, bone mineral density.

## Potential experiment signals

- fracture risk
- bone mineral density

## Safety notes

Safety details were extracted only where the accessible source text or abstract-level record included them. Otherwise, this page intentionally leaves adverse-event counts unresolved rather than inventing them.

## Limitations and mismatch

This is not a collagen supplement review and should never be used to support HCP efficacy. Population mismatch: Older adults in calcium/vitamin D trials; no collagen exposure.
