This source is included for **exercise-performance-recovery**.

**Findings:** Compared with placebo, collagen plus training produced larger gains in fat-free mass (+4.2 ± 2.31 kg vs +2.9 ± 1.84 kg), larger quadriceps-strength gains (+16.5 ± 12.9 Nm vs +7.3 ± 13.2 Nm), and larger fat-mass reduction (−5.4 ± 3.17 kg vs −3.5 ± 2.16 kg); reported p<0.05 for these between-group differences.

**Why it matters:** Foundational direct RCT for the training-adjunct/body-composition claim boundary.

**Potential experiment signals:** fat-free mass, fat mass, quadriceps strength, training adherence, adverse events.

**Protocol takeaway:** Collagen peptides may add to resistance-training adaptations in older sarcopenic men, but the evidence is training-adjunct evidence.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

**Directness and boundary:** `direct_protocol`. Do not generalize to stand-alone collagen use or to non-sarcopenic populations.

**Safety notes:** Safety blood parameters and adverse events were monitored; no specific adverse-event signal was extracted from accessible text.

**Limitations:** Older sarcopenic men only; results may not generalize to women, younger adults, or athletes.; Collagen was tested as a resistance-training adjunct, not as a stand-alone supplement.; Dietary intake and training adherence can confound body-composition outcomes.

**Population mismatch:** Clinical sarcopenia population; not a general healthy-athlete population.
