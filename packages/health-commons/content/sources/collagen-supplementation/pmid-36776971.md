This source is included for **tendon-loading-ligament**.

**Findings:** Patellar tendon stiffness and Young’s modulus increased more with collagen; knee extensor strength, vastus lateralis thickness, and tendon cross-sectional area did not clearly change.

**Population and intervention:** 17 high-level female soccer players during the competitive season. Intervention/exposure: 30 g collagen hydrolysate plus 500 mg vitamin C after each training session, 3 days per week. Comparator/control: Energy-matched maltodextrin/fructose placebo plus 500 mg vitamin C. Duration/follow-up: 10 weeks, 3 training-linked doses per week.

**Endpoints:** Patellar tendon stiffness, Young’s modulus, cross-sectional area, knee extensor maximal voluntary isometric contraction, vastus lateralis thickness.

**Safety notes:** Adverse events were not reported in the extracted record.

**Limitations:** Small sample.; Single-blind design with lead researcher unblinded.; In-season training and menstrual-cycle factors may affect tendon outcomes.; No tendon-pain population.

**Population mismatch:** Female athlete adaptation study; not clinical tendinopathy and not general adult users.

**Why it matters:** This source helps anchor female-athlete tendon adaptation evidence, a population often underrepresented in training-nutrition trials.

**Potential experiment signals:** patellar tendon stiffness; patellar tendon Young’s modulus; patellar tendon CSA; knee extensor maximal strength

**Protocol takeaway:** Collagen hydrolysate may complement repeated tendon loading for patellar tendon mechanical properties in female athletes, but not all muscle or tendon size endpoints improved.
