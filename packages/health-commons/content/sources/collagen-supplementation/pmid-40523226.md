This source is included for **exercise-performance-recovery**.

**Findings:** Hydrolyzed collagen and free amino acids increased circulating amino acids, especially glycine, but neither increased myofibrillar nor muscle connective protein synthesis rates versus placebo in rested or exercised legs (P>0.05).

**Why it matters:** Important mechanistic boundary source for HCP-alone acute physiology.

**Potential experiment signals:** plasma glycine, MyoPS, muscle connective protein synthesis.

**Protocol takeaway:** A single 30 g HCP bolus should not be claimed to acutely stimulate muscle or muscle-connective protein synthesis after exercise.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness and boundary:** `same_mechanism`. Does not rule out effects through other pathways or longer dosing/training.

**Safety notes:** No adverse-event signal extracted from accessible abstract text.

**Limitations:** Acute mechanistic trial; not long-term training.; Young recreationally active adults.; Protein synthesis endpoints only.

**Population mismatch:** Young healthy recreationally active adults; mechanistic rather than clinical.
