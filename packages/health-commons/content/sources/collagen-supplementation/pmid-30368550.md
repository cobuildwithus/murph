This source is included for **joint-osteoarthritis**.

**Findings:** Collagen supplementation improved total WOMAC and VAS pain in pooled OA trials, while WOMAC pain and function subscales were not significant. Use as direct OA evidence but preserve its mixed endpoint pattern.

**Why it matters:** It captures older OA RCTs and shows why endpoint-specific wording matters.

**Potential experiment signals:** joint pain, joint function, joint stiffness, womac total.

**Protocol takeaway:** Supports cautious OA symptom framing; do not imply all WOMAC subdomains improved.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

## Extraction details

- **Source type:** Systematic review and meta-analysis (2019).
- **People studied or addressed:** Adults with osteoarthritis in randomized placebo-controlled collagen supplementation trials.
- **Intervention or exposure:** Collagen supplementation; formulations varied across included trials.
- **Comparator or control:** Placebo.
- **Duration or follow-up:** Varied; some trials had durations of 13 weeks or less, which the review flagged as potentially insufficient.
- **Most relevant endpoints:** joint pain, joint function, joint stiffness, womac total.

## Safety notes

Safety details were extracted only where the accessible source text or abstract-level record included them. Otherwise, this page intentionally leaves adverse-event counts unresolved rather than inventing them.

## Limitations and mismatch

Few RCTs, small sample base, mixed collagen formulations, and short durations limit confidence. Population mismatch: Clinical OA trials, not healthy joint-maintenance use.
