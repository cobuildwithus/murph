This source is included for **exercise-performance-recovery**.

**Findings:** Both groups had transient impairment in maximal voluntary contractions after the first session and creatine kinase approximately doubled; training improved CMJ performance by 8% and maximal voluntary dynamic contraction by 10%, and increased serum P1NP by 10%, but no between-group differences were found for any outcome.

**Why it matters:** Negative/boundary exercise-training source.

**Potential experiment signals:** CK, MVIC, MVDC, CMJ, P1NP.

**Protocol takeaway:** Partly substituting whey with collagen peptides did not improve recovery or muscle-damage indices versus the same total protein dose from whey.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness and boundary:** `adjacent_variant`. Does not test collagen versus placebo or collagen without whey.

**Safety notes:** No adverse-event signal extracted from accessible text.

**Limitations:** Adjacent variant: collagen was combined with whey and compared with whey alone.; Young fit males only.; Short training period.; No direct HCP-alone inference.

**Population mismatch:** Fit male protein-substitution cohort; adjacent variant only.
