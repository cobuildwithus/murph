This source is included for **joint-osteoarthritis**.

**Findings:** Collagen hydrolysate was among supplements with large short-term pain reduction, but no supplement showed clinically important long-term pain benefit. Use as comparative OA nutraceutical context, not direct HCP-specific proof.

**Why it matters:** It shows how collagen sits within a broader nutraceutical OA evidence base and avoids selective positive framing.

**Potential experiment signals:** joint pain, joint function, adverse events.

**Protocol takeaway:** Context only; good for comparing short-term versus long-term OA claims.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Source type:** Systematic review and meta-analysis (2018).
- **People studied or addressed:** People with hand, hip, or knee osteoarthritis in dietary-supplement randomized trials.
- **Intervention or exposure:** Dietary supplements, including collagen hydrolysate and undenatured type II collagen among many other supplements.
- **Comparator or control:** Placebo/control.
- **Duration or follow-up:** Short-, medium-, and long-term outcomes across included trials.
- **Most relevant endpoints:** joint pain, joint function, adverse events.

## Limitations and mismatch

Broad supplement scope and mixed OA sites/products; evidence certainty varied widely. Population mismatch: OA patients and many non-collagen supplements; not HCP-specific.
