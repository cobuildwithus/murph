This source is included for **tendon-loading-ligament**.

**Findings:** PINP AUC increased in a dose-response pattern: both 15 g and 30 g exceeded 0 g, and 30 g exceeded 15 g. β-CTX decreased independent of dose.

**Population and intervention:** 8 resistance-trained middle-aged men, about 49 years old. Intervention/exposure: 15 g or 30 g vitamin C-enriched hydrolyzed collagen before lower-limb resistance exercise. Comparator/control: 0 g hydrolyzed collagen control condition. Duration/follow-up: Acute crossover response around lower-limb resistance exercise.

**Endpoints:** Serum PINP AUC, β-CTX, plasma amino acid availability.

**Safety notes:** Adverse events were not reported in the extracted record.

**Limitations:** Small male-only acute physiology study.; Biomarker outcome, not clinical or tendon-structural outcome.; Vitamin C-enriched hydrolyzed collagen cointervention.

**Population mismatch:** Middle-aged trained men; not symptomatic tendon population.

**Why it matters:** This companion physiology source suggests dose-response signals may differ by age/training status and helps contextualize 15 g vs 30 g dosing.

**Potential experiment signals:** serum PINP AUC; β-CTX; plasma amino acid AUC; dose response

**Protocol takeaway:** Mechanistic evidence supports timed pre-exercise HCP dosing in middle-aged men, but it remains outcome-adjacent.
