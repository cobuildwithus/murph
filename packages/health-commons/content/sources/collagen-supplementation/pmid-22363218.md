This source is included for **safety-quality-contaminants**.

**Findings:** Population/exposure: 4-week-old Sprague-Dawley rats, 20 males and 20 females per group across five diet groups Intervention or exposure: Marine collagen peptides from chum salmon skin incorporated into feed at 2.25%, 4.5%, 9%, or 18% wt/wt. Comparator/control: Control diet containing 0% marine collagen peptides. Duration/follow-up: 24 months Endpoints: survival, body weight, food consumption, urinalysis, clinical biochemistry, hematology, organ weights, gross pathology, histopathology. Direction/effect: No toxicologically significant differences versus control were reported through the chronic feeding period; no liver or kidney target-organ toxicity was identified. Safety notes: No treatment-related adverse toxicity signal was reported at dietary intakes up to 18% wt/wt. Limitations: Animal toxicology study, not a human tolerability trial.; Marine chum-salmon collagen peptide preparation may not match bovine, porcine, mixed-source, or branded hydrolyzed collagen supplements.; Does not address allergy in fish-allergic humans or contaminants in commercial supplement lots.. Population mismatch: Sprague-Dawley rats rather than human supplement users.

**Why it matters:** Provides a toxicology boundary for marine collagen peptides, while remaining animal-only safety context rather than human efficacy evidence.
