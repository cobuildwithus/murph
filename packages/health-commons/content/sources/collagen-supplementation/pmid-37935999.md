This source is included for **exercise-performance-recovery**.

**Findings:** Running distance improved more with collagen (1727 ± 705 m) than placebo (1018 ± 976 m), p≤0.05; velocity at lactate threshold improved more (0.680 ± 1.27 vs −0.135 ± 0.978 km/h), p≤0.05; IAT velocity improved more (1.660 ± 1.022 vs 0.606 ± 0.974 km/h), p≤0.01; no significant group differences in fat mass or fat-free mass.

**Why it matters:** Direct but adjacent-to-resistance exercise performance evidence.

**Potential experiment signals:** 12-minute run distance, lactate threshold velocity, individual anaerobic threshold velocity, fat-free mass, fat mass.

**Protocol takeaway:** Collagen peptides may improve selected endurance-performance indicators during concurrent training in men, while body-composition effects were not separated.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

**Directness and boundary:** `direct_protocol`. Do not merge with resistance-training hypertrophy claims.

**Safety notes:** No adverse-event signal extracted from accessible article text.

**Limitations:** Moderately trained men only.; Concurrent training co-intervention; no stand-alone collagen effect.; Product-specific collagen peptides.

**Population mismatch:** Male moderately trained concurrent-training cohort; not resistance-only and not sedentary/clinical.
