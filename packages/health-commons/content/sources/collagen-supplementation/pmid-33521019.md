This source is included for **skin-aging**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial with climate-adjusted analyses.
- **People studied or addressed:** Women aged 40–60 years; 74 completed the trial.
- **Intervention/exposure:** 1 g/day collagen tripeptide from Nile tilapia skin.
- **Comparator/control:** Placebo.
- **Duration/follow-up:** 12 weeks with baseline, 6-week, and 12-week assessments.
- **Role in Murph:** direct protocol evidence for Hydrolyzed Collagen Peptides.

**Findings:** TEWL decreased more in the collagen-tripeptide group after 12 weeks and remained significant after climate/UVA adjustment in accessible reports. Hydration/moisture improved more in the collagen group, but the clearest statistically preserved signal in accessible summaries was TEWL. Under-50 subgroup analyses suggested additional subjective moisture/skin-score signals.

**Adverse events or safety:** No notable adverse events were reported in accessible summaries.

**Why it matters:** Direct trial explicitly models environmental confounding for skin hydration/TEWL outcomes.

**Potential experiment signals:**

- skin hydration/moisture
- transepidermal water loss
- skin elasticity
- subjective skin scores
- temperature, humidity, and UVA-adjusted analyses

**Protocol takeaway:** Supports TEWL/barrier tracking and shows why humidity, season, and UV context should be logged for skin experiments.

**Limitations and population fit:**

- Women aged 40–60 only.
- Some findings depend on climate-adjusted or subgroup analyses.
- Hydration signal should be kept distinct from TEWL until full table extraction is reviewed.

**Population mismatch:** Women aged 40–60 years; 74 completed the trial may not match all Murph users; do not generalize to different ages, sexes, baseline skin status, or collagen product types without caveats.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.
