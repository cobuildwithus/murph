This source is included for **tendon-loading-ligament**.

**Findings:** Preclinical studies suggested increased type I collagen synthesis and reduced oxidative stress; clinical evidence was limited and did not replicate the preclinical strength.

**Population and intervention:** 10 included studies across animal and human musculoskeletal injury contexts. Intervention/exposure: Vitamin C supplementation or vitamin C-related interventions in injury/healing contexts. Comparator/control: Varied comparators across included studies. Duration/follow-up: Varied by included study.

**Endpoints:** Collagen synthesis, oxidative stress, bone/tendon/ligament healing markers, adverse effects.

**Safety notes:** No adverse effects were reported in the included animal or human studies according to the extracted review record.

**Limitations:** Vitamin C review, not HCP evidence.; Mostly preclinical and heterogeneous evidence.; No direct clinical tendon-healing evidence sufficient for protocol efficacy claims.

**Population mismatch:** Mechanistic/injury context; not hydrolyzed collagen peptide supplementation.

**Why it matters:** Many tendon-loading collagen protocols co-administer vitamin C; this review helps bound what can and cannot be inferred about that component.

**Potential experiment signals:** type I collagen synthesis; oxidative stress markers; injury healing outcomes; adverse effects

**Protocol takeaway:** Vitamin C co-administration has mechanistic rationale, but vitamin C evidence alone does not establish HCP efficacy.
