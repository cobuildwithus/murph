This source is included for **safety-quality-contaminants**.

**Findings:** Population/exposure: Animal bone broth preparations and three commercial bone-broth-based foods; no human participants Intervention or exposure: Bone broth cooking conditions varying acidity, bone type, animal species, and cooking time. Comparator/control: Different broth-preparation conditions and commercial bone-broth-based foods. Duration/follow-up: Cooking experiments varied by recipe and cooking time; no human follow-up Endpoints: calcium, magnesium, lead, cadmium. Direction/effect: Acidity and longer cooking increased calcium and magnesium extraction, but minerals per serving remained low; lead and cadmium were reported at low microgram-per-serving levels in the tested products. Safety notes: No human adverse events; contaminant exposure was assessed analytically. Limitations: Bone broth is adjacent to, not the same as, hydrolyzed collagen peptides.; Recipe and product sampling may not represent all bone broths.; No human biomarker or clinical outcomes.. Population mismatch: Food preparations rather than supplement users.

**Why it matters:** Supports adjacent-variant boundaries around bone broth and prevents treating it as interchangeable with collagen peptide supplements.
