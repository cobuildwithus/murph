This source is included for **joint-osteoarthritis**.

**Findings:**

- **mechanism — Absorption/cartilage mechanism hypothesis:** Use mechanistic rationale only with a review-context label. Effect/direction: Review reports orally administered collagen hydrolysate is absorbed and may accumulate in cartilage; mechanism evidence is not clinical causality.
- **outcome — Reviewed clinical evidence:** Historical evidence context supports why the protocol is studied but not a primary effect estimate. Effect/direction: Review found some early open-label and double-blind studies reported pain/function improvements, but many lacked statistical detail.
- **safety — Reviewed tolerability:** Use for background safety context only; no pooled AE ledger extracted. Effect/direction: Review described collagen hydrolysate as generally safe/well tolerated in the early literature.
- **limitation — Narrative review boundary:** Do not substitute this source for primary-trial claims. Effect/direction: Narrative review is not primary RCT evidence and predates modern trial/reporting standards.

**Why it matters:** Foundational review often cited in the collagen hydrolysate joint-health literature.

**Potential experiment signals:** joint pain, joint function, cartilage accumulation/mechanism, safety/tolerability.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use only for background: direct protocol claims should come from primary RCTs.

**Murph takeaway:** Mechanism plausibility does not prove personal symptom response.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness and boundary:** `background`. Background review spanning OA and other joint disorders; not direct protocol trial evidence.

**Safety notes:** Review described collagen hydrolysate as safe/well tolerated in the reviewed literature; detailed pooled adverse-event extraction was not performed here.

**Limitations:**

- Narrative review; not a source of primary effect estimates.
- Pre-2006 evidence base had limited statistical detail and small/heterogeneous studies.
- Mechanistic claims should not be promoted to clinical causality.
