This source is included for **skin-aging**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled dose study.
- **People studied or addressed:** Healthy Japanese women aged 35–50 years.
- **Intervention/exposure:** 1 g/day or 5 g/day collagen peptides.
- **Comparator/control:** Placebo.
- **Duration/follow-up:** 12 weeks with assessments at baseline and weeks 4, 8, and 12.
- **Role in Murph:** direct protocol evidence for Hydrolyzed Collagen Peptides.

**Findings:** Collagen peptides increased skin water content and decreased TEWL in accessible summaries. Natural moisturizing factor content in the stratum corneum increased, supporting a hydration mechanism. Skin elasticity and thickness were reported as unchanged.

**Adverse events or safety:** Adverse-event details were not extracted from accessible materials.

**Why it matters:** Direct hydration/barrier trial with a mechanistic stratum-corneum endpoint.

**Potential experiment signals:**

- stratum-corneum and epidermal water content
- transepidermal water loss
- natural moisturizing factor in the stratum corneum
- skin elasticity
- skin thickness

**Protocol takeaway:** Supports hydration and barrier-mechanism claims, but explicitly preserves null elasticity and thickness findings.

**Limitations and population fit:**

- Paywalled primary article limits full extraction.
- Healthy Japanese women aged 35–50 only.
- Hydration/NMF findings should not be converted into wrinkle or firmness claims.
- Elasticity and thickness were null.

**Population mismatch:** Healthy Japanese women aged 35–50 years may not match all Murph users; do not generalize to different ages, sexes, baseline skin status, or collagen product types without caveats.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.
