This source is included for **joint-osteoarthritis**.

**Findings:** Collagen peptides improved knee-OA pain in pooled RCTs, while adverse-event risk was not statistically different from placebo. Use as direct knee-OA pain evidence while retaining risk-of-bias and safety caveats.

**Why it matters:** It directly addresses knee-OA analgesic outcomes and adverse-event comparisons.

**Potential experiment signals:** joint pain, joint function, adverse events.

**Protocol takeaway:** Supports cautious knee-OA pain claims, not generalized joint-health or disease-modification claims.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

## Extraction details

- **Source type:** Systematic review and meta-analysis (2023).
- **People studied or addressed:** Adults with knee osteoarthritis in randomized controlled trials.
- **Intervention or exposure:** Collagen peptides or hydrolyzed collagen.
- **Comparator or control:** Placebo.
- **Duration or follow-up:** Varied across RCTs; search through 27 May 2023.
- **Most relevant endpoints:** joint pain, joint function, adverse events.

## Safety notes

Safety details were extracted only where the accessible source text or abstract-level record included them. Otherwise, this page intentionally leaves adverse-event counts unresolved rather than inventing them.

## Limitations and mismatch

The batch extraction did not recover every pooled effect estimate; risk of bias and small-study issues need primary-trial audit. Population mismatch: Clinical knee-OA patients; not general healthy joint maintenance.
