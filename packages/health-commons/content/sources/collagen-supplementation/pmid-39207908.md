This source is included for **tendon-loading-ligament**.

**Findings:** Patellar tendon stiffness and Young’s modulus increased more with collagen; patellar tendon CSA increased over time without a group interaction.

**Population and intervention:** 11 professional female soccer athletes; collagen n=6 and placebo n=5. Intervention/exposure: 30 g hydrolyzed collagen plus 500 mg vitamin C before each training session, 3 days per week. Comparator/control: Energy-matched placebo plus 500 mg vitamin C before the same training sessions. Duration/follow-up: 10 weeks of preseason high-intensity training exposure.

**Endpoints:** Patellar tendon stiffness, Young’s modulus, cross-sectional area, strength and performance-adjacent measures.

**Safety notes:** Adverse events were not reported in the extracted record.

**Limitations:** Very small sample.; Professional female athletes only.; Surrogate tendon mechanics rather than pain or injury endpoints.

**Population mismatch:** Elite female athlete training study; not general adults or symptomatic tendinopathy treatment.

**Why it matters:** This source extends female-athlete evidence into professional sport and high-intensity preseason loading.

**Potential experiment signals:** patellar tendon stiffness; Young’s modulus; patellar tendon CSA; training session timing

**Protocol takeaway:** Hydrolyzed collagen may support patellar tendon mechanical adaptation when paired with structured high-intensity loading in elite women, but sample size is a major constraint.
