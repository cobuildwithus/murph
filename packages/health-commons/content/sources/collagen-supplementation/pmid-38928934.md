This source is included for **joint-osteoarthritis**.

**Findings:**

- **dose — Dose and duration:** Schulze 2024 supports a 5 g/day daily-activity discomfort dose. Effect/direction: 5 g/day specific collagen peptides for 12 weeks.
- **outcome — Lower-extremity daily-activity pain:** Specific collagen peptides improved selected daily-activity pain/restriction endpoints. Effect/direction: Physician pain at rest p=0.018 and walking p=0.032 favored collagen; participant stair-climbing p=0.040, kneeling p<0.001, squatting restriction p=0.014 favored collagen.
- **population — Population mismatch:** Use as functional discomfort evidence rather than OA evidence. Effect/direction: Healthy adults with functional knee/hip pain, not diagnosed OA.
- **limitation — Clinical meaningfulness:** Protocol claims should avoid overstating magnitude until MCID context is checked. Effect/direction: Statistical significance on NRS activities may not equal clinically meaningful change.

**Why it matters:** Recent open-access RCT extends collagen-peptide joint discomfort evidence beyond athletes to daily activities.

**Potential experiment signals:** NRS pain at rest, NRS walking pain, stair-climbing pain, kneeling pain, squatting restriction.

**Protocol takeaway:** 5 g/day for 12 weeks may reduce selected daily-activity joint discomfort in otherwise healthy adults.

**Murph takeaway:** Use standardized activities such as stairs, walking, kneeling, and squatting as repeatable signals.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

**Directness and boundary:** `direct_protocol`. Healthy functional joint-discomfort population, not diagnosed OA.

**Safety notes:** No detailed adverse-event extraction available from accessible summaries.

**Limitations:**

- Functional knee/hip pain population rather than diagnosed OA.
- Clinical meaningfulness of statistically significant NRS differences should be reviewed before strong claims.
