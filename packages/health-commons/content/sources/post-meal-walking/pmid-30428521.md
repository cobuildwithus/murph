This source is included for **direct-core-glycemic-dose-timing**.

**Findings:** Activity begun later after the carbohydrate challenge changed the glycemic response and reduced mean blood glucose at 60 minutes; early activity did not show the same effect. Because the intervention was light cycling, the result is timing-context evidence rather than direct walking evidence.

**Why it matters:** It informs timing-window decisions but must be separated from direct walking claims because the tested modality was light cycling.

**Potential experiment signals:** Post-meal glycemic response, Timing of activity onset, Mean blood glucose at 60 minutes, Healthy-adult carbohydrate challenge response.

**Protocol takeaway:** Adjacent timing evidence suggests waiting until around 30 minutes after eating may reduce glucose response more than very early light activity in healthy adults, but this was not a walking trial.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Healthy adults.
- **Intervention/exposure:** light cycling after eating (adjacent to walking)
- **Comparator/control:** Sedentary control; adjacent active timing arms.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Acute white-bread carbohydrate challenge with 10 minutes of light cycling started at different post-baseline times.
- **Endpoints:** Post-meal glycemic response, Timing of activity onset, Mean blood glucose at 60 minutes, Healthy-adult carbohydrate challenge response
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse-event signal was extracted from the accessible record.
- **Limitations:** Cycling rather than walking; healthy adults; acute white-bread challenge; not an every-meal protocol.
- **Population mismatch:** Healthy-adult timing evidence; adjacent modality and population mismatch for diabetes-focused walking claims.
- **Artifact candidates and rights:** Open-access MDPI/PMC article; preserve license metadata if archiving full text.
