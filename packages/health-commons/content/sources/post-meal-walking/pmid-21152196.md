This source is included for **safety-older-pregnancy-pediatric-hypotension**.

**Findings:** PPH was much more frequent in geriatric patients than healthy elderly controls, and detection changed by threshold and measurement interval.

**Why it matters:** Shows that older-adult post-meal hypotension risk depends strongly on frailty/clinical selection and diagnostic definition.

**Potential experiment signals:** SBP fall ≥20 mmHg after meals, PPH symptoms, measurement timing after meals.

**Protocol takeaway:** In older adults, especially frail geriatric patients, post-meal walking should be bounded by hypotension screening and symptom history.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Clinical geriatric patients and healthy elderly individuals.
- **Participant count:** 42 participants: 22 geriatric patients and 20 healthy elderly individuals.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Postprandial blood-pressure measurement during and after breakfast.
- **Comparator/control:** Geriatric patients versus healthy elderly participants; ≥20 mmHg versus >30 mmHg diagnostic thresholds.
- **Duration/follow-up:** During and after a breakfast challenge; monitoring schedule included frequent beat-to-beat measures.
- **Endpoints:** SBP fall ≥20 mmHg after meals, PPH symptoms, measurement timing after meals
- **Effect estimates or direction:** PPH by ≥20 mmHg SBP fall occurred in 91% of geriatric patients and 40% of healthy elderly; by >30 mmHg it occurred in 45% and 15%, respectively.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** Sparse BP checks may miss PPH; symptoms and BP changes do not align perfectly across diagnostic thresholds.
- **Limitations:** Small selected sample; Breakfast-only test; Not walking-specific
- **Population mismatch:** Safety-boundary evidence rather than routine adult/general wellness protocol evidence.
- **Directness to Walking After Every Meal:** safety_boundary
