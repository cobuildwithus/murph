This source is included for **secondary-metabolism-lipids-insulin-cgm**.

**Findings:** A 7-day feasibility study found CGM acceptable in healthy free-living adults, supporting feasibility context but not efficacy claims.

**Why it matters:** Important for interpreting burden and acceptability of CGM-based post-meal walking experiments.

**Potential experiment signals:** CGM acceptability, device usefulness and relevance, wearable-biosensor feasibility for diet and physical activity research.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as implementation context only; it does not support metabolic claims.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Nondiabetic free-living adults.

- **Participant count:** 30

- **Intervention/exposure:** Use of a Dexcom G4 CGM sensor, receiver, MyFitnessPal dietary logging, and accelerometer in free-living research.

- **Comparator/control:** No efficacy comparator; acceptability/usability study.

- **Duration/follow-up:** Seven days of CGM, diet/app, and accelerometer use.

- **Endpoints:** CGM acceptability; device usefulness and relevance; wearable-biosensor feasibility for diet and physical activity research

- **Effect estimates or direction:** More than 90% of participants agreed CGM was easy to use, useful, and relevant in extracted summaries. Acceptability scores were about 4.06 ± 0.55 for the CGM sensor, 4.05 ± 0.58 for the receiver, 4.10 ± 0.68 for MyFitnessPal, and 3.73 ± 0.76 for the accelerometer.

- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No intervention efficacy or adverse-event endpoint was extracted; the source is primarily burden/acceptability context.

- **Limitations:** Not an intervention trial; Healthy/nondiabetic sample; Acceptability rather than metabolic efficacy; Older-generation CGM and device ecosystem; Potential self-selection bias

- **Population mismatch:** CGM acceptability source, not postmeal walking efficacy evidence.
