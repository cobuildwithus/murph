This source is included for **sedentary-breaks-standing-micro-walks**.

**Findings:** In healthy normal-weight adults, frequent brief walking interruptions reduced postprandial glycemia during a laboratory sitting protocol.

**Why it matters:** It anchors the idea that frequent short walking bouts may be metabolically meaningful even when total activity is modest.

**Potential experiment signals:** Postprandial glucose, Insulin, Triglycerides.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as dose/frequency context only; do not turn it into direct every-meal advice.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Healthy, normal-weight adults.
- **Participant count:** 70 healthy normal-weight adults.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Walking breaks of about 1 minute 40 seconds every 30 minutes during prolonged sitting.
- **Comparator/control:** Uninterrupted sitting and a continuous walking bout.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Single-day acute laboratory postprandial protocol spanning repeated meal/testing windows.
- **Endpoints:** Postprandial glucose, Insulin, Triglycerides
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Regular short walking breaks reduced postprandial glycemia compared with prolonged sitting and were compared with a continuous bout; exact estimates were not restated in this extraction.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse-event signal was extracted from available metadata.
- **Limitations:** Healthy normal-weight adults; Laboratory sitting-break design; Short walking interruptions are not meal-triggered every-meal walks
- **Population mismatch:** Adjacent variant: frequent micro-walk sitting breaks, not a simple walk after every meal.
