## Findings
- Stair ascending-descending exercise produced a greater short-window blood-glucose decrease than bicycle exercise.
- Serum insulin did not differ between exercise conditions in accessible extraction.

## Why it matters
It highlights that non-walking substitutions can differ meaningfully in glucose response and practical safety burden.

## Potential experiment signals
- Blood glucose reduction between 90 and 105 minutes
- Net blood glucose reduction
- Serum insulin
- Blood lactate
- Respiratory exchange ratio

## Protocol takeaway
Use as adjacent substitution evidence only; stair prescriptions have different feasibility and fall-risk considerations than walking.

## Claim use
`context-only`.

## Extraction details
- **Source kind:** journal_article
- **Study design:** experimental_exercise_comparison (Acute stair versus bicycle postprandial exercise comparison)
- **Participant count:** 14 (reported)
- **Population:** Seven people with type 2 diabetes and seven people with impaired glucose tolerance.
- **Intervention or exposure:** Stair ascending-descending exercise after a meal.
- **Comparator or control:** Bicycle exercise after a meal.
- **Duration or follow-up:** Acute post-meal window, especially 90-105 minutes after meal.
- **Endpoints:** Blood glucose reduction and insulin response.
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Blood glucose reduction between 90 and 105 minutes was greater with stair exercise (-4.0±0.7 mmol/L) than bicycle exercise (-2.7±0.9 mmol/L); net reduction was also greater (-3.2±0.7 vs -2.0±0.6 mmol/L). Serum insulin did not differ.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse-event counts were extracted; stair activity has a different practical safety profile than level walking.
- **Population mismatch:** T2D/IGT population is metabolically relevant, but stairs are not walking and may be less accessible/safe for some users.
- **Directness to Walking After Every Meal:** adjacent_variant
- **Artifact candidates / rights:** PMCID is available; rights status in ledger is unknown, so do not commit PDF unless license is verified. `pdfRightsStatus=unknown`.

## Limitations
- Stair exercise, not walking.
- Acute comparison and small cohort.
- Mixed T2D/IGT population.
- Different fall and exertion burden from casual walking.
