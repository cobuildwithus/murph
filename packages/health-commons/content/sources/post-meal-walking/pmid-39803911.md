This source is included for **sedentary-breaks-standing-micro-walks**.

**Findings:** In university students, intermittent standing did not acutely improve postprandial glucose, insulin, or triglyceride metabolism compared with sitting in extracted metadata.

**Why it matters:** Recent negative standing-only source to prevent overclaiming posture replacement.

**Potential experiment signals:** Postprandial glucose, Insulin, Triglycerides.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as a boundary/null source for standing, not as walking evidence.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** University students or young normal-weight adults.
- **Participant count:** Participant count not resolved in the extracted record.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Intermittent standing after prolonged sitting or meal exposure.
- **Comparator/control:** Prolonged sitting.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Acute postprandial metabolism protocol.
- **Endpoints:** Postprandial glucose, Insulin, Triglycerides
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Intermittent standing did not acutely improve postprandial metabolism in extracted metadata.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** A source-context safety note mentioned heightened fainting risk during standing blood samples; this should be treated as procedural caution rather than a generalized adverse-event rate.
- **Limitations:** Standing-only intervention; Young university-student sample; Sample count and full adverse-event detail not resolved; No walking arm
