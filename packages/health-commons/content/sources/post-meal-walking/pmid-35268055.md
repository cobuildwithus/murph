This source is included for **direct-core-glycemic-dose-timing**.

**Findings:** Thirty minutes of brisk walking begun after ingestion lowered glucose peaks after carbohydrate drinks and mixed meals. AUC results were more mixed: effects differed across low- versus high-carbohydrate loads and mixed-meal versus glucose-drink conditions.

**Why it matters:** It helps bound when post-meal walking affects peak glucose versus total AUC and whether meal composition modifies the response.

**Potential experiment signals:** Glucose peak, 0-60 minute iAUC, 0-120 minute iAUC, Meal-composition subgroup response.

**Protocol takeaway:** Post-meal walking reliably lowered glucose peaks in healthy participants, but AUC effects varied by meal type, carbohydrate dose, and time window.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Healthy young volunteers; Study 1 included 10 participants and Study 2 included 11 participants.
- **Intervention/exposure:** brisk postprandial walking
- **Comparator/control:** Resting control after matched meal or glucose drink.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Acute meal tests with 30 minutes of brisk walking starting about 15 minutes after meal or glucose-drink ingestion.
- **Endpoints:** Glucose peak, 0-60 minute iAUC, 0-120 minute iAUC, Meal-composition subgroup response
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No major adverse-event signal was extracted; the sample was healthy and young.
- **Limitations:** Healthy young participants; acute lab meal tests; small study-specific samples; not an every-meal or clinical T2D protocol.
- **Population mismatch:** Direct walking modality, but population is healthier and younger than many glycemic-risk users.
- **Artifact candidates and rights:** Open-access MDPI/PMC article; preserve license metadata if full text is archived.
