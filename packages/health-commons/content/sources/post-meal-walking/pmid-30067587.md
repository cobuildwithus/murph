This source is included for **reviews-meta-analyses-mechanisms**.

**Findings:** Systematic review of exercise timing in type 2 diabetes that found more consistent glycemic and insulin responses when exercise occurred after meals, while noting sparse direct timing comparisons.

**Why it matters:** It helps separate premeal, postmeal, and broader time-of-day exercise timing claims in T2D.

**Potential experiment signals:** Glucose concentration; Glucose AUC; Insulin AUC; Timing after meal.

**Protocol takeaway:** Post-meal exercise timing appears promising in T2D, but direct timing evidence is too sparse to declare one exact after-meal rule.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Adults with type 2 diabetes in randomized controlled trials of exercise timing.
- **Participant count:** 346
- **Participant count kind:** `reported_total_across_included_trials`
- **Intervention/exposure:** Exercise performed before meals, after meals, fasting, or at different times of day depending on included study.
- **Comparator/control:** Alternative timing or no-exercise controls.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Acute and short-term exercise timing interventions; follow-up varied by included trial.
- **Endpoints:** Glucose concentration; Glucose AUC; Insulin AUC; Timing after meal
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Improvements in glucose concentration/AUC and insulin AUC appeared more consistent for postmeal exercise than premeal exercise, but direct head-to-head timing evidence was limited.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No batch-level adverse-event rate was extracted.
- **Limitations:** Only two direct timing-comparison studies; Methodological heterogeneity; T2D population; Not specific to walking after every meal
- **Population mismatch:** T2D-only timing review; not a general-population protocol and not limited to walking.
- **Directness to Walking After Every Meal:** `same_mechanism`.
