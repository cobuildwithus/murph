This source is included for **sedentary-breaks-standing-micro-walks**.

**Findings:** In dysglycemic postmenopausal women, breaking sitting with standing or light walking attenuated postprandial metabolic responses compared with unbroken sitting.

**Why it matters:** It preserves higher-risk female comparator evidence, including a standing-positive result.

**Potential experiment signals:** Postprandial glucose, Insulin, Nonesterified fatty acids, Triglycerides.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use for context on modality and population boundaries; do not claim direct protocol support.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Overweight or obese postmenopausal women with dysglycemia or high cardiometabolic risk.
- **Participant count:** 22 overweight or obese dysglycemic postmenopausal women.
- **Intervention/exposure:** 5-minute standing breaks or 5-minute light walking breaks every 30 minutes during prolonged sitting.
- **Comparator/control:** Unbroken prolonged sitting.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Acute 7.5-hour laboratory day with follow-up into the next day in extracted notes.
- **Endpoints:** Postprandial glucose, Insulin, Nonesterified fatty acids, Triglycerides
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Standing and walking attenuated postprandial metabolic responses versus sitting; extracted notes indicate walking affected insulin more clearly and effects persisted into the next day.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse-event signal was extracted from available metadata.
- **Limitations:** Postmenopausal high-risk female sample; Acute sitting-break design; Standing/walking breaks not framed as after-every-meal walking
- **Population mismatch:** Adjacent variant: standing/walking interruptions to sitting in dysglycemic postmenopausal women, not direct every-meal walking.
