## Findings
- Both walking and control groups improved multiple symptoms from baseline, including belching, flatus, postprandial fullness/bloating, gas incontinence, and abdominal discomfort/pain (p<0.001 within groups). [source_artifact:pmid-33868611]
- Between-group changes were generally not significant except postprandial epigastric fullness/bloating, which favored walking (p=0.002). [source_artifact:pmid-33868611]

## Extraction details
- **Population:** Adults with functional abdominal bloating; 24 men and 70 women in the extracted report, mean age about 44 years.
- **Intervention or exposure:** A 10- to 15-minute walk after each meal for 4 weeks.
- **Comparator or control:** Medication-based control in the extracted report: daily domperidone plus activated dimethicone.
- **Duration or follow-up:** 4 weeks.
- **Endpoints:** belching; flatus; postprandial epigastric fullness/bloating; gas incontinence; abdominal discomfort or pain
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Both walking and control groups improved multiple symptoms from baseline, including belching, flatus, postprandial fullness/bloating, gas incontinence, and abdominal discomfort/pain (p<0.001 within groups). Between-group changes were generally not significant except postprandial epigastric fullness/bloating, which favored walking (p=0.002).
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No specific walking-related adverse events were extracted from accessible text; limitations emphasized monitoring and subjective outcomes.
- **Population mismatch:** Functional bloating population and medication comparator; not general glycemic or reflux protocol evidence.
- **Directness to Walking After Every Meal:** same_mechanism
- **Claim-use boundary:** context-only
- **Artifact rights status:** open_access

## Limitations
- No monitoring during the study period.
- Only end-of-study assessment after 4 weeks.
- Subjective symptom outcomes.
- Bloating is multifactorial and the control treatment was not a standard universal comparator.
