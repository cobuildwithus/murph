This source is included for **sedentary-breaks-standing-micro-walks**.

**Findings:** In a small crossover trial, 2-minute walking breaks every 20 minutes lowered postprandial glucose AUC compared with both sitting and standing, while standing did not differ from sitting.

**Why it matters:** Core comparator showing walking breaks can outperform standing breaks for postprandial glucose in a small acute setting.

**Potential experiment signals:** Postprandial glucose, Insulin, Blood pressure, Lipids.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context that walking may be different from standing; do not present as direct every-meal walking evidence.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Healthy, non-obese adults in an acute laboratory sitting protocol.
- **Participant count:** 10 healthy non-obese adults.
- **Intervention/exposure:** 2-minute light-intensity walking every 20 minutes.
- **Comparator/control:** Uninterrupted sitting and 2-minute standing every 20 minutes.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Five-hour acute postprandial protocol.
- **Endpoints:** Postprandial glucose, Insulin, Blood pressure, Lipids
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Glucose AUC was lower with walking than sitting or standing; the extracted abstract reported glucose AUC 18.5 mmol·L/5 h for walking versus 22.0 for sitting and 22.2 for standing. Standing did not differ from sitting.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse-event signal was extracted from available metadata.
- **Limitations:** Very small healthy sample; Frequent sitting interruptions rather than meal-triggered walking; Short laboratory follow-up
- **Population mismatch:** Adjacent variant: walking breaks every 20 minutes rather than walks after every meal.
