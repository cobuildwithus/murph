This source is included for **secondary-metabolism-lipids-insulin-cgm**.

**Findings:** Ten 3-minute brisk-walking bouts lowered postprandial triglyceride AUC similarly to one 30-minute bout in healthy young men.

**Why it matters:** Supports short-bout accumulation as a plausible lipid mechanism while remaining context-only for this protocol.

**Potential experiment signals:** postprandial plasma triacylglycerol AUC, resting systolic blood pressure.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as short-bout lipid context; do not generalize to after-every-meal glucose claims.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Fifteen healthy young men.

- **Participant count:** 15

- **Intervention/exposure:** Either ten 3-minute brisk-walking bouts or one 30-minute brisk walk matched for gross energy expenditure.

- **Comparator/control:** No-exercise control trial.

- **Duration/follow-up:** Three 2-day trials with high-fat meal testing on the second day.

- **Endpoints:** postprandial plasma triacylglycerol AUC; resting systolic blood pressure

- **Effect estimates or direction:** Triacylglycerol AUC was 16% lower after accumulated and continuous walking versus control (9.98 ± 0.67 and 9.99 ± 0.76 vs 11.90 ± 1.02 mmol*7h/L; p=0.005). Resting systolic blood pressure was 6–7% lower after both walking patterns.

- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse-event signal was extracted from the available source record.

- **Limitations:** Healthy young men only; Walking timing was adjacent to a test-meal protocol rather than after every meal; Lipid endpoint, not glucose-focused; Acute laboratory design

- **Population mismatch:** Same walking-bout mechanism but not direct after-each-meal protocol evidence.

- **Directness to Walking After Every Meal:** same_mechanism
