This source is included for **direct-core-glycemic-dose-timing**.

**Findings:** All three walking programs were completed. Fast walking produced intensity-dependent improvement in postprandial glucose excursions compared with natural walking.

**Why it matters:** It supports an intensity gradient for post-meal walking: faster walking may attenuate glucose excursions more than natural pace in type 2 diabetes.

**Potential experiment signals:** Post-lunch blood glucose, Walking pace/intensity, Heart rate, Blood pressure, Adverse effects.

**Protocol takeaway:** For people who can do it safely, brisker post-meal walking may improve glucose lowering compared with natural pace, but this was an acute lunch-only study.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Hospitalized adults with type 2 diabetes undergoing educational admission.
- **Intervention/exposure:** fast postprandial walking
- **Comparator/control:** Natural walking pace versus faster walking paces.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Three acute post-lunch walking sessions separated by 1-day washouts; 30 minutes of walking started 1 hour after lunch.
- **Endpoints:** Post-lunch blood glucose, Walking pace/intensity, Heart rate, Blood pressure, Adverse effects
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** All participants completed the walking programs, and no adverse effects were reported in the extracted record; heart rate and blood pressure were monitored.
- **Limitations:** Small hospitalized type 2 diabetes sample; acute post-lunch exposure only; not a free-living every-meal intervention.
- **Population mismatch:** Direct for T2D post-lunch walking but not general adult self-experimenters.
- **Artifact candidates and rights:** Open-access/PMCID article; check license terms before archiving full text.
