This source is included for **secondary-metabolism-lipids-insulin-cgm**.

**Findings:** Moderate, but not low, treadmill walking lowered postprandial lipemia in healthy young adults after a long prior-day exercise dose.

**Why it matters:** Supports intensity-boundary context for lipid outcomes.

**Potential experiment signals:** postprandial triacylglycerol/lipemia, fat oxidation.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as lipid intensity context only; it does not define a practical post-meal walking dose.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Healthy young adults (six men and six women; mean age about 27 years).

- **Participant count:** 12

- **Intervention/exposure:** Ninety minutes of treadmill walking at low intensity or moderate intensity.

- **Comparator/control:** No-exercise control trial.

- **Duration/follow-up:** Three 2-day trials with treadmill walking on day 1 and fatty-meal postprandial testing on day 2.

- **Endpoints:** postprandial triacylglycerol/lipemia; fat oxidation

- **Effect estimates or direction:** Moderate-intensity, but not low-intensity, treadmill walking reduced postprandial triacylglycerol AUC versus control in extracted records; fat oxidation increased after both walking intensities.

- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse-event signal was extracted from the available source record.

- **Limitations:** Prior-day exercise rather than postmeal walking; Long 90-minute dose; Healthy young adults; Lipid endpoint only; Not a repeated after-every-meal intervention

- **Population mismatch:** Adjacent intensity/lipemia study, not direct postmeal walking evidence.

- **Directness to Walking After Every Meal:** adjacent_variant
