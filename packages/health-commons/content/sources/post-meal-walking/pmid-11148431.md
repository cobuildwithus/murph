## Findings
- Walking produced a mild, short-duration reduction in acid contact time in reflux patients during the first postprandial hour. [source_artifact:pmid-11148431]
- The first-hour walking-versus-sitting difference in reflux patients was reported as 17% (95% CI 0 to 34). [source_artifact:pmid-11148431]
- Walking was not statistically significant in controls; chewing gum had a longer effect than walking. [source_artifact:pmid-11148431]

## Why it matters
This is one of the most direct reflux sources for the protocol, but its effect was brief and subgroup-specific. [source_artifact:pmid-11148431]

## Extraction details
- **Population:** Adult men, including 12 patients with gastroesophageal reflux disease and 24 controls.
- **Intervention or exposure:** After a standardized breakfast, participants completed randomized postprandial conditions: 1 hour walking, 1 hour chewing gum, or sitting control, followed by sitting.
- **Comparator or control:** Sitting control condition; chewing-gum arm as an active comparator.
- **Duration or follow-up:** Three 5-hour postprandial pH-metry sessions on separate days.
- **Endpoints:** esophageal acid contact time; postprandial acid reflux; between-group reflux response
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Walking produced a mild, short-duration reduction in acid contact time in reflux patients during the first postprandial hour. The first-hour walking-versus-sitting difference in reflux patients was reported as 17% (95% CI 0 to 34). Walking was not statistically significant in controls; chewing gum had a longer effect than walking.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse events were extracted from the accessible abstracted material.
- **Population mismatch:** GERD case/control physiology sample; not a general wellness sample.
- **Directness to Walking After Every Meal:** direct_protocol
- **Claim-use boundary:** supports-protocol
- **Artifact rights status:** paywalled

## Limitations
- All participants were men.
- The walking effect was short-lived and primarily observed in reflux patients during walking.
- Comparator chewing gum appeared to have a longer effect.
- This was an acute crossover physiology study, not an every-meal intervention over weeks.
