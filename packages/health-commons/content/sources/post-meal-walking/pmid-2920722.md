This source is included for **gi-reflux-gastric-emptying-digestion**.

## Findings
- Gastric emptying increased during exercise at most tested intensities compared with rest, except at the highest running intensity. [source_artifact:pmid-2920722]
- Walking and lower/moderate running showed similar increases, while running at about 74-75% VO2max reduced volume emptied relative to lower intensities. [source_artifact:pmid-2920722]

## Why it matters
It supports keeping the post-meal walk gentle or moderate rather than high intensity. [source_artifact:pmid-2920722]

## Potential experiment signals
- walk intensity
- running intensity
- nausea/fullness with fast pace

## Protocol takeaway
Gastric-emptying effects depend on intensity; do not equate high-intensity exercise with walking after meals. [source_artifact:pmid-2920722]

## Claim use
`safety-only`.

## Extraction details
- **Population:** Healthy men in an exercise-intensity gastric-emptying physiology study.
- **Intervention or exposure:** Walking and running at graded intensities after ingestion of 400 mL water.
- **Comparator or control:** Seated rest and different walking/running intensities.
- **Duration or follow-up:** Six 15-minute exercise bouts or 15-minute seated rest conditions.
- **Endpoints:** gastric emptying of water; exercise intensity effects; walking versus running
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Gastric emptying increased during exercise at most tested intensities compared with rest, except at the highest running intensity. Walking and lower/moderate running showed similar increases, while running at about 74-75% VO2max reduced volume emptied relative to lower intensities.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse events extracted; safety relevance is intensity boundary.
- **Population mismatch:** Exercise physiology with water ingestion, not walking after a normal meal.
- **Directness to Walking After Every Meal:** safety_boundary
- **Claim-use boundary:** safety-only
- **Artifact rights status:** permission_required

## Limitations
- Water-emptying physiology, not a mixed meal.
- Not post-meal comfort or reflux outcome evidence.
- Small male sample.
