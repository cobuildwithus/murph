## Findings
- Thirty minutes of light post-meal bicycling blunted the post-meal glucose increase after a carbohydrate-rich meal.
- The post-exercise glucose rebound and healthy-population setting make it context-only.

## Why it matters
It strengthens the general mechanism that light postprandial muscle activity can blunt glucose excursions, while also flagging that bicycle exercise in healthy women is not equivalent to walking after each meal.

## Potential experiment signals
- Capillary/blood glucose response
- Peak glucose
- Post-exercise rebound glucose

## Protocol takeaway
Use as same-mechanism context for light post-meal activity, not as direct support for a walking prescription.

## Claim use
`context-only`.

## Extraction details
- **Source kind:** journal_article
- **Study design:** acute_physiology (Two-day acute postprandial light-activity experiment)
- **Participant count:** 39 (reported)
- **Population:** Healthy young and middle-aged women, split into sedentary and trained cohorts.
- **Intervention or exposure:** Thirty minutes of light bicycling immediately after finishing a carbohydrate-rich cornflakes meal.
- **Comparator or control:** Identical meal with resting/no post-meal activity on a separate day.
- **Duration or follow-up:** Blood glucose measured before and every 15 minutes for 120 minutes after the meal.
- **Endpoints:** Postprandial blood glucose rise and peak glucose.
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Light bicycling blunted the blood glucose rise in all trials; glucose rose again after exercise stopped, but peak glucose remained lower than without exercise.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse-event counts were extracted from accessible records.
- **Population mismatch:** Healthy women rather than people with diabetes; cycling rather than walking.
- **Directness to Walking After Every Meal:** same_mechanism
- **Artifact candidates / rights:** Paywalled/publisher article; do not commit PDF. `pdfRightsStatus=paywalled`.

## Limitations
- Healthy female-only sample.
- Bicycling rather than walking.
- Acute high-carbohydrate meal test.
- Does not test walking after every meal.
