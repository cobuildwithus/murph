## Findings
- Reflux was more likely to reach the proximal esophagus postprandially than fasting (60% ± 4 versus 29% ± 3; p<0.05). [source_artifact:pmid-15929753]
- Early postprandial stationary reflux reached proximally more than late postprandial reflux (15 ± 3% versus 8 ± 4%; p<0.05). [source_artifact:pmid-15929753]
- Ambulatory conditions were associated with greater proximal reflux extent than stationary conditions (29 ± 5% versus 15 ± 3%; p<0.05). [source_artifact:pmid-15929753]

## Why it matters
This counters over-simple claims that walking after meals always helps reflux. [source_artifact:pmid-15929753]

## Potential experiment signals
- regurgitation during early walk
- large meal fullness
- proximal burning/throat symptoms

## Protocol takeaway
People with reflux should keep post-meal walking gentle and stop or slow down if heartburn/regurgitation worsens, especially soon after large meals. [source_artifact:pmid-15929753]

## Claim use
`safety-only`.

## Extraction details
- **Population:** Ambulant patients with GERD, with additional stationary assessments in controls and GERD patients.
- **Intervention or exposure:** Postprandial/fullness and ambulatory physical-activity conditions in reflux monitoring.
- **Comparator or control:** Fasting versus postprandial periods and stationary versus ambulatory conditions.
- **Duration or follow-up:** Ambulatory pH-impedance monitoring; exact full monitoring duration not extracted.
- **Endpoints:** proximal extent of reflux; postprandial reflux; ambulatory versus stationary reflux
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Reflux was more likely to reach the proximal esophagus postprandially than fasting (60% ± 4 versus 29% ± 3; p<0.05). Early postprandial stationary reflux reached proximally more than late postprandial reflux (15 ± 3% versus 8 ± 4%; p<0.05). Ambulatory conditions were associated with greater proximal reflux extent than stationary conditions (29 ± 5% versus 15 ± 3%; p<0.05).
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** Proximal reflux during early postprandial activity is the safety-relevant signal.
- **Population mismatch:** Safety-boundary reflux study, not walking-after-meal benefit evidence.
- **Directness to Walking After Every Meal:** safety_boundary
- **Claim-use boundary:** safety-only
- **Artifact rights status:** paywalled

## Limitations
- Mechanistic reflux study, not a gentle walking intervention.
- Patients had GERD; general users may differ.
- Proximal reflux extent is not the same as symptom improvement.
