This source is included for **sedentary-breaks-standing-micro-walks**.

**Findings:** In postmenopausal women, regular short walking bouts during a sitting day reduced postprandial triglyceride exposure compared with sitting and a continuous-walk comparator.

**Why it matters:** It is an important secondary-metabolic endpoint source for whether regular short walks affect postprandial triglycerides.

**Potential experiment signals:** Postprandial triglycerides, Lipemia, Secondary glucose/metabolic signals.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as lipid context and dose-comparator evidence only.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Postmenopausal women.
- **Participant count:** 15 postmenopausal women.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Twenty 90-second walking bouts over an 8-hour sitting day.
- **Comparator/control:** Prolonged sitting and prolonged sitting preceded by one continuous 30-minute walking bout.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Acute 8-hour postprandial assessment with breakfast and lunch.
- **Endpoints:** Postprandial triglycerides, Lipemia, Secondary glucose/metabolic signals
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Regular walking bouts attenuated postprandial triglyceride incremental AUC by about 15% versus sitting and 14% versus the continuous-walk comparator in extracted notes.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse-event signal was extracted from available metadata.
- **Limitations:** Postmenopausal female sample; Triglyceride-primary source; Walking bouts interrupt sitting rather than being prescribed after every meal; Acute assessment
- **Population mismatch:** Adjacent variant: distributed walking breaks for lipemia, not every-meal walking.
