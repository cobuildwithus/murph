This source is included for **safety-older-pregnancy-pediatric-hypotension**.

**Findings:** Structured balance, multi-component, and strength exercises improved several fall-risk proxies in DPN, while some modalities had limited or null dynamic-gait effects.

**Why it matters:** Recent synthesis clarifies that DPN fall-risk interventions are structured and modality-specific, so safety screening matters before recommending routine walking.

**Potential experiment signals:** Timed Up and Go, gait speed, Berg Balance Scale, one-legged standing time, falls or near-falls.

**Protocol takeaway:** DPN users with fall risk may benefit from structured balance/strength work under guidance; do not assume after-meal walking alone is safe or sufficient.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Older adults with diabetic peripheral neuropathy.
- **Participant count:** Participant total not extracted; 21 articles included.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Balance, multi-component, strength, whole-body vibration, and foot-ankle functional training interventions.
- **Comparator/control:** Inactive or non-exercising control groups.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Included RCTs through February 17, 2025; intervention duration varied.
- **Endpoints:** Timed Up and Go, gait speed, Berg Balance Scale, one-legged standing time, falls or near-falls
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Balance and multi-component exercise improved multiple fall-risk measures; strength exercise improved TUG/gait speed; WBV improved static balance only; foot-ankle functional training did not affect TUG/gait speed.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** Findings support structured fall-risk exercise context, not unscreened casual walking after meals in DPN.
- **Limitations:** Adjacent variant; Not meal-timed; Participant total not extracted; Fall-risk proxy outcomes
- **Population mismatch:** Safety-boundary evidence rather than routine adult/general wellness protocol evidence.
