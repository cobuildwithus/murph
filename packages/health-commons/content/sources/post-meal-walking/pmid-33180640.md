This source is included for **sedentary-breaks-standing-micro-walks**.

**Findings:** In healthy adults, short walks or repeated chair stands interrupting prolonged sitting reduced postprandial insulinemia compared with sitting.

**Why it matters:** It adds insulin-focused context and a non-walking comparator.

**Potential experiment signals:** Postprandial insulinemia, Glucose, Muscle/metabolic response.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as insulin/mechanism context only.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Healthy young adults.
- **Participant count:** 14 healthy adults; extracted record noted 7 men and 7 women.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Short walks every 30 minutes during a prolonged sitting day.
- **Comparator/control:** Prolonged sitting and repeated chair stands/squats.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Single 7.5-hour laboratory day with mixed-macronutrient breakfast and lunch.
- **Endpoints:** Postprandial insulinemia, Glucose, Muscle/metabolic response
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Repeated chair stands or short walks reduced postprandial insulinemia versus prolonged sitting in extracted metadata.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse-event signal was extracted from available metadata.
- **Limitations:** Healthy young sample; Insulinemia-focused source; Sitting-break timing rather than walking after every meal
- **Population mismatch:** Adjacent variant: sitting interruptions with short walks or chair stands, not every-meal walking.
