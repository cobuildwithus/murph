This source is included for **sedentary-breaks-standing-micro-walks**.

**Findings:** Over three days, brief walking breaks during prolonged sitting lowered postprandial glucose and insulin versus uninterrupted sitting, with sustained but not enhanced effects.

**Why it matters:** It tests whether acute sitting-break effects persist over several days.

**Potential experiment signals:** Postprandial glucose, Insulin, Triglycerides.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as short-term persistence context only.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Overweight or obese adults.
- **Participant count:** 19 overweight or obese adults.
- **Intervention/exposure:** 2-minute light walking breaks every 20 minutes across three days of prolonged sitting.
- **Comparator/control:** Three days of prolonged sitting.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Three intervention days with mixed-meal testing on day 1 and day 3.
- **Endpoints:** Postprandial glucose, Insulin, Triglycerides
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Walking breaks lowered postprandial glucose and insulin versus sitting on day 1 and day 3, but the effect was sustained rather than enhanced; triglyceride differences were not clear.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse-event signal was extracted from available metadata.
- **Limitations:** Overweight/obese adults; Outpatient/laboratory hybrid sitting-break design; Not an every-meal walking prescription; Short three-day follow-up
- **Population mismatch:** Adjacent variant: repeated sitting breaks, not after-every-meal walking.
