This source is included for **safety-older-pregnancy-pediatric-hypotension**.

**Findings:** ISPAD supports youth physical activity but emphasizes insulin, hypoglycemia, carbohydrate, ketone, and caregiver/clinician safety management.

**Why it matters:** Children/adolescents with diabetes should not be folded into a routine adult post-meal walking protocol without diabetes-team and caregiver safety planning.

**Potential experiment signals:** CGM/SMBG before and after activity, hypoglycemia symptoms, blood or urine ketones, carbohydrate rescue availability.

**Protocol takeaway:** Treat pediatric diabetes as outside routine adult self-experiment scope; require caregiver and clinician guidance.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Children and adolescents with diabetes.
- **Participant count:** Not applicable; consensus guideline.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Exercise planning and safety guidance for youth with diabetes.
- **Comparator/control:** Not applicable.
- **Duration/follow-up:** During, shortly after, and up to 24 hours after exercise for hypoglycemia risk; ongoing activity guidance.
- **Endpoints:** CGM/SMBG before and after activity, hypoglycemia symptoms, blood or urine ketones, carbohydrate rescue availability
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Guideline-level safety recommendations: monitor glucose, have carbohydrate available, check ketones when indicated, and avoid exercise in severe-risk states.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** Hypoglycemia, hyperglycemia/ketones, insulin adjustment, caregiver planning, and prior severe hypoglycemia are key safety boundaries.
- **Limitations:** Pediatric age mismatch; Guideline not primary trial; Not specific to walking after meals
- **Population mismatch:** Safety-boundary evidence rather than routine adult/general wellness protocol evidence.
- **Directness to Walking After Every Meal:** safety_boundary
