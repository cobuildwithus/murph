This source is included for **secondary-metabolism-lipids-insulin-cgm**.

**Findings:** Premeal walking before moderate-fat meals did not significantly reduce postprandial lipemia, including no benefit after 30 minutes.

**Why it matters:** Important null/mixed lipid boundary evidence for walking timing and meal-fat context.

**Potential experiment signals:** postprandial triacylglycerol delta AUC, postprandial lipemia.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use to temper lipid claims: premeal walking before moderate-fat meals produced no statistically significant lipemia reduction in this small study.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Sixteen sedentary healthy young men.

- **Participant count:** 16

- **Intervention/exposure:** Thirty, 60, or 90 minutes of treadmill walking at 50% VO2max immediately before the first meal.

- **Comparator/control:** No-activity control condition.

- **Duration/follow-up:** Postprandial lipemia assessment across two mixed moderate-fat meals served 3 hours apart.

- **Endpoints:** postprandial triacylglycerol delta AUC; postprandial lipemia

- **Effect estimates or direction:** Compared with control, postprandial TAG delta AUC changed by +2% after 30 minutes (p=1.00), -14% after 60 minutes (p=0.24), and -15% after 90 minutes (p=0.23). Reductions with 60/90 minutes were not statistically significant.

- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse-event signal was extracted from the source record.

- **Limitations:** Premeal walking rather than postmeal walking; Healthy young men only; Moderate-fat meal setting; Effects were not statistically significant; Short acute study

- **Population mismatch:** Adjacent premeal walking lipid study, not walking after every meal.

- **Directness to Walking After Every Meal:** adjacent_variant
