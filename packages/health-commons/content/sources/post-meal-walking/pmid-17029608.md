## Findings
- Gas retention was lower during exercise than rest (24% ± 7 versus 45% ± 9 retained; p<0.05 versus rest). [source_artifact:pmid-17029608]
- Symptoms decreased during exercise from 3.6 to 2.8 on the extracted score (p<0.05 versus rest). [source_artifact:pmid-17029608]
- Distension was related to retained gas (r=0.68, p<0.05). [source_artifact:pmid-17029608]

## Why it matters
This is important adjacent mechanism evidence for bloating comfort claims, but it cannot be promoted to direct walking-after-meal efficacy. [source_artifact:pmid-17029608]

## Extraction details
- **Population:** Patients complaining of bloating; extracted summaries describe 7 with IBS and 1 with functional bloating by Rome II criteria.
- **Intervention or exposure:** Intermittent pedaling exercise during jejunal gas infusion and duodenal lipid perfusion.
- **Comparator or control:** Rest under the same infusion model.
- **Duration or follow-up:** Acute 120-minute gas infusion protocol.
- **Endpoints:** gas retention; bloating symptoms; abdominal distension
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Gas retention was lower during exercise than rest (24% ± 7 versus 45% ± 9 retained; p<0.05 versus rest). Symptoms decreased during exercise from 3.6 to 2.8 on the extracted score (p<0.05 versus rest). Distension was related to retained gas (r=0.68, p<0.05).
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse events were extracted; the safety relevance is adjacent symptom relief during activity.
- **Population mismatch:** Mechanistic bloating model rather than a meal-walking protocol.
- **Directness to Walking After Every Meal:** same_mechanism
- **Claim-use boundary:** context-only
- **Artifact rights status:** paywalled

## Limitations
- Not walking-specific.
- Experimental gas infusion differs from everyday post-meal bloating.
- Small patient sample.
