This source is included for **secondary-metabolism-lipids-insulin-cgm**.

**Findings:** Postprandial treadmill walking improved glucose and insulin excursions but produced mixed lipoprotein/lipemia signals in healthy young women.

**Why it matters:** Adds insulin and lipoprotein context while preserving mixed lipid findings.

**Potential experiment signals:** serum glucose, insulin, free fatty acids, apoB48, triglycerides, remnant-like particle triglycerides.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context-only metabolic physiology; avoid broad claims that postmeal walking improves all lipid endpoints.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Healthy young Japanese women with sedentary lifestyles and normal BMI.

- **Participant count:** 14

- **Intervention/exposure:** Thirty minutes of treadmill walking at about 50% VO2max beginning 20 minutes after glucose-only or glucose-plus-fat beverage load.

- **Comparator/control:** Same beverage loads without postprandial exercise.

- **Duration/follow-up:** Six-hour post-ingestion metabolic assessment.

- **Endpoints:** serum glucose; insulin; free fatty acids; apoB48; triglycerides; remnant-like particle triglycerides

- **Effect estimates or direction:** Postprandial exercise alleviated sharp rises in serum glucose and insulin for glucose-only and glucose-plus-fat loads. With the glucose-plus-fat load, exercise stimulated early apoB48, TG, and RLP-TG secretion, but subsequent lipemia was not significantly changed in extracted summaries.

- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse-event signal was extracted from the source record.

- **Limitations:** Healthy young women only; Acute beverage/lipid challenge rather than normal meals; Treadmill walking after a test load, not repeated after every meal; Lipoprotein effects were transient or mixed

- **Population mismatch:** Adjacent postprandial walking physiology source; not a free-living after-every-meal protocol.

- **Directness to Walking After Every Meal:** adjacent_variant
