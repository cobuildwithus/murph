This source is included for **reviews-meta-analyses-mechanisms**.

**Findings:** Meta-analysis of human intervention studies comparing preprandial and postprandial physical activity, reporting no significant overall timing difference for glycemic outcomes.

**Why it matters:** It is a recent null/mixed timing synthesis that prevents overclaiming post-meal timing superiority.

**Potential experiment signals:** 24-hour glycemia; Glucose tolerance test AUC; Pre- versus post-meal timing.

**Protocol takeaway:** Preserve as a null/mixed timing source: post-meal physical activity was not consistently superior to pre-meal physical activity across human intervention studies.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Human participants in acute and multi-week intervention studies comparing physical activity before versus after meals.
- **Participant count:** Participant count not applicable or not resolved in the extracted record.
- **Participant count kind:** `not_resolved_in_extraction`
- **Intervention/exposure:** Postprandial physical activity.
- **Comparator/control:** Preprandial physical activity.
- **Duration/follow-up:** 21 acute-response studies and seven randomized controlled trials lasting multiple weeks.
- **Endpoints:** 24-hour glycemia; Glucose tolerance test AUC; Pre- versus post-meal timing
- **Effect estimates or direction:** No significant differences were found between preprandial and postprandial physical activity for glycemic outcomes; multi-week RCT GTT AUC slightly favored preprandial activity but was not significant (-0.29 mmol/L, 95% CI -0.66 to 0.08).
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse-event rate was extracted.
- **Limitations:** Participant count not resolved; Heterogeneous physical activity protocols; Not walking-specific; High heterogeneity in some analyses
- **Population mismatch:** Mixed physical activity interventions, not specific to walking after every meal.
