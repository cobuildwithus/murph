This source is included for **sedentary-breaks-standing-micro-walks**.

**Findings:** In children with overweight/obesity, short moderate activity breaks during sitting improved insulin-related markers during glucose-tolerance testing without clear energy-intake changes.

**Why it matters:** It adds pediatric context for activity interruptions and glucose tolerance.

**Potential experiment signals:** Glucose tolerance, Insulin AUC, Estimated insulin sensitivity, Energy intake.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use only as pediatric adjacent evidence.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Children aged about 7 to 11 years with overweight or obesity.
- **Participant count:** Participant count not resolved in the extracted record.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Three-minute moderate-intensity walking bouts every 30 minutes during a 3-hour sitting period.
- **Comparator/control:** Three hours of continuous sitting.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Acute 3-hour laboratory glucose-tolerance protocol.
- **Endpoints:** Glucose tolerance, Insulin AUC, Estimated insulin sensitivity, Energy intake
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Moderate activity breaks lowered insulin AUC by about 18% and improved estimated insulin sensitivity in extracted notes; energy intake and macronutrient intake did not differ significantly.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse-event signal was extracted from available metadata.
- **Limitations:** Pediatric overweight/obesity population; Moderate activity breaks rather than light post-meal walking; Acute controlled setting; Sample count not resolved here
- **Population mismatch:** Population and protocol mismatch: children with overweight/obesity, not adult every-meal walking.
