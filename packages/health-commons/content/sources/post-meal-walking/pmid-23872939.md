This source is included for **secondary-metabolism-lipids-insulin-cgm**.

**Findings:** A single exercise bout improved next-day free-living postprandial glycemia in type 2 diabetes, but it was not a repeated after-meal walking protocol.

**Why it matters:** Useful for 24-hour CGM context and for separating one-bout effects from repeated meal-timed walking.

**Potential experiment signals:** average 24-hour glucose by CGM, postprandial glycemia after subsequent meals.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as same-mechanism CGM context only; do not infer that a single exercise bout equals walking after every meal.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Sedentary adults with type 2 diabetes.

- **Participant count:** 9

- **Intervention/exposure:** One 60-minute exercise bout at 60–75% heart-rate reserve before subsequent free-living meals.

- **Comparator/control:** Non-exercise/free-living comparison condition.

- **Duration/follow-up:** Free-living CGM follow-up across the first 24 hours after exercise, with later follow-up noted in source summaries.

- **Endpoints:** average 24-hour glucose by CGM; postprandial glycemia after subsequent meals

- **Effect estimates or direction:** A single exercise bout produced a significant 10% reduction in average glucose during the first 24 hours after exercise and reduced postprandial glycemic responses to subsequent meals in extracted summaries; the effect was not described as sustained at later follow-up.

- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse-event signal was extracted from the source record.

- **Limitations:** One exercise bout rather than walking after each meal; Exercise was not necessarily postmeal walking; Small T2D sample; Short-lived effect window

- **Population mismatch:** Same glucose mechanism in T2D, but not direct repeated post-meal walking evidence.

- **Directness to Walking After Every Meal:** same_mechanism
