This source is included for **sedentary-breaks-standing-micro-walks**.

**Findings:** In inactive adults with type 2 diabetes, brief light-walking and resistance-activity breaks interrupted sitting and improved glycemic/insulin-related outcomes versus sitting, with modality-specific secondary effects.

**Why it matters:** Clinically relevant type 2 diabetes evidence that sitting interruptions can affect postprandial metabolism.

**Potential experiment signals:** Postprandial glucose, Insulin, C-peptide, Triglycerides.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context for clinical populations and non-walking comparators only.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Inactive adults with type 2 diabetes.
- **Participant count:** Participant count not resolved in the extracted record.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Brief bouts of light walking or simple resistance activities interrupting prolonged sitting.
- **Comparator/control:** Uninterrupted sitting.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Acute laboratory postprandial sitting protocol.
- **Endpoints:** Postprandial glucose, Insulin, C-peptide, Triglycerides
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Light walking and simple resistance activity breaks improved several glycemic/insulinemic outcomes versus sitting; extracted notes indicate triglyceride attenuation was clearer for simple resistance activities than for light walking.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse-event signal was extracted from available metadata.
- **Limitations:** Type 2 diabetes population; Sitting interruptions rather than meal-triggered walks; Comparator includes resistance activity rather than standing; Sample count was not resolved in extraction
- **Population mismatch:** Adjacent variant: T2D sitting-break trial, not walking after every meal.
