## Findings
- Postprandial running induced gastroesophageal reflux in a pattern correlated with running intensity. [source_artifact:pmid-11306744]
- The report linked reflux to disorganized esophageal motility rather than a gentle-walking mechanism. [source_artifact:pmid-11306744]

## Why it matters
This source helps set an intensity boundary: gentle walking should not be conflated with running after meals. [source_artifact:pmid-11306744]

## Protocol takeaway
For reflux-prone users, keep post-meal movement gentle and avoid running-like intensity immediately after meals. [source_artifact:pmid-11306744]

## Claim use
`safety-only`.

## Extraction details
- **Population:** Healthy male fed volunteers.
- **Intervention or exposure:** Treadmill running in the fed state at graded intensities, including 40% and 70% of maximal heart rate.
- **Comparator or control:** Lower versus higher running intensity and resting periods.
- **Duration or follow-up:** Acute fed-state physiology test with running bouts after a meal.
- **Endpoints:** gastroesophageal reflux; esophageal motility
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Postprandial running induced gastroesophageal reflux in a pattern correlated with running intensity. The report linked reflux to disorganized esophageal motility rather than a gentle-walking mechanism.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** Exercise-induced reflux is the safety-relevant event; no broader harms were extracted.
- **Population mismatch:** Fed-state running differs materially from slow walking after a meal.
- **Directness to Walking After Every Meal:** safety_boundary
- **Claim-use boundary:** safety-only
- **Artifact rights status:** unknown

## Limitations
- Running in fed volunteers is a safety boundary, not a test of gentle post-meal walking.
- Small sample of healthy men.
