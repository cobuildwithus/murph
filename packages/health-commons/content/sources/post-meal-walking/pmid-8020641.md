This source is included for **safety-older-pregnancy-pediatric-hypotension**.

**Findings:** In frail stroke-recovery patients, food and exercise testing produced significant postprandial BP/HR changes and mixed exercise-capability results.

**Why it matters:** Identifies frailty and cerebrovascular disease as a strong boundary for unsupervised post-meal walking.

**Potential experiment signals:** erect post-exercise blood pressure, heart rate response, rehabilitation exercise capability, post-meal symptoms.

**Protocol takeaway:** Frail post-stroke or rehabilitation users should not self-direct after-meal walking without clinician or therapist guidance.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Fifteen frail elderly patients recovering from stroke/cerebrovascular disease.
- **Participant count:** 15 participants.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Food ingestion with pre/postprandial exercise capability and BP/HR measurement during rehabilitation testing.
- **Comparator/control:** Water-only control condition in crossover design.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Pre- and postprandial testing; key erect BP changes 15 minutes after eating.
- **Endpoints:** erect post-exercise blood pressure, heart rate response, rehabilitation exercise capability, post-meal symptoms
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Exercise capability decreased in water-only control but not food test; erect post-exercise BP dropped after eating and HR increased, with other changes not significant.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** Frail stroke-rehabilitation patients may need supervised activity timing and BP monitoring around meals.
- **Limitations:** Small trial; Frail stroke recovery population; Not routine post-meal walking; Older study
- **Population mismatch:** Safety-boundary evidence rather than routine adult/general wellness protocol evidence.
- **Directness to Walking After Every Meal:** safety_boundary
