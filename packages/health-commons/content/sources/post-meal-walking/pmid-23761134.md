This source is included for **direct-core-glycemic-dose-timing**.

**Findings:** The postmeal protocol used three 15-minute treadmill walks at about 3 METs, beginning 30 minutes after each meal. The postmeal condition improved 24-hour glycemic control and reduced 3-hour post-dinner glucose more than sustained walking conditions in this small older high-risk cohort.

**Why it matters:** It maps closely to the protocol pattern: brief moderate walking after breakfast, lunch, and dinner, with 24-hour glucose monitoring.

**Potential experiment signals:** 24-hour mean glucose, 3-hour post-dinner glucose, Glucose AUC, CGM-derived glycemic control.

**Protocol takeaway:** Three 15-minute walks after meals improved 24-hour glycemic control and were especially useful after dinner compared with a sustained morning or afternoon walk.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Inactive older adults at risk for impaired glucose tolerance; age 60 years or older; nonsmoking; BMI below 35 kg/m²; fasting glucose 105-125 mg/dL.
- **Intervention/exposure:** moderate treadmill walking after each meal
- **Comparator/control:** No-exercise control day plus sustained 45-minute morning or afternoon walking conditions.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Three 48-hour calorimeter stays separated by about 4 weeks; exercise exposures occurred on the second day of each stay.
- **Endpoints:** 24-hour mean glucose, 3-hour post-dinner glucose, Glucose AUC, CGM-derived glycemic control
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No specific adverse-event signal was extracted from the accessible record; small supervised lab setting limits safety inference.
- **Limitations:** Very small sample; older high-risk adults rather than broad adult population; tightly controlled calorimeter setting; short acute exposure.
- **Population mismatch:** Close for people at risk for impaired glucose tolerance, but not a free-living or younger general adult sample.
