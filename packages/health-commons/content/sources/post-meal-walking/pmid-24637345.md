This source is included for **sedentary-breaks-standing-micro-walks**.

**Findings:** Alternating sitting and standing appeared to attenuate postprandial glucose in sedentary overweight/obese desk-based adults, but this is standing-only context.

**Why it matters:** It provides standing-only positive comparator evidence and prevents assuming walking is the only possible sitting-interruption mechanism.

**Potential experiment signals:** Postprandial glucose, Insulin.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context for standing-versus-walking boundaries, not as direct walking evidence.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Sedentary desk-based adults with overweight or obesity.
- **Participant count:** 23 sedentary overweight/obese desk-based adults were reported in the extracted study metadata.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Alternating sitting and standing bouts, with standing replacing part of prolonged sitting.
- **Comparator/control:** Prolonged sitting.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Acute multi-hour laboratory/office-style postprandial protocol.
- **Endpoints:** Postprandial glucose, Insulin
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Alternating sitting and standing attenuated postprandial glucose responses; insulin effects were less clear in the extracted notes.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse-event signal was extracted from available metadata.
- **Limitations:** Standing-only intervention; Small acute sample; Overweight/obese desk-based population; No walking arm
- **Population mismatch:** Adjacent variant: posture replacement, not walking after meals.
