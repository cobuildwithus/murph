## Findings
- A short high-intensity interval cycling session reduced hyperglycemia prevalence in a small T2D cohort.
- This is a substitution-variant source and should not be used as direct walking evidence.

## Why it matters
It separates walking from other exercise modes: high-intensity intervals may improve glycemia, but they carry different burden, feasibility, and safety assumptions.

## Potential experiment signals
- Postprandial glucose response
- Time above 10 mmol/L
- 24-hour CGM hyperglycemia prevalence

## Protocol takeaway
Use only to say that non-walking exercise substitutions have evidence too; do not translate intensity or feasibility to casual post-meal walking.

## Claim use
`context-only`.

## Extraction details
- **Source kind:** journal_article
- **Study design:** experimental_high_intensity_interval_exercise_study (Acute high-intensity interval cycling study with CGM)
- **Participant count:** 7 (reported)
- **Population:** Adults with type 2 diabetes.
- **Intervention or exposure:** Ten 60-second cycling efforts at roughly 90% maximal heart rate separated by 60 seconds of rest.
- **Comparator or control:** Non-exercise control day.
- **Duration or follow-up:** Acute session followed by 24-hour CGM.
- **Endpoints:** Postprandial glucose response and hyperglycemia prevalence above 10 mmol/L.
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Time in hyperglycemia above 10 mmol/L was lower after HIT (4.5±4.4%) than control (15.2±12.3%; p=0.04) in accessible extraction.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse-event counts were extracted; high-intensity exercise is a different safety category than walking.
- **Population mismatch:** T2D population is relevant, but modality/intensity differ strongly from walking after meals.
- **Directness to Walking After Every Meal:** adjacent_variant
- **Artifact candidates / rights:** Rights status unknown; do not commit PDF without license verification. `pdfRightsStatus=unknown`.

## Limitations
- Very small sample.
- High-intensity cycling rather than walking.
- Acute study only.
- Does not evaluate every-meal implementation.
