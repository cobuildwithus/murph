This source is included for **secondary-metabolism-lipids-insulin-cgm**.

**Findings:** Post-cookie walking lowered early insulin exposure without a significant glucose difference in healthy young women.

**Why it matters:** Preserves insulin-positive/glucose-null evidence rather than overclaiming universal glucose benefit.

**Potential experiment signals:** postprandial glucose, postprandial insulin, postprandial lipid metabolism.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context-only evidence that insulin may change even when glucose does not.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Healthy young women with sedentary lifestyles and normal weight.

- **Participant count:** 15

- **Intervention/exposure:** Thirty minutes of walking exercise beginning 20 minutes after cookie ingestion.

- **Comparator/control:** Cookie ingestion without exercise.

- **Duration/follow-up:** Six-hour post-cookie metabolic assessment.

- **Endpoints:** postprandial glucose; postprandial insulin; postprandial lipid metabolism

- **Effect estimates or direction:** Postprandial glucose change was not significantly different, but insulin at 1 hour and insulin iAUC over 0–2 hours were significantly lower with exercise. Lipid/lipoprotein effects were not fully resolved in the extracted record.

- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse-event signal was extracted from the source record.

- **Limitations:** Healthy young women only; Cookie challenge rather than full meals; Acute laboratory design; Glucose outcome was null while insulin improved; Lipid effects not fully resolved from available extraction

- **Population mismatch:** Adjacent acute physiology; not a free-living after-every-meal protocol.

- **Directness to Walking After Every Meal:** adjacent_variant
