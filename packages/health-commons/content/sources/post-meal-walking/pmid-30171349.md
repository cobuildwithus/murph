This source is included for **secondary-metabolism-lipids-insulin-cgm**.

**Findings:** Extremely short interval exercise improved 24-hour hyperglycemia time in men with T2D, but it was not walking or meal-timed.

**Why it matters:** Adds 24-hour CGM context and a clear modality boundary.

**Potential experiment signals:** 24-hour CGM time in hyperglycemia, glycemic variability.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use only as adjacent 24-hour CGM evidence for exercise timing/dose context.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Men with type 2 diabetes.

- **Participant count:** 11

- **Intervention/exposure:** Very short reduced-exertion high-intensity interval training, moderate-intensity continuous training, or high-intensity interval training.

- **Comparator/control:** Control/non-exercise condition in the experimental design.

- **Duration/follow-up:** Acute exercise-condition testing with 24-hour CGM follow-up.

- **Endpoints:** 24-hour CGM time in hyperglycemia; glycemic variability

- **Effect estimates or direction:** Time in hyperglycemia was reduced for REHIT (-112 ± 63 minutes, p=0.002), MICT (-115 ± 127 minutes, p=0.08), and HIIT (-125 ± 122 minutes, p=0.04) in extracted summaries; glycemic variability was not significantly altered.

- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse-event signal was extracted from the source record.

- **Limitations:** Interval exercise rather than walking; Not meal-timed; Men with T2D only; Small sample; Glycemic variability was null

- **Population mismatch:** Adjacent time-efficient exercise/CGM evidence, not after-meal walking.

- **Directness to Walking After Every Meal:** adjacent_variant
