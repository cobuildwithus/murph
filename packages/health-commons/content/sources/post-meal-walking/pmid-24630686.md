This source is included for **safety-older-pregnancy-pediatric-hypotension**.

**Findings:** The review found broad but heterogeneous evidence that PPH is common in older adults, clinically important, mechanistically complex, and incompletely managed.

**Why it matters:** Provides a high-level safety and mechanism basis for cautioning older adults about meal-related BP drops before walking.

**Potential experiment signals:** post-meal blood pressure, dizziness/syncope, falls, PPH symptom timing.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as safety-only context for dizziness/falls and post-meal hypotension; do not claim the walking protocol treats PPH.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Older adults and clinical populations with postprandial hypotension or autonomic/meal-related hypotension risk.
- **Participant count:** Participant total not extracted; review included 167 eligible papers after 417 full-text records were retrieved and 248 retained.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Systematic review of PPH epidemiology, pathophysiology, and management.
- **Comparator/control:** Not applicable.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Search from database inception through January 2014; post-meal monitoring windows varied.
- **Endpoints:** post-meal blood pressure, dizziness/syncope, falls, PPH symptom timing
- **Effect estimates or direction:** PPH occurs commonly in older people and is a major morbidity; management evidence was described as suboptimal.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** PPH may involve mechanisms that can create dizziness, falls, or cerebral/cardiovascular symptoms after meals.
- **Limitations:** Heterogeneous literature; Not walking-specific; Does not test a post-meal walking protocol
- **Population mismatch:** Safety-boundary evidence rather than routine adult/general wellness protocol evidence.
- **Directness to Walking After Every Meal:** safety_boundary
