## Findings
- Post-breakfast cycling lowered glucose during the exercise period, but intensity did not clearly change 24-hour AUC.
- This supports a dose/intensity context claim, not a direct walking claim.

## Why it matters
It informs intensity boundaries: stronger intensity may not always be necessary for glucose response, but this is healthy-male cycling evidence.

## Potential experiment signals
- Postprandial glucose during exercise
- 24-hour glucose AUC
- Exercise enjoyment/preference

## Protocol takeaway
Use as adjacent intensity context; do not cite as direct walking-after-meals evidence.

## Claim use
`context-only`.

## Extraction details
- **Source kind:** journal_article
- **Study design:** experimental_postprandial_exercise_intensity_study (Randomized four-arm crossover post-breakfast cycling intensity study)
- **Participant count:** 10 (reported)
- **Population:** Healthy insufficiently active men.
- **Intervention or exposure:** Thirty minutes of low-intensity, moderate-intensity, or high-intensity interval cycling starting about 30 minutes after breakfast.
- **Comparator or control:** No-exercise control condition.
- **Duration or follow-up:** Post-breakfast test day with 24-hour CGM.
- **Endpoints:** Postprandial glucose and 24-hour glucose AUC; enjoyment/preference.
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Glucose was lower during exercise conditions versus no exercise around 25 minutes into exercise, but there were no differences between exercise intensities and 24-hour AUC was not affected by intensity (p=0.75 in accessible extraction).
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse-event counts were extracted from accessible records.
- **Population mismatch:** Healthy men, cycling, breakfast-only intervention.
- **Directness to Walking After Every Meal:** adjacent_variant
- **Artifact candidates / rights:** Rights status unknown; do not commit PDF without license verification. DOI was recovered during extraction although the source ledger DOI field was null. `pdfRightsStatus=unknown`.

## Limitations
- Healthy insufficiently active men only.
- Cycling rather than walking.
- Breakfast-only acute condition.
- No intensity difference in 24-hour AUC.
