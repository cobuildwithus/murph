This source is included for **secondary-metabolism-lipids-insulin-cgm**.

**Findings:** In type 2 diabetes, free-living interval walking improved several CGM and fitness outcomes, but the program was not meal-timed.

**Why it matters:** Shows that free-living walking can be tracked and adhered to over months in T2D, while separating training effects from meal timing.

**Potential experiment signals:** continuous glucose monitoring mean and maximum glucose, fasting insulin and glycemic control, body composition, physical fitness, walking-training adherence.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as adherence and CGM context only; not direct post-meal timing evidence.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Adults with type 2 diabetes randomized to control, continuous walking training, or interval walking training.

- **Participant count:** 32

- **Intervention/exposure:** Free-living interval-walking training or continuous-walking training.

- **Comparator/control:** No-training control group and continuous-walking comparator.

- **Duration/follow-up:** Four months of free-living walking training.

- **Endpoints:** continuous glucose monitoring mean and maximum glucose; fasting insulin and glycemic control; body composition; physical fitness; walking-training adherence

- **Effect estimates or direction:** Interval walking decreased body mass/adiposity and lowered mean and maximum CGM glucose, whereas continuous walking showed no glycemic-control change and controls worsened glycemia/fasting insulin in extracted summaries.

- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse-event signal was extracted from the source record.

- **Limitations:** Chronic interval-walking training, not meal-timed walking; Type 2 diabetes population; Interval intensity differs from simple postmeal walking; Does not isolate after-meal timing

- **Population mismatch:** Adjacent free-living walking-training evidence; not walking after every meal.

- **Directness to Walking After Every Meal:** adjacent_variant
