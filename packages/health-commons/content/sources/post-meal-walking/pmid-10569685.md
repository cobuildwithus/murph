## Findings
- In frail elderly patients with meal-related hypotension, post-meal walking increased blood pressure and heart rate during the walking period.
- The hemodynamic effect was limited to the exercise period and was not sustained during subsequent rest.
- This supports safety-boundary language rather than a broad efficacy claim for the main protocol.

## Why it matters
Frail older adults and people with meal-related hypotension are a special safety stratum: post-meal walking may change blood pressure during movement, but the fall-risk context cannot be generalized to casual wellness users.

## Potential experiment signals
- Systolic blood pressure
- Heart rate
- Postprandial hypotension symptoms
- Falls or near-falls
- Exercise tolerance

## Protocol takeaway
Use only for safety language: hypotension-prone or frail users may need supervision and fall-risk precautions rather than a blanket after-meal walking recommendation.
