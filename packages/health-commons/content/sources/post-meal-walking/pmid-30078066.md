This source is included for **reviews-meta-analyses-mechanisms**.

**Findings:** Systematic review and meta-analysis of acute prolonged-sitting interruption studies, reporting lower postprandial glucose and insulin with regular activity breaks but no overall triglyceride effect.

**Why it matters:** It preserves adjacent evidence that light activity breaks can affect postprandial metabolism even when they are not framed as after-every-meal walks.

**Potential experiment signals:** Postprandial glucose; Postprandial insulin; Triglycerides; Vascular function.

**Protocol takeaway:** Activity breaks during sitting can lower acute glucose and insulin, but standing or break protocols should not be treated as equivalent to a meal-specific walking protocol.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Apparently healthy males and females of any age in acute sitting-interruption studies.
- **Participant count:** Participant count not applicable or not resolved in the extracted record.
- **Participant count kind:** `not_resolved_in_extraction`
- **Intervention/exposure:** Regular activity breaks interrupting prolonged sitting.
- **Comparator/control:** Uninterrupted prolonged sitting.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Acute prolonged-sitting protocols lasting less than 24 hours; 20 studies entered meta-analysis.
- **Endpoints:** Postprandial glucose; Postprandial insulin; Triglycerides; Vascular function
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Activity breaks lowered postprandial glucose (d=-0.36, 95% CI -0.50 to -0.21) and insulin (d=-0.37, 95% CI -0.53 to -0.20); triglycerides were not lower overall (d=0.06, 95% CI -0.15 to 0.26).
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No batch-level adverse-event rate was extracted.
- **Limitations:** Not meal-specific; Acute laboratory protocols; Participant count not resolved in extraction; Activity break modalities varied
- **Population mismatch:** Mostly apparently healthy participants in lab sitting protocols, not walking after every meal.
