This source is included for **secondary-metabolism-lipids-insulin-cgm**.

**Findings:** CGM-personalized daily exercise timing lowered 24-hour peak glucose and improved endothelial function but did not change HbA1c over 8 weeks.

**Why it matters:** Relevant to implementation timing logic and to mixed short-term versus HbA1c outcomes.

**Potential experiment signals:** HbA1c, 24-hour peak glucose, flow-mediated dilation, adherence.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as timing-context evidence only; individualized peak timing is not the same as walking after each meal.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Adults with type 2 diabetes randomized to waitlist control or personalized exercise-timing groups.

- **Participant count:** 82

- **Intervention/exposure:** Daily 22-minute exercise prescribed either about 30 minutes before the individual peak hyperglycemia time or about 90 minutes after the peak.

- **Comparator/control:** Waitlist control and alternate non-peak timing condition.

- **Duration/follow-up:** Eight-week exercise-timing intervention after a 14-day habitual CGM period.

- **Endpoints:** HbA1c; 24-hour peak glucose; flow-mediated dilation; adherence

- **Effect estimates or direction:** There was no HbA1c intervention effect. Both timing groups lowered 24-hour peak glucose versus control and increased endothelial function (%FMD); adherence was reported above 90% in extracted records.

- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse-event signal was extracted from the available source record.

- **Limitations:** Exercise timing was individualized to CGM peaks rather than fixed after meals; Modality was not limited to walking in the extracted record; T2D population; HbA1c effect was null over 8 weeks

- **Population mismatch:** Same timing/CGM mechanism in T2D, not a walking-after-every-meal protocol.

- **Directness to Walking After Every Meal:** same_mechanism
