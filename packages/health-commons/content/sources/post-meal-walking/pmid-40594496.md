This source is included for **direct-core-glycemic-dose-timing**.

**Findings:** The 10-minute immediate walk and 30-minute delayed walk both lowered 2-hour glucose AUC and mean glucose versus control. Only the 10-minute immediate walk significantly lowered peak glucose versus control. RPE was lower for the 10-minute condition, and gastrointestinal discomfort was very low with no significant difference between walking conditions.

**Why it matters:** It is a recent timing/duration anchor for the feasibility of a very short immediate walk, while also highlighting a glucose-drink rather than mixed-meal limitation.

**Potential experiment signals:** 2-hour glucose AUC, Mean blood glucose, Peak blood glucose, RPE, Gastrointestinal discomfort.

**Protocol takeaway:** A 10-minute immediate walk lowered 2-hour AUC, mean glucose, and peak glucose versus control; a 30-minute delayed walk lowered AUC and mean glucose but not peak glucose versus control.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Healthy young adults; 6 females; no diabetes or glucose intolerance reported in eligibility criteria.
- **Intervention/exposure:** comfortable-speed treadmill walking after glucose intake
- **Comparator/control:** Resting control and 30-minute delayed walking condition.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Acute 75-g oral glucose trial with 120-minute glucose sampling.
- **Endpoints:** 2-hour glucose AUC, Mean blood glucose, Peak blood glucose, RPE, Gastrointestinal discomfort
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** RPE was significantly lower during the 10-minute walk than the 30-minute walk, and gastrointestinal discomfort did not differ significantly between walking conditions in the extracted article record.
- **Limitations:** Glucose drink rather than mixed meal; healthy young adults; acute lab setting; no every-meal behavioral follow-up.
- **Population mismatch:** Useful direct walking timing evidence, but population and meal model are not representative of diabetes or free-living mixed meals.
- **Artifact candidates and rights:** Open-access Nature article; preserve license metadata if archiving full text/PDF.
