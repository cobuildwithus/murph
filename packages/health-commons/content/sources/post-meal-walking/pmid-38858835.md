This source is included for **secondary-metabolism-lipids-insulin-cgm**.

**Findings:** Post-meal exercise improved meal-level glucose under ecological conditions but not 24-hour CGM control.

**Why it matters:** Preserves the important mixed pattern: postprandial improvement does not necessarily translate to whole-day glucose improvement.

**Potential experiment signals:** postprandial glucose levels, 24-hour glucose control by CGM.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context-only evidence; track both meal-level and 24-hour CGM endpoints separately.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Physically inactive, metabolically healthy young women.

- **Participant count:** 17

- **Intervention/exposure:** Post-meal exercise under ecological conditions.

- **Comparator/control:** No post-meal exercise/control condition under ecological conditions.

- **Duration/follow-up:** Free-living/ecological monitoring period with CGM and activity assessment.

- **Endpoints:** postprandial glucose levels; 24-hour glucose control by CGM

- **Effect estimates or direction:** Post-meal exercise improved post-prandial glucose levels but did not improve 24-hour glucose regulation/control in extracted source records.

- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse-event signal was extracted from the source record.

- **Limitations:** Small sample of healthy young women; Ecological/free-living conditions may introduce adherence and meal variability; 24-hour glucose control was null; Not long-term adherence evidence

- **Population mismatch:** Same postmeal-exercise mechanism, but population and implementation differ from broad walking-after-every-meal claims.

- **Directness to Walking After Every Meal:** same_mechanism
