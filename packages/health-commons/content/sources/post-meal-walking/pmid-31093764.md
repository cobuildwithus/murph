## Findings
- Pre-dinner walking did not improve extracted 24-hour glucose profiles or postprandial outcomes.
- It did lower glucose during the walking bout itself.

## Why it matters
It is key negative timing-boundary evidence: walking before dinner is not equivalent to walking after meals.

## Potential experiment signals
- Fasting glucose
- Postprandial glucose
- Glucose variability
- 24-hour glucose profile
- Glucose during walking

## Protocol takeaway
Use to contrast pre-dinner walking with post-meal walking; preserve the null findings.

## Claim use
`context-only`.

## Extraction details
- **Source kind:** journal_article
- **Study design:** crossover (Multi-site randomized crossover pre-dinner walking study)
- **Participant count:** 80 (reported)
- **Population:** Adults with type 2 diabetes.
- **Intervention or exposure:** 50 minutes treadmill walking at 5.0 km/h before the evening meal.
- **Comparator or control:** 50 minutes sitting/no-exercise control condition.
- **Duration or follow-up:** Two-day standardized conditions separated by at least 72 hours with CGM.
- **Endpoints:** Fasting glucose, postprandial glucose, glucose variability, 24-hour profiles, and glucose during walking.
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Exercise did not affect fasting glucose, postprandial glucose, glucose variability, or 24-hour glucose profiles; glucose during the walking bout was lower than sitting by about -1.56 mmol/L (95% CI -2.18 to -0.95).
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse-event counts were extracted from accessible records.
- **Population mismatch:** T2D is relevant, but timing is pre-dinner rather than after-meal.
- **Directness to Walking After Every Meal:** adjacent_variant
- **Artifact candidates / rights:** Paywalled article; do not commit PDF. `pdfRightsStatus=paywalled`.

## Limitations
- Pre-dinner timing, not post-meal timing.
- Short two-day protocol.
- Treadmill setting.
- Heterogeneous individual responses.
