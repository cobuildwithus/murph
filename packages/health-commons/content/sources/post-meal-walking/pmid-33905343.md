This source is included for **sedentary-breaks-standing-micro-walks**.

**Findings:** In adults with type 2 diabetes, one frequency pattern of simple resistance activity interruptions reduced postprandial glucose and insulin, providing adjacent dose/frequency evidence.

**Why it matters:** Dose/frequency separation is useful even though the modality differs.

**Potential experiment signals:** Postprandial glucose, Insulin, Dose frequency, Burden.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use only as dose/frequency context and comparator boundary.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Adults with medication-controlled type 2 diabetes.
- **Participant count:** Participant count not resolved in the extracted record.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Different frequencies of simple resistance activity interruptions to sitting.
- **Comparator/control:** Uninterrupted sitting and/or alternate interruption frequencies.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Acute postprandial laboratory sitting-interruption protocol.
- **Endpoints:** Postprandial glucose, Insulin, Dose frequency, Burden
- **Effect estimates or direction:** A 6-minute simple-resistance-activity interruption every 60 minutes reduced postprandial glucose and insulin in extracted notes; this is not walking evidence.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse-event signal was extracted from available metadata.
- **Limitations:** Resistance-activity rather than walking intervention; Type 2 diabetes population; Sample count not resolved here; Acute sitting-break design
- **Population mismatch:** Adjacent variant: dose/frequency for non-walking interruptions, not walking after every meal.
