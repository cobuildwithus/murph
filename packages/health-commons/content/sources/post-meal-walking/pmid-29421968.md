This source is included for **direct-core-glycemic-dose-timing**.

**Findings:** The 5-minute walking-break condition lowered 2-hour positive incremental AUC compared with uninterrupted sitting, while 2-minute breaks did not reach significance. Across the full 4-hour window, AUC values were similar across conditions.

**Why it matters:** It informs micro-walk dosing around meals, while remaining an adjacent sedentary-break pattern rather than one discrete post-meal walk.

**Potential experiment signals:** 2-hour glucose positive iAUC, 4-hour glucose AUC, CGM post-meal curve, Sedentary-break adherence.

**Protocol takeaway:** Five-minute walking breaks after a meal lowered the 2-hour positive glucose AUC, but 2-minute breaks were not clearly effective and 4-hour AUC was not different.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Sedentary women with obesity; mean age about 36 years; mean BMI about 38 kg/m².
- **Intervention/exposure:** moderate walking breaks during post-meal sitting
- **Comparator/control:** Uninterrupted sedentary time after standardized meal.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Acute 4-hour post-meal sedentary period with walking breaks every 30 minutes; 48-hour washout between conditions.
- **Endpoints:** 2-hour glucose positive iAUC, 4-hour glucose AUC, CGM post-meal curve, Sedentary-break adherence
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse-event signal was extracted from accessible metadata.
- **Limitations:** Small sample; women with obesity only; sedentary-break design rather than a single after-meal walk; acute standardized meal study.
- **Population mismatch:** Relevant for obese sedentary adults but adjacent to the exact after-each-meal protocol.
- **Artifact candidates and rights:** No PMCID; permission likely required for full text/PDF.
