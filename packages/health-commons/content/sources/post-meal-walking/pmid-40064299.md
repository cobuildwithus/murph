This source is included for **secondary-metabolism-lipids-insulin-cgm**.

**Findings:** In the TREx crossover study, postmeal walking lowered insulin exposure but not 14-hour glucose AUC, and time-restricted eating did not add a glucose benefit.

**Why it matters:** Important recent insulin/CGM-related source with a walking component and a glucose-null boundary.

**Potential experiment signals:** 14-hour blood glucose AUC, 14-hour insulin AUC.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context-only evidence: postmeal walking may reduce insulin exposure without changing short-term glucose AUC in some settings.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Adults with type 2 diabetes.

- **Participant count:** 14

- **Intervention/exposure:** Time-restricted eating and/or 15-minute walking at 60% VO2peak 45 minutes after meals.

- **Comparator/control:** A 12-hour eating-window control condition without postmeal walking and matched diet conditions.

- **Duration/follow-up:** Short-term laboratory crossover conditions with 14-hour blood sampling.

- **Endpoints:** 14-hour blood glucose AUC; 14-hour insulin AUC

- **Effect estimates or direction:** Reducing the eating window had little effect on 14-hour glucose AUC. Postmeal walking lowered 14-hour insulin AUC (p=0.01) but did not alter 14-hour blood glucose versus the 12-hour eating-window condition; no additive TRE effect was evident in extracted records.

- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse-event signal was extracted from the source record.

- **Limitations:** Small sample; Diet-plus-exercise co-intervention limits separability; Acute laboratory setting; T2D population; Glucose effect was null while insulin improved

- **Population mismatch:** Adjacent postmeal walking component embedded in a time-restricted-eating trial; not standalone long-term walking after every meal.

- **Directness to Walking After Every Meal:** adjacent_variant
