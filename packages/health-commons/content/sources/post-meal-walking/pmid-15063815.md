## Findings
- Gas retention was lower during exercise than rest (-84 ± 303 mL versus 143 ± 219 mL; p<0.05). [source_artifact:pmid-15063815]
- Rest increased abdominal girth; exercise reduced distension versus rest. [source_artifact:pmid-15063815]
- Perception scores were not significantly different between rest and exercise in healthy subjects. [source_artifact:pmid-15063815]

## Why it matters

## Extraction details
- **Population:** Healthy adults in a controlled intestinal gas transit physiology study.
- **Intervention or exposure:** Intermittent physical activity/pedaling during jejunal gas infusion and duodenal lipid perfusion.
- **Comparator or control:** Rest condition under the same gas and lipid infusion model.
- **Duration or follow-up:** Acute 120-minute gas-infusion protocol.
- **Endpoints:** intestinal gas retention; gas evacuation; abdominal girth/distension; abdominal perception
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Gas retention was lower during exercise than rest (-84 ± 303 mL versus 143 ± 219 mL; p<0.05). Rest increased abdominal girth; exercise reduced distension versus rest. Perception scores were not significantly different between rest and exercise in healthy subjects.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** Protocol was described as well tolerated in the extracted abstracted data.
- **Population mismatch:** Mechanistic gas-transit model in healthy adults; not walking after meals.
- **Directness to Walking After Every Meal:** same_mechanism
- **Claim-use boundary:** context-only
- **Artifact rights status:** permission_required

## Limitations
- Not meal-specific and not a walking intervention.
- Experimental gas infusion differs from everyday bloating.
- Small healthy-subject sample.
