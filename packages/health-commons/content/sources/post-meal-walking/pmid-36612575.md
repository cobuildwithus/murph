This source is included for **direct-core-glycemic-dose-timing**.

**Findings:** Walking reduced post-meal glucose peak compared with control and some alternatives; 3-hour glucose iAUC was reduced for walking and voluntary calf NMES compared with control and passive NMES. Study findings support walking as the most consistently effective condition in this comparison set.

**Why it matters:** It separates direct walking effects from practical indoor substitutes and helps prevent over-promoting adjacent modalities as if they were walking.

**Potential experiment signals:** Glucose peak, 3-hour glucose iAUC, Walking versus indoor alternatives, Practical modality substitution.

**Protocol takeaway:** Walking was the strongest tested modality for lowering post-meal glucose peak/AUC, while some indoor alternatives also showed partial benefit.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Healthy active young adults; Study 1 included 12 and Study 2 included 11 participants.
- **Intervention/exposure:** postprandial walking plus adjacent indoor alternatives
- **Comparator/control:** Resting control and adjacent indoor alternatives.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Acute high-carbohydrate meal tests with 30-minute postprandial activity conditions.
- **Endpoints:** Glucose peak, 3-hour glucose iAUC, Walking versus indoor alternatives, Practical modality substitution
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No major adverse-event signal was extracted from accessible metadata.
- **Limitations:** Healthy active young adults; acute high-carbohydrate meal challenge; alternatives are adjacent modalities; no clinical population.
- **Population mismatch:** Direct walking condition but healthy active sample and single-meal lab context limit clinical generalization.
- **Artifact candidates and rights:** Open-access MDPI/PMC article; preserve license metadata if archiving full text/PDF.
