## Findings
- VO2max at 70% or higher correlated with reflux during ergometric testing only in the erosive disease group (p=0.032). [source_artifact:pmid-24676289]
- Other variables did not show significant correlations between gastroesophageal reflux and physical activity (p>0.05). [source_artifact:pmid-24676289]
- The authors concluded highly intensive activity can predispose reflux in erosive GERD, while light or short sessions had no influence regardless of BMI. [source_artifact:pmid-24676289]

## Why it matters
This supports an intensity boundary rather than a warning against all walking. [source_artifact:pmid-24676289]

## Protocol takeaway
Gentle walking should be distinguished from high-intensity exercise testing, especially for people with erosive GERD. [source_artifact:pmid-24676289]

## Extraction details
- **Population:** GERD patients undergoing ergometric stress testing: 29 with erosive disease and 10 with non-erosive disease.
- **Intervention or exposure:** Ergometric stress testing and physical activity intensity, including VO2max thresholds.
- **Comparator or control:** Erosive versus non-erosive disease groups and patients with versus without acid reflux during exercise testing.
- **Duration or follow-up:** Acute ergometric stress testing.
- **Endpoints:** gastroesophageal reflux during exercise testing; GERD physiology
- **Effect estimates or direction:** VO2max at 70% or higher correlated with reflux during ergometric testing only in the erosive disease group (p=0.032). Other variables did not show significant correlations between gastroesophageal reflux and physical activity (p>0.05). The authors concluded highly intensive activity can predispose reflux in erosive GERD, while light or short sessions had no influence regardless of BMI.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** Exercise-associated reflux in GERD patients is the safety-relevant concern.
- **Population mismatch:** GERD exercise testing, not gentle post-meal walking.

## Limitations
- Not a post-meal walking study.
- Participant count and estimates not extracted.
- Exercise testing may be more intense than casual walking.
