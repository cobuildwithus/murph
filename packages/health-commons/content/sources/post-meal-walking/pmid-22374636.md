This source is included for **sedentary-breaks-standing-micro-walks**.

**Findings:** In overweight/obese adults, brief walking interruptions to sitting reduced postprandial glucose and insulin compared with uninterrupted sitting.

**Why it matters:** Foundational evidence that very short walking interruptions can reduce postprandial glycemic and insulin exposure.

**Potential experiment signals:** Postprandial glucose, Insulin.

**Protocol takeaway:** Context for the mechanism that small bouts of walking can matter; not direct evidence for the every-meal walking rule.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Overweight or obese adults aged in midlife/older adulthood in an acute laboratory sitting protocol.
- **Participant count:** 19 overweight or obese adults.
- **Intervention/exposure:** 2-minute light- or moderate-intensity walking breaks every 20 minutes during prolonged sitting and standardized postprandial testing.
- **Comparator/control:** Uninterrupted prolonged sitting.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Single-day acute laboratory postprandial protocol.
- **Endpoints:** Postprandial glucose, Insulin
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Breaking up sitting with brief walking reduced postprandial glucose and insulin responses versus uninterrupted sitting; exact effect estimates were not restated in this extraction.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse-event signal was extracted from available metadata.
- **Limitations:** Walking breaks interrupt sitting rather than follow each meal; Small acute laboratory crossover; Overweight/obese adult population; No standing comparator arm
- **Population mismatch:** Adjacent variant: micro-walking breaks during sitting, not a meal-triggered walk after every meal.
