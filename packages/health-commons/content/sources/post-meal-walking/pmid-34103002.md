This source is included for **free-living-adherence-registries-external-claims**.

**Findings:** Fitbit Charge 3 step counts aligned with hand-tally counts at many normal cadence conditions, and interviews found motivation plus practical barriers such as competing responsibilities and physical discomfort.

**Why it matters:** It informs whether consumer wearables and stepping-in-place can support adherence measurement in pregnancy/GDM contexts.

**Potential experiment signals:** step-count validity, walking cadence, stepping-in-place acceptance, time barriers, wearable self-monitoring.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as background for wearable/adherence feasibility; do not cite as evidence that post-meal walking lowers glucose.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** 15 pregnant women with GDM completed study visits; mean age 27.0 years, prepregnancy BMI 29.4 kg/m², gestational age 32.8 weeks.

- **Participant count:** 15 for the metronome-assisted bouts; 10 completed the optional 200-m self-paced walk.

- **Intervention/exposure:** Six 2-minute metronome-assisted walking or stepping-in-place bouts at 67, 84, and 100 steps/min; optional 200-m self-paced walk; Fitbit Charge 3 compared with hand-tallied direct observation; semi-structured interviews.

- **Comparator/control:** Hand-tally/direct observation criterion and interview themes; no metabolic control group.

- **Duration/follow-up:** Single visit.

- **Endpoints:** Fitbit step-count equivalence; mean absolute percent error; self-paced walking pace/cadence; acceptance and barriers for walking/stepping and wearable use.

- **Effect estimates or direction:** Fitbit Charge 3 was equivalent to hand-tally at 84 and 100 steps/min for several walking/stepping bouts and during the 200-m walk; MAPE was within 10% for walking at 84/100, stepping at 100, and 200-m walking. Slow 67 steps/min conditions were non-equivalent. Interviews found motivation for PA, acceptance of stepping-in-place, and time/competing-responsibility barriers.

- **Adverse events/safety notes:** Study physicians ensured participants were free of absolute contraindications to physical activity during pregnancy; no adverse-event signal was extracted.

- **Limitations:** Small sample; GDM/third-trimester population; not a post-meal walking efficacy study; device-specific; slow-cadence errors and wide limits of agreement.

- **Population mismatch:** Pregnancy/GDM wearable feasibility, not the general protocol efficacy population.

- **Directness to Walking After Every Meal:** background
