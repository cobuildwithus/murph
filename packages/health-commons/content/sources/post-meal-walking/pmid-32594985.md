This source is included for **free-living-adherence-registries-external-claims**.

**Findings:** A 3-month walking prescription increased self-rated health and self-reported walking time, while interviews identified routine, family support, observed benefits, monitoring, and evening safety barriers.

**Why it matters:** This is one of the clearest same-population adherence/barrier sources for translating walking prescriptions into daily life.

**Potential experiment signals:** self-rated health, self-reported walking time, routine formation, family/social support, evening safety barriers.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use to inform things-to-watch/adherence language; do not use it as primary glucose-outcome evidence.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** 28 adults with type 2 diabetes; mean age 60 ± 9 years, BMI 32.3 ± 4.0 kg/m², HbA1c 59 ± 16 mmol/mol.

- **Participant count:** 28 adults.

- **Intervention/exposure:** Walking prescription meeting physical activity guidelines: at least 10 minutes after breakfast, lunch, and dinner each day for 3 months; reminder contact every 2 weeks; no objective walking monitor.

- **Comparator/control:** Pre/post self-report and qualitative interviews; no randomized control group for the 3-month prescription.

- **Duration/follow-up:** 3 months.

- **Endpoints:** Self-rated health; self-reported physical activity/walking time; recorded interview themes for facilitators and barriers.

- **Effect estimates or direction:** Self-rated health increased 14% (95% CI 7–22%); time spent walking increased by 11 minutes/day (95% CI 4–18). Themes motivating walking included routine, family support, observed benefits, and health-professional monitoring; barriers centered on evening walking, insecurity in the dark, and sedentary preference.

- **Adverse events/safety notes:** Evening darkness/insecurity was a barrier, especially among female participants; no adverse-event count was extracted.

- **Limitations:** Walking was not objectively monitored; convenience sample from a prior trial; self-reported outcomes; no glucose endpoint in this qualitative follow-up.

- **Population mismatch:** T2D population is relevant; the source focuses on adherence and prescription support rather than direct glucose outcomes.
