## Findings
- Fourteen pregnant women with GDM completed 20-minute postprandial interval walks after breakfast, lunch, and dinner during a four-day intervention period.
- Accessible summaries report lower four-day daytime mean glucose during intervention (5.31 mmol/L) than control (5.53 mmol/L), with day-level reductions on days 1–3 and not day 4.
- The intervention was interval walking with fixed diet, making it a supervised GDM context source rather than general-protocol evidence.

## Why it matters
This is one of the closest pregnancy/GDM matches to walking after every meal, but its clinical pregnancy population and short fixed-diet crossover design require context-only use.

## Extraction details

- **Source kind:** journal_article
- **Study design:** crossover (Controlled crossover trial)
- **Participant count:** 14 (reported)
- **Population:** Pregnant women with gestational diabetes at approximately 31.8 ± 1.3 weeks gestation.
- **Intervention or exposure:** 20 minutes after breakfast, lunch, and dinner, alternating 3-minute slow and fast walking intervals.
- **Comparator or control:** No-exercise control period with the same fixed diet.
- **Duration or follow-up:** Four-day intervention period and four-day control period separated by a three-day washout.
- **Endpoints:** CGM daytime mean glucose, glycemic control, glucose variability.
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Accessible text reports 4-day daytime mean glucose 5.31 mmol/L during intervention vs 5.53 mmol/L during control (P<.05), with reductions on days 1–3 but not day 4.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse-event counts were extracted from accessible summaries.
- **Population mismatch:** Pregnant women with GDM; high directness to meal timing but context-only for general adults.
- **Directness to Walking After Every Meal:** clinical_supervised
- **Artifact candidates / rights:** Article/PDF identified as CC BY open access; if archived, preserve license metadata. `pdfRightsStatus=open_access`.

## Limitations
- n=14.
- Four-day intervention only.
- Gestational diabetes pregnancy population.
- Interval walking and fixed diet may not match casual walking after every meal.
- No long-term delivery or neonatal outcome inference from this short crossover.
