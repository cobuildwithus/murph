This source is included for **secondary-metabolism-lipids-insulin-cgm**.

**Findings:** This foundational intensity study suggests moderate but not low intensity walking reduced postprandial lipemia, with exact effect estimates unresolved.

**Why it matters:** Preserves early lipid intensity evidence and its uncertainty.

**Potential experiment signals:** postprandial lipemia, triglycerides, walking intensity.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as low/moderate-intensity lipid boundary evidence only.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Healthy normolipidemic adults (five men and four women; mean age about 28 years).

- **Participant count:** 9

- **Intervention/exposure:** Low- versus moderate-intensity walking with energy expenditure held constant, performed before the fat meal.

- **Comparator/control:** No-exercise control condition.

- **Duration/follow-up:** Single high-fat meal lipemia challenge after intensity-matched walking conditions.

- **Endpoints:** postprandial lipemia; triglycerides; walking intensity

- **Effect estimates or direction:** When walking was performed before the fat meal, moderate but not low intensity attenuated postprandial lipemia in extracted summaries; exact effect estimates were not resolved.

- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse-event signal was extracted from the available source record.

- **Limitations:** Walking was before the meal, not after; Small healthy young-adult sample; Exact effect estimates unresolved; Lipid endpoint only; Not a practical after-every-meal protocol

- **Population mismatch:** Adjacent walking-intensity lipid source, not walking after every meal.

- **Directness to Walking After Every Meal:** adjacent_variant
