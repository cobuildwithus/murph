## Findings
- Brief periodic exercise reduced some postprandial glucose peaks more than pre- or postprandial exercise.
- AUC endpoints did not differ, preserving a mixed peak-versus-exposure result.

## Why it matters
It is a timing-disambiguation source: activity spread through the day may affect peaks differently than consolidated pre- or post-meal exercise.

## Potential experiment signals
- Peak postprandial glucose after breakfast/lunch/dinner
- 24-hour glucose AUC
- 2-hour postprandial AUC

## Protocol takeaway
Use as adjacent timing context only; the best-performing pattern was not a simple walking-after-meal prescription.

## Claim use
`context-only`.

## Extraction details
- **Source kind:** journal_article
- **Study design:** crossover (Randomized crossover exercise-timing study with 24-hour glucose monitoring)
- **Participant count:** 11 (reported)
- **Population:** Young active healthy men.
- **Intervention or exposure:** Brief periodic exercise: three sets of 1-minute jogging plus 30 seconds rest every 30 minutes, 20 times total; comparator timing arms used matched intermittent exercise before or after meals.
- **Comparator or control:** No exercise, preprandial exercise, and postprandial exercise patterns.
- **Duration or follow-up:** 24-hour monitoring across randomized test patterns separated by at least 5 days.
- **Endpoints:** Peak glucose after meals and 24-hour/2-hour glucose AUC.
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Brief periodic exercise lowered breakfast peak glucose versus preprandial and postprandial exercise and lowered lunch peak versus postprandial exercise; dinner peaks and 24-hour/2-hour AUCs did not significantly differ among patterns.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse-event counts were extracted from accessible records.
- **Population mismatch:** Young active healthy men and jogging; not type 2 diabetes and not walking after every meal.
- **Directness to Walking After Every Meal:** same_mechanism
- **Artifact candidates / rights:** Publisher article likely requires permission; do not commit PDF. `pdfRightsStatus=permission_required`.

## Limitations
- Young active healthy men.
- Jogging/intermittent exercise, not walking.
- Peak effects did not translate to AUC differences.
- Not a clinical T2D protocol.
