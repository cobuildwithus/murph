This source is included for **reviews-meta-analyses-mechanisms**.

**Findings:** Systematic review of exercise-and-eating timing studies in adults, concluding that post-meal exercise generally benefits postprandial glycemia while evidence is insufficient to define an ideal pre- versus post-meal timing rule.

**Why it matters:** It provides broad timing evidence across adult populations and highlights why protocol claims must account for meal timing, dose, and population differences.

**Potential experiment signals:** Postprandial glucose; Glucose iAUC; Time of day; Dinner glucose response.

**Protocol takeaway:** Post-meal exercise is promising for glycemia, but the review does not settle an optimal universal timing or dose.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Adults who were healthy, overweight or obese, or had type 2 diabetes; pregnancy, type 1 diabetes, and gestational diabetes were excluded.
- **Participant count:** 332
- **Participant count kind:** `reported_total_across_included_studies`
- **Intervention/exposure:** Exercise before or after meals, with varied modality, duration, intensity, and time of day.
- **Comparator/control:** No exercise or alternate exercise timing.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Mostly short-term controlled studies of postprandial responses after standardized meals.
- **Endpoints:** Postprandial glucose; Glucose iAUC; Time of day; Dinner glucose response
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Review concluded post-meal exercise benefitted postprandial glycemia regardless of time of day, but evidence was insufficient for a definitive pre- versus post-meal timing conclusion.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse-event rate was extracted.
- **Limitations:** Small, controlled studies; Heterogeneous protocols; No meta-analysis estimate extracted; Not specific to walking after every meal
- **Population mismatch:** Mixed adult populations and exercise modes; not a direct every-meal walking protocol.
- **Directness to Walking After Every Meal:** `same_mechanism`.
