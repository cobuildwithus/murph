This source is included for **safety-older-pregnancy-pediatric-hypotension**.

**Findings:** Post-meal systolic blood-pressure reductions in residential-care older adults were associated with fall history, but full numeric extraction was limited by access.

**Why it matters:** Provides fall-boundary evidence for frail/residential-care older adults where walking immediately after meals may coincide with low BP.

**Potential experiment signals:** post-meal systolic blood pressure, fall history, dizziness or near-fall during walk.

**Protocol takeaway:** Do not generalize routine after-meal walking to frail residential-care older adults without checking post-meal dizziness, low BP, and fall history.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Older people living in residential care.
- **Participant count:** Not fully extracted from accessible abstract-level record; snippets report 40 residents with two or more falls in the falls-history subgroup.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Post-meal systolic blood-pressure response, including after standing/walking contexts.
- **Comparator/control:** Different postprandial SBP levels and fall-history groups.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Post-meal assessment; exact duration not extracted from accessible record.
- **Endpoints:** post-meal systolic blood pressure, fall history, dizziness or near-fall during walk
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Postprandial SBP below 115 mmHg was associated with a history of falls; exact adjusted effect estimate not extracted.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** Low post-meal SBP may create fall risk if walking soon after eating in residential-care older adults.
- **Limitations:** Permission-required article; full numeric extraction incomplete; Observational association; Residential-care population mismatch
- **Population mismatch:** Safety-boundary evidence rather than routine adult/general wellness protocol evidence.
