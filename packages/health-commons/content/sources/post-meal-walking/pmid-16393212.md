## Findings
- Shorter dinner-to-bed time was significantly associated with increased odds of GERD after adjustment for smoking, drinking, and body mass index (p<0.0001). [source_artifact:pmid-16393212]
- Dinner-to-bed time less than 3 hours had OR 7.45 (95% CI 3.38 to 16.4) compared with 4 hours or more. [source_artifact:pmid-16393212]
- Findings were reported as consistent in non-erosive GERD and erosive esophagitis. [source_artifact:pmid-16393212]

## Why it matters
Post-dinner walks may also delay lying down, so reflux effects can be confounded by upright time. [source_artifact:pmid-16393212]

## Potential experiment signals
- time from dinner to bed
- lying down soon after eating
- nighttime reflux

## Protocol takeaway
Separate walking effects from the separate advice of avoiding bed soon after dinner. [source_artifact:pmid-16393212]

## Extraction details
- **Population:** Matched case-control sample with 147 GERD patients and 294 age- and sex-matched controls without GERD symptoms in the previous year.
- **Intervention or exposure:** Shorter dinner-to-bed interval, especially less than 3 hours after dinner.
- **Comparator or control:** Dinner-to-bed interval of 4 hours or more.
- **Duration or follow-up:** Matched case-control questionnaire assessment; no prospective intervention follow-up.
- **Endpoints:** GERD association; meal timing; recumbency timing
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Shorter dinner-to-bed time was significantly associated with increased odds of GERD after adjustment for smoking, drinking, and body mass index (p<0.0001). Dinner-to-bed time less than 3 hours had OR 7.45 (95% CI 3.38 to 16.4) compared with 4 hours or more. Findings were reported as consistent in non-erosive GERD and erosive esophagitis.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse events extracted.
- **Population mismatch:** Meal timing/upright interval source rather than a walking protocol source.
- **Directness to Walking After Every Meal:** adjacent_variant
- **Claim-use boundary:** context-only
- **Artifact rights status:** paywalled

## Limitations
- Adjacent meal-timing evidence, not post-meal walking.
- Participant count and effect estimates were not extracted.
- Observational timing association cannot prove walking effects.
