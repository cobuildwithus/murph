## Findings
- The 12-week daily postprandial light-activity prescription did not improve extracted glycemic variables.
- Self-reported daily physical activity increased, creating a mixed behavior-versus-biomarker result.

## Why it matters
It is an important non-supportive source: a plausible daily post-meal activity intervention did not translate into glycemic-variable improvement in this longer trial.

## Potential experiment signals
- Self-reported physical activity
- Blood glucose variables
- Longer-term glycemic control

## Protocol takeaway
Preserve as a null/mixed longer-term context source and avoid overstating durability of daily postprandial light activity.

## Claim use
`context-only`.

## Extraction details
- **Source kind:** journal_article
- **Study design:** rct (Twelve-week randomized controlled trial of daily postprandial light activity)
- **Participant count:** 56 (randomized; 40 completed)
- **Population:** Individuals with or at risk of hyperglycemia.
- **Intervention or exposure:** 30 minutes of light exercise within 30 minutes after a meal daily for 12 weeks.
- **Comparator or control:** Control group.
- **Duration or follow-up:** 12 weeks.
- **Endpoints:** Daily physical activity and glycemic variables.
- **Effect estimates or direction:** The intervention group had a greater increase in self-reported daily physical activity, but no glycemic variable changed and no between-group glycemic difference was detected after 12 weeks in accessible extraction.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse-event counts were extracted from accessible records.
- **Population mismatch:** Hyperglycemia-risk population, not necessarily T2D; activity was not a strict walk-after-every-meal protocol.
- **Directness to Walking After Every Meal:** same_mechanism
- **Artifact candidates / rights:** Paywalled article; do not commit PDF. `pdfRightsStatus=paywalled`.

## Limitations
- Attrition: 56 randomized and 40 completed.
- Self-reported physical activity.
- Not a precise walking-after-every-meal intervention.
- Population included people with or at risk of hyperglycemia.
