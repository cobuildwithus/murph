This source is included for **secondary-metabolism-lipids-insulin-cgm**.

**Findings:** Continuous and short-bout walking patterns lowered postprandial triglyceride iAUC in older inactive women with hypertriglyceridemia.

**Why it matters:** Preserves older-women lipid evidence and bout-pattern context.

**Potential experiment signals:** serum triglyceride incremental AUC, postprandial lipemia.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as lipid/bout-pattern context; do not convert to direct glycemic protocol claims.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Inactive older women with fasting hypertriglyceridemia (mean age about 71 years).

- **Participant count:** 12

- **Intervention/exposure:** Different walking patterns, including continuous walking and multiple short walking bouts.

- **Comparator/control:** Control condition without walking.

- **Duration/follow-up:** Eight-hour postprandial triglyceride assessment.

- **Endpoints:** serum triglyceride incremental AUC; postprandial lipemia

- **Effect estimates or direction:** Serum triglyceride iAUC was 35% and 33% lower in continuous and multiple-short-bout walking conditions versus control (8.2 ± 3.1 and 8.5 ± 5.4 vs 12.7 ± 5.8 mmol*8h/L; main effect p=0.001).

- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse-event signal was extracted from the source record.

- **Limitations:** Older women with hypertriglyceridemia; not a general population sample; Lipid endpoint rather than glucose/CGM; Laboratory meal testing; Walking pattern/timing not identical to walking after every meal

- **Population mismatch:** Same walking-bout mechanism for lipemia, but not direct after-every-meal walking protocol evidence.

- **Directness to Walking After Every Meal:** same_mechanism
