This source is included for **safety-guidelines-medication-boundaries**.

**Findings:** Most participants had substantial glucose declines; 30% became hypoglycemic or were treated for low glucose during/immediately after exercise.

**Why it matters:** It highlights that even walking-like aerobic exercise can produce clinically important glucose drops in youth with type 1 diabetes.

**Potential experiment signals:** Plasma glucose decline, Hypoglycemia during or immediately after exercise, Counterregulatory hormone response, Carbohydrate treatment response.

**Protocol takeaway:** For children/adolescents with type 1 diabetes, any walking experiment needs caregiver/clinical planning and glucose rescue procedures.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Children and adolescents with type 1 diabetes, ages 10 to under 18 years.
- **Participant count:** 50 (reported).
- **Intervention/exposure:** 75-minute aerobic treadmill protocol with repeated walking periods targeting moderate heart rate.
- **Comparator/control:** Within-session pre-exercise glucose/hormone state and response monitoring.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Single 75-minute exercise session with immediate monitoring.
- **Endpoints:** Plasma glucose decline, Hypoglycemia during or immediately after exercise, Counterregulatory hormone response, Carbohydrate treatment response
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Most participants had substantial glucose declines; 30% became hypoglycemic or were treated for low glucose during/immediately after exercise.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** Hypoglycemia was common when starting glucose was lower; a 15 g oral glucose dose was often not enough in the acute protocol.
- **Limitations:** Pediatric sample; Longer and more structured than casual post-meal walking; Not meal-timed after every meal.
- **Population mismatch:** Pediatric T1D safety boundary; not direct for general adult protocol.
- **Directness to Walking After Every Meal:** safety_boundary / adjacent_variant.
