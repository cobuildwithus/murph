This source is included for **direct-core-glycemic-dose-timing**.

**Findings:** Participants completed either three 10-minute walks after breakfast, lunch, and dinner; one 30-minute post-dinner walk; or no exercise. During the 2 hours after dinner, both walking conditions were lower than no exercise by up to about 1.01 mmol/L, and the accumulated and single-bout walking conditions did not differ.

**Why it matters:** It directly tests accumulated after-meal walking versus a single bout of equal duration in a non-diabetes adult population.

**Potential experiment signals:** 2-hour postprandial glucose, CGM glucose pattern, Accumulated versus continuous walking pattern, Dinner glucose response.

**Protocol takeaway:** Three 10-minute walks after meals and one 30-minute after-dinner walk both lowered dinner glucose compared with no exercise; they were not different from each other for the dinner response.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Apparently healthy, insufficiently active adults; mean age about 50 years; mean BMI about 29 kg/m².
- **Intervention/exposure:** moderate-intensity treadmill walking after meals
- **Comparator/control:** No exercise and single 30-minute post-dinner walking condition.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Acute day-long meal and walking-bout protocols with CGM-derived postprandial response windows.
- **Endpoints:** 2-hour postprandial glucose, CGM glucose pattern, Accumulated versus continuous walking pattern, Dinner glucose response
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse-event signal was extracted from the accessible PubMed record.
- **Limitations:** Small sample; apparently healthy insufficiently active adults; acute design; primary reported difference concentrated after dinner.
- **Population mismatch:** Direct walking pattern evidence, but not a diabetes population and not a longer free-living intervention.
- **Artifact candidates and rights:** No PMCID and likely paywalled Elsevier article; do not place PDF in Git.
