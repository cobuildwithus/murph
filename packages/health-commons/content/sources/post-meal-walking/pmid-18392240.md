## Findings
- Walking accelerated gastric emptying versus water control: t1/2 107 ± 5 minutes versus 123 ± 5 minutes, p=0.02. [source_artifact:pmid-18392240]
- Alcoholic digestifs and espresso did not significantly accelerate gastric emptying. [source_artifact:pmid-18392240]
- Dyspeptic symptoms were unchanged by walking. [source_artifact:pmid-18392240]

## Extraction details
- **Population:** Healthy young adult male volunteers; nonsmokers without regular medication use.
- **Intervention or exposure:** Slow treadmill walking at 4 km/hour after a large standardized meal.
- **Comparator or control:** Water control and postprandial alcoholic digestifs, espresso, ethanol, or glucose drink conditions in a randomized crossover protocol.
- **Duration or follow-up:** Each participant was studied up to nine times, with postprandial ultrasonography for 3 hours after the meal.
- **Endpoints:** ultrasound gastric emptying half-time; dyspeptic symptoms; blood ethanol
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Walking accelerated gastric emptying versus water control: t1/2 107 ± 5 minutes versus 123 ± 5 minutes, p=0.02. Alcoholic digestifs and espresso did not significantly accelerate gastric emptying. Dyspeptic symptoms were unchanged by walking.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No blood alcohol was detected after alcoholic drinks in the extracted report; no walking-related adverse events were reported in the accessible text.
- **Population mismatch:** Healthy male volunteer physiology study; symptom-free population limits digestive-comfort claims.
- **Directness to Walking After Every Meal:** direct_protocol
- **Claim-use boundary:** supports-protocol
- **Artifact rights status:** open_access

## Limitations
- Only 10 healthy male volunteers were studied.
- Walking was a treadmill exposure during one experimental meal, not a practical every-meal trial.
- The study measured gastric emptying, not glycemic outcomes or long-term symptoms.
- No dyspeptic symptom improvement was observed.
