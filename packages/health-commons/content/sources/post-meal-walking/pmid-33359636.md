This source is included for **reviews-meta-analyses-mechanisms**.

**Findings:** Network meta-analysis of prolonged-sitting interruption strategies in adults without chronic diseases, finding light- and moderate-intensity activity interruptions lowered postprandial glucose and insulin versus sitting.

**Why it matters:** It helps rank light and moderate activity breaks as adjacent ways to blunt postprandial responses during sitting.

**Potential experiment signals:** Postprandial glucose; Postprandial insulin; Break frequency; Break intensity.

**Protocol takeaway:** Frequent light or moderate activity interruptions are favorable in healthy-adult sitting studies, but the evidence is adjacent to an after-meal walking rule.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Adults without chronic diseases in crossover randomized controlled trials.
- **Participant count:** Participant count not applicable or not resolved in the extracted record.
- **Participant count kind:** `not_resolved_in_extraction`
- **Intervention/exposure:** Light- or moderate-intensity physical activity interruptions to prolonged sitting.
- **Comparator/control:** Uninterrupted prolonged sitting and other interruption modalities in a network.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Acute prolonged-sitting interruption protocols with postprandial glycemia and insulin assessment.
- **Endpoints:** Postprandial glucose; Postprandial insulin; Break frequency; Break intensity
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Light-intensity intermittent breaks reduced postprandial glycemia (SMD=-0.46, 95% CI -0.70 to -0.21) and insulin (SMD=-0.46, 95% CI -0.66 to -0.26); moderate-intensity intermittent breaks reduced glycemia (SMD=-0.69, 95% CI -1.00 to -0.37) and insulin (SMD=-0.47, 95% CI -0.77 to -0.17).
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse-event rate was extracted.
- **Limitations:** Network comparisons are indirect; Adults without chronic diseases; Not meal-timed; Participant count not resolved
- **Population mismatch:** Adults without chronic diseases and sitting-break protocols, not clinical or every-meal walking populations.
