This source is included for **sedentary-breaks-standing-micro-walks**.

**Findings:** Walking interruptions during sitting suppressed postprandial glycemia in sedentary adults, while appetite and gut hormone concentrations were not affected.

**Why it matters:** It adds boundary evidence that glycemic changes do not necessarily translate into appetite or gut-hormone changes.

**Potential experiment signals:** Postprandial glycemia, Appetite, Gut hormones, Energy balance.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use for outcomes and appetite-boundary context only.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Sedentary adults.
- **Participant count:** 13 sedentary adults; extracted metadata included 7 women.
- **Intervention/exposure:** 2-minute light- or moderate-intensity walking breaks every 20 minutes during prolonged sitting.
- **Comparator/control:** Uninterrupted sitting.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Five-hour acute postprandial protocol.
- **Endpoints:** Postprandial glycemia, Appetite, Gut hormones, Energy balance
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Walking breaks suppressed postprandial glycemia and induced an energy deficit but did not affect appetite or gut hormone concentrations in extracted notes.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse-event signal was extracted from available metadata.
- **Limitations:** Small sedentary sample; Acute sitting-break protocol; Not meal-triggered after-every-meal walking; Appetite/gut hormone effects were null
- **Population mismatch:** Adjacent variant: sitting interruptions rather than every-meal walking.
