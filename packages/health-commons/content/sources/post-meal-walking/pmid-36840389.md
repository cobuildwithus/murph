This source is included for **sedentary-breaks-standing-micro-walks**.

**Findings:** In sedentary prediabetic adults, frequent shorter standing breaks improved mean glucose more than less frequent longer standing breaks in extracted notes.

**Why it matters:** Standing-only dose/frequency source that helps define posture-boundary evidence.

**Potential experiment signals:** Postprandial glucose, Standing-break frequency, Standing-break duration.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as standing-boundary context only.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Sedentary adults with prediabetes or impaired glucose regulation.
- **Participant count:** 11 sedentary prediabetic adults.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Standing breaks with different frequency and duration during office-task sitting.
- **Comparator/control:** Continuous sitting and alternate standing-break schedules.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Three 7.5-hour laboratory visits.
- **Endpoints:** Postprandial glucose, Standing-break frequency, Standing-break duration
- **Effect estimates or direction:** High-frequency/low-duration standing breaks lowered mean glucose more than low-frequency/high-duration standing breaks in extracted notes.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse-event signal was extracted from available metadata.
- **Limitations:** Standing-only intervention; Very small prediabetic sample; Laboratory office-task visits; No walking arm
- **Population mismatch:** Adjacent variant: standing dose/frequency, not walking after meals.
