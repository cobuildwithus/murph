This source is included for **safety-older-pregnancy-pediatric-hypotension**.

**Findings:** PPH is clinically important in older adults and may cause syncope, falls, dizziness, weakness, angina, and stroke through multiple meal-related hemodynamic mechanisms.

**Why it matters:** Supplies clear clinical safety language for older-adult meal-related hypotension and falls.

**Potential experiment signals:** post-meal SBP fall, dizziness/syncope, falls, weakness or cerebral ischemic symptoms.

**Protocol takeaway:** Users with meal-related dizziness, fainting, falls, angina, or neurologic symptoms should not self-direct immediate post-meal walking without clinical evaluation.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Older adults and patients with autonomic failure or meal-related hypotension.
- **Participant count:** Not applicable; narrative clinical review.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Review of PPH definition, clinical consequences, mechanisms, and management.
- **Comparator/control:** Not applicable.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Narrative review; postprandial period after meals.
- **Endpoints:** post-meal SBP fall, dizziness/syncope, falls, weakness or cerebral ischemic symptoms
- **Effect estimates or direction:** PPH is defined by SBP fall ≥20 mmHg and may cause syncope, falls, dizziness, weakness, angina, and stroke.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** Food intake can have hypotensive effects in older adults; consider PPH in syncope, falls, dizziness, and cerebral ischemic symptoms.
- **Limitations:** Older narrative review; Not protocol-specific; Management recommendations may have evolved
- **Population mismatch:** Safety-boundary evidence rather than routine adult/general wellness protocol evidence.
