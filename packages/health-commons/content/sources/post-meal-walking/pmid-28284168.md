This source is included for **safety-guidelines-medication-boundaries**.

**Findings:** Post-breakfast exercise lowered glucose during the exercise bout; accessible metadata indicates the effect was not sustained for later meals.

**Why it matters:** Shows both potential glucose lowering and the need to avoid broad medication-population claims.

**Potential experiment signals:** Peak postprandial glucose, Glucose AUC during exercise, 2-hour post-breakfast AUC, Later meal glucose response.

**Protocol takeaway:** For users on glucose-lowering medications, suggest monitoring rather than assuming post-meal walking is universally safe or durable.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Adults with type 2 diabetes treated with add-on hypoglycemic agents.
- **Participant count:** 8 (reported).
- **Intervention/exposure:** Post-breakfast exercise during a standardized meal day while continuing drug treatment.
- **Comparator/control:** Drug-treated sedentary control condition.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Acute standardized meal day with CGM-derived postprandial metrics.
- **Endpoints:** Peak postprandial glucose, Glucose AUC during exercise, 2-hour post-breakfast AUC, Later meal glucose response
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Post-breakfast exercise lowered glucose during the exercise bout; accessible metadata indicates the effect was not sustained for later meals.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** Medication-treated T2D population makes this important for hypoglycemic-agent boundaries even though adverse-event details were limited.
- **Limitations:** Small sample; Acute day only; Exercise mode/dose not necessarily casual after-every-meal walking; Effects did not sustain to later meals.
- **Population mismatch:** Direct postmeal exercise in medicated T2D, but not every meal and safety-only in this batch.
- **Directness to Walking After Every Meal:** safety_boundary / adjacent_variant.
