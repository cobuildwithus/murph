This source is included for **safety-guidelines-medication-boundaries**.

**Findings:** Accessible metadata reports lower postprandial glucose peaks during exercise days than rest days in the small sample.

**Why it matters:** Historical evidence shows post-meal exercise can lower glucose in insulin-dependent diabetes but requires modern safety reinterpretation.

**Potential experiment signals:** Meal-related glucose intolerance, Peak postprandial glucose, Postprandial glucose response.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use only as historical safety/mechanism context, not as a user-facing recommendation.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Insulin-dependent diabetic individuals in an older physiology study.
- **Participant count:** 8 (reported).
- **Intervention/exposure:** Moderate postprandial exercise after meals while insulin conditions were controlled/usual.
- **Comparator/control:** Rest/no-exercise meal conditions.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Acute breakfast/lunch meal test sessions.
- **Endpoints:** Meal-related glucose intolerance, Peak postprandial glucose, Postprandial glucose response
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Accessible metadata reports lower postprandial glucose peaks during exercise days than rest days in the small sample.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** Older insulin-dependent source; demonstrates glucose lowering but predates modern insulin analogs, pumps, and CGM safety practices.
- **Limitations:** Very small older study; Insulin-dependent terminology predates modern classification; Not walking after every meal; Older diabetes treatment context.
- **Population mismatch:** Postprandial exercise in insulin-dependent diabetes, not modern protocol evidence.
- **Directness to Walking After Every Meal:** safety_boundary / same_mechanism.
