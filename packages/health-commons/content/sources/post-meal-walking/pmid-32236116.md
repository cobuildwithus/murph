## Findings
- Fourteen participants completed a randomized crossover comparison of 15–20 minutes of post-meal walking after one meal per day versus one prandial insulin injection.
- HbA1c and fructosamine changes were not significantly different between conditions in intention-to-treat or per-protocol analyses, and the authors described the effect magnitude as small.
- The paper reported hypoglycemia below 70 mg/dL in two participants and no other adverse events.

## Why it matters
This is a direct clinical walking-after-meal medication-context trial, but it is not an after-every-meal general protocol and its insulin-treated population requires safety interpretation.

## Protocol takeaway
Use as an insulin-treated safety boundary and null/mixed clinical trial: one-meal-per-day walking did not clearly outperform or differ from adding one prandial insulin injection.

## Extraction details

- **Source kind:** journal_article
- **Study design:** crossover (Randomized controlled crossover study)
- **Participant count:** 14 (reported)
- **Population:** Adults with type 2 diabetes treated with basal insulin and oral hypoglycemic drugs.
- **Intervention or exposure:** 15–20 minutes of post-meal walking after one meal per day, daily, for 6 weeks.
- **Comparator or control:** One prandial insulin injection before breakfast or the main meal for 6 weeks.
- **Duration or follow-up:** Two 6-week intervention periods in a crossover design.
- **Endpoints:** HbA1c primary outcome, fructosamine, SMBG excursions, hypoglycemia, adverse events, adherence.
- **Effect estimates or direction:** No significant difference in HbA1c or fructosamine changes between walking and basal-plus/prandial-insulin periods; reported effect magnitude was small.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** Hypoglycemia (<70 mg/dL) occurred in two participants; no other adverse events were reported in the extracted text.
- **Population mismatch:** Basal-insulin-treated type 2 diabetes; safety-only for general protocol and medication decisions.
- **Directness to Walking After Every Meal:** safety_boundary
- **Artifact candidates / rights:** PLOS ONE article is open access under CC BY; source page metadata can include the article, but full-text storage should preserve license metadata if added. `pdfRightsStatus=open_access`.

## Limitations
- n=14 completed.
- Only one post-meal walk per day, not after every meal.
- Six-week periods may be short for HbA1c.
- Small sample, possible carryover, nonadherence noted by authors.
- Medication comparison limits general wellness interpretation.
