This source is included for **sedentary-breaks-standing-micro-walks**.

**Findings:** In young men with central obesity, light or moderate walking interruptions reduced postprandial glucose iAUC but not insulin, C-peptide, triglycerides, or NEFA outcomes in extracted notes.

**Why it matters:** It adds central-obesity population context and preserves null secondary endpoints.

**Potential experiment signals:** Postprandial glucose, Insulin, C-peptide, Triglycerides, Nonesterified fatty acids.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as population-boundary and mixed-outcome context.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Young Chinese men with central obesity.
- **Participant count:** 18 young Chinese men with central obesity.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Light or moderate walking breaks interrupting prolonged sitting; extracted record notes 3-minute light or 1.5-minute moderate breaks every 30 minutes.
- **Comparator/control:** Prolonged sitting.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Acute 6-hour postprandial experiment.
- **Endpoints:** Postprandial glucose, Insulin, C-peptide, Triglycerides, Nonesterified fatty acids
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Walking interruptions reduced glucose incremental AUC versus sitting, but extracted notes did not show reductions for insulin, C-peptide, triglycerides, or nonesterified fatty acids.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse-event signal was extracted from available metadata.
- **Limitations:** Young men with central obesity; Acute sitting-break design; Not every-meal walking; Several secondary metabolic outcomes were null
- **Population mismatch:** Adjacent variant and population-specific: central obesity in young men, not general every-meal walking.
