This source is included for **direct-core-glycemic-dose-timing**.

**Findings:** Fifteen minutes of slow walking lowered glucose during walking and delayed peak glucose. Forty minutes of slow walking blunted the glycemic rise, delayed peak glucose, and reduced 2-hour incremental AUC; walking duration was negatively associated with iAUC.

**Why it matters:** It informs the low-intensity/light-walking boundary and suggests that even slow post-meal walking can reduce glucose response, especially at longer duration.

**Potential experiment signals:** 2-hour glucose iAUC, Peak glucose timing, Walking duration response, Finger-stick post-meal glucose.

**Protocol takeaway:** Slow walking can be useful, but the stronger AUC signal came from 40 minutes rather than 15 minutes in this small healthy-women sample.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Healthy women aged over 50 years.
- **Intervention/exposure:** slow postmeal walking
- **Comparator/control:** Seated control after carbohydrate-rich meal.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Acute carbohydrate-rich meal tests with 2-hour postprandial blood glucose sampling.
- **Endpoints:** 2-hour glucose iAUC, Peak glucose timing, Walking duration response, Finger-stick post-meal glucose
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse-event signal was extracted from accessible metadata.
- **Limitations:** Small sample; healthy women only; carbohydrate-rich meal; acute exposure rather than repeated daily protocol; publisher PDF likely paywalled.
- **Population mismatch:** Direct walking exposure but not a diabetes sample and not male or broad adult population.
- **Artifact candidates and rights:** No PMCID and likely paywalled; do not place PDF in Git.
