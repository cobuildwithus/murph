This source is included for **secondary-metabolism-lipids-insulin-cgm**.

**Findings:** Moderate treadmill walking 12 hours before a high-fat meal did not significantly reduce postprandial lipemia or inflammation in overweight men.

**Why it matters:** Important null lipid/inflammation boundary evidence for secondary-metabolism claims.

**Potential experiment signals:** postprandial lipemia, triglycerides, glucose, insulin, inflammatory markers.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use to temper claims about lipids and inflammation; timing and population may matter.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Overweight, insufficiently active men.

- **Participant count:** 12

- **Intervention/exposure:** Thirty or 60 minutes of moderate-intensity brisk treadmill walking at 60% VO2peak the evening before a high-fat meal.

- **Comparator/control:** Passive no-exercise control condition.

- **Duration/follow-up:** High-fat meal challenge about 12 hours after exercise with postprandial sampling.

- **Endpoints:** postprandial lipemia; triglycerides; glucose; insulin; inflammatory markers

- **Effect estimates or direction:** Neither 30 nor 60 minutes of moderate-intensity exercise about 12 hours before the high-fat meal significantly attenuated postprandial lipemia or inflammatory markers in extracted summaries. Glucose and insulin measures were not significantly different, though glucose time-to-peak increased after 60 minutes.

- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse-event signal was extracted from the source record.

- **Limitations:** Exercise was prior-evening/pretest rather than after meals; Only overweight men; High-fat meal challenge; Acute crossover; Null lipid and inflammation effects

- **Population mismatch:** Adjacent lipemia/inflammation source, not postmeal walking after every meal.

- **Directness to Walking After Every Meal:** adjacent_variant
