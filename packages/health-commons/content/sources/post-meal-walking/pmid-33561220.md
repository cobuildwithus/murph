This source is included for **direct-core-glycemic-dose-timing**.

**Findings:** Walking at 50% VO2max for 30 minutes beginning 20 minutes before the predicted individual postprandial glucose peak reduced 4-hour glucose iAUC and substantially reduced insulin and C-peptide iAUC. Walking started at the peak did not produce the same glucose iAUC reduction, though insulin-related endpoints improved.

**Why it matters:** It adds timing precision and insulin/C-peptide endpoints, but the personalized schedule is more complex than a simple after-every-meal instruction.

**Potential experiment signals:** 4-hour postprandial glucose iAUC, Insulin iAUC, C-peptide iAUC, Timing relative to individual glucose peak.

**Protocol takeaway:** Starting walking before the predicted glucose peak reduced glucose response more clearly than starting at the peak in young men with overweight or obesity.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Young men with overweight or obesity; mean age about 23 years and mean BMI about 27.4 kg/m².
- **Intervention/exposure:** moderate walking timed to individual postprandial glucose peak
- **Comparator/control:** Sitting and walking at the individual postprandial glucose peak.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Three 240-minute acute post-meal trials separated by 6-14 days.
- **Endpoints:** 4-hour postprandial glucose iAUC, Insulin iAUC, C-peptide iAUC, Timing relative to individual glucose peak
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No explicit adverse-event signal was extracted from accessible metadata.
- **Limitations:** Young men only; overweight/obesity sample; acute personalized timing; not a general every-meal instruction; paywalled full text.
- **Population mismatch:** Direct walking after a meal, but narrow sex/age/BMI population and personalized CGM timing reduce generalizability.
- **Artifact candidates and rights:** No PMCID and likely paywalled; do not place PDF in Git.
