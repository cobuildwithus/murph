This source is included for **sedentary-breaks-standing-micro-walks**.

**Findings:** A dose-response trial found that 5-minute light-walking breaks every 30 minutes attenuated glucose iAUC, while lower doses had clearer blood-pressure than glucose effects.

**Why it matters:** It helps estimate how short and frequent walking bouts may need to be for glucose versus blood-pressure effects.

**Potential experiment signals:** Postprandial glucose, Blood pressure, Fatigue, Dose response.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as dose-response context only.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Middle-aged or older adults exposed to prolonged sitting.
- **Participant count:** 11 middle-aged or older adults.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Light-intensity walking breaks varying by duration and frequency: 1 or 5 minutes every 30 or 60 minutes.
- **Comparator/control:** Uninterrupted sitting.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Acute laboratory prolonged-sitting day with cardiometabolic outcomes.
- **Endpoints:** Postprandial glucose, Blood pressure, Fatigue, Dose response
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Only the 5-minute walking break every 30 minutes significantly attenuated glucose iAUC in extracted notes; all walking-break doses lowered systolic blood pressure.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse-event signal was extracted from available metadata.
- **Limitations:** Very small sample; Sitting-break schedule rather than after each meal; Acute laboratory dose-response design
- **Population mismatch:** Adjacent variant: walking-break dose response, not every-meal walking.
