This source is included for **sedentary-breaks-standing-micro-walks**.

**Findings:** In office workers, light walking breaks during seated work reduced interstitial glucose exposure compared with uninterrupted sitting, while standing breaks were not clearly significant.

**Why it matters:** Applied workplace evidence separating walking from standing arms using interstitial glucose.

**Potential experiment signals:** Interstitial glucose concentration, Postprandial glucose.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use for workplace context and modality boundary only.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Middle-aged office workers.
- **Participant count:** 17 middle-aged office workers.
- **Intervention/exposure:** 2-minute light-intensity walking breaks every 20 minutes during seated office work.
- **Comparator/control:** Uninterrupted sitting and 2-minute standing breaks every 20 minutes.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Five-hour office-work-style acute protocol with standardized drinks and interstitial glucose monitoring.
- **Endpoints:** Interstitial glucose concentration, Postprandial glucose
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Walking breaks reduced interstitial glucose iAUC by about 55.5% versus uninterrupted sitting in extracted notes; standing was not statistically significant.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse-event signal was extracted from available metadata.
- **Limitations:** Small office-worker sample; Interstitial glucose rather than plasma glucose; Sitting-break office protocol rather than after-every-meal walks
- **Population mismatch:** Adjacent variant: office-work interruptions, not every-meal walking.
