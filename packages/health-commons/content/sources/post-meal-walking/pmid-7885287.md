This source is included for **sedentary-breaks-standing-micro-walks**.

**Findings:** A 12-week brisk-walking training program in sedentary middle-aged women improved fitness and postprandial insulinemia but did not reduce postprandial lipemia in extracted notes.

**Why it matters:** It preserves long-term walking-training context for secondary metabolic endpoints.

**Potential experiment signals:** Postprandial lipemia, Postprandial insulinemia, Endurance fitness.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use only as background training/lipid context.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Sedentary middle-aged women.
- **Participant count:** 26 sedentary middle-aged women.
- **Intervention/exposure:** 12 weeks of brisk walking training.
- **Comparator/control:** Pre-training or control comparison as reported in the training study.
- **Duration/follow-up:** 12 weeks, with postprandial lipemia and insulinemia assessed after training.
- **Endpoints:** Postprandial lipemia, Postprandial insulinemia, Endurance fitness
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Training improved endurance fitness and decreased postprandial insulinemia in extracted notes, but did not reduce postprandial lipemia 48 hours after the last training session.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse-event signal was extracted from available metadata.
- **Limitations:** Training adaptation study rather than acute post-meal walking; Middle-aged female sample; Older evidence base; Lipemia not improved in extracted notes
- **Population mismatch:** Adjacent variant: chronic brisk walking training, not acute walking after each meal.
