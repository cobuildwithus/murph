This source is included for **direct-core-glycemic-dose-timing**.

**Findings:** Post-meal blood glucose was measured every 15 minutes after high-glycemic food. Peak glucose and incremental AUC decreased with increasing duration of very light walking, and blood pressure was also reported to decrease.

**Why it matters:** It expands direct walking evidence to a high-risk minority population and supports feasibility of very light post-meal walking.

**Potential experiment signals:** Blood glucose peak, 2-hour glucose iAUC, Blood pressure, Feasibility in high-risk population.

**Protocol takeaway:** Very light slow post-meal walking can attenuate glucose response, but the evidence is exploratory and population-specific.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Female Pakistani immigrants in Norway; high-risk or diabetes-prone population context.
- **Intervention/exposure:** very light slow postmeal walking
- **Comparator/control:** Resting control after high-glycemic food.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Acute high-glycemic-food tests with repeated blood glucose measures over about 2 hours.
- **Endpoints:** Blood glucose peak, 2-hour glucose iAUC, Blood pressure, Feasibility in high-risk population
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No explicit adverse-event signal was extracted from accessible metadata.
- **Limitations:** Very small exploratory sample; only female Pakistani immigrants; high-glycemic-food challenge; limited accessible protocol details; not a repeated every-meal trial.
- **Population mismatch:** Direct post-meal walking modality, but narrow population and food challenge context limit generalization.
- **Artifact candidates and rights:** No PMCID in canonical ledger; use metadata and avoid storing publisher PDF without permission.
