This source is included for **direct-core-glycemic-dose-timing**.

**Findings:** After-meal walking reduced 3-hour post-meal iAUC versus a single daily walking instruction, with a ratio of geometric means of 0.88 (95% CI 0.78-0.99). Dinner showed a larger effect, ratio 0.78 (95% CI 0.67-0.91).

**Why it matters:** It directly tests the behavioral instruction that is closest to Walking After Every Meal in a clinical population.

**Potential experiment signals:** 3-hour post-meal glucose iAUC, Dinner iAUC, CGM glucose pattern, Accelerometer-measured adherence.

**Protocol takeaway:** In type 2 diabetes, specifying 10-minute walks after each main meal lowered postprandial glycemia more than generic daily walking advice with the same nominal activity target.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Adults with type 2 diabetes; mean age about 60 years; mean diabetes duration about 10 years.
- **Intervention/exposure:** free-living walking after main meals
- **Comparator/control:** Advice to walk 30 minutes daily without specifying timing.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Two 2-week walking-advice periods separated by a 30-day washout; glycemia measured by 7-day CGM.
- **Endpoints:** 3-hour post-meal glucose iAUC, Dinner iAUC, CGM glucose pattern, Accelerometer-measured adherence
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** One participant died during the washout period; study participation was not judged to have contributed to the event.
- **Limitations:** Short intervention periods; advice-based free-living exposure; adults with type 2 diabetes rather than general adults; unblinded behavior change study.
- **Population mismatch:** Direct for type 2 diabetes self-management; cautious extrapolation to healthy users or weight-loss claims.
