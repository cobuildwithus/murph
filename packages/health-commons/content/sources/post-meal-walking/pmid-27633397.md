This source is included for **sedentary-breaks-standing-micro-walks**.

**Findings:** In inactive middle-aged men, intermittent walking improved postprandial insulin and glucose relative to sustained sitting, whereas intermittent standing did not.

**Why it matters:** It directly tests whether standing is enough or whether walking is needed in a postprandial sitting context.

**Potential experiment signals:** Postprandial glucose, Insulin.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use to distinguish walking from standing, but keep as context-only.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Inactive middle-aged men.
- **Participant count:** Participant count not resolved in the extracted record.
- **Intervention/exposure:** 2-minute light walking breaks every 20 minutes.
- **Comparator/control:** Sustained sitting and 2-minute standing breaks every 20 minutes.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Acute postprandial laboratory protocol.
- **Endpoints:** Postprandial glucose, Insulin
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Intermittent walking, but not standing, improved postprandial insulin and glucose relative to sustained sitting in extracted metadata.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse-event signal was extracted from available metadata.
- **Limitations:** Male inactive middle-aged population; Sample count not resolved in extraction; Sitting-break schedule rather than every-meal walking
- **Population mismatch:** Adjacent variant: walking or standing breaks during sitting, not a meal-triggered walking protocol.
