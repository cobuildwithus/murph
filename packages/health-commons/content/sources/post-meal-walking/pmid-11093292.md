This source is included for **secondary-metabolism-lipids-insulin-cgm**.

**Findings:** This small crossover study found accumulated or continuous brisk walking patterns lowered postprandial lipemia similarly, but the timing was premeal/adjacent rather than after every meal.

**Why it matters:** Foundational evidence that short walking accumulation can affect secondary lipid outcomes.

**Potential experiment signals:** postprandial plasma triacylglycerol, day-long postprandial lipemia.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use only as lipid-pattern context; do not promote as direct postmeal walking evidence.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Sedentary adults: 3 men and 7 postmenopausal women.

- **Participant count:** 10

- **Intervention/exposure:** Either one 30-minute brisk walk before breakfast or three 10-minute brisk walks before the three meals.

- **Comparator/control:** No-exercise control day.

- **Duration/follow-up:** Day-long postprandial assessment across three standardized meals.

- **Endpoints:** postprandial plasma triacylglycerol; day-long postprandial lipemia

- **Effect estimates or direction:** Thirty minutes of brisk walking, either as one session or accumulated through the day before meals, decreased postprandial plasma triacylglycerol; the different patterns were reported as similarly effective.

- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse-event signal was extracted from the available source record.

- **Limitations:** Walking occurred before meals or testing rather than after meals; Small sample; Lipid endpoint rather than primary glucose endpoint; Laboratory standardized meals

- **Population mismatch:** Adjacent walking-pattern lipid study; not walking after every meal.

- **Directness to Walking After Every Meal:** adjacent_variant
