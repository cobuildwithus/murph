This source is included for **direct-core-glycemic-dose-timing**.

**Findings:** In a small acute crossover trial, walking shortly after dinner lowered plasma glucose at the end of exercise compared with the same time point when exercise occurred before dinner. Total 4-hour glucose AUC was not significantly different across trial conditions, so the finding is timing-specific rather than a full-window superiority claim.

**Why it matters:** It is one of the closest adult timing anchors showing that moving after the meal can lower dinner-period glucose more than moving before the meal, while also preserving a null finding for total 4-hour AUC.

**Potential experiment signals:** Post-dinner blood glucose curve, 4-hour glucose AUC, Timing of walking relative to the meal, Symptoms or exercise tolerance.

**Protocol takeaway:** For a dinner-centered type 2 diabetes use case, post-meal walking appears more targeted than pre-meal walking, but the study does not prove a whole-day or every-meal benefit.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Older adults with uncomplicated type 2 diabetes; 6 women and 6 men; mean age about 61 years; mean HbA1c about 7.0%.
- **Intervention/exposure:** self-paced postprandial walking
- **Comparator/control:** Pre-dinner walking and no-exercise dinner control.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Single dinner test sessions with 4-hour post-dinner glucose follow-up.
- **Endpoints:** Post-dinner blood glucose curve, 4-hour glucose AUC, Timing of walking relative to the meal, Symptoms or exercise tolerance
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** The extracted abstract-level record did not identify adverse events; safety reporting was limited in accessible metadata.
- **Limitations:** Small acute study; dinner-only exposure; type 2 diabetes population; no repeated every-meal intervention; total 4-hour AUC was not significantly different.
- **Population mismatch:** Direct for adults with type 2 diabetes, but not a general adult free-living population and not an every-meal protocol.
- **Artifact candidates and rights:** No PMCID and likely paywalled publisher PDF; keep source page metadata and do not place the PDF in Git.
