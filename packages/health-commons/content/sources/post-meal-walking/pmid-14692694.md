## Findings
- Among all 69 patients, postprandial reflux percentage was similar across normal, delayed, and rapid gastric-emptying groups. [source_artifact:pmid-14692694]
- In patients with a positive pH study, normal gastric emptying had higher postprandial reflux percentage than delayed gastric emptying (22.0% versus 12.1%; p<0.05). [source_artifact:pmid-14692694]
- Patients with normal postprandial reflux had a higher prevalence of delayed gastric emptying than those with positive postprandial reflux (6/8 versus 9/27; p<0.05). [source_artifact:pmid-14692694]

## Why it matters
It prevents overclaiming that walking-induced faster emptying necessarily improves reflux. [source_artifact:pmid-14692694]

## Extraction details
- **Population:** Sixty-nine consecutive patients undergoing 24-hour esophageal pH monitoring and gastric emptying assessment.
- **Intervention or exposure:** Gastric emptying status after a solid radiolabeled meal as a mechanistic exposure.
- **Comparator or control:** Normal, delayed, and rapid gastric-emptying groups; positive versus negative pH study groups.
- **Duration or follow-up:** Twenty-four-hour esophageal pH monitoring with 2-hour postprandial reflux extraction after meals; solid-meal gastric emptying assessment.
- **Endpoints:** postprandial acid reflux; gastric emptying
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Among all 69 patients, postprandial reflux percentage was similar across normal, delayed, and rapid gastric-emptying groups. In patients with a positive pH study, normal gastric emptying had higher postprandial reflux percentage than delayed gastric emptying (22.0% versus 12.1%; p<0.05). Patients with normal postprandial reflux had a higher prevalence of delayed gastric emptying than those with positive postprandial reflux (6/8 versus 9/27; p<0.05).
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse events extracted.
