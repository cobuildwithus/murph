This source is included for **safety-older-pregnancy-pediatric-hypotension**.

**Findings:** The review found a small, heterogeneous non-drug PPH evidence base with possible benefits for several interventions and persistent need for larger, longer studies.

**Why it matters:** Places walking among broader PPH strategies and prevents overconfident claims that walking alone solves older-adult PPH risk.

**Potential experiment signals:** post-meal BP decline, dizziness/syncope symptoms, test-meal context, PPH intervention tolerance.

**Protocol takeaway:** For older adults with known or suspected PPH, walking after meals should be clinician-guided and considered among alternatives, not treated as a universal default.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Older adults with postprandial hypotension.
- **Participant count:** Participant total not extracted; review included 2 randomized controlled trials and 7 quasi-experimental studies.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Non-pharmacological PPH interventions including meal size, exercise, fiber, green tea, water therapy, and positioning.
- **Comparator/control:** Varied by included study.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Varied postprandial intervention and BP measurement windows.
- **Endpoints:** post-meal BP decline, dizziness/syncope symptoms, test-meal context, PPH intervention tolerance
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Small meals, exercise interventions, fiber with meals, green tea, and water therapy were reported as potentially effective; position changes had no impact on postprandial BP decrease.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** The review emphasizes need for larger samples and long-term follow-up; BP methods and test meals may affect trial effects.
- **Limitations:** Scoping review; Few intervention studies; Participant total not extracted; Heterogeneous interventions and methods
- **Population mismatch:** Safety-boundary evidence rather than routine adult/general wellness protocol evidence.
