This source is included for **sedentary-breaks-standing-micro-walks**.

**Findings:** In healthy workers, lunch-break exercise timing affected post-lunch glucose patterns, with post-lunch aerobic activity highlighted as a practical suppressive strategy in extracted notes.

**Why it matters:** Practical workplace timing source for lunch-specific postprandial movement.

**Potential experiment signals:** Post-lunch glucose, Exercise timing, Workplace feasibility.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as timing and feasibility context, not direct every-meal evidence.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Healthy working adults.
- **Participant count:** 11 healthy working adults completed the protocol.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Aerobic or resistance exercise before or after lunch break, including post-lunch aerobic exercise timing.
- **Comparator/control:** Alternative exercise type/timing patterns around lunch.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Workplace-style lunch-break experimental sessions with frequent glucose monitoring.
- **Endpoints:** Post-lunch glucose, Exercise timing, Workplace feasibility
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Extracted notes suggest 20-minute aerobic exercise after lunch, following lunch plus rest, may help prevent post-lunch glucose increases; timing conclusions depend on lunch-break constraints.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse-event signal was extracted from available metadata.
- **Limitations:** Small healthy-worker sample; Lunch-only timing source; Exercise type may exceed easy walking; Not every-meal walking
- **Population mismatch:** Adjacent variant: lunch-break exercise timing rather than repeated walking after every meal.
