## Findings
- A prior meal enhanced the glucose-lowering effect of exercise compared with the fasted state in sedentary men with type 2 diabetes.
- This is a mechanism/timing boundary source, not a walking-protocol source.

## Why it matters
It helps explain why after-meal movement can lower glucose more than fasted exercise in some type 2 diabetes contexts, while staying outside direct walking-protocol evidence.

## Potential experiment signals
- Plasma glucose
- Insulin
- Glucagon
- Free fatty acids
- Hepatic/peripheral glucose balance

## Protocol takeaway
Use as adjacent timing physiology only: do not cite it as direct evidence that walking after every meal works.

`context-only`.

## Extraction details
- **Source kind:** journal_article
- **Study design:** acute_physiology (Randomized acute fed-versus-fasted exercise physiology study)
- **Participant count:** 10 (reported)
- **Population:** Sedentary men with type 2 diabetes treated with oral agents and not under strict metabolic control.
- **Intervention or exposure:** 1 hour of exercise performed 2 hours after a standardized breakfast.
- **Comparator or control:** A matched exercise bout performed in the fasted state.
- **Duration or follow-up:** Acute exercise sessions, randomized order, about 1 week apart.
- **Endpoints:** Plasma glucose and related metabolic markers during and after exercise.
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Fasted exercise had minimal impact on plasma glucose in moderately hyperglycemic participants, whereas the postprandial/fed condition enhanced the glucose-lowering effect of exercise.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse-event counts were extracted from accessible records.
- **Population mismatch:** Sedentary men with type 2 diabetes; oral-agent users; exercise mode was not extracted as a walking-after-meal protocol.
- **Directness to Walking After Every Meal:** adjacent_variant
- **Artifact candidates / rights:** Publisher article likely requires permission; do not commit PDF. `pdfRightsStatus=permission_required`.

## Limitations
- Small sample of men only.
- Acute physiology setting.
- Exercise mode is adjacent rather than a practical every-meal walking prescription.
- Full-text details may require permission.
