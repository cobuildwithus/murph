## Findings
- Postprandial timing, especially starting around 15 minutes after a meal, appeared important for glucose-peak attenuation in healthy cohorts.
- Thirty versus 45 minutes of brisk walking were similar, and shorter cumulative activity-break patterns also reduced first-hour glycemia.

## Why it matters
It is a compact dose/timing/modality source for implementation hypotheses, but it is healthy-population evidence and not direct protocol evidence.

- **Intervention or exposure:** Six acute crossover studies testing brisk walking timing, exercise type, aerobic modality, 30 versus 45 minutes walking, and fragmented walking/activity breaks.

- **Duration or follow-up:** Acute post-meal experiments, mainly breakfast-focused.
- **Endpoints:** Postprandial capillary glucose, glucose AUC, and glucose peak.
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Postprandial but not preprandial exercise improved glycemic response in extracted studies; glycemic peak attenuation was reported only when exercise started 15 minutes after the meal; different exercise types produced similar reductions; 30 and 45 minutes brisk walking were similar; 10 and 20 cumulative minutes of activity breaks were sufficient to attenuate first-hour glycemia.
