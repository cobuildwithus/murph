This source is included for **safety-guidelines-medication-boundaries**.

**Findings:** Higher daily physical activity was associated with lower postmeal glucose AUC in adjusted observational models.

**Why it matters:** Wearables can capture real-world activity exposure relevant to meal glucose, but observational associations should not become protocol claims.

**Potential experiment signals:** Five-hour postmeal glucose AUC, Daily steps, Activity intensity time, Mealtime glucose control.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use for measurement design only: steps/activity and CGM around meals can be tracked in T1D experiments.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** People with type 1 diabetes contributing free-living CGM, meal, insulin, and wearable activity data.
- **Participant count:** 37 (reported).
- **Intervention/exposure:** Observed daily physical activity measured by steps and time above light intensity.
- **Comparator/control:** Lower versus higher daily physical activity exposure within observational models.
- **Duration/follow-up:** About 4 weeks of free-living monitoring per participant.
- **Endpoints:** Five-hour postmeal glucose AUC, Daily steps, Activity intensity time, Mealtime glucose control
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Higher daily physical activity was associated with lower postmeal glucose AUC in adjusted observational models.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** Observational source does not establish safety or causality; useful for measurement context in T1D.
- **Limitations:** Observational secondary analysis; Not prescribed post-meal walking; Cannot infer causality; Safety outcomes not the primary extracted focus.
- **Population mismatch:** Free-living T1D context, not intervention evidence.
