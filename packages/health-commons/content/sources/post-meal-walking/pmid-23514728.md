This source is included for **safety-guidelines-medication-boundaries**.

**Findings:** Large insulin reductions preserved early glycemia and reduced early hypoglycemia, but late-onset/nocturnal hypoglycemia still occurred across conditions.

**Why it matters:** Delayed hypoglycemia is a critical boundary often missed by simple after-meal movement advice.

**Potential experiment signals:** Early-onset hypoglycemia, Late-onset/nocturnal hypoglycemia, Glycemia after postexercise meal, Ketones.

**Protocol takeaway:** Warn that preventing early lows does not guarantee protection from later/nocturnal lows in insulin-treated users.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Male adults with type 1 diabetes using basal-bolus insulin.
- **Participant count:** 11 (reported).
- **Intervention/exposure:** Large pre-exercise and postexercise rapid-acting insulin reductions around a treadmill running session and postexercise meal.
- **Comparator/control:** Full or smaller postexercise insulin reduction conditions.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Lab exercise day plus later free-living/nocturnal follow-up.
- **Endpoints:** Early-onset hypoglycemia, Late-onset/nocturnal hypoglycemia, Glycemia after postexercise meal, Ketones
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Large insulin reductions preserved early glycemia and reduced early hypoglycemia, but late-onset/nocturnal hypoglycemia still occurred across conditions.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** Shows that even carefully reduced rapid-acting insulin may not eliminate delayed hypoglycemia risk after exercise in type 1 diabetes.
- **Limitations:** Small male sample; Running rather than walking; Not after every meal; Insulin strategy should not be generalized without clinician input.
- **Population mismatch:** T1D exercise medication study; adjacent and safety-only for post-meal walking.
