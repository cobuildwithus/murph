This source is included for **mechanism-and-dose**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Nine-day controlled-feeding isocaloric fructose-restriction study.
- **People studied or addressed:** Latino and African-American children with obesity and metabolic syndrome.
- **Duration or horizon:** 9 days.
- **Protocol role:** context-only; directness: `same_mechanism`.

## What it contributes

Use as mechanistic context for why fructose-containing added sugars are plausible targets; do not describe it as evidence that a self-directed no-added-sugar diet works in general adults.

## Potential Murph endpoints

fasting triglycerides, LDL-C, diastolic blood pressure, fasting insulin or OGTT-derived insulin sensitivity, body weight change as a confounder

## Important limits

- Population boundary: Children with obesity and metabolic syndrome; not healthy adults or general self-experimenters.
- No randomized usual-diet comparator was extracted for this source page.
- The intervention lasted only 9 days.
- Participants lost about 0.9 kg on average, so weight change cannot be fully separated from metabolic changes.
- Safety note: No adverse-event findings were extracted for protocol use.

## Plain-language takeaway

Metabolic endpoints such as triglycerides, fasting insulin, and blood pressure may be worth tracking only when clinically appropriate and interpreted with diet/weight changes.
