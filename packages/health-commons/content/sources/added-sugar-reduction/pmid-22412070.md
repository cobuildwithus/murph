This source is included for **sweetener-substitution**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Prospective cohort analysis in the Health Professionals Follow-up Study.
- **People studied or addressed:** US male health professionals in the Health Professionals Follow-up Study.
- **Duration or horizon:** 1986 to 2008; 22 years of follow-up.
- **Protocol role:** context-only; directness: `adjacent_variant`.

## What it contributes

Use only as adjacent SSB/ASB coronary-risk context. Do not cite ASB findings as proof that diet beverages are safe or that no-added-sugar avoidance works.

## Potential Murph endpoints

incident coronary heart disease, triglycerides, C-reactive protein, HDL cholesterol, leptin

## Important limits

- Population boundary: Male health-professional cohort and beverage exposure rather than general adult no-added-sugar self-experimenters.
- Prospective observational design cannot establish causality.
- Population was male health professionals, limiting generalizability.
- Exposure was beverage-specific, not a whole-diet no-added-sugar intervention.
- ASB nonassociation should not be converted into a blanket sweetener-safety claim.
- Safety note: ['No intervention adverse-event extraction applies.', 'Artificially sweetened beverage findings are safety/context boundaries, not protocol efficacy evidence.']

## Plain-language takeaway

Track beverage type separately from total added-sugar avoidance and include cardiometabolic markers if this source informs measurement.
