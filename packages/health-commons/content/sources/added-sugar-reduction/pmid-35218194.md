## Quick read

- **Source type:** 12-week parallel-group randomized controlled trial.
- **People studied or addressed:** Latino youth with obesity and elevated liver fat risk.
- **Duration or horizon:** 12 weeks.
- **Protocol role:** context-only; directness: `direct_protocol`.

## What it contributes

Sugar reduction can be achieved without producing a clear liver-fat advantage in all pediatric clinical settings.

## Potential Murph endpoints

free sugar intake, MRI liver fat, liver fibrosis, liver enzymes, anthropometrics, PNPLA3 interaction

## Important limits

- Population boundary: Latino adolescents with obesity and liver-fat risk.
- Pediatric Latino youth with obesity; not broad adults.
- Target was ≤10% calories from free sugar, not strict no-added-sugar.
- No significant liver or anthropometric effects despite reduced sugar intake.
- Exploratory fat-mass analysis was not the randomized treatment contrast.
- Safety note: No adverse-event signal was extracted from accessible abstract.

## Plain-language takeaway

Use as a prominent null/mixed result and a caveat against promising liver-fat improvement from sugar reduction alone.
