## What it contributes

Use only as early-life natural-experiment context; it does not test adult self-directed added-sugar avoidance.

## Potential Murph endpoints

Type 2 diabetes context, Hypertension context, Early-life exposure boundary

## Important limits

- Population boundary: First-1000-days exposure in mid-20th-century UK; major timing and setting mismatch for adult Murph protocol users.
- Exact sample size was not extracted here.
- Historical rationing affects whole dietary and social context, not added sugar alone.
- Early-life developmental exposure cannot be generalized to adult short-term protocols.
- Natural-experiment confounding and selection issues remain possible.
- Safety note: No adverse-event data extracted; use safety interpretation only where the source itself addresses population or risk boundaries.

## Plain-language takeaway

Use only as early-life natural-experiment context; it does not test adult self-directed added-sugar avoidance. Preserve confounding, measurement-error, and comparator uncertainty.
