This source is included for **sweetener-substitution**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Systematic review and meta-analyses of randomized, non-randomized, and observational studies.
- **People studied or addressed:** Adults and children in studies of non-sugar sweetener exposure.
- **Duration or horizon:** Study durations varied; many randomized studies were short-term.
- **Protocol role:** context-only; directness: `adjacent_variant`.

## What it contributes

Use as sweetener-context evidence only; do not recommend non-sugar sweeteners as a default no-added-sugar strategy based on this source.

## Potential Murph endpoints

body weight, BMI, fasting glucose, oral health, cancer, cardiovascular disease, kidney disease, mood, neurocognition, adverse effects

## Important limits

- Population boundary: Mixed adult and child sweetener studies, not a no-added-sugar diet trial.
- Broad mix of study designs and comparators.
- Many randomized studies were small or short.
- Certainty was limited for many outcomes.
- Non-sugar sweetener use is not added-sugar elimination.
- Safety note: ['No compelling evidence of important benefit was extracted; absence of clear benefit is not proof of long-term safety.', 'Treat as safety/context only for substitution decisions.']

## Plain-language takeaway

Monitor sweetener use separately and avoid overstating benefit or harm from low-certainty evidence.
