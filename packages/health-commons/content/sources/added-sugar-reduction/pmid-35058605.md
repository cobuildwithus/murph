## Quick read

- **Source type:** 12-week randomized clinical trial.
- **People studied or addressed:** Overweight or obese adults with FibroScan-proven NAFLD.
- **Duration or horizon:** 12 weeks.
- **Protocol role:** supports-protocol; directness: `clinical_supervised`.

## What it contributes

A low-free-sugar diet may improve NAFLD-related biomarkers under clinical supervision, but this should be framed as clinical-population evidence rather than a universal wellness result.

## Potential Murph endpoints

hepatic steatosis, fibrosis score, ALT, triglycerides, total cholesterol, fasting glucose, insulin or HOMA-IR, inflammatory markers

## Important limits

- Population boundary: Overweight/obese adults with FibroScan-proven NAFLD; not a general adult prevention cohort.
- Small completed sample.
- Adult NAFLD clinical population rather than general wellness users.
- Accessible abstract does not provide detailed adverse-event reporting.
- Subscription article; PDF rights not cleared.
- Safety note: No adverse-event details were extracted from the accessible abstract.

## Plain-language takeaway

Use as a high-priority clinical anchor and caveat broad claims by disease status, supervision, and 12-week duration.
