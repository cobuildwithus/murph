## What it contributes

Use as safety-boundary language for artificial sweetener substitution. Do not cite as no-added-sugar efficacy.

## Potential Murph endpoints

cardiovascular disease, cerebrovascular disease, coronary heart disease

## Important limits

- Population boundary: French cohort artificial-sweetener exposure, not no-added-sugar diet intervention.
- Prospective observational design cannot establish causality.
- Dietary exposure came from foods, beverages, and tabletop sweeteners, not a defined substitution protocol.
- Exact analytic sample size was not asserted because it was not carried in the canonical ledger for this run.
- Residual confounding and reverse causation remain possible.
- Safety note: ['Safety-only source for artificial sweeteners.', 'Do not use to support no-added-sugar efficacy or to claim confirmed adverse events from a protocol.']

## Plain-language takeaway

If sweeteners are used, track type and amount separately and consider cardiovascular-risk context as observational only.
