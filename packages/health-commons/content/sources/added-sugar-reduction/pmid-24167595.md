This source is included for **mechanism-and-dose**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Randomized double-blind beverage-substitution trial / satiety substudy.
- **People studied or addressed:** Children aged 7–11 years in a beverage-substitution trial subset.
- **Duration or horizon:** 18 months.
- **Protocol role:** context-only; directness: `same_mechanism`.

## What it contributes

Mention only as mechanism context: replacing sugar-sweetened beverages did not produce a clear short-term satiety penalty, but liking/wanting favored the sugared beverage.

## Potential Murph endpoints

hunger/satiety rating after sweet-beverage substitution, liking/wanting for sweet beverages, craving or desire to consume sugar-sweetened drinks

## Important limits

- Population boundary: Pediatric beverage intervention; not adults following a whole-diet no-added-sugar protocol.
- 203 children were randomized but 146 completed the satiety/liking assessments.
- Outcomes were beverage-specific and subjective.
- The study does not test whole-diet no-added-sugar adherence or clinical efficacy.
- Safety note: Adverse events were not a central mechanistic endpoint; any reported events should not be used to compare overall safety of no-added-sugar diets.

## Plain-language takeaway

Track hunger, cravings, and beverage preference separately from weight or metabolic markers during a no-added-sugar experiment.
