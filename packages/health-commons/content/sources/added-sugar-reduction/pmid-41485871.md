This source is included for **mechanism-and-dose**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Six-month parallel-group low/regular/high dietary sweet-taste exposure RCT.
- **People studied or addressed:** Healthy adults, female/male 123/57, mean age 35 ± 15 years, BMI 23 ± 3 kg/m².
- **Duration or horizon:** 6 months plus 4-month follow-up.
- **Protocol role:** context-only; directness: `same_mechanism`.

## What it contributes

Avoid claiming that a no-added-sugar diet works by automatically lowering sweet-taste liking; frame as uncertain and measurable.

## Potential Murph endpoints

sweet taste liking, sweet taste intensity, sweet-food choice, energy intake, body weight, cardiometabolic markers, adverse events

## Important limits

- Population boundary: Healthy adults in a sweet-taste exposure trial rather than added-sugar-only elimination.
- Sweet-taste exposure included sugars, low-calorie sweeteners, fruits, and dairy.
- Partial food provision may not reflect fully free-living no-added-sugar protocols.
- Null effects should be preserved rather than ignored.
- Safety note: No between-group differences in adverse events were reported in the abstract.

## Plain-language takeaway

Taste liking, sweet-food intake, energy intake, and weight should be measured independently; sweet exposure alone may not shift them.
