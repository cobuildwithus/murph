This source is included for **mechanism-and-dose**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Two brief online information-framing experiments.
- **People studied or addressed:** Online adult participants across two studies.
- **Duration or horizon:** Brief online interventions.
- **Protocol role:** context-only; directness: `same_mechanism`.

## What it contributes

Frame recommendations around positive outcomes of low-sugar choices rather than fear-based high-sugar consequences when possible.

## Potential Murph endpoints

mood after protocol messaging, appetite for high-sugar foods, appetite for low-sugar foods, visual cue attention, message framing preference

## Important limits

- Population boundary: Online intervention experiments; not a dietary protocol trial.
- Self-reported appetite and mood endpoints.
- Brief online tasks, not diet change.
- No effect on visual attention to food images.
- Safety note: Negative framing reduced positive mood in Study I, which is a messaging harm/burden signal.

## Plain-language takeaway

Track mood and appetite responses to different planning/messaging approaches.
