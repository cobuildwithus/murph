This source is included for **sweetener-substitution**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Prospective cohort analysis of beverage-intake changes in three US cohorts.
- **People studied or addressed:** US women and men in three prospective health-professional cohorts.
- **Duration or horizon:** Long-term cohort follow-up; repeated beverage-change windows in three cohorts.
- **Protocol role:** context-only; directness: `adjacent_variant`.

## What it contributes

Use for substitution caution only. Do not claim ASB replacement reduces diabetes risk from this source.

## Potential Murph endpoints

incident type 2 diabetes, beverage substitution

## Important limits

- Population boundary: Health-professional beverage-change cohorts, not randomized no-added-sugar avoidance.
- Prospective observational substitution models cannot establish causality.
- ASB changes may reflect reverse causation or surveillance bias.
- Sugary-beverage category includes 100% fruit juice, so it must not be reduced to added sugar alone.
- Health-professional cohorts limit generalizability.
- Safety note: ['ASB increase is a cautionary observational signal, not proof of harm.', 'Fruit juice remains a separate boundary exposure.']

## Plain-language takeaway

Log what beverage replaces sugary drinks; water, coffee, tea, ASB, and fruit juice are not interchangeable.
