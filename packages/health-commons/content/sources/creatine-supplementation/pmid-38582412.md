## Quick read

- **Source type:** Systematic review of creatine, brain creatine, and cognition evidence (2024).
- **People studied or addressed:** Adults across cognition-focused creatine studies, including stressed and non-stressed contexts.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Creatine supplementation for brain creatine or cognitive outcomes.
- **Comparator/control:** Placebo/control or non-supplemented comparator depending on included studies.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Varied across included cognition studies.
- **Role in Murph:** context-only / adjacent_variant.

## Findings

**Findings:** The review supports a cautious reading: brain-creatine changes and cognitive-performance claims do not translate cleanly into broad cognition benefits, with stronger plausibility in stressed contexts than in non-stressed adults [source_artifact:pmid-38582412].

## Why it matters

It preserves disagreement in the cognition literature and prevents only-positive brain claims from dominating the protocol narrative. [source_artifact:pmid-38582412]

## Potential experiment signals

Optional cognition tasks should be treated as exploratory and especially sensitive to sleep deprivation, fatigue, stress, and task choice [source_artifact:pmid-38582412].

## Protocol takeaway

Keep cognition as an off-scope or optional exploratory claim; do not use this source to support the core monohydrate protocol. [source_artifact:pmid-38582412]

## Key limitations and population mismatch

- Participant totals and study counts were not extracted from the available batch metadata [source_artifact:pmid-38582412]
- Systematic-review conclusion is cognition-specific [source_artifact:pmid-38582412]
- Not designed to evaluate exercise performance or body-composition protocol outcomes [source_artifact:pmid-38582412]
- Population mismatch: Cognition-focused adult literature, especially stressed cognition contexts, is adjacent to a healthy gym supplementation protocol. [source_artifact:pmid-38582412]
