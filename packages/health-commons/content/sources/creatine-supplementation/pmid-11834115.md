This source is included for **repeated_sprint_power_trials**.

**Findings:** Accessible summary reported creatine was more effective when recovery intervals were short; effects were most relevant when recovery was less than about 6 minutes.

**Population and intervention:** Healthy adult men; Acute creatine supplementation before repeated sprint cycling with varied recovery intervals. Comparator: Placebo/control trials with different recovery intervals. Duration/follow-up: Acute supplementation before multiple-bout sprint cycling tests.

**Endpoints:** eight 15-second sprint cycling bouts, peak power, fatigue index, recovery-interval sensitivity.

**Safety notes:** No adverse-event extraction was available from the accessible record for this batch.

**Limitations:** Exact group allocation and numeric effect estimates not fully extracted. Acute cycling protocol may not generalize to all sports.

**Population mismatch:** Adult men in lab cycling protocol.

**Why it matters:** Directly informs the interval-structure caveat for repeated-sprint outcomes.

**Potential experiment signals:** eight 15-second sprint cycling bouts, peak power, fatigue index, recovery-interval sensitivity.

**Protocol takeaway:** Creatine effects may be more apparent when repeated efforts have limited recovery.

**Murph takeaway:** Record rest intervals alongside repeated-sprint performance.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`. Directness: `direct_protocol`. Result boundary: `positive`.
