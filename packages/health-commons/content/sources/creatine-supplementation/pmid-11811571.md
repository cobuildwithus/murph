This source is included for **background_guidelines_external**.

**Findings:**
- From `source_artifact:pmid-11811571`: in an accessible abstract, five creatine non-users consumed creatine supplied in meat or solution, with plasma creatine followed for 6 hours.
- The meat condition reportedly produced a lower peak but a longer-maintained plasma concentration, with broadly similar overall exposure compared with solution.
- A separate solid-form comparison reported a lower peak and smaller exposure than solution, while still raising plasma creatine several-fold; the accessible abstract did not support extracting the full participant count for that portion.
- This is acute absorption evidence only, not evidence of training adaptation or long-term safety.

**Why it matters:** It helps distinguish formulation/absorption questions from actual efficacy outcomes.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- GI tolerance
- plasma creatine
- adherence by formulation
- body weight

**Protocol takeaway:** Context-only: formulation may affect absorption profile, but this does not prove outcome differences for a Murph protocol.

**Murph takeaway:** Creatine can be absorbed in several forms; use tolerability and adherence rather than assuming acute plasma peaks equal better results.

**Limitations and population mismatch:** Paywalled article; accessible abstract provides partial study details; acute plasma kinetics cannot be promoted to direct protocol outcome claims.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
