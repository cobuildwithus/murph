This source is included for **strength_hypertrophy_trials**.

**Findings:** `source_artifact:pmid-14967873` — Previously nonresistance-trained men completed four weeks of resistance training. Creatine was given as 20 g/day plus 140 g/day glucose for seven days, then 5 g/day plus 35 g/day glucose for 21 days. In creatine responders defined by body-mass response, isokinetic and isometric force changes were greater than placebo; the extracted abstract emphasizes responder-dependent benefit rather than a uniform whole-group effect.

**Why it matters:** Direct trial that helps explain why some users see larger early gains than others.

**Potential experiment signals:** isokinetic force at 180 degrees/second; isometric force; body mass; estimated creatine uptake.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as direct but mixed/responder-dependent evidence: creatine may improve strength with training when uptake/body-mass response occurs.

**Limitations and extraction boundary:** Small male novice sample. Responder subgroup emphasis limits whole-group claim strength. Glucose co-administration makes the supplement context partly co-ingestion-specific. Accessible record did not report adverse events.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.
