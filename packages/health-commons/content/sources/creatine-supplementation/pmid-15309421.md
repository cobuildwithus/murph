This source is included for **safety_tolerability_adverse_events**.

**Findings:**
- Eighteen professional basketball players participated in a longitudinal safety study.
- Available PubMed summary states that low-dose creatine monohydrate did not produce laboratory abnormalities for the majority of parameters tested.
- The authors discussed intense eccentric training load as a potential explanation for high creatine kinase patterns rather than a supplement side effect.

**Why it matters:**
Preserves longer-term athlete safety monitoring in this batch without overclaiming beyond the study design.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- safety / clinical laboratory markers
- biomarker / serum markers
- safety / long term tolerability
- adverse_event / reported side effects

**Protocol takeaway:**
Use as athlete safety-boundary context, not as a direct efficacy or default-dose claim.

**Population mismatch / boundary:**
Professional male team-sport athletes; not general healthy adults or clinical groups.

**Limitations:**
- No clean randomized placebo control in the extracted record.
- Elite basketball training load limits generalization.
- Small sample.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.
