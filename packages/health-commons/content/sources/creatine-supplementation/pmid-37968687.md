## Quick read

- **Source type:** Preregistered randomized double-blind placebo-controlled crossover cognition trial (2023).
- **People studied or addressed:** Adults in a cognition-focused trial, with vegetarian and omnivore groups represented.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Creatine 5 g/day for 6 weeks in each crossover period.
- **Comparator/control:** Placebo crossover period.
- **Duration/follow-up:** 6 weeks per treatment period.
- **Role in Murph:** context-only / adjacent_variant.

## Findings

**Findings:** In 123 adults in a randomized double-blind placebo-controlled crossover trial using creatine 5 g/day for 6 weeks, Bayesian analyses supported a small cognitive benefit, but primary frequentist tests were not conventionally significant and exploratory tasks did not improve; side effects were reported more often with creatine than placebo (p=0.002; RR=4.25) [source_artifact:pmid-37968687].

## Why it matters

It is a recent primary cognition trial that preserves both mixed efficacy and adverse-event context. [source_artifact:pmid-37968687]

## Potential experiment signals

Optional reasoning or working-memory tests can be exploratory; side-effect check-ins should be preserved when cognition variants are discussed [source_artifact:pmid-37968687].

## Protocol takeaway

Keep as context-only cognition evidence; use the side-effect-report signal for things-to-watch if cognition variants are discussed. [source_artifact:pmid-37968687]

## Key limitations and population mismatch

- Cognition-only trial [source_artifact:pmid-37968687]
- Primary frequentist tests did not meet conventional significance thresholds [source_artifact:pmid-37968687]
- No gym-performance or body-composition outcomes [source_artifact:pmid-37968687]
- Side-effect categories were not extracted in detail here [source_artifact:pmid-37968687]
- Population mismatch: Cognitive-performance RCT in adults is adjacent to the default gym supplementation protocol. [source_artifact:pmid-37968687]
