This source is included for **strength_hypertrophy_trials**.

**Findings:** `source_artifact:pmid-32599716` — Physically active young adults took 0.0055 g/kg creatine or placebo after each training set during a six-week, five-day-per-week resistance-training program. Creatine improved leg press, chest press, total body strength, and leg press endurance; total body endurance improved in both groups but favored creatine. One participant reported gastrointestinal irritation during the first week.

**Why it matters:** Modern open-access direct trial with clear resistance-training endpoints and reported adverse-event/tolerability detail.

**Potential experiment signals:** leg press 1RM; chest press 1RM; total body strength; leg press endurance; total body endurance; GI irritation.

**Protocol takeaway:** Supports strength and muscular-endurance outcomes with creatine during a resistance-training program, but does not prove that intra-workout dosing is better than other creatine timing.

**Limitations and extraction boundary:** Small sample and below a priori power target. Intra-workout dosing was compared with placebo, not with morning, post-workout, or daily maintenance dosing. Population was young and physically active; results may not apply to older adults or clinical populations. Dietary creatine intake may not have been fully captured.
