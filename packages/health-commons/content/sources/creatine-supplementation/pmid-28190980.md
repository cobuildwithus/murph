This source is included for **population_boundary_adjacent_claims**.

**Findings:** Low-dose short-term creatine produced post-supplementation differences in total work between creatine and placebo; authors concluded it beneficially affected muscle power output in elite youth soccer players.

**Why it matters:** Low-dose short-term youth athlete boundary source.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- muscle power output
- Wingate total work
- youth athlete performance

**Protocol takeaway:** Keep youth use separate from default adult creatine monohydrate protocol.

**Murph takeaway:** Youth athlete use needs separate guardian/clinician context.

**Population:** Elite male youth soccer players, about 17 years old.

**Intervention or exposure:** Low-dose creatine monohydrate 0.03 g/kg/day.

**Comparator or control:** Placebo.

**Duration or follow-up:** 14 days.

**Endpoints:**
- muscle power output
- Wingate total work
- youth athlete performance

**Effect estimate or direction:** Low-dose short-term creatine produced post-supplementation differences in total work between creatine and placebo; authors concluded it beneficially affected muscle power output in elite youth soccer players.

**Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse-event details were recovered in the accessible batch extraction.

**Limitations:**
- Youth elite athlete population outside default adult protocol.
- Small sample and short duration.

**Population mismatch:** Youth athletes; context-only for adult protocol.
