This source is included for **repeated_sprint_power_trials**.

**Findings:** All groups improved with training; accessible summaries indicated the creatine-plus-glucose group had the greatest strength improvements.

**Population and intervention:** NCAA Division I college football athletes; Creatine supplementation during resistance training, including a creatine-plus-glucose condition in accessible summaries. Comparator: Placebo and/or creatine-alone comparison arms as reported in the trial. Duration/follow-up: 9 weeks of resistance training.

**Endpoints:** bench press strength, squat strength, power clean, training performance, body composition.

**Safety notes:** No adverse-event extraction was available from the accessible record for this batch. 

**Limitations:** Exact arm sizes and effect estimates not extracted. Co-ingestion/glucose arm complicates monohydrate-only interpretation.

**Population mismatch:** College football athletes in supervised training; not general sedentary users.

**Why it matters:** Direct trained-athlete resistance-training source with practical strength endpoints.

**Potential experiment signals:** bench press strength, squat strength, power clean, training performance, body composition.

**Protocol takeaway:** Creatine can be relevant during strength training, but co-ingestion context matters.

**Murph takeaway:** Track strength outcomes and note carbohydrate co-ingestion when interpreting response.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`. Directness: `direct_protocol`. Result boundary: `mixed`.
