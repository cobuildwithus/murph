This source is included for **safety_tolerability_adverse_events**.

**Findings:**
- The review focused on side effects of creatine supplementation in athletes.
- Available PubMed summary states that a review of the literature reveals a 1.0% to 2.3% increase in body mass.
- Use as safety-only context because it is not a controlled primary outcome study.

**Why it matters:**
Athlete side-effect claims are a common reason people stop creatine; this review helps locate those claims in the literature.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- safety / side effects
- biomarker / body mass
- symptom / water retention
- symptom / gastrointestinal distress
- symptom / cramps

**Protocol takeaway:**
Use for athlete side-effect context, especially separating expected mass/water changes from unsupported injury or dehydration claims.

**Population mismatch / boundary:**
Athletes; not a general population sample.

**Limitations:**
- Narrative review rather than systematic review.
- Athlete-focused and may not represent non-athlete populations.
- Not a substitute for primary trial data.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.
