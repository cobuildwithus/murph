This source is included for **body_weight_water_gi**.

**Findings:**
- Forty rheumatoid arthritis patients were randomized to creatine or placebo.
- Creatine increased appendicular lean mass by 0.52 ± 0.13 kg versus placebo (p=0.004) and the lean-mass change concurred with intracellular water gain of 0.64 ± 0.22 L (p=0.035).
- Creatine did not improve isometric knee extensor strength, handgrip strength, or objectively assessed physical function versus placebo; no treatment-related adverse effects were reported in available summaries.

**Why it matters:**
Shows lean-mass/water signal in a clinical population while preserving null function outcomes.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- population / rheumatoid arthritis
- biomarker / appendicular lean mass
- biomarker / intracellular water
- outcome / knee extensor strength
- outcome / handgrip strength
- outcome / objective physical function

**Protocol takeaway:**
Clinical body-composition context; do not generalize to default healthy-adult outcomes.

**Population mismatch / boundary:**
Rheumatoid arthritis patients; clinical supervised context.

**Limitations:**
- Clinical RA population; disease and cachexia mechanisms differ from healthy users.
- Function outcomes were null despite lean-mass change.
- Not a default protocol RCT.
