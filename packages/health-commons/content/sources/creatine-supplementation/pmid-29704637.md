## Quick read

- **Source type:** Systematic review of randomized controlled trials (2018).
- **People studied or addressed:** Healthy individuals in cognition-focused randomized controlled trials.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Oral creatine supplementation; formulations and regimens varied across included RCTs.
- **Comparator/control:** Placebo or control conditions in included RCTs.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Varied across included cognition RCTs; not a single gym-protocol duration.
- **Role in Murph:** context-only / adjacent_variant.

## Findings

**Findings:** The review included 6 randomized controlled trials with 281 healthy individuals and reported generally positive findings for short-term memory and intelligence/reasoning, while other cognitive domains were less clear [source_artifact:pmid-29704637].

## Why it matters

Cognition claims can easily bleed into general creatine copy; this source keeps them separated as adjacent, cognition-only evidence. [source_artifact:pmid-29704637]

## Potential experiment signals

Optional cognitive self-tests may be exploratory only; no strength, body-composition, or exercise-adaptation signal should be inferred [source_artifact:pmid-29704637].

## Protocol takeaway

Do not promote cognition results into the default creatine-monohydrate gym protocol; cite only as context for off-scope cognitive claims. [source_artifact:pmid-29704637]

## Key limitations and population mismatch

- Small evidence base with 6 RCTs and 281 participants [source_artifact:pmid-29704637]
- Cognition-only endpoints do not test strength, hypertrophy, sprint, water-weight, or renal-safety protocol endpoints [source_artifact:pmid-29704637]
- Regimens and cognitive tests varied across studies [source_artifact:pmid-29704637]
- Population mismatch: Healthy cognition trials are adjacent to the creatine-monohydrate protocol but do not directly test healthy-adult gym outcomes. [source_artifact:pmid-29704637]
