This source is included for **population_boundary_adjacent_claims**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** 2-year randomized double-blind placebo-controlled trial (2020).
- **People studied or addressed:** Postmenopausal/older women with osteopenia.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Creatine monohydrate 3 g/day.
- **Comparator/control:** Placebo.
- **Duration/follow-up:** 2 years.
- **Role in Murph:** context-only / adjacent_variant.

## Findings

**Findings:** In 200 older women with osteopenia randomized to creatine monohydrate 3 g/day or placebo for 2 years, the trial reported no improvement in bone health, lean mass, or muscle function and no effect on health-related laboratory parameters [source_artifact:pmid-31257405].

## Why it matters

It is a long-duration, adequately sized null trial that prevents bone-health benefits from being overpromised. [source_artifact:pmid-31257405]

## Potential experiment signals

Bone-density, falls/fractures, lean mass, muscle function, and lab-parameter outcomes are relevant to a bone/subgroup variant, not to the default protocol claim stack [source_artifact:pmid-31257405].

## Protocol takeaway

Use as context-only null evidence for bone-health variants; do not translate into default protocol benefits or harms. [source_artifact:pmid-31257405]

## Key limitations and population mismatch

- Older women with osteopenia, not general adult gym users [source_artifact:pmid-31257405]
- Bone-health endpoints are outside default protocol scope [source_artifact:pmid-31257405]
- Adverse-event details were not fully extracted here beyond laboratory-parameter statement [source_artifact:pmid-31257405]
- Population mismatch: Osteopenia/bone-health trial in older women is an adjacent clinical/subgroup context. [source_artifact:pmid-31257405]
