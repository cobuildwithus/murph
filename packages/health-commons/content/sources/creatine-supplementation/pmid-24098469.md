## Quick read

- **Source type:** 24-week randomized double-blind placebo-controlled factorial study (2013).
- **People studied or addressed:** Older women randomized to placebo, creatine, placebo plus strength training, or creatine plus strength training.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Creatine loading 4 × 5 g/day for 5 days followed by 5 g/day, with or without strength training.
- **Comparator/control:** Dextrose placebo with or without strength training.
- **Duration/follow-up:** 24 weeks.
- **Role in Murph:** context-only / same_mechanism.

## Findings

**Findings:** In a 24-week double-blind randomized study of older women allocated to placebo, creatine, placebo plus strength training, or creatine plus strength training, creatine did not significantly improve cognitive or emotional outcomes; strength training groups improved emotional state and strength [source_artifact:pmid-24098469].

## Why it matters

It preserves a null result in an older-women cognition/emotion context and prevents overclaiming beyond gym outcomes. [source_artifact:pmid-24098469]

## Potential experiment signals

Mood and cognitive tasks are optional context only; the study reported no self-reported adverse events and noted lack of tissue creatine assessment as a limitation [source_artifact:pmid-24098469].

## Protocol takeaway

Do not cite this trial for default creatine efficacy; use it to qualify cognition/mood claims in older women. [source_artifact:pmid-24098469]

## Key limitations and population mismatch

- Cognitive and emotional endpoints were the focus for this batch [source_artifact:pmid-24098469]
- Older women and factorial strength-training context may not generalize to default adults [source_artifact:pmid-24098469]
- No tissue brain or muscle creatine assessment [source_artifact:pmid-24098469]
- Population mismatch: Older-women emotional/cognitive study is not direct evidence for default healthy-adult gym outcomes, despite including a strength-training arm. [source_artifact:pmid-24098469]
