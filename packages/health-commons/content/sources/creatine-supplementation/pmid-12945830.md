This source is included for **synthesis_background**.

**Findings:**
- **outcome / Body composition:** The meta-analysis reported a small positive body-composition effect. Directness: `same_mechanism`. Claim use: `context-only`.
- **outcome / Exercise-performance categories:** Small positive effects were reported across several performance categories, with stronger relevance to short high-intensity work. Directness: `same_mechanism`. Claim use: `context-only`.
- **mixed_result / Protocol and task moderators:** Subgroup differences suggested stronger effects in loading-only, repetitive-bout, upper-body, and laboratory tasks. Directness: `same_mechanism`. Claim use: `context-only`.
- **null_result / Running and swimming performance:** This provides an early endurance/aquatic-performance boundary for creatine claims. Directness: `same_mechanism`. Claim use: `context-only`.

**Why it matters:** This broad meta-analysis reported small positive effects for body composition and high-intensity performance categories, while running and swimming performance did not appear to benefit.

- body mass

**Protocol takeaway:** Creatine’s expected benefit is more plausible for short high-intensity or strength-related outcomes than running/swimming endurance outcomes.
