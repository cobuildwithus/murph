This source is included for **strength_hypertrophy_trials**.

**Findings:** `source_artifact:pmid-11581551` — Untrained men were randomized to control, placebo plus training, or creatine plus training. The placebo and creatine groups trained three times weekly for 12 weeks, and creatine supplementation was 6 g/day. Strength, total body mass, fat-free mass, thigh volume, myofibrillar protein, and myosin-heavy-chain expression increased with training, with the creatine group reported as greater than placebo for several adaptation markers.

**Why it matters:** Adds direct trial evidence that links creatine plus training to both measurable training adaptations and muscle-protein-expression markers.

**Potential experiment signals:** muscle strength; fat-free mass; thigh volume; myofibrillar protein; MHC type I/IIa/IIx mRNA; MHC protein.

**Protocol takeaway:** Supports strength and lean-mass adaptation claims, while mechanistic endpoints should remain mechanism/context rather than standalone user outcomes.

**Limitations and extraction boundary:** Small untrained male sample. Mechanistic biopsy outcomes are not direct consumer endpoints. Accessible extraction did not provide full numeric effect estimates or adverse-event detail. Training was supervised and specific, limiting transfer to self-directed programs.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.
