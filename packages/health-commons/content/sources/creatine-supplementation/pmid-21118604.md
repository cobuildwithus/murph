## Quick read

- **Source type:** Randomized double-blind placebo-controlled cognition trial (2011).
- **People studied or addressed:** Young adult females, including vegetarians and omnivores.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Creatine supplement 20 g/day for 5 days.
- **Comparator/control:** Placebo.
- **Duration/follow-up:** 5 days.
- **Role in Murph:** context-only / direct_protocol.

## Findings

**Findings:** In 128 young adult females randomized to creatine 20 g/day for 5 days or placebo, the trial reported no effect on verbal fluency or vigilance, a vegetarian memory advantage, and reduced variability in choice reaction time irrespective of diet [source_artifact:pmid-21118604].

## Why it matters

The vegetarian subgroup often appears in creatine discussions; this source helps keep that subgroup claim tied to cognition rather than exercise outcomes. [source_artifact:pmid-21118604]

## Potential experiment signals

Diet pattern and optional cognitive tests can be logged as context, but this trial supplies no default strength, hypertrophy, or sprint-performance endpoint [source_artifact:pmid-21118604].

## Protocol takeaway

Cite as context-only for vegetarian/cognition claims; do not use for default gym efficacy. [source_artifact:pmid-21118604]

## Key limitations and population mismatch

- Cognition-only endpoint [source_artifact:pmid-21118604]
- Short 5-day intervention [source_artifact:pmid-21118604]
- Young adult female sample and diet-subgroup design [source_artifact:pmid-21118604]
- No exercise-performance or body-composition endpoints [source_artifact:pmid-21118604]
- Population mismatch: Healthy young female cognition trial is not a default gym-outcome trial, even though the exposure is creatine. [source_artifact:pmid-21118604]
