This source is included for **strength_hypertrophy_synthesis**.

**Findings:**
- **outcome / Lean body mass:** Creatine added to resistance training increased lean body mass. Directness: `direct_protocol`. Claim use: `supports-protocol`.
- **outcome / Body-fat percentage and fat mass:** Creatine plus resistance training reduced body-fat percentage and fat mass in the meta-analysis. Directness: `direct_protocol`. Claim use: `supports-protocol`.
- **null_result / Moderators: carbohydrate, training status, training volume:** Carbohydrate co-ingestion was not shown to improve body-composition effects in this synthesis. Directness: `direct_protocol`. Claim use: `supports-protocol`.
- **limitation / Risk of bias:** The evidence base was not uniformly low risk of bias. Directness: `direct_protocol`. Claim use: `supports-protocol`.

**Why it matters:** In adults younger than 50 performing resistance training, creatine increased lean body mass and reduced body-fat percentage and fat mass compared with resistance training alone.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- lean body mass
- body-fat percentage
- fat mass
- resistance-training adherence

**Protocol takeaway:** For body-composition outcomes, creatine is most defensible when paired with resistance training.

**Limitations and boundary notes:**
- Adults under 50 only
- Resistance-training context
- Training required
- Moderator analyses may be underpowered
- Protocol details varied
- Risk-of-bias concerns remain in about half of the included evidence
