This source is included for **population_boundary_adjacent_claims**.

**Findings:** Accessible summaries indicate improved upper-body strength in female athletes; body-fat findings were mixed by measurement method, and repeated isokinetic work/fatigue did not clearly improve.

**Why it matters:** Direct women-specific creatine monohydrate study.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- upper-body strength
- body composition
- isokinetic fatigue/work
- liver and kidney blood markers

**Protocol takeaway:** Creatine may support upper-body strength in women during training, but body-composition/fatigue claims are mixed.

**Murph takeaway:** For women-specific experiments, track upper-body strength and use consistent body-composition measurement.

**Population:** Healthy young women/female athletes performing resistance training or preseason conditioning.

**Intervention or exposure:** Oral creatine monohydrate during approximately 5 weeks of training/conditioning.

**Comparator or control:** Placebo during the same training program.

**Duration or follow-up:** Approximately 5 weeks.

**Endpoints:**
- upper-body strength
- body composition
- isokinetic fatigue/work
- liver and kidney blood markers

**Effect estimate or direction:** Accessible summaries indicate improved upper-body strength in female athletes; body-fat findings were mixed by measurement method, and repeated isokinetic work/fatigue did not clearly improve.

**Adverse events or safety notes:** Accessible practical-application text reported no indication of liver or kidney malfunction by blood metabolites over 5 weeks.

**Limitations:**
- Participant count was not recovered.
- Paywalled older source.
- Female athlete/preseason context may not generalize to all women.

**Population mismatch:** Women-specific athlete/training context.
