This source is included for **population_boundary_adjacent_claims**.

**Findings:** Both placebo and creatine training groups increased isokinetic force by about 6%, but creatine did not add a higher force increase. Creatine increased body mass by about 2 kg, partly attributable to body-water changes, especially intracellular water; relative water compartment volumes remained constant.

**Why it matters:** Shows scale-weight/water changes can occur without extra single-movement force gains.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- single-movement isokinetic force
- body mass
- body water compartments

**Protocol takeaway:** Creatine-related scale-weight increases may reflect water/lean compartment changes; strength effects are endpoint-dependent.

**Murph takeaway:** Pair scale data with performance tests.

**Population:** Healthy male adults; resistance-training subgroup plus non-training controls.

**Intervention or exposure:** Creatine supplementation during 42 days of strength training plus follow-up.

**Comparator or control:** Placebo during same training and a no-training/no-supplement control group.

**Duration or follow-up:** 42 days training plus 21 days detraining/follow-up; supplements about 9 weeks.

**Endpoints:**
- single-movement isokinetic force
- body mass
- body water compartments

**Effect estimate or direction:** Both placebo and creatine training groups increased isokinetic force by about 6%, but creatine did not add a higher force increase. Creatine increased body mass by about 2 kg, partly attributable to body-water changes, especially intracellular water; relative water compartment volumes remained constant.

**Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse-event details were recovered in the accessible batch extraction.

**Limitations:**
- Male-only small study.
- Single-movement strength endpoint may miss repeated-effort effects.
- Older paywalled source.

**Population mismatch:** Healthy trained male adults; body-water focus.
