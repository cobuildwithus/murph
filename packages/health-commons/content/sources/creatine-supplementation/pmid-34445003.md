This source is included for **strength_hypertrophy_synthesis**.

**Findings:**
- From `source_artifact:pmid-34445003`: common loading of about 20 g/day for 5-7 days is described as sufficient to saturate muscle creatine stores, while 3-5 g/day over about 28 days may reach a similar endpoint more gradually.
- From `source_artifact:pmid-34445003`: emerging evidence suggests post-exercise creatine may be more beneficial than pre-exercise creatine, but methodological limitations prevent a firm conclusion.
- The review identifies response modifiers such as baseline creatine stores, type II fiber content or size, diet, aging, and exercise status.
- The authors state that physiology and mechanism data for timing around exercise remain lacking.

**Why it matters:** It is a focused timing review that can prevent overconfident claims about pre- versus post-exercise creatine timing.

**Protocol takeaway:** Direct timing context with mixed evidence: loading and gradual dosing are both discussed, but timing superiority remains uncertain.

**Murph takeaway:** Taking creatine near training is reasonable, but this review does not make post-workout timing mandatory.

**Limitations and population mismatch:** Narrative timing review; limited number of direct pre/post trials; mechanistic data incomplete; do not overclaim timing superiority.
