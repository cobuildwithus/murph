This source is included for **timing_coingestion**.

**Findings:** [source_artifact:pmid-10444618] reported that one-legged submaximal exercise promoted creatine and glycogen accumulation in human skeletal muscle. The finding is direct to creatine monohydrate uptake but not a head-to-head pre/post supplementation outcome.

**Why it matters:** It supports an exercise-proximity mechanism for later timing hypotheses.

**Potential experiment signals:** Workout timing, dosing time, body mass, and strength progression can be logged; muscle uptake is not a typical Murph endpoint.

**Protocol takeaway:** Exercise may make the exercised muscle more receptive to creatine/glycogen accumulation, but this remains mechanistic rather than a definitive timing prescription.

**Limitations / boundary notes:** Exercise-limb physiology design; biopsy endpoints; limited generalizability to unsupervised dosing and strength outcomes.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.
