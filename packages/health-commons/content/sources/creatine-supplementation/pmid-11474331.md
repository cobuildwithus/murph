This source is included for **strength_hypertrophy_trials**.

**Findings:** `source_artifact:pmid-11474331` — Resistance-trained subjects loaded with creatine 20 g/day for seven days, then 5 g/day for eight weeks, or placebo. During electrically stimulated left-quadriceps overload, maximal torque, torque-time integral, fatigue/recovery markers, and trained quadriceps cross-sectional area changed with training but creatine did not significantly augment these responses. A nonstimulated quadriceps cross-sectional-area increase was reported in the creatine group.

**Why it matters:** Retains a direct creatine monohydrate but adjacent-modality null result to prevent positive-review bias.

**Potential experiment signals:** quadriceps cross-sectional area; maximal torque; torque-time integral; fatigue resistance; recovery between sets.

**Protocol takeaway:** Context-only/boundary: creatine did not enhance the trained-limb mechanical or hypertrophic response to this EMS-based resistive-overload protocol.

**Limitations and extraction boundary:** Adjacent overload modality: electrically stimulated conditioning rather than ordinary self-directed resistance training. Small sample. Subjects continued voluntary lower-limb resistance training outside the stimulated protocol. Accessible record did not report adverse events.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
