This source is included for **body_weight_water_gi**.

**Findings:**
- Thirteen healthy under-20 soccer players were randomized to placebo (n=6) or creatine (n=7).
- After 7 days, creatine increased total body water by 2.3 ± 1.0 L by deuterium oxide dilution and increased total body weight by 1.0 ± 0.4 kg.
- Bioelectrical impedance equations were not uniformly sensitive, so method choice matters for interpreting body-water changes.

**Why it matters:**
Deuterium oxide dilution provides stronger total-body-water evidence than scale weight or BIA alone.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- biomarker / total body water
- biomarker / body weight
- biomarker / bia total body water
- method / deuterium oxide dilution

**Protocol takeaway:**
Directly supports the protocol explanation that rapid early body-mass gains can reflect water changes.

**Population mismatch / boundary:**
Youth male athletes; direct water effect but not full default adult population.

**Limitations:**
- Very small sample.
- Under-20 male soccer players may not generalize to adults, women, or sedentary users.
- Short loading protocol only.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.
