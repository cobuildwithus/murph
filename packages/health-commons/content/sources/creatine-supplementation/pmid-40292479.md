This source is included for **body_weight_water_gi**.

**Findings:**
- Sixty-three healthy adults were randomized to 5 g/day creatine monohydrate or control for 13 weeks.
- After the 7-day wash-in, the supplement group gained 0.51 ± 1.79 kg more lean body mass than control (p=0.03).
- After 12 weeks of resistance training, both groups gained about 2 kg lean body mass with no between-group difference (p=0.71); females in the creatine group gained 0.59 ± 1.61 kg more after wash-in in sex-disaggregated analysis.

**Why it matters:**
Recent RCT directly asks whether short-term creatine-related lean mass persists as additional training gain.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- biomarker / lean body mass
- biomarker / body weight
- training / resistance training
- biomarker / sex disaggregated lean mass

**Protocol takeaway:**
Supports distinguishing early lean-mass/water signal from sustained training-associated hypertrophy claims.

**Population mismatch / boundary:**
Healthy adults but study design includes structured training and a non-placebo control.

**Limitations:**
- Control without placebo in available summaries.
- Healthy 18-50 sample with low recent resistance training; not trained athletes.
- Lean body mass may be influenced by water shifts.
