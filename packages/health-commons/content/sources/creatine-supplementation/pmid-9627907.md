This source is included for **background_guidelines_external**.

**Findings:**
- From `source_artifact:pmid-9627907`: early evidence suggested creatine may enhance some high-intensity short-duration tasks that rely on phosphocreatine, especially repeated laboratory bouts with limited recovery, but not all studies were positive.
- The review noted that field performance corroboration was still needed.
- The review reported short-term body-mass increases in males, with the initial increase likely related to water.
- Chronic creatine plus resistance training was described as potentially increasing lean body mass, but the review called for confirmation.

**Why it matters:** It documents early uncertainty, body-weight changes, and the distinction between lab performance signals and real-world performance.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- short-bout high-intensity performance
- body weight
- lean mass
- training performance
- water-retention symptoms

**Protocol takeaway:** Context-only: early review supports plausibility for short intense efforts but emphasizes mixed evidence and confirmation needs.

**Murph takeaway:** Expect possible body-weight gain and variable performance response; do not overinterpret early lab signals.

**Limitations and population mismatch:** Classic but old review; early evidence base; not a modern systematic effect estimate; permission-required rights.
