This source is included for **body_weight_water_gi**.

**Findings:**
- The study examined a creatine-containing supplement during training in athletes.
- Available summaries report greater body-weight and fat/bone-free-mass gains with the creatine-containing formulation than comparator.
- Because creatine was included with glucose/taurine/electrolytes, this is adjacent-variant evidence only.

**Why it matters:**
Classic study frequently cited for creatine and body composition, but it needs boundary labeling.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- biomarker / body weight
- biomarker / fat bone free mass
- outcome / strength
- outcome / sprint performance

**Protocol takeaway:**
Use only as adjacent-variant context for body-composition measurement during training.

**Population mismatch / boundary:**
Trained football athletes; adjacent formula rather than pure default protocol.

**Limitations:**
- Participant count and detailed effect estimates not captured in canonical extraction.
- Multi-ingredient formulation confounds creatine-alone attribution.
- Athlete training context.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
