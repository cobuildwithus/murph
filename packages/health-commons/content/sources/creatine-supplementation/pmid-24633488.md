This source is included for **population_boundary_adjacent_claims**.

**Findings:** Study conclusion indicates post-exercise creatine did not provide greater enhancement of body composition or muscle strength than resistance training with placebo.

**Why it matters:** Important null result in an otherwise positive older-adult batch.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- body composition
- muscle strength
- training-induced adaptations
- post-exercise timing

**Protocol takeaway:** Do not promise post-exercise creatine enhances older male RT adaptations in every study.

**Murph takeaway:** Timing and population context matter.

**Population:** Middle-to-older-aged males, approximately 55 to 70 years.

**Intervention or exposure:** Post-exercise creatine monohydrate during resistance training.

**Comparator or control:** Post-exercise placebo during same RT program.

**Duration or follow-up:** 12 weeks.

**Endpoints:**
- body composition
- muscle strength
- training-induced adaptations
- post-exercise timing

**Effect estimate or direction:** Study conclusion indicates post-exercise creatine did not provide greater enhancement of body composition or muscle strength than resistance training with placebo.

**Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse-event details were recovered in the accessible batch extraction.

**Limitations:**
- Small male-only sample.
- Post-exercise timing design.

**Population mismatch:** Middle-to-older male RT context.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.
