This source is included for **repeated_sprint_power_trials**.

**Findings:** Creatine group maintained or slightly improved countermovement jump while placebo declined; group-by-time p-value was not clearly significant, but fewer creatine athletes showed CMJ reductions.

**Population and intervention:** Brazilian elite soccer players; Creatine monohydrate 20 g/day for 1 week, then 5 g/day for 6 weeks. Comparator: Dextrose placebo during the same training period. Duration/follow-up: 7 weeks of pre-season training.

**Endpoints:** countermovement jump, lower-limb muscle power, pre-season power decrement.

**Safety notes:** No adverse-event extraction was available from the accessible record for this batch.

**Limitations:** Only 14 of 23 recruited athletes completed/analyzed. No muscle creatine measurements. Group-by-time result was not clearly significant.

**Population mismatch:** Elite male soccer players during pre-season; not a general population.

**Why it matters:** Open-access direct soccer power trial with clearly described dosing.

**Potential experiment signals:** countermovement jump, lower-limb muscle power, pre-season power decrement.

**Protocol takeaway:** Creatine may help preserve lower-limb power during demanding training blocks.

**Murph takeaway:** Track CMJ/jump height and training phase when interpreting power changes.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`. Directness: `direct_protocol`. Result boundary: `mixed`.
