## Quick read

- **Source type:** 2-year randomized controlled trial during exercise training (2023).
- **People studied or addressed:** Postmenopausal women, mean age about 59 years, participating in resistance training and walking.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Creatine approximately 0.14 g/kg/day during resistance training and walking.
- **Comparator/control:** Placebo during the same exercise program.
- **Duration/follow-up:** 2 years.
- **Role in Murph:** context-only / adjacent_variant.

## Findings

**Findings:** In 237 postmenopausal women randomized to creatine or placebo during resistance training and walking for 2 years, the primary BMD outcome was null; selected secondary findings included bone-geometry or function signals, but these remain bone-specific adjacent evidence [source_artifact:pmid-37144634].

## Why it matters

It is a large, recent, open-access bone trial that directly informs whether bone-health claims should remain quarantined. [source_artifact:pmid-37144634]

## Potential experiment signals

Track DXA/geometry only in bone-health variants; the default protocol should not promise BMD improvement [source_artifact:pmid-37144634].

## Protocol takeaway

Use for bone-health variant synthesis only; do not treat secondary signals as default creatine benefits. [source_artifact:pmid-37144634]

## Key limitations and population mismatch

- Postmenopausal women and structured exercise program differ from default protocol [source_artifact:pmid-37144634]
- Primary BMD outcome was null [source_artifact:pmid-37144634]
- Selected secondary findings should not be overpromoted [source_artifact:pmid-37144634]
- Safety/adverse-event details were not extracted in this batch [source_artifact:pmid-37144634]
- Population mismatch: Bone-health trial in postmenopausal women during exercise is adjacent to the default protocol. [source_artifact:pmid-37144634]
