This source is included for **repeated_sprint_power_trials**.

**Findings:** Accessible abstract reported enhanced fatigue resistance associated with higher phosphocreatine and lower lactate, while peak power and jump performance were not enhanced.

**Population and intervention:** Male subjects performing short-duration high-intensity exercise; Creatine 20 g/day before repeated short high-intensity cycling and biopsy assessment. Comparator: Placebo/control condition. Duration/follow-up: 6 days of creatine supplementation.

**Endpoints:** muscle phosphocreatine, fatigue resistance, blood/muscle lactate, peak power, jump performance.

**Safety notes:** No adverse-event extraction was available from the accessible record for this batch. 

**Limitations:** Very small sample. Mechanistic same-mechanism scope rather than clean sport-performance RCT. Not all power/jump endpoints improved.

**Population mismatch:** Small male physiology sample; limited generalizability.

**Why it matters:** Links creatine to PCr availability and repeated-effort fatigue in a direct exercise model.

**Potential experiment signals:** muscle phosphocreatine, fatigue resistance, blood/muscle lactate, peak power, jump performance.

**Protocol takeaway:** Mechanistic support is stronger for fatigue resistance than for all peak-power endpoints.

**Murph takeaway:** Track fatigue slope and repeated-rep sustainability, not just peak output.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`. Directness: `same_mechanism`. Result boundary: `mixed`.
