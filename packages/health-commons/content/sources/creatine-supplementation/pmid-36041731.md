This source is included for **repeated_sprint_power_synthesis**.

**Findings:**
- **outcome / Repeated-sprint mean power output:** Short-term creatine increased repeated-sprint mean power output. Directness: `direct_protocol`. Claim use: `supports-protocol`.
- **adverse_event / Body mass:** Short-term creatine loading increased body mass. Directness: `direct_protocol`. Claim use: `supports-protocol`.
- **null_result / Peak power, fatigue, post-test blood lactate:** Peak power, fatigue, and lactate outcomes were not clearly improved. Directness: `direct_protocol`. Claim use: `supports-protocol`.

**Why it matters:** This short-term repeated-sprint meta-analysis found increased mean power and body mass, but no clear effects on peak power, fatigue, or post-test blood lactate.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- repeated-sprint mean power
- peak power
- fatigue index
- body weight

**Protocol takeaway:** A short loading protocol may improve repeated-sprint mean power, but users should not expect every sprint metric to improve.

**Limitations and boundary notes:**
- Short-term loading protocols
- Muscle creatine content was not measured
- Body mass gain may be desired or undesirable depending on sport/context
- Mechanism not directly measured in every study
- Outcome heterogeneity
- Short supplementation window

**Extraction notes:**
- None beyond the source-specific caveats above.
