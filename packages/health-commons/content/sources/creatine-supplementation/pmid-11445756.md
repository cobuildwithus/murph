This source is included for **safety_tolerability_adverse_events**.

**Findings:**
- Twenty healthy men were randomized to creatine monohydrate or placebo for 7 days.
- Creatine increased body mass by about 0.75 kg.
- Heart rate, blood pressure, sweat rate, and rectal-temperature responses during exercise in heat were not significantly different between groups in available summaries.

**Why it matters:**
Often cited in the creatine/heat debate and provides direct physiologic measurements after loading.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- biomarker / body weight
- safety / heat tolerance
- biomarker / sweat rate
- biomarker / rectal temperature
- biomarker / blood pressure
- biomarker / renal response

**Protocol takeaway:**
Supports treating early weight change as a likely short-term water/body-mass signal rather than a heat-safety red flag in healthy exercising men.

**Population mismatch / boundary:**
Healthy men exercising in heat; not women, children, pregnancy, or clinical users.

**Limitations:**
- Small sample; healthy men only.
- Short duration and acute laboratory heat-exercise test.
- Not designed to detect rare heat illness events.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
