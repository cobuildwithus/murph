This source is included for **repeated_sprint_power_trials**.

**Findings:** Creatine increased late-sprint mean power/speed and blunted speed reduction; peak power/speed were unchanged; body mass increased by about 1 kg.

**Population and intervention:** Recreationally active males; Oral creatine supplementation, reported as 75 mg/kg/day in accessible full-text record. Comparator: Placebo condition/baseline in crossover-style repeated-sprint testing. Duration/follow-up: Short-term oral supplementation before repeated treadmill sprint testing; exact duration not restated in accessible extraction.

**Endpoints:** six 10-second non-motorized treadmill sprints, mean power, mean speed, peak power, fatigue/speed reduction, body mass, plasma ammonia.

**Safety notes:** Body mass increased by about 1 kg after supplementation; no serious adverse-event signal was extracted. 

**Limitations:** Small male recreational sample. Body-mass increase can be relevant for weight-sensitive outcomes. Peak-power endpoints were not improved.

**Population mismatch:** Recreationally active males; not female or elite-athlete sample.

**Why it matters:** Open-access modern direct repeated-sprint study with mechanistic fatigue signals.

**Potential experiment signals:** six 10-second non-motorized treadmill sprints, mean power, mean speed, peak power, fatigue/speed reduction, body mass, plasma ammonia.

**Protocol takeaway:** Creatine may improve later-bout repeated sprint output while increasing body mass.

**Murph takeaway:** Track mean power across later reps, fatigue slope, body weight, and perceived heaviness.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`. Directness: `direct_protocol`. Result boundary: `mixed`.
