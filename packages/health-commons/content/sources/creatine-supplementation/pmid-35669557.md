**Findings:**
- From `source_artifact:pmid-35669557`: after a 5 g creatine dose, peak plasma creatine is described as occurring within about 2 hours and remaining elevated for roughly 4 hours.
- From `source_artifact:pmid-35669557`: exercise-related blood flow and transporter activity provide a plausible reason timing might matter, but this remains mechanistic rationale rather than definitive outcome evidence.
- The review cites short-term uptake data in which exercised muscle showed greater creatine accumulation than resting muscle, including one report of about 37.3% versus 25.7% total creatine increase and another reporting about 68% greater accumulation in the exercised limb.
- The review concludes that pre- and post-exercise creatine doses in the reviewed range were generally well tolerated and that current evidence does not support a strict timed prescription.
- Methodological limitations include few direct studies, limited placebo control, and missing measures of intramuscular creatine, diet, or fiber-type response modifiers.

**Why it matters:** It is a modern open-access synthesis of timing mechanisms, uptake studies, and practical timing conclusions.

**Protocol takeaway:** Direct timing evidence is mixed: taking creatine before or after training appears viable, and strict timing is not supported.

**Murph takeaway:** Use whichever timing you can sustain; co-ingestion and workout proximity are plausible but not proven requirements.

**Limitations and population mismatch:** Narrative review; timing evidence base remains small; mechanism-to-outcome translation uncertain; do not convert plausible mechanisms into causal protocol claims.
