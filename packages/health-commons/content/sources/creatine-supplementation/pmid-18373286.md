This source is included for **safety_tolerability_adverse_events**.

**Findings:**
- The paper tested whether gastrointestinal distress after creatine supplementation was dose dependent in athletes.
- Reported GI complaints included diarrhoea (39.0%), stomach upset (23.8%), and belching (16.9%) across treatment groups; available summaries indicate that diarrhea risk was higher with 10 g as a single serving than with 10 g/day split into two 5 g doses.
- This is safety/tolerability evidence, not evidence of performance or body-composition benefit.

**Why it matters:**
This is a dedicated creatine GI-tolerance source and is directly relevant to dose-splitting cautions in user-facing protocols.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- symptom / gastrointestinal distress
- symptom / diarrhea
- symptom / stomach upset
- symptom / belching
- adherence / dose tolerability

**Protocol takeaway:**
Use this source to support practical tolerability language such as splitting larger creatine doses instead of taking a large single bolus.

**Population mismatch / boundary:**
Athletes only; not a general clinical population.

**Limitations:**
- Participant count and detailed allocation were not available in the extracted record.
- Athlete population may not represent sedentary or medically complex users.
- GI symptom reporting may be influenced by diet, co-supplements, and timing.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.
