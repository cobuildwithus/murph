**Findings:**
- **muscle creatine content, body composition, strength, and anaerobic capacity** — Buffered creatine, whether low-dose or dose-matched to monohydrate, did not produce greater changes than creatine monohydrate over 28 days. Source key: `source_artifact:pmid-22971354`.
- **clinical safety markers and self-reported side effects** — The study did not find evidence that buffered creatine reduced side effects or improved clinical safety markers compared with monohydrate. Source key: `source_artifact:pmid-22971354`.

**Why it matters:** Buffered creatine is a common marketed alternative; the study directly tests superiority claims against monohydrate.

**Potential experiment signals:** biomarker:muscle-creatine-content, performance:bench-press, performance:leg-press, performance:wingate, body-composition:dxa, formulation:buffered-creatine.

**Protocol takeaway:** Do not present buffered creatine as superior to standard creatine monohydrate.
