This source is included for **repeated_sprint_power_trials**.

**Findings:** Creatine increased 15-second work output and fat-free mass but did not change lactate accumulation or estimated glycolytic contribution.

**Population and intervention:** Trained cyclists; Creatine monohydrate 4 × 5 g/day for 5 days. Comparator: Maltodextrin placebo 4 × 5 g/day. Duration/follow-up: 5 days of supplementation in a nonrandomized placebo-controlled crossover sequence.

**Endpoints:** 15-second all-out sprint cycling work output, fat-free mass, capillary lactate, glycolytic contribution.

**Safety notes:** No adverse-event extraction was available from the accessible record for this batch. 

**Limitations:** Nonrandomized fixed-order crossover design. Short-term trained-cyclist context. Mechanistic glycolytic endpoint was null.

**Population mismatch:** Trained cyclists; not general population or field sport athletes.

**Why it matters:** Newest direct open-access sprint-cycling evidence in this batch.

**Potential experiment signals:** 15-second all-out sprint cycling work output, fat-free mass, capillary lactate, glycolytic contribution.

**Protocol takeaway:** Creatine may improve sprint cycling output, but the mechanism was not glycolytic contribution.

**Murph takeaway:** Track sprint work/power and body composition separately from lactate-based mechanism claims.
