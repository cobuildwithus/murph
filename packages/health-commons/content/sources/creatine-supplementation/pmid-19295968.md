This source is included for **safety_tolerability_adverse_events**.

**Findings:**
- The review selected 10 studies on creatine, thermoregulation, and/or hydration status.
- It concluded that creatine supplementation at recommended dosages did not appear to hinder heat dissipation or negatively affect athlete body-fluid balance.
- The authors highlighted remaining gaps for longer protocols and field studies that mimic practices or games.

**Why it matters:**
Common creatine objections focus on dehydration, cramping, or heat illness; this is a dedicated synthesis for that concern cluster.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- safety / heat tolerance
- safety / hydration status
- biomarker / body fluid balance
- biomarker / core temperature
- symptom / cramping dehydration

**Protocol takeaway:**
Use as context for debunking dehydration and heat-intolerance concerns, while keeping hydration and heat-stress advice standard for exercise.

**Population mismatch / boundary:**
Athletes and exercising adults; not pregnant, pediatric, renal-disease, or medically complex populations.

**Limitations:**
- Included trials were mostly short-term and exercise/athlete-specific.
- Participant totals were not captured in the extracted record.
- Heat illness outcomes are uncommon and may be underpowered in controlled trials.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
