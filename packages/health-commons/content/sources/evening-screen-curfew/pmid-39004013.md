# Associations between bedtime media use and sleep outcomes in an adult population-based cohort
## Extraction boundary
- **Directness to Digital Sunset No Personal Screens Before Bed:** `same_mechanism`.
- **Claim-use boundary:** `supports-protocol`. Observational/context findings should not be written as causal intervention claims.
- **Artifact handling:** metadata/link only unless rights review explicitly clears redistribution.

## Population and design
- **Design:** Adult population-based cohort questionnaire analysis.
- **Participants:** 4188 (cohort_questionnaire_participants); Adults aged 19-94 years in the Specchio cohort, Geneva, Switzerland; 59% women.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Questionnaire analysis with adjustment for prior sleep and covariates.

## Exposure and comparator
- **Exposure:** Often using a screen in the 30 minutes before going to sleep at night.
- **Comparator/control:** Less frequent/no screen use in the 30 minutes before sleep.

## Endpoints
- Late bedtime
- Short sleep duration
- Daytime sleepiness
- Sleep quality
- Insomnia

## Extracted result
Frequent screen use in the 30 minutes before sleep was associated with late bedtime (OR 1.90, 95% CI 1.44-2.51), short sleep duration (OR 1.21, 95% CI 1.01-1.46), and excessive daytime sleepiness (OR 1.47, 95% CI 1.25-1.74) after covariate adjustment.

## Safety/adverse events
No intervention adverse events reported; mental health was assessed as moderator/context.

## Limitations and population mismatch
- Questionnaire-based observational analysis; no causal inference.
- Exposure is 30-minute pre-bed media use, not necessarily in-bed use.
- Adult Geneva cohort may not generalize globally.
