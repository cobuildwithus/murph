# Sleep and use of electronic devices in adolescence: results from a large population-based study
## Extraction boundary
- **Directness to Digital Sunset No Personal Screens Before Bed:** `adjacent_variant`.
- **Claim-use boundary:** `context-only`. Observational/context findings should not be written as causal intervention claims.
- **Artifact handling:** metadata/link only unless rights review explicitly clears redistribution.

## Population and design
- **Design:** Population-based cross-sectional adolescent study.
- **Participants:** 9846 (analyzed_participants); Norwegian adolescents aged 16-19 years in the youth@hordaland study.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Single assessment.

## Exposure and comparator
- **Exposure:** Electronic devices used in the bedroom during the last hour before sleep and total leisure screen time.
- **Comparator/control:** No or lower bedtime/overall device use.

## Endpoints
- Short sleep duration
- Sleep onset latency
- Wake after sleep onset
- Sleep deficit

## Extracted result
Bedtime device use and screen time were associated with short sleep duration, long sleep onset latency, and sleep deficit; cell-phone use was associated with <5h sleep (OR 1.85, 95% CI 1.45-2.35) and sleep-onset latency >60 min (OR 1.48, 95% CI 1.30-1.68).

## Safety/adverse events
No intervention adverse events reported.

## Limitations and population mismatch
- Cross-sectional design; no causal inference.
- Self-reported sleep and device use.
- Bedroom use during the last hour before sleep is adjacent to, not identical with, a personal-screen curfew.
