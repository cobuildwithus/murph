## Extraction summary

- **Population:** Adults aged 18–94 years interviewed face-to-face.
- **Exposure/intervention:** Use of media as a sleep aid, including books, television, music, internet, and videogames.
- **Comparator/control:** Non-use of each medium as a sleep aid.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Cross-sectional face-to-face interviews.
- **Endpoints:** sleep-aid media use prevalence, fatigue, Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index
- **Effect or direction:** Substantial proportions used books (39.8%), television (31.2%), music (26.0%), internet (23.2%), and videogames (10.3%) as sleep aids; media-as-sleep-aid use was associated with increased fatigue and higher PSQI scores indicating poorer sleep quality.
- **Safety/adverse events:** No adverse events; deliberately using media to initiate sleep is an adherence barrier.

## Directness and use boundary

- **Directness to Digital Sunset No Personal Screens Before Bed:** `same_mechanism`
- **Claim-use boundary:** `context-only`
- **Boundary note:** Many adults intentionally use media to fall asleep, so digital-sunset guidance may face adherence tradeoffs and reverse-causality risk.

## Limitations and mismatch

- Cross-sectional self-report.
- Media as sleep aid may reflect underlying sleep problems rather than cause them.
- Not a curfew intervention.
