## Extraction summary

- **Population:** Information-technology workers.
- **Exposure/intervention:** Duration and frequency of work-related electronic communication during non-working hours after working mostly from home or office.
- **Comparator/control:** Lower WREC duration/frequency and workstyle strata (home vs office).
- **Duration/follow-up:** 9-day observational study with daily records, actigraphy, and 4 days of brief PVT.
- **Endpoints:** fatigue, depression, sleepiness, psychological detachment, PVT lapses, actigraphic sleep
- **Effect or direction:** WREC was more frequent and longer when working mostly from home; longer WREC was associated with worse fatigue/depression and lower PVT lapse before bedtime when working mostly from the office. Sleep-specific extracted effects were mixed/not fully retained.
- **Safety/adverse events:** No intervention adverse events; fatigue/depression and right-to-disconnect burden are safety notes.

## Directness and use boundary

- **Directness to Digital Sunset No Personal Screens Before Bed:** `adjacent_variant`
- **Claim-use boundary:** `context-only`
- **Boundary note:** After-hours work communication showed workstyle-dependent associations with fatigue, mood, and vigilance, supporting boundary concerns but not a simple screen-curfew claim.

## Limitations and mismatch

- Adjacent work-communication exposure, not personal-screen curfew.
- Short observational period.
- WREC effects differed by workstyle and endpoint.
