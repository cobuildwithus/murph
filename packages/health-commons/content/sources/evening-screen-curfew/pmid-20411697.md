This source is included for **Adolescent, family, school, and device-type observational context**.

## Quick read

- **Source key:** `source_artifact:pmid-20411697`
- **Design:** Controlled/laboratory exposure study.
- **People studied or addressed:** Male adolescent evening types; mean age 16.6 years (SD 1.1) as reported in the open article.
- **Participant count:** 13 (reported_in_open_article).
- **Role for Digital Sunset:** adjacent_variant / context-only.

## Extracted facts

- **Intervention or exposure:** 50 minutes of active video-game playing before bed.
- **Comparator or control:** Passive DVD watching before bed.
- **Duration or follow-up:** Controlled presleep exposure night; acute laboratory comparison.
- **Endpoints:** sleep-onset latency; subjective sleepiness; EEG alpha power/cognitive alertness; heart rate; REM sleep; slow-wave sleep.
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Video-game playing modestly increased sleep-onset latency and reduced subjective sleepiness/cognitive sleepiness signals versus DVD; heart-rate arousal and REM/slow-wave sleep differences were not significant in accessible summaries.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse-event signal extracted.

## Limitations and population mismatch

- Very small male adolescent sample.
- Acute gaming versus DVD contrast, not a no-screen curfew.
- Authors characterize direct sleep effect as modest.

## Protocol boundary

Use as controlled interactive-screen mechanism context; preserve the mixed/modest result.
