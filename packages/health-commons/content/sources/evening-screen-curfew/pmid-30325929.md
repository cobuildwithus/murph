# Overnight smartphone use: A new public health challenge? A novel study design based on high-resolution smartphone data
## Extraction boundary
- **Directness to Digital Sunset No Personal Screens Before Bed:** `same_mechanism`.
- **Claim-use boundary:** `context-only`. Observational/context findings should not be written as causal intervention claims.
- **Artifact handling:** metadata/link only unless rights review explicitly clears redistribution.

## Population and design
- **Design:** Four-week high-resolution smartphone tracking study.
- **Participants:** 815 (young_adults_with_tracking_data); Young adults in the Copenhagen Networks Study.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Four weeks.

## Exposure and comparator
- **Exposure:** High-resolution smartphone activity during self-reported bed/sleep hours.
- **Comparator/control:** No or infrequent smartphone-interrupted sleep.

## Endpoints
- Smartphone activity during bed hours
- Self-reported sleep duration
- BMI
- Mental and physical health symptoms

## Extracted result
More than 12% had smartphone activity 3-5 hours after bedtime and 41% had smartphone-interrupted sleep on at least one weekday; frequent interrupted sleep was associated with 48 minutes shorter self-reported sleep duration and higher BMI, but not differences in physical or mental health symptoms.

## Safety/adverse events
No intervention adverse events reported; BMI and health-symptom findings are context signals.

## Limitations and population mismatch
- Observational tracking design; no causal inference.
- Self-reported bedtime/sleep duration used for defining sleep windows.
- Young-adult sample and overnight-use measurement context, not an intervention.

## Protocol-page use note
Context-only; mental/physical symptom differences were null except BMI signal. Any future protocol-page claim using this source should cite `source_artifact:pmid-30325929` and preserve the `context-only` boundary.
