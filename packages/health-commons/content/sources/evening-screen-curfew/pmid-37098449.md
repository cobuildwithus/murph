# Bedtime screen use behaviors and sleep outcomes: Findings from the Adolescent Brain Cognitive Development (ABCD) Study
## Extraction boundary
- **Directness to Digital Sunset No Personal Screens Before Bed:** `same_mechanism`.
- **Claim-use boundary:** `context-only`. Observational/context findings should not be written as causal intervention claims.
- **Artifact handling:** metadata/link only unless rights review explicitly clears redistribution.

## Population and design
- **Design:** ABCD Study cross-sectional analysis.
- **Participants:** 10280 (ABCD_year_2_participants); Early adolescents aged 10-14 years in the ABCD Study; 48.8% female.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Single ABCD Year 2 assessment.

## Exposure and comparator
- **Exposure:** Bedtime screen behaviors, bedroom TV/internet device, and overnight phone ringer status.
- **Comparator/control:** No bedroom device or phone turned off/outside room at bedtime.

## Endpoints
- Trouble falling/staying asleep
- Overall sleep disturbance

## Extracted result
A TV or internet-connected device in the bedroom was associated with trouble falling/staying asleep (ARR 1.27, 95% CI 1.12-1.44) and overall sleep disturbance (ARR 1.15, 95% CI 1.06-1.25); phone ringer activated overnight was also associated with more sleep problems versus phone off.

## Safety/adverse events
No intervention adverse events reported.

## Limitations and population mismatch
- Cross-sectional analysis; no causal inference.
- Adolescent caregiver/self-report outcomes.
- Bedtime screen behaviors are not the same as an implemented Digital Sunset protocol.
