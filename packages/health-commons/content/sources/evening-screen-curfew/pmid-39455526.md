## Extraction summary

- **Population:** Young adults with poor sleep in a pilot randomized-trial dataset analyzed observationally.
- **Exposure/intervention:** Bedtime electronic device use on a given night.
- **Comparator/control:** Nights without bedtime device use.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Daily/nightly repeated measures; exact number of nights not extracted.
- **Endpoints:** self-reported sleep duration, sleep latency, Fitbit-measured sleep variables
- **Effect or direction:** Bedtime device use on a given night was associated with shorter self-reported sleep duration (b = -19.80 minutes, p=.011) but not sleep latency; concordance with Fitbit variables was low and bedtime device use was not associated with Fitbit-measured sleep variables.
- **Safety/adverse events:** No adverse events; higher-need poor-sleep sample suggests burden relevance.

## Directness and use boundary

- **Directness to Digital Sunset No Personal Screens Before Bed:** `same_mechanism`
- **Claim-use boundary:** `supports-protocol`
- **Boundary note:** In young adults with poor sleep, bedtime device use predicted shorter self-reported sleep but not Fitbit-measured sleep variables, yielding mixed evidence.

## Limitations and mismatch

- Small sample.
- Observational secondary analysis within pilot RCT data.
- Objective Fitbit outcomes did not align with self-report.
