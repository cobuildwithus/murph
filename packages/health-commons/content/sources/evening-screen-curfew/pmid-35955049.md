# The Associations between Daytime Physical Activity, While-in-Bed Smartphone Use, Sleep Delay, and Sleep Quality: A 24-h Investigation among Chinese College Students
## Extraction boundary
- **Directness to Digital Sunset No Personal Screens Before Bed:** `same_mechanism`.
- **Claim-use boundary:** `context-only`. Observational/context findings should not be written as causal intervention claims.
- **Artifact handling:** metadata/link only unless rights review explicitly clears redistribution.

## Population and design
- **Design:** One-day cross-sectional 24-hour survey.
- **Participants:** 828 (completed_questionnaires); Chinese college students from two colleges.
- **Duration/follow-up:** 24-hour weekend recall (21-22 May 2021).

## Exposure and comparator
- **Exposure:** Daytime physical activity and self-reported while-in-bed smartphone use before sleep.
- **Comparator/control:** Lower/no while-in-bed smartphone use and lower/higher physical activity within regression/correlation models.

## Endpoints
- While-in-bed smartphone use
- Smartphone-related sleep delay
- While-in-bed screen time
- Sleep quality

## Extracted result
While-in-bed smartphone use was associated with a greater chance of smartphone-related sleep delay, longer in-bed screen time, and worse sleep quality; physical-activity duration was associated with lower odds of while-in-bed phone use (OR 0.907, 95% CI 0.836-0.984) and phone-related sleep delay (OR 0.932, 95% CI 0.881-0.986), but not sleep quality.

## Safety/adverse events
No intervention adverse events reported.

## Limitations and population mismatch
- One-day cross-sectional design; temporal sequence helps framing but does not establish causality.
- College-student and weekend context may not generalize.
- Physical activity is not the target protocol; while-in-bed smartphone exposure is the relevant same-mechanism context.
