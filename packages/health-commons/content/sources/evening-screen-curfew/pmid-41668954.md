## Methods snapshot
- **Design:** Randomized controlled crossover trial.
- **Participants:** participants in crossover exposure/control conditions; Male elite-level soccer players.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Evening smartphone exposure for two hours before bedtime for five nights.
- **Comparator/control:** Reading magazines as the control condition, separated by a one-week washout.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Five nights per condition with one-week washout.
- **Endpoints extracted:** sleepiness, total sleep time, sleep onset latency, sleep efficiency, reaction time, jumping and reactive agility performance.

## Results extracted
- Evening smartphone exposure worsened sleep-quality indices, including higher sleepiness, reduced total sleep time, longer sleep-onset latency, and reduced sleep efficiency.
- Afternoon simple/choice reaction times and some physical performance tests worsened after smartphone exposure.
- Because the comparison was forced exposure versus magazines, it informs mechanism/context more than a practical curfew prescription.

## Limitations and population mismatch
- Small sample of elite male soccer players.
- Exposure-control trial, not a self-directed no-personal-screens curfew.
- Sports-performance setting limits generalization.
