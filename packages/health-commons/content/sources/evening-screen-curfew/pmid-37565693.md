## Quick read

- **Source type:** Systematic Review (2023).
- **People studied or addressed:** Adults in experimental social-media-use intervention studies.
- **Exposure/intervention:** Social media interventions including therapy-based approaches, limiting use, and full abstinence.
- **Comparator/control:** Control, usual use, or alternative intervention conditions depending on included study.
- **Role for Digital Sunset:** adjacent_variant; context-only. This source is not upgraded to direct Digital Sunset efficacy unless the source itself directly tested no personal screens before bed.

## Extracted source-local finding

In 23 included studies, therapy-based interventions were reported as more consistently associated with mental-well-being improvements than limiting-use or full-abstinence interventions; limiting and abstinence approaches had mixed or sparse positive findings. Digital Sunset should frame mental-health effects cautiously.

## Endpoints extracted

depression, anxiety, stress, loneliness, mental well-being

## Duration or follow-up

Intervention durations varied across included studies; review searched studies from 2004 to July 2022.

## Safety, adverse events, and clinical boundaries

No protocol-specific adverse events extracted. Use to temper claims and to watch for burden or mismatch when restriction-only approaches are used.

## Important limits and population mismatch

- Adjacent social-media intervention evidence, not bedtime screen-curfew evidence.
- Narrative synthesis across heterogeneous interventions and outcomes.
- The review noted need for more experimental and longitudinal research with representative samples.
