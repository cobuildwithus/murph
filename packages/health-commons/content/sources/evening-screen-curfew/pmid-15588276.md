This source is included for **safety, burden, withdrawal, and clinical-boundary evidence** in the Digital Sunset extraction batch.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Narrative Review (2004).
- **People studied or addressed:** Workers in occupations requiring on-call availability.
- **Exposure/intervention:** On-call work and availability outside regular hours.
- **Comparator/control:** Not a controlled intervention; review context only.
- **Role for Digital Sunset:** safety_boundary; safety-only. This source is not upgraded to direct Digital Sunset efficacy unless the source itself directly tested no personal screens before bed.

## Extracted source-local finding

The review addresses health and burden issues around on-call work. For Digital Sunset, it supports a practical boundary: people with on-call or safety-critical duties may need monitored after-hours communication access and should not be labeled nonadherent for necessary availability.

## Endpoints extracted

on-call burden, sleep disruption, work-life fit, occupational health

## Duration or follow-up

Varied occupational settings and time frames.

## Safety, adverse events, and clinical boundaries

Safety boundary for on-call roles: allow emergency and work-duty exceptions, and avoid protocols that could cause missed urgent responsibilities.

## Important limits and population mismatch

- Older narrative review, not a systematic review of screen curfews.
- No Digital Sunset intervention was tested.
- Occupational contexts and health endpoints were heterogeneous.
