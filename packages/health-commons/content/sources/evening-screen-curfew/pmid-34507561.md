## Extraction summary

- **Population:** Chinese college students.
- **Exposure/intervention:** Problematic mobile phone use, bedtime procrastination, sleep quality, and depressive symptoms over time.
- **Comparator/control:** Lower levels of problematic mobile phone use/bedtime procrastination.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Two-wave longitudinal study with 12-month interval.
- **Endpoints:** bedtime procrastination, sleep quality, depressive symptoms, problematic mobile phone use
- **Effect or direction:** Cross-lagged analysis found bidirectional relations of problematic mobile phone use with bedtime procrastination and depressive symptoms; problematic mobile phone use predicted subsequent sleep quality one-way, and bedtime procrastination predicted subsequent depressive symptoms one-way.
- **Safety/adverse events:** No adverse events; depressive symptoms and poor sleep are safety/context endpoints.

## Directness and use boundary

- **Directness to Digital Sunset No Personal Screens Before Bed:** `adjacent_variant`
- **Claim-use boundary:** `context-only`
- **Boundary note:** Longitudinal data support reciprocal links among problematic phone use, bedtime procrastination, sleep quality, and depressive symptoms, without testing a screen curfew.

## Limitations and mismatch

- Adjacent problematic-use exposure, not curfew.
- College-student population.
- Longitudinal observational design still cannot prove intervention effect.
