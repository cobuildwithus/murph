- **Source key:** `source_artifact:pmid-30366713`

- **People studied or addressed:** Adolescents aged 14-18 years; accessible abstract reports 51 female; cellphone-use subsample n=63.

## Extracted facts

- **Intervention or exposure:** Bedtime autonomy, parental monitoring of bedtime, and nighttime cellphone use.
- **Comparator or control:** Lower autonomy/greater parental monitoring and less frequent nighttime cellphone use.
- **Duration or follow-up:** Two weeks of actigraphy; cellphone-use subsample analysis.
- **Endpoints:** actigraphy total sleep time; bedtime autonomy; cellphone-use moderation.
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Accessible abstract reports older age was associated with shorter total sleep time r(98)=-.28, p=.006; age-related sleep-duration decline was mediated by bedtime autonomy. Bedtime autonomy was more problematic among frequent nighttime cellphone users (B=-10.44, SE=4.64, CI [-21.3749,-2.8139]) than less frequent users (B=-1.94, CI [-9.8694,3.6205]).
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse-event monitoring; insufficient sleep context only.

## Limitations and population mismatch

- Observational; cannot establish rule intervention causality.
- Cellphone-use subsample smaller than total sample.
- Adolescent parent/autonomy context.

## Protocol boundary

Use to flag autonomy and cellphone use as implementation moderators for adolescent/family contexts.
