## Methods snapshot
- **Design:** Randomized controlled trial.
- **Participants:** randomized; Emerging adults aged 18-24 years who owned iPhones; 71.3% female.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Use an iPhone for the hour before bedtime with Night Shift enabled for seven consecutive nights.
- **Comparator/control:** iPhone use without Night Shift, or no iPhone use in the hour before bedtime.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Seven consecutive nights.
- **Endpoints extracted:** actigraphy sleep latency, actigraphy sleep duration, sleep efficiency, wake after sleep onset.

## Results extracted
- Across the full sample, sleep outcomes did not differ significantly across Night Shift, no Night Shift, and no-phone conditions.
- In a post hoc subgroup who averaged more than 6.8 hours of sleep, phone condition was associated with sleep efficiency (p=.014) and wake after sleep onset (p=.013), with no-phone doing best.
- Night Shift did not clearly mitigate smartphone-use effects in the full sample.

## Safety/adverse events
- **Adverse events:** Not reported in the extracted abstract.
- **Safety notes:** No adverse events extracted; main safety/relevance point is that a color-temperature setting should not be treated as a substitute for reduced pre-bed phone use.

## Limitations and population mismatch
- Short one-week exposure.
- Young adult iPhone owners; generalizability to children, older adults, or non-iPhone devices is uncertain.
- Post hoc subgroup findings are lower certainty than the main randomized comparison.
