## Methods snapshot
- **Design:** Non-blinded parallel randomized controlled trial.
- **Participants:** analyzed; 125 randomized, intervention n=58 and control n=53 analyzed; Healthy students aged 18-29 years; analyzed mean age 22.68 years (SD 2.6), 70 female.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Reduce smartphone screen time to no more than 2 hours/day for 3 weeks.
- **Comparator/control:** Usual smartphone use.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Three-week intervention with follow-up six weeks after post-intervention assessment.
- **Endpoints extracted:** well-being, depressive symptoms, sleep quality, stress, smartphone screen time.

## Results extracted
- Post-intervention group effects favored smartphone reduction for sleep quality (MD 2.59; 95% CI 0.66-4.51) and depressive symptoms, well-being, and stress.
- Effects were small to medium by reported eta-squared values; smartphone screen time returned toward baseline at follow-up.
- Timing was not bedtime-specific, so this remains adjacent evidence.

## Limitations and population mismatch
- Healthy student sample.
- Total daily smartphone cap rather than bedtime curfew.
- Self-reported mental-health and sleep-quality outcomes; screen time based on uploaded screenshots rather than direct device import.
- Follow-up dropout was substantial.
