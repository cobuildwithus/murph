# "To sleep, perchance to tweet": in-bed electronic social media use and its associations with insomnia, daytime sleepiness, mood, and sleep duration in adults
## Extraction boundary
- **Directness to Digital Sunset No Personal Screens Before Bed:** `same_mechanism`.
- **Claim-use boundary:** `context-only`. Observational/context findings should not be written as causal intervention claims.
- **Artifact handling:** metadata/link only unless rights review explicitly clears redistribution.

## Population and design
- **Design:** Cross-sectional online questionnaire.
- **Participants:** 855 (survey_respondents); Hospital employees and university students; mean age 43.6 years; 85% female.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Single assessment.

## Exposure and comparator
- **Exposure:** In-bed electronic social media use on mobile devices.
- **Comparator/control:** No in-bed electronic social media use.

## Endpoints
- Insomnia
- Daytime sleepiness
- Anxiety
- Depression
- Weeknight sleep duration

## Extracted result
Nearly 70% reported in-bed electronic social media use; high use was associated with insomnia, anxiety, and short weeknight sleep duration, but not depression or daytime sleepiness.

## Safety/adverse events
No intervention adverse events reported; anxiety is a contextual mental-health endpoint.

## Limitations and population mismatch
- Cross-sectional survey; no causal inference.
- Mostly female employee/student sample.
- Social media in bed is a same-mechanism exposure but not a curfew intervention.

## Protocol-page use note
Context-only observational evidence. Any future protocol-page claim using this source should cite `source_artifact:pmid-29555130` and preserve the `context-only` boundary.
