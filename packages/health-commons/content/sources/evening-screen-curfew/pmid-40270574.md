This source is included for **Adolescent, family, school, and device-type observational context**.

## Quick read

- **Source key:** `source_artifact:pmid-40270574`
- **Design:** Cross-sectional observational study.
- **People studied or addressed:** Norwegian university students aged 18-28 years.
- **Participant count:** 45202 (reported_in_open_article_abstract).
- **Role for Digital Sunset:** same_mechanism / supports-protocol.

## Extracted facts

- **Intervention or exposure:** Screen time in bed after going to bed, including social media and other screen activities.
- **Comparator or control:** No screen use in bed and different screen activity categories.
- **Duration or follow-up:** Cross-sectional survey.
- **Endpoints:** insomnia; sleep duration; screen time in bed; screen activity type.
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Open article abstract reports each one-hour increase in screen use after going to bed was associated with 59% higher odds of insomnia and a 24-minute reduction in sleep duration. Activity-type associations did not show social media as worse than other screen activities; exclusively social-media users had lower odds/longer sleep than other or mixed screen users in extracted notes.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse-event monitoring extracted; insomnia and shorter sleep are outcome signals.

## Limitations and population mismatch

- Cross-sectional; cannot infer direction of effects.
- University students aged 18-28, not adolescents and not necessarily general adults.
- Screen use in bed after bedtime is same-mechanism context rather than a curfew trial.

## Protocol boundary

Use as same-mechanism support for avoiding in-bed post-bedtime screens, while preserving activity-type mixedness and population mismatch.
