## Extracted facts

- **Intervention or exposure:** Single excessive computer-game exposure and television exposure before subsequent sleep measurement.
- **Comparator or control:** Different exposure nights/conditions, including television and non-media/basal context as available in the study design.
- **Duration or follow-up:** Single exposure with subsequent overnight polysomnography and memory testing.
- **Endpoints:** slow-wave sleep; sleep-onset latency; stage 2 sleep; REM sleep; sleep efficiency; verbal memory; visual memory.
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Computer-game playing reduced slow-wave sleep and verbal-memory performance, prolonged sleep-onset latency, and increased stage 2 sleep; no REM effect was observed. Television viewing reduced sleep efficiency but did not affect sleep patterns.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse-event signal extracted.

## Limitations and population mismatch

- Small lab exposure study; participant count not extracted in accessible records used here.
- School-aged child population; not adults.
- Computer gaming/TV exposure is adjacent to a general no-personal-screens curfew.
