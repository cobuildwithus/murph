# Links Between Objectively-Measured Hourly Smartphone Use and Adolescent Wake Events Across Two Weeks
## Extraction boundary
- **Directness to Digital Sunset No Personal Screens Before Bed:** `same_mechanism`.
- **Claim-use boundary:** `supports-protocol`. Observational/context findings should not be written as causal intervention claims.
- **Artifact handling:** metadata/link only unless rights review explicitly clears redistribution.

## Population and design
- **Design:** 14-day objective smartphone and Fitbit field study.
- **Participants:** 59 (adolescents_plus_hourly_cases); Racially and ethnically diverse adolescents aged 15-18 years; 4,287 hourly sleep-session cases.
- **Duration/follow-up:** 14 days.

## Exposure and comparator
- **Exposure:** Hourly iPhone screen time, pickups, and notifications during sleep sessions.
- **Comparator/control:** Hours with lower screen time, pickups, or notifications within multilevel models.

## Endpoints
- Wake-event duration during sleeping hours

## Extracted result
During sleep-session hours, more screen time or pickups were associated with longer wake-event duration; notifications in the same hour were not associated with wake-event duration.

## Safety/adverse events
No intervention adverse events reported; wake events are disruption endpoints.

## Limitations and population mismatch
- Small adolescent sample despite many hourly observations.
- Observational field design; cannot infer causality.
- iPhone/Fitbit data may not generalize to all devices or populations.

## Protocol-page use note
Notifications showed a null same-hour association; preserve this guardrail. Any future protocol-page claim using this source should cite `source_artifact:pmid-38039087` and preserve the `supports-protocol` boundary.
