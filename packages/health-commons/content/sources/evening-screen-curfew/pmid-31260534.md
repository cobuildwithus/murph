## Quick read

Large adolescent school intervention/observational follow-up asked participants to stop screen-device use after 9 pm; useful direct evidence with causal limits.

## Extracted intervention or exposure

- **Population / N:** Adolescents aged 12-19 years in Geneva-area schools (N=569 ; count kind: started; active intervention n=183, passive n=284, dropout n=102 in extracted source).
- **Intervention / exposure:** After a 40-minute sleep education workshop, adolescents were asked to stop using screen devices after 9 pm on school nights.
- **Comparator / control:** Within-person baseline; passive participation group was also described, but the intervention was not randomized.
- **Duration / follow-up:** Two-week baseline, sleep-education workshop, then two-week screen curfew phase

## Extracted endpoints and results

- **Endpoints:** Actigraphy sleep onset and total sleep time, activity diaries, sustained-attention/vigilance task, mood, and exploratory melatonin profile subset.
- **Effect or direction:** Reduced screen time after 9 pm was associated with earlier sleep onset, longer total sleep time, and improved daytime vigilance, especially in older adolescents; mood effects were not significant and melatonin findings were preliminary/non-significant in a very small subset.

## Directness and claim boundary

- **Directness to Digital Sunset:** direct_protocol.
- **Claim use:** supports-protocol.
- **Boundary:** Nonrandomized design, education bundled with curfew request, self-reported activity timestamps, small melatonin subset, and adolescent population. Treat as associated change, not definitive causal proof.

## Safety / adverse events

No adverse events were extracted. Feasibility burden included diaries, actigraphy, and school-based participation.
