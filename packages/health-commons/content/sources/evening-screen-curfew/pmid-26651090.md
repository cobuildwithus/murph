# Impact of Media Use on Adolescent Sleep Efficiency
## Extraction boundary
- **Directness to Digital Sunset No Personal Screens Before Bed:** `same_mechanism`.
- **Claim-use boundary:** `context-only`. Observational/context findings should not be written as causal intervention claims.
- **Artifact handling:** metadata/link only unless rights review explicitly clears redistribution.

## Population and design
- **Design:** Cross-sectional media-use survey with one week of actigraphy.
- **Participants:** 55 (analyzed_adolescents); Adolescents, mean age 14.89 years; 53% African-American and 47% White in the accessible abstract.
- **Duration/follow-up:** One week of actigraphy with self-reported media-use measures.

## Exposure and comparator
- **Exposure:** Daily text messaging, media use after bed, and awakenings by mobile phone at night.
- **Comparator/control:** Lower or no reported post-bed media use/phone awakenings.

## Endpoints
- Actigraphic sleep efficiency
- Sleep offset

## Extracted result
Sleep efficiency was negatively correlated with text messaging (r=-0.29), media use after bed (r=-0.32), and nighttime mobile-phone awakenings (r=-0.33); media-use variables accounted for 30% of sleep-efficiency variance in the reported model.

## Safety/adverse events
No intervention adverse events reported.

## Limitations and population mismatch
- Small adolescent sample.
- Self-reported media exposure paired with actigraphy; no intervention or causal design.
- Accessible extraction was abstract-level despite PMCID.
