This source is included for **safety, burden, withdrawal, and clinical-boundary evidence** in the Digital Sunset extraction batch.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Randomized Experimental Study with Follow-up (2024).
- **People studied or addressed:** Adult female participants with smartphone and social-media use in the main analysis.
- **Exposure/intervention:** 14-day social media abstinence with social-media apps removed from the smartphone.
- **Comparator/control:** Control group continuing usual use in the main analysis.
- **Role for Digital Sunset:** adjacent_variant; context-only. This source is not upgraded to direct Digital Sunset efficacy unless the source itself directly tested no personal screens before bed.

## Extracted source-local finding

In the main analysis of 86 female participants, 14-day social-media abstinence was associated with decreased screen time and body-image dissatisfaction, but depression/anxiety symptoms, problematic smartphone use, trait/state FoMO, and loneliness decreased over the overall intervention time with no significant group differences.

## Endpoints extracted

screen time, body image dissatisfaction, depression, anxiety, FoMO, loneliness, problematic smartphone use

## Duration or follow-up

14-day intervention plus 14-day follow-up.

## Safety, adverse events, and clinical boundaries

No protocol-specific adverse-event extraction. Watch for social burden, FoMO, loneliness, and adherence problems when using abstinence-style variants.

## Important limits and population mismatch

- Adjacent social-media abstinence evidence, not bedtime screen-curfew evidence.
- Main analysis was limited to 86 female participants, reducing generalizability.
- App deinstallation was not monitored, and social media could be accessed on other devices.
