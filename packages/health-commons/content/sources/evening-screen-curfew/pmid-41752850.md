## Quick read

Small 2026 RCT comparing abrupt vs progressive smartphone restriction in physically active students with nomophobia; bundled safety/adherence context.

## Extracted intervention or exposure

- **Population / N:** Physically active physical-education students with moderate-high nomophobia (N=30 ; count kind: randomized; standard n=15 and adapted n=15).
- **Intervention / exposure:** Adapted progressive restriction increased the pre-bed phone-free window from 30 to 60 to 120 minutes and added psychoeducation plus nightly slow-paced breathing.
- **Comparator / control:** Standard abrupt two-hour pre-bed smartphone restriction with sleep hygiene.
- **Duration / follow-up:** Seven-day baseline plus 14-day intervention

## Extracted endpoints and results

- **Endpoints:** Actigraphy sleep efficiency and sleep-onset latency, anxiety/stress, psychomotor vigilance and choice reaction time, and physical performance.
- **Effect or direction:** The adapted approach outperformed abrupt standard restriction on sleep efficiency, sleep-onset latency, pre-sleep anxiety/stress, and some afternoon cognitive-performance outcomes. Physical performance was stable.

## Directness and claim boundary

- **Directness to Digital Sunset:** direct_protocol.
- **Claim use:** context-only.
- **Boundary:** Small specialized sample, two active bundled interventions, no no-restriction control, and psychoeducation/breathing confound the pure smartphone-restriction dose.

## Safety / adverse events

Nomophobia and restriction-related anxiety are directly relevant. The trial implies progressive restriction and regulation supports may be safer or more tolerable for high-anxiety phone users than abrupt restriction.
