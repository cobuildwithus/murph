## Extraction summary

- **Population:** First-year university students.
- **Exposure/intervention:** Digital media use recorded in 15-minute blocks during the 2 hours before bedtime across repeated diary occasions.
- **Comparator/control:** Shorter duration or lower diversity of prebedtime digital media use; different media activity types.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Nine diary occasions covering the 2 hours before bedtime.
- **Endpoints:** bedtime, total sleep time, sleep-onset latency, sleep quality
- **Effect or direction:** Longer prebedtime digital-media duration was associated with reduced total sleep time and later bedtime; greater media diversity showed an unexpected association with increased total sleep time and earlier bedtime. Computer work, internet surfing, and music in the last hour had the strongest relationships with multiple sleep variables.
- **Safety/adverse events:** No intervention adverse events; displacement burden suggested by later bedtime and reduced total sleep time.

## Directness and use boundary

- **Directness to Digital Sunset No Personal Screens Before Bed:** `same_mechanism`

## Limitations and mismatch

- University-student population.
- Observational diary design; no curfew test.
- Some findings were mixed rather than uniformly harmful.
