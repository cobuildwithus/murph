## Extraction summary

- **Population:** Daytime information-technology employees.
- **Exposure/intervention:** Work e-mail after hours and off-job duration.
- **Comparator/control:** Less after-hours work e-mail and longer off-job duration.
- **Duration/follow-up:** 1-month observational study.
- **Endpoints:** psychological detachment, actigraphic sleep quality, actigraphic sleep duration, saliva cortisol, fatigue/rumination
- **Effect or direction:** After-hours work e-mail was associated with self-reported outcomes and actigraphic sleep quality but not cortisol awakening response or actigraphic sleep duration; shorter off-job duration was associated with larger cortisol response.
- **Safety/adverse events:** No intervention adverse events; work-boundary burden and recovery disruption are safety notes.

## Directness and use boundary

- **Directness to Digital Sunset No Personal Screens Before Bed:** `adjacent_variant`
- **Claim-use boundary:** `context-only`
- **Boundary note:** After-hours work e-mail related to some sleep/recovery measures but not all objective endpoints, so it is boundary-context evidence with mixed results.

## Limitations and mismatch

- Adjacent work-boundary exposure, not a personal-screen curfew.
- Small IT-worker sample.
- Mixed objective/subjective findings.
