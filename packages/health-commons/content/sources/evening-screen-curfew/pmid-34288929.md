## Quick read

Large Danish citizen-science feasibility cohort on changing night-time smartphone behavior; behavioral feasibility, not randomized efficacy.

## Extracted intervention or exposure

- **Population / N:** Danish adults and older adolescents aged 16 years or older (N=8894 ; count kind: web-based survey participants aged 16+).
- **Intervention / exposure:** Mass-media citizen-science campaign and web-based self-monitoring around night-time smartphone behavior.
- **Comparator / control:** Baseline vs follow-up; no randomized control group.
- **Duration / follow-up:** One-week survey/campaign with follow-up about two weeks after campaign exposure

## Extracted endpoints and results

- **Endpoints:** Self-reported night-time smartphone behavior, precautions taken, motivations, and behavior change among baseline sleep-hour smartphone users.
- **Effect or direction:** Among baseline sleep-hour smartphone users, 15% reported a change in night-time smartphone behavior; 83% of changers reported using the smartphone less. Common precautions included silent mode and reducing pre-sleep or sleep-hour smartphone use.
