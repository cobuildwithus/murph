## Methods snapshot
- **Design:** Cluster randomized clinical trial.
- **Participants:** 89 families; 181 children and 164 adults; Families in Denmark with children aged 4-14 years; eligible parent had high recreational screen use and full-time work/education.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Limit recreational screen media use to no more than 3 hours/week/person for two weeks; hand over most portable devices and receive non-smart phones, with necessary-use allowances.
- **Comparator/control:** Usual recreational screen-media habits.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Two-week intervention.
- **Endpoints extracted:** nonsedentary leisure time, sleep duration, sleep parameters by single-channel EEG, physical activity.

## Results extracted
- Children increased nonsedentary leisure time by 45.8 minutes/day (95% CI 27.9-63.6; p<.001).
- EEG-based sleep outcomes showed no significant between-group differences in children or adults.
- Compliance was high, but this total recreational screen restriction did not show a clear sleep signal.

## Limitations and population mismatch
- Total recreational screen restriction is broader than bedtime-specific curfew.
- Open-label intervention and some screen-use reduction in controls.
- Sleep assessment may have been underpowered or limited for detecting changes.
