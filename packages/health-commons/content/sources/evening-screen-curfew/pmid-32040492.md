## Quick read

Core randomized pilot trial of a 30-minute pre-bed mobile-phone restriction in poor-sleeping college students.

## Extracted intervention or exposure

- **Population / N:** College students with poor sleep quality and more than 30 minutes of bedtime mobile-phone use (N=38 ; count kind: randomized; 19 intervention and 19 control).
- **Intervention / exposure:** Avoid mobile-phone use during the 30 minutes before bedtime for four weeks, supported by phone lock/screen-time tools, reminders, and calls.
- **Comparator / control:** No restriction instructions/usual behavior control.
- **Duration / follow-up:** Four-week intervention

## Extracted endpoints and results

- **Endpoints:** Sleep diary and PSQI, pre-sleep arousal, positive/negative affect, and n-back working memory.
- **Effect or direction:** The intervention group showed shorter sleep latency, longer sleep duration, better sleep quality, lower pre-sleep arousal, and improved positive affect. Approximate reported changes included about 12 minutes less sleep latency and 18 minutes more sleep duration; working-memory signals were mixed.

## Directness and claim boundary

- **Directness to Digital Sunset:** direct_protocol.
- **Claim use:** supports-protocol.
- **Boundary:** Small pilot sample, short duration, subjective sleep measures, and a motivated poor-sleeping college-student population.

## Safety / adverse events

No adverse events were extracted. Practical burden included phone-lock setup and reminders.
