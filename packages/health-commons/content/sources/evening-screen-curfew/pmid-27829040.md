This source page is a batch-009 draft for **Digital Sunset — No Personal Screens Before Bed**. It is included for measurement, adherence, or confounder interpretation, not for protocol synthesis.

## Quick read

- **People studied or addressed:** Adults in the Health eHeart Study who downloaded an Android smartphone app; sleep-survey subset available.
- **Participant count:** 653 total screen-time participants; 136 with complete PSQI sleep data; 56 with complete bedtime screen-use data.
- **Intervention or exposure:** Directly measured smartphone screen-time exposure over 30-day windows.
- **Comparator or control:** Lower versus higher measured screen-time; self-reported sleep outcomes from PSQI components.
- **Duration or follow-up:** 30-day screen-time windows; bedtime period analysis for participants with complete bedtime data.
- **Endpoints:** Screen-time, sleep duration, sleep efficiency, sleep quality, sleep-onset latency, bedtime/sleeping-period phone use.
- **Directness to Digital Sunset:** same_mechanism
- **Claim-use boundary:** context-only

## Extracted atomic finding

Longer total screen-time was associated with shorter sleep duration and worse sleep efficiency; screen-time during bedtime/sleeping periods was associated with poorer sleep quality, lower efficiency, and longer sleep-onset latency. The study explicitly cannot infer causality.

## Safety and adverse events

No adverse-event signal was extracted for this source in this batch. Where this is a consumer-technology or clinical-guideline source, use it as a measurement or safety-boundary anchor rather than as a claim that consumer devices can diagnose or treat sleep problems.

## Limitations and population mismatch

- Observational cross-sectional analyses cannot establish causality or directionality.
- Android-only app users and sleep-data subsets may be selected.
- Screen-time from other devices was not captured.
