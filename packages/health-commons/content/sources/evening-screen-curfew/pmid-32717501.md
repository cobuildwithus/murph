## Extraction summary

- **Population:** Chinese university students aged 18+ years.
- **Exposure/intervention:** Smartphone addiction, self-regulation, and bedtime procrastination.
- **Comparator/control:** Lower smartphone-addiction scores.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Cross-sectional survey / mediation analysis.
- **Endpoints:** sleep quality, sleep latency, sleep duration, subjective sleep quality, bedtime procrastination
- **Effect or direction:** Smartphone addiction was associated with poorer sleep-quality indicators through self-regulation and bedtime procrastination pathways; reported direct effects on sleep indicators were not significant after mediators.
- **Safety/adverse events:** No adverse events; problematic-use pathway may indicate higher-burden users.

## Directness and use boundary

- **Directness to Digital Sunset No Personal Screens Before Bed:** `adjacent_variant`
- **Claim-use boundary:** `context-only`
- **Boundary note:** Problematic smartphone use was modeled as affecting sleep quality through self-regulation and bedtime procrastination, but this is adjacent-variant evidence.

## Limitations and mismatch

- Adjacent variant: addiction/problematic-use exposure, not a screen-curfew intervention.
- Cross-sectional mediation cannot establish causality.
- Student population.
