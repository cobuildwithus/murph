## Quick read

Elite-athlete training-camp device-removal study that found no significant sleep, cognitive, or physical-performance differences.

## Extracted intervention or exposure

- **Population / N:** Elite Australian judo athletes during a six-day training camp (N=23 ; count kind: reported; 14 device-restricted and 9 control).
- **Intervention / exposure:** Removal of electronic devices for 48 hours during a training camp.
- **Comparator / control:** Control group continued usual electronic-device access.
- **Duration / follow-up:** 48-hour device removal during camp days 3 and 4

## Extracted endpoints and results

- **Endpoints:** Readiband actigraphy sleep quantity/quality, sleep diary, device use, perceived exertion, Cogstate cognitive testing, and single-leg triple-hop physical performance.
- **Effect or direction:** No significant differences were reported between groups for sleep-related outcomes, cognitive performance, or physical performance. Athletes also overestimated sleep duration and underestimated sleep-onset latency compared with actigraphy.

## Directness and claim boundary

- **Directness to Digital Sunset:** direct_protocol.
- **Claim use:** supports-protocol-boundary.
- **Boundary:** Small elite-athlete sample, training-camp context, short 48-hour dose, and not a general adult bedtime screen curfew. Ledger described RCT, but PubMed classification should be preserved as observational/controlled intervention unless full text verifies randomization.

No adverse events were extracted from the abstract/metadata. The setting was a supervised elite-sport camp.
