## Quick read

- **Source key:** `source_artifact:pmid-36084495`
- **Design:** Observational or other study design.
- **People studied or addressed:** Adolescents aged 12-18 years from South Australia; accessible abstract reports 46% female, mean age 15.1 years (SD 1.2).

- **Role for Digital Sunset:** adjacent_variant / context-only.

## Extracted facts

- **Intervention or exposure:** Time spent using technology devices and apps in the hour before bed and in bed before sleep onset.
- **Comparator or control:** Lower/no use and comparisons across device/app types.
- **Duration or follow-up:** Cross-sectional self-report survey focused on school nights.
- **Endpoints:** bedtime; lights-out time; insufficient sleep; daytime sleepiness.
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Accessible abstract snippets report 30 minutes of phone use in the hour before bed was associated with a 9-minute bedtime delay; 30 minutes using laptops, gaming consoles, and YouTube was associated with later lights-out times of about 9, 16, and 11 minutes respectively, while watching TV was associated with about 9 minutes earlier lights-out time. Gaming consoles and YouTube were associated with greater odds of insufficient sleep (≤7h).
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** Daytime sleepiness and insufficient sleep are burden/safety-relevant contextual endpoints; no adverse events extracted.

## Limitations and population mismatch

- Cross-sectional self-report; cannot infer causality.
- Device/app-specific associations may be confounded by user traits, bedtime procrastination, and school schedule.
- Adolescent context.

## Protocol boundary

Use to refine which devices/apps a curfew covers and to preserve that TV may not behave like phones/gaming/YouTube.
