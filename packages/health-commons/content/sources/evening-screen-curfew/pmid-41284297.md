This source is included for **safety, burden, withdrawal, and clinical-boundary evidence** in the Digital Sunset extraction batch.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Prospective Cohort with Voluntary Intervention (2025).
- **People studied or addressed:** US young adults aged 18-24 years with smartphones and English fluency.
- **Exposure/intervention:** Optional 1-week social media detox targeting Facebook, Instagram, Snapchat, TikTok, and X after a 2-week baseline.
- **Comparator/control:** Within-participant baseline and participants who did not opt into detox; intervention was voluntary.
- **Role for Digital Sunset:** adjacent_variant; context-only. This source is not upgraded to direct Digital Sunset efficacy unless the source itself directly tested no personal screens before bed.

## Extracted source-local finding

In a cohort of 373 young adults, 295 opted into a 1-week detox; reported changes included reduced anxiety by 16.1%, depression by 24.8%, and insomnia by 14.5%, with no significant loneliness change. Because the detox was optional and not bedtime-specific, use this as adjacent context only.

## Endpoints extracted

depression, anxiety, insomnia, loneliness, screen use, communication, mobility, momentary mental health

## Duration or follow-up

2-week observational baseline followed by optional 1-week detox.

## Safety, adverse events, and clinical boundaries

Adjacent context. Monitor loneliness, social disruption, adherence, and rebound/compensatory behavior; do not treat the finding as clinical treatment evidence.

## Important limits and population mismatch

- Voluntary intervention creates self-selection bias.
- No randomized blinded control group for the detox phase.
- Young-adult social-media detox is not the same as no personal screens before bed.
- Durability of symptom changes needs further study.
