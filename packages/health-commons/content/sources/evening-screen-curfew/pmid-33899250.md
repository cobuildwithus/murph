# Daytime and nighttime smartphone use: A study of associations between multidimensional smartphone behaviours and sleep among 24,856 Danish adults
## Extraction boundary
- **Directness to Digital Sunset No Personal Screens Before Bed:** `adjacent_variant`.
- **Claim-use boundary:** `context-only`. Observational/context findings should not be written as causal intervention claims.
- **Artifact handling:** metadata/link only unless rights review explicitly clears redistribution.

## Population and design
- **Design:** Large web-based citizen-science cross-sectional survey.
- **Participants:** 24856 (survey_respondents); Danish adults aged 16 years and older in the SmartSleep Experiment.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Single web-based survey.

## Exposure and comparator
- **Exposure:** Frequency of daytime use, problematic use, use before sleep, and use during the sleep period.
- **Comparator/control:** No use during the sleep period and lower/no other smartphone behaviors.

## Endpoints
- Karolinska Sleep Questionnaire disturbed sleep score
- Short sleep duration
- Long sleep duration

## Extracted result
Use during the sleep period was the only smartphone dimension consistently associated with disturbed sleep after considering other smartphone behaviors; weekly use during the sleep period was associated with KSQ score +0.96 (95% CI 0.90-1.02), short sleep OR 1.32 (95% CI 1.08-1.62), and long sleep OR 1.94 (95% CI 1.63-2.32).

## Safety/adverse events
No intervention adverse events reported.

## Limitations and population mismatch
- Cross-sectional web survey; no causal inference.
- Self-reported smartphone behavior and sleep measures.
- Sleep-period use is adjacent to Digital Sunset but not a tested protocol.
