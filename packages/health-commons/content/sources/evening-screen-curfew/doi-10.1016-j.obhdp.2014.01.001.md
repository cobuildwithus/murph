## Extraction summary

- **Population:** Working adults in two U.S. experience-sampling studies: mid/high-level managers and employees from diverse occupations.
- **Exposure/intervention:** Late-night smartphone use, especially work-related smartphone use after normal work hours.
- **Comparator/control:** Lower late-night smartphone use and, in Study 2, other technology use comparators such as television/laptop/tablet use.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Study 1 and Study 2 used repeated daily/experience-sampling assessments; Study 2 covered 10 working days.
- **Endpoints:** sleep quantity, sleep quality, morning depletion, work engagement
- **Effect or direction:** Late-night smartphone use was associated with less sleep, greater next-morning depletion, and lower work engagement; smartphone findings were reported as distinct from other technology use in Study 2.
- **Safety/adverse events:** No adverse-event reporting extracted; work-boundary burden and next-day depletion are safety/burden considerations.

## Limitations and mismatch

- Work-related smartphone context may not generalize to leisure personal-screen use.
- Exact model coefficients not extracted in this batch.
