- **Source key:** `source_artifact:pmid-31641035`

- **People studied or addressed:** UK adolescents aged 13-15 years in the Millennium Cohort Study.

## Extracted facts

- **Intervention or exposure:** Daily social media use categorized by amount of use.
- **Comparator or control:** Average or lower social media use.
- **Duration or follow-up:** Cross-sectional analysis.
- **Endpoints:** late sleep onset; late wake times; trouble falling back asleep; sleep patterns.
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Open article reports heavier social media use was associated with poorer sleep patterns. Very high users versus average users were more likely to report late sleep onset (OR 2.14, 95% CI 1.83-2.50), late wake times (OR 1.97, 95% CI 1.32-2.93), and trouble falling back asleep (OR 1.36, 95% CI 1.10-1.66).
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** Sleep-pattern disturbance context only; no adverse-event monitoring extracted.

## Limitations and population mismatch

- Cross-sectional; causality cannot be inferred.
- Social media use is not the same as all personal screens.
- Self-reported sleep/social-media measures.
