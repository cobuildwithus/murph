This source page is a batch-009 draft for **Digital Sunset — No Personal Screens Before Bed**. It is included for measurement, adherence, or confounder interpretation, not for protocol synthesis.

## Quick read

- **People studied or addressed:** Adults in a smartphone touchscreen-interaction and actigraphy sleep-wake study; mostly young-adult sample per source discussion.
- **Participant count:** 79 participants and approximately 1,400 nights.
- **Intervention or exposure:** Passive smartphone touchscreen-interaction traces (“tappigraphy”) used to estimate sleep-wake cycles.
- **Comparator or control:** Actigraphy and sleep diary estimates.
- **Duration or follow-up:** Day-to-day monitoring across approximately 1,400 nights; exact per-person follow-up varies.
- **Endpoints:** Sleep onset, wake-up time, sleep duration, touchscreen activity around sleep windows.
- **Directness to Digital Sunset:** same_mechanism
- **Claim-use boundary:** context-only

## Extracted atomic finding

Touchscreen-derived sleep onset and wake-up estimates correlated strongly with actigraphy and diary timing, but sleep duration was less well captured and passive/phone-free wakefulness could be missed.

## Safety and adverse events

No adverse-event signal was extracted for this source in this batch. Where this is a consumer-technology or clinical-guideline source, use it as a measurement or safety-boundary anchor rather than as a claim that consumer devices can diagnose or treat sleep problems.

## Limitations and population mismatch

- Cannot detect naps or phone-free wakefulness well.
- Mostly young-adult sample; shift workers and insomnia populations may differ.
- Smartphone interaction is an adherence proxy, not a full sleep measure.
