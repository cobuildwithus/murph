# Bedtime Screen Use Behaviors and Sleep Outcomes in Early Adolescents: A Prospective Cohort Study
## Extraction boundary
- **Directness to Digital Sunset No Personal Screens Before Bed:** `adjacent_variant`.
- **Claim-use boundary:** `context-only`. Observational/context findings should not be written as causal intervention claims.
- **Artifact handling:** metadata/link only unless rights review explicitly clears redistribution.

## Population and design
- **Design:** Prospective ABCD cohort analysis.
- **Participants:** 9398 (ABCD_participants); Early adolescents aged 11-12 years in the ABCD Study; 48.4% female; 45% racial/ethnic minority.
- **Duration/follow-up:** One-year follow-up from ABCD Year 2 to Year 3.

## Exposure and comparator
- **Exposure:** Bedtime screen-use behaviors, bedroom TV/internet device, phone ringer activated overnight, texting/talking, music, and social media.
- **Comparator/control:** No bedroom device or phone off at bedtime; lower/no specific bedtime screen behaviors.

## Endpoints
- Sleep duration
- Caregiver-reported sleep disturbance
- Initiating/maintaining sleep-disorder factor

## Extracted result
Bedroom TV/internet device was prospectively associated with shorter sleep duration one year later; leaving the phone ringer activated overnight and several bedtime behaviors were associated with shorter sleep duration and/or greater sleep disturbance.

## Safety/adverse events
No intervention adverse events reported.

## Limitations and population mismatch
- Prospective observational design; no causal inference.
- Self-reported/caregiver-reported exposure and outcomes.
- Adjacent adolescent screen-behavior evidence, not a Digital Sunset intervention.
