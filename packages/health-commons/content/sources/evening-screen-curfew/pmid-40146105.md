## Extraction summary

- **Population:** Adults in the American Cancer Society Cancer Prevention Study–3, from 35 U.S. states and Puerto Rico.
- **Exposure/intervention:** Self-reported electronic screen use in the hour before bed.
- **Comparator/control:** No screen use before bed.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Cross-sectional analysis of 2018 survey responses; analyses conducted 2023–2025.
- **Endpoints:** sleep duration, bedtime timing, self-reported sleep quality, chronotype
- **Effect or direction:** Daily screen use before bed was associated with 5.04 fewer minutes of sleep on nonworkdays in one extracted model, later bedtimes, approximately 48–50 fewer minutes of sleep per week, and 33% higher prevalence of poor sleep quality (PR 1.33; 95% CI 1.27–1.39); associations were more pronounced in later chronotypes.
- **Safety/adverse events:** No adverse events; poor sleep quality and shorter sleep are burden endpoints.
