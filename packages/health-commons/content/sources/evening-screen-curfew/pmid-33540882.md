## Quick read

- **Source key:** `source_artifact:pmid-33540882`
- **Design:** Cohort/observational study.
- **People studied or addressed:** Adolescents; open article reports mean age about 13.86 years.
- **Participant count:** 2021 (reported_in_open_article).
- **Role for Digital Sunset:** adjacent_variant / context-only.

## Extracted facts

- **Intervention or exposure:** Social media use and parental rules about internet use before sleep.
- **Comparator or control:** Lower social media use and stricter versus less strict parental rules; longitudinal observational comparison.
- **Duration or follow-up:** Longitudinal adolescent survey; exact wave spacing not reproduced in this draft.
- **Endpoints:** bedtime; sleep quality; parental-rule moderation.
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Open-article extraction notes frequency of social media use at T1 predicted later bedtime at T2 (β about 0.14; combined β about 0.13). Parental rules appeared protective only among lower-engagement users and not among high-engagement users.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse-event monitoring extracted.

## Limitations and population mismatch

- Observational longitudinal design; no random assignment to rules.
- Rules were about internet/social media before sleep, not a complete personal-screen curfew.
- Protective rule effect was mixed by engagement level.

## Protocol boundary

Use as nuanced family-rule evidence: rules may help some adolescents but are not uniformly protective.
