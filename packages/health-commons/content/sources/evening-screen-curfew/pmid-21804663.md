# The Association between Use of Mobile Phones after Lights Out and Sleep Disturbances among Japanese Adolescents: A Nationwide Cross-Sectional Survey
## Extraction boundary
- **Directness to Digital Sunset No Personal Screens Before Bed:** `adjacent_variant`.
- **Claim-use boundary:** `context-only`. Observational/context findings should not be written as causal intervention claims.
- **Artifact handling:** metadata/link only unless rights review explicitly clears redistribution.

## Population and design
- **Design:** Nationwide cross-sectional survey.
- **Participants:** 94777 (analyzed_students); Japanese junior and senior high school students from a nationwide cluster-sampled survey.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Single assessment.

## Exposure and comparator
- **Exposure:** Mobile-phone calls or text messages after lights out.
- **Comparator/control:** No or less frequent after-lights-out mobile phone use.

## Endpoints
- Short sleep duration
- Poor subjective sleep quality
- Excessive daytime sleepiness
- Insomnia symptoms

## Extracted result
After-lights-out calling/texting was associated with short sleep duration, poor subjective sleep quality, excessive daytime sleepiness, and insomnia symptoms in mutually adjusted logistic models; daily calling was reported by 8.3% and daily texting by 17.6%.

## Safety/adverse events
No intervention adverse events reported.

## Limitations and population mismatch
- Cross-sectional self-report; no causal inference.
- Authors noted effect sizes were small.
- After-lights-out use is adjacent to the Digital Sunset behavioral boundary.

## Protocol-page use note
Adjacent-variant, not intervention evidence. Any future protocol-page claim using this source should cite `source_artifact:pmid-21804663` and preserve the `context-only` boundary.
