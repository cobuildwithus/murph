This source is included for **safety, burden, withdrawal, and clinical-boundary evidence** in the Digital Sunset extraction batch.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Cross-sectional Survey Analysis (2013).
- **People studied or addressed:** Adults in the 2009 Behavioral Risk Factor Surveillance System who were asked about drowsy driving.
- **Exposure/intervention:** Self-reported short or long sleep duration and subjective sleep insufficiency.
- **Comparator/control:** Reference sleep-duration and sleep-sufficiency groups.
- **Role for Digital Sunset:** safety_boundary; safety-only. This source is not upgraded to direct Digital Sunset efficacy unless the source itself directly tested no personal screens before bed.

## Extracted source-local finding

In a 2009 BRFSS analysis of 31,522 respondents, 3.6% reported drowsy driving; short sleepers and people reporting insufficient sleep had more frequent drowsy-driving reports. This supports sleep-opportunity and driving-safety guardrails for Digital Sunset.

## Endpoints extracted

drowsy driving, sleep duration, subjective sleep insufficiency

## Duration or follow-up

Past 30-day drowsy-driving self-report with usual sleep-duration categories.

## Safety, adverse events, and clinical boundaries

Driving-safety boundary. Users who are sleep restricted should avoid driving when drowsy regardless of screen-curfew adherence.

## Important limits and population mismatch

- Self-reported survey data.
- Cross-sectional association; causal direction cannot be established.
- Not a screen-curfew intervention.
