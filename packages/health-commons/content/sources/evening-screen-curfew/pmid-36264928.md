## Quick read

- **Source key:** `source_artifact:pmid-36264928`
- **Design:** Cross-sectional observational study.
- **People studied or addressed:** Adolescents/students; open abstract reports 53.4% female and median age 15 years.
- **Participant count:** 2513 (reported_in_open_article).
- **Role for Digital Sunset:** adjacent_variant / context-only.

## Extracted facts

- **Intervention or exposure:** Evening and night-time screen use timing and duration; multiple activity types.
- **Comparator or control:** Lower/no evening or night-time screen use; different durations.
- **Duration or follow-up:** Cross-sectional school questionnaire.
- **Endpoints:** sleep deprivation; sleep restriction; insomnia; non-refreshing sleep; daytime sleepiness; lack of energy; irritability.
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Open article reports 20% sleep deprived and 41% sleep restricted. All levels of night-time screen use showed dose effects; >2h night-time use: sleep deprivation OR 5.23 [3.03-9.00], sleep restriction OR 2.05 [1.23-3.42]. >2h evening use: sleep deprivation OR 2.72 [2.15-3.44], sleep restriction OR 1.69 [1.36-2.11]. Moderate evening use was not associated with both outcomes after adjustment.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** Daytime function outcomes include sleepiness, lack of energy, and irritability; no adverse-event monitoring.

## Limitations and population mismatch

- Cross-sectional; no causal inference.
- Self-reported screen use and sleep.
- Adolescent school population, not adult intervention.

## Protocol boundary

Use as strong observational timing/duration boundary: night-time and >2h evening exposure matter more than moderate evening use.
