# Daily links between objective smartphone use and sleep among adolescents
## Extraction boundary
- **Directness to Digital Sunset No Personal Screens Before Bed:** `same_mechanism`.
- **Claim-use boundary:** `supports-protocol`. Observational/context findings should not be written as causal intervention claims.
- **Artifact handling:** metadata/link only unless rights review explicitly clears redistribution.

## Population and design
- **Design:** 14-day objective smartphone/Fitbit plus daily diary study.
- **Participants:** 71 (adolescents); Ethnically diverse U.S. adolescents; mean age 16.49 years; 56% girls.
- **Duration/follow-up:** 14 days in 2021.

## Exposure and comparator
- **Exposure:** Nighttime and daytime smartphone screen time, pickups, and notifications from iPhone screenshots.
- **Comparator/control:** Within-person days with lower smartphone use and daytime versus nighttime use.

## Endpoints
- Fitbit-recorded sleep onset
- Self-reported sleep onset
- Self-reported sleep quality

## Extracted result
Greater nighttime screen time was associated with later self-reported and Fitbit-recorded sleep onset and poorer self-reported sleep quality; greater pickups were associated with later sleep onset; daytime smartphone use did not relate to sleep outcomes.

## Safety/adverse events
No intervention adverse events reported.

## Limitations and population mismatch
- Observational intensive longitudinal design; no causal inference.
- Small U.S. adolescent sample.
- Smartphone use was captured by screenshots, not continuous passive logs.
