## Methods snapshot
- **Design:** Prospective comparative group intervention; publication reports nonrandomized group assignment.
- **Participants:** parent-adolescent dyads enrolled; 35 intervention and 35 control; Young adolescents aged 10-12 years and their parents in rural northern Israel; 35 girls and 35 boys; mostly two-parent, above-average socioeconomic-status families.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Parents attended six interactive workshop sessions over three months addressing adolescent sleep patterns and electronic media exposure.
- **Comparator/control:** Parents received written information only.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Baseline, post-intervention, and 3-month follow-up.
- **Endpoints extracted:** actigraphy sleep timing, actigraphy sleep duration, sleep efficiency, self-reported electronic media exposure, video-game/computer-game time.

## Results extracted
- Intervention adolescents had earlier bedtimes, longer sleep duration, and higher sleep efficiency at post-intervention/follow-up.
- Computer/video-game exposure decreased significantly in the intervention group; extracted evidence did not isolate a bedtime personal-screen curfew.
- The publication notes a design mismatch with the registered RCT framing: group division was nonrandom by recruitment period.

## Digital Sunset relevance
This source is `adjacent_variant` for **Digital Sunset No Personal Screens Before Bed**. It should be used as `supports-protocol` evidence only. Adjacent or mechanism findings must not be promoted into direct protocol efficacy claims.

## Safety/adverse events

## Limitations and population mismatch
- Nonrandomized group assignment in publication despite registry/ledger RCT framing.
- Parent-mediated bundle; bedtime screen curfew not isolated.
- Mobile-phone use was not measured.
- Short follow-up and narrow 10-12-year-old sample limit generalization.
