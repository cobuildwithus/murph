This source is included for **safety, burden, withdrawal, and clinical-boundary evidence** in the Digital Sunset extraction batch.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Systematic Review (2020).
- **People studied or addressed:** Participants in short-term abstinence studies across exercise, gambling, gaming, mobile phone, pornography, and social media domains.
- **Exposure/intervention:** Short-term abstinence from a potentially addictive behavior.
- **Comparator/control:** Baseline, no abstinence, or study-specific comparison conditions.
- **Role for Digital Sunset:** background; safety-only. This source is not upgraded to direct Digital Sunset efficacy unless the source itself directly tested no personal screens before bed.

## Extracted source-local finding

The review included 47 prospective studies across potential behavioral addictions and identified craving as a common abstinence-induced effect, with evidence gaps around relapse, rebound, and compensatory behaviors. For Digital Sunset, this supports monitoring withdrawal-like discomfort and noncompliance during restriction-style changes.

## Endpoints extracted

craving, withdrawal symptoms, negative affect, noncompliance, rebound behavior

## Duration or follow-up

Short-term abstinence periods varied by study.

## Safety, adverse events, and clinical boundaries

Withdrawal/adherence boundary. Users with problematic use patterns may need gradual tapering, replacement routines, and support rather than abrupt abstinence.

## Important limits and population mismatch

- Background review across multiple behavioral domains, not bedtime screen use specifically.
- Direct evidence for Digital Sunset withdrawal burden is limited.
- Rebound and compensatory behaviors were not adequately assessed in many studies.
