## Quick read

- **Source key:** `source_artifact:pmid-31751354`
- **Design:** Controlled/laboratory exposure study.
- **People studied or addressed:** Male adolescents/young men as described in the open article context; candidate metadata flags adults or young adults, so population mismatch remains.
- **Participant count:** 18 (reported_in_open_article).
- **Role for Digital Sunset:** adjacent_variant / context-only.

## Extracted facts

- **Intervention or exposure:** Five-hour violent videogame session (Counter-Strike: Global Offensive) on a Saturday night.
- **Comparator or control:** Five-hour board game session (Monopoly) on a separate Saturday night.
- **Duration or follow-up:** Repeated-measures/crossover acute exposure with overnight sleep study and next-morning memory/vigilance assessment.
- **Endpoints:** sleep efficiency; wake after sleep onset; arousal index; declarative memory recall; bedtime melatonin; cortisol.
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Open article reports slightly reduced sleep efficiency after videogaming (-3.5%, p=.017), impaired declarative memory recall (p=.005), lower bedtime melatonin (p=.005), and elevated cortisol while playing (p=.031); findings were not large but consistent with increased wake after sleep onset/arousal context.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse events extracted; hormonal arousal and memory impairment are mechanism/outcome signals.

## Limitations and population mismatch

- Small male sample.
- Very prolonged 5-hour gaming; not comparable to typical brief screen use.
- Gaming versus board-game design cannot isolate light, excitement, and displacement fully.

## Protocol boundary

Use as prolonged gaming/arousal mechanism evidence, not as a universal screen-curfew effect.
