# Bedtime mobile phone use and sleep in adults
## Extraction boundary
- **Directness to Digital Sunset No Personal Screens Before Bed:** `same_mechanism`.
- **Claim-use boundary:** `supports-protocol`. Observational/context findings should not be written as causal intervention claims.
- **Artifact handling:** metadata/link only unless rights review explicitly clears redistribution.

## Population and design
- **Design:** Cross-sectional face-to-face adult survey.
- **Participants:** 844 (survey_respondents); Flemish adults aged 18-94 years; 56.2% women; mean age 46.0 years.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Single assessment.

## Exposure and comparator
- **Exposure:** Taking a mobile phone into the bedroom and sending/receiving texts or calls after lights out.
- **Comparator/control:** Lower/no bedtime mobile-phone use.

## Endpoints
- PSQI sleep quality/components
- Bergen Insomnia Scale
- Fatigue Assessment Scale
- Rise time
- Sleep duration

## Extracted result
After-lights-out texts/calls predicted PSQI scores, especially longer latency, worse efficiency, more disturbance, and daytime dysfunction; bedtime mobile phone use predicted later rise time, higher insomnia score, and increased fatigue.

## Safety/adverse events
No intervention adverse events reported.

## Limitations and population mismatch
- Cross-sectional observational design; no causal inference.
- Adult Flemish survey may not generalize to all populations.
- Supports protocol plausibility, not intervention efficacy.
