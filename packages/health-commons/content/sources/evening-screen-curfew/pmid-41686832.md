# Sleep and smartphone use: Within and between-person relationships from an objective longitudinal smartphone and wearable data donation study
## Extraction boundary
- **Directness to Digital Sunset No Personal Screens Before Bed:** `same_mechanism`.
- **Claim-use boundary:** `supports-protocol`. Observational/context findings should not be written as causal intervention claims.
- **Artifact handling:** metadata/link only unless rights review explicitly clears redistribution.

## Population and design
- **Design:** 14-day objective iPhone and Apple Watch data-donation study.
- **Participants:** 68 (participants_with_952_daily_datapoints); Belgian adults with iPhones and Apple Watches in a citizen-science data-donation subsample.
- **Duration/follow-up:** 14 consecutive days.

## Exposure and comparator
- **Exposure:** Total smartphone use and in-bed smartphone use inferred from iPhone Screen Time and Apple Watch accelerometry.
- **Comparator/control:** Within-person days and between-person differences in total and in-bed smartphone use.

## Endpoints
- Total sleep duration
- REM/core/deep sleep
- Next-day smartphone use habits

## Extracted result
At the within-person level, more total smartphone use increased same-day in-bed smartphone use (β=.25, 95% CI .20-.31), and in-bed smartphone use was associated with more same-day overall sleep (β=.08, 95% CI .02-.14); strong between-person habit associations were observed.

## Safety/adverse events
No intervention adverse events reported; authors cautioned that longer sleep may not mean better or more restorative sleep.

## Limitations and population mismatch
- Convenience sample of iPhone/Apple Watch owners; limited generalizability.
- N=68 with 14 days; no phone-content data.
- Findings contradict simple disruption assumptions and should be treated as mixed evidence.

## Protocol-page use note
Mixed/contrary observational evidence; not proof of benefit or harm. Any future protocol-page claim using this source should cite `source_artifact:pmid-41686832` and preserve the `supports-protocol` boundary.
