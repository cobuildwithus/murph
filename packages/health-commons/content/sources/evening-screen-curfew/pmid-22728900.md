This source is included for **safety, burden, withdrawal, and clinical-boundary evidence** in the Digital Sunset extraction batch.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Cross-sectional Survey (2012).
- **People studied or addressed:** Japanese adolescents in early and late adolescent grade groups.
- **Exposure/intervention:** Mobile phone use after lights out.
- **Comparator/control:** No mobile phone use after lights out or lower/no exposure groups in adjusted cross-sectional analysis.
- **Role for Digital Sunset:** safety_boundary; safety-only. This source is not upgraded to direct Digital Sunset efficacy unless the source itself directly tested no personal screens before bed.

## Extracted source-local finding

In 17,920 adolescents, mobile phone use after lights out was significantly associated with poor mental health, suicidal feelings, and self-injury after adjustment for sleep length and other confounders. This is safety context for youth protocols, not causal efficacy evidence.

## Endpoints extracted

poor mental health, suicidal feelings, self-injury, sleep length

## Duration or follow-up

Cross-sectional questionnaire assessment.

## Safety, adverse events, and clinical boundaries

Safety-only psychosocial context. If suicidal feelings or self-injury are present, Digital Sunset should be secondary to crisis support and clinical care.

## Important limits and population mismatch

- Cross-sectional design cannot establish causality or directionality.
- Adolescent population; not directly generalizable to adults.
- Mobile phone use after lights out is not identical to a planned pre-bed screen curfew.
