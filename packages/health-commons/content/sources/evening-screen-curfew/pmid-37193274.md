# Night-time smartphone use, sleep duration, sleep quality, and menstrual disturbances in young adult women: A population-based study with high-resolution tracking data
## Extraction boundary
- **Directness to Digital Sunset No Personal Screens Before Bed:** `same_mechanism`.
- **Claim-use boundary:** `context-only`. Observational/context findings should not be written as causal intervention claims.
- **Artifact handling:** metadata/link only unless rights review explicitly clears redistribution.

## Population and design
- **Design:** Population-based high-resolution smartphone tracking plus survey study.
- **Participants:** 1068 (survey_respondents_with_764_tracking_participants); Women aged 18-40 years in the SmartSleep Study.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Median tracking time four nights (IQR 2-8).

## Exposure and comparator
- **Exposure:** Objectively tracked smartphone use between self-reported sleep onset and offset.
- **Comparator/control:** Lower frequency/duration of nighttime smartphone use.

## Endpoints
- Long sleep duration
- Short sleep duration
- Poor sleep quality
- Menstrual disturbances

## Extracted result
Higher frequency and longer duration of nighttime smartphone use were associated with long sleep duration, but not poor sleep quality, short sleep duration, or menstrual disturbances; short sleep duration and poor sleep quality were associated with menstrual disturbances.

## Safety/adverse events
No phone-use adverse events reported; reproductive/menstrual outcomes were assessed and phone-use associations were null.

## Limitations and population mismatch
- Cross-sectional tracking/survey design; no causal inference.
- Young adult women only; median tracking only four nights.
- Objectively tracked sleep-window phone use is same mechanism, but not a Digital Sunset intervention.

Null/mixed context-only evidence. Any future protocol-page claim using this source should cite `source_artifact:pmid-37193274` and preserve the `context-only` boundary.
