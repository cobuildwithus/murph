# Nighttime smartphone use, sleep quality, and mental health: investigating a complex relationship
## Extraction boundary
- **Directness to Digital Sunset No Personal Screens Before Bed:** `adjacent_variant`.
- **Claim-use boundary:** `context-only`. Observational/context findings should not be written as causal intervention claims.
- **Artifact handling:** metadata/link only unless rights review explicitly clears redistribution.

## Population and design
- **Design:** SmartSleep survey/register/tracking study with causal-discovery analysis.
- **Participants:** 5798 (survey_register_participants_with_4239_tracking_participants); Danish adults aged 16-89 years across three SmartSleep Study samples.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Up to 14 nights of smartphone tracking plus survey/register follow-up for medication outcome.

## Exposure and comparator
- **Exposure:** Self-reported and tracked nighttime smartphone use; sleep quality.
- **Comparator/control:** Less/no nighttime smartphone use and good sleep quality.

## Endpoints
- High perceived stress
- Severe depressive symptoms
- Poor sleep quality
- Psychotropic medication use

## Extracted result
Frequent self-reported nighttime smartphone use was associated with high perceived stress (OR 2.24, 95% CI 1.42-3.55) and severe depressive symptoms (OR 2.96, 95% CI 2.04-4.28); tracked nighttime use showed no clear mental-health associations except repeated use throughout the sleep period with severe depressive symptoms (OR 1.69, 95% CI 1.24-2.31).

## Safety/adverse events
Mental-health endpoints provide safety/context; no intervention adverse events reported.

## Limitations and population mismatch
- Observational design; authors state causal conclusions cannot be made.
- Low response rates and selected samples may limit representativeness.
- Sleep quality was cross-sectional, and objective sleep tracking was not included.
