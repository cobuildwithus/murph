This source is included for **safety, burden, withdrawal, and clinical-boundary evidence** in the Digital Sunset extraction batch.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Systematic Review (2023).
- **People studied or addressed:** Drivers or simulated-driving participants across experimental, naturalistic, longitudinal, case-control, and cross-sectional studies.
- **Exposure/intervention:** Prior sleep duration before driving or driving-performance assessment.
- **Comparator/control:** Longer prior sleep or reference sleep duration, commonly about 8 or more hours where available.
- **Role for Digital Sunset:** safety_boundary; safety-only. This source is not upgraded to direct Digital Sunset efficacy unless the source itself directly tested no personal screens before bed.

## Extracted source-local finding

The review found that 6-7 hours of prior sleep produced modest driving impairment and roughly 30% greater crash likelihood versus 8 or more hours, while 4-5 hours produced large decrements and about double the crash likelihood. This supports a Digital Sunset safety boundary: protect sleep opportunity and avoid driving when the user is underslept.

## Endpoints extracted

driving impairment, crash risk, prior sleep duration, drowsy-driving safety

## Duration or follow-up

Prior sleep duration windows varied across included studies.

## Safety, adverse events, and clinical boundaries

Safety-only source. The protocol should prioritize adequate sleep and avoid advice that leaves a user too tired to drive, work, or care for others safely.

## Important limits and population mismatch

- Systematic review of driving safety, not a bedtime screen-curfew trial.
- Included studies varied in design, driving outcomes, and prior-sleep thresholds.
- Use is safety-only; do not infer that Digital Sunset directly changes crash risk.
