# Screen Use at Bedtime and Sleep Duration and Quality Among Youths
## Extraction boundary
- **Directness to Digital Sunset No Personal Screens Before Bed:** `same_mechanism`.
- **Claim-use boundary:** `supports-protocol`. Observational/context findings should not be written as causal intervention claims.
- **Artifact handling:** metadata/link only unless rights review explicitly clears redistribution.

## Population and design
- **Design:** Repeated-measures home observation with cameras and accelerometry.
- **Participants:** 79 (youths_with_323_observed_nights); Healthy youths aged 11-14.9 years in Dunedin, New Zealand; 47 male; mean age 12.9 years.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Four nonconsecutive nights per participant, March-December 2021.

## Exposure and comparator
- **Exposure:** Objectively coded screen use in the two hours before bedtime and once in bed, including interactive, passive, gaming, and multitasking use.
- **Comparator/control:** Nights or intervals with less/no screen use.

## Endpoints
- Sleep onset timing
- Total sleep time
- Wake after sleep onset
- Sleep quality measures

## Extracted result
General screen use in the two hours before bed had no association with most sleep-health measures, whereas screen use once in bed was associated with shorter total sleep time (-3 minutes per additional 10 minutes); interactive, gaming, and multitasking in bed had larger negative associations.

## Safety/adverse events
No intervention adverse events reported.

## Limitations and population mismatch
- Small youth sample from one New Zealand city.
- Repeated-measures observational design; no causal inference.
- Objective observation may alter behavior and does not test a curfew intervention.

## Protocol-page use note
Mixed result; do not claim all pre-bed screen use is harmful. Any future protocol-page claim using this source should cite `source_artifact:pmid-39226046` and preserve the `supports-protocol` boundary.
