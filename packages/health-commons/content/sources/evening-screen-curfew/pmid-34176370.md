## Methods snapshot
- **Design:** Single-blind randomized controlled trial.
- **Participants:** randomized; experimental n=29, active control n=26; Young adults at a large public university; mean age 19.45 years; 78% female; used smartphone or laptop in the two hours before bed.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Automated blue-light-reducing screen temperature variation using f.lux software for one week.
- **Comparator/control:** Active-control f.lux installation without activated blue-light-reducing features.
- **Duration/follow-up:** One-week manipulation with seven nights of actigraphy before/after.
- **Endpoints extracted:** objective sleep, self-reported sleep, pre-sleep arousal, daytime sleepiness, mental health.

## Results extracted
- The experimental group did not improve more than control on objective sleep, self-reported sleep, or mental health outcomes.
- The experimental group rated the software as more distracting and disabled it more often.

## Digital Sunset relevance
This source is `adjacent_variant` for **Digital Sunset No Personal Screens Before Bed**. It should be used as `safety-only` evidence only. Adjacent or mechanism findings must not be promoted into direct protocol efficacy claims.

## Safety/adverse events
- **Adverse events:** Software distraction and disabling were extracted as acceptability/usability signals, not clinical adverse events.
- **Safety notes:** A screen-temperature intervention may create usability burden and should not be presented as equivalent to a screen curfew.

## Limitations and population mismatch
- Blue-light/temperature manipulation only, not a no-screen behavior.
- Young adult university sample.
- Short duration and small sample.
