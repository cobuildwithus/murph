- Population: Forty-five smartphone users started the study; nine did not complete all four lab sessions. Sample was recruited from Lancaster University psychology subject pool and campus advertising; 33% male; average age 22.4 years.
- Intervention: Twenty-four-hour smartphone abstinence. Participants had their phone placed in an evidence bag and were asked not to open or use it; other digital technologies were not fully restricted.
- Comparator: Within-subject four-session comparison: one week before abstinence, immediately before abstinence, immediately after the 24-hour abstinence period, and the following day.
- Duration: 24-hour full smartphone abstinence; no 72-hour or 7-day condition; not social-media-only fasting.
- Effect direction: mixed_adjacent_burden: 24-hour smartphone abstinence increased craving/urge but did not show significant mood or anxiety change; not direct evidence for social media abstinence.

## Claim-Safe Summary

Use only as adjacent evidence that total smartphone abstinence can raise craving without clear mood or anxiety worsening. Do not use as direct evidence for social media abstinence or fast efficacy.
