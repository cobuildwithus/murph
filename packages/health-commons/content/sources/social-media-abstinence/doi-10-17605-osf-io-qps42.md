- Population: N = 237 digital media users contributing T = 12,407 experience-sampling situations over 2 weeks.
- Intervention: Naturally occurring digital disconnection in daily-life situations, defined broadly as temporary non-use or limitation of digital media; not a prescribed social media fast.
- Comparator: Within-person situations with more versus less disconnection relative to the participant's usual level, plus between-person comparisons of participants who generally disconnected more versus less; models considered contextual controls including work versus leisure media use.
- Duration: Two-week experience-sampling observation with situational and lagged analyses; no 24-hour, 72-hour, or 7-day social-media-abstinence protocol.
- Effect direction: mixed_adjacent: positive situational associations for affective well-being, energy, and social connectedness; null for stress, controlled between-person differences, and durable over-time effects; no causal abstinence estimate.

## Claim-Safe Summary

Use only as adjacent measurement and mechanism context for momentary digital disconnection and ESM endpoint selection. Not safe as evidence that a 24-hour, 72-hour, or 7-day social media fast improves well-being.
