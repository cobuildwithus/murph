- Population: Eighty-two young adults enrolled in Ann Arbor, Michigan; mean age 19.52 years, 53 female; participants had a Facebook account and touchscreen smartphone. Analyses used experience-sampling responses over 14 days with attrition and low-compliance pruning reported.
- Intervention: No abstinence intervention. Naturally occurring Facebook use was assessed repeatedly by text-message experience sampling.
- Comparator: Within-person lagged associations comparing higher versus lower Facebook use between prompts; direct face-to-face or phone interaction was assessed as a contextual comparator; life satisfaction analyses controlled baseline levels.
- Duration: Fourteen-day observational experience-sampling period with pre/post life satisfaction assessment; no social-media-abstinence duration.
- Effect direction: negative_observational_context: higher Facebook use was associated with lower subsequent affect and lower life satisfaction over time; no abstinence effect was tested.

## Claim-Safe Summary

Use only to contextualize why Facebook or social-media-use reduction might be hypothesized to affect well-being. Not safe for claims that social media abstinence or fasting improves mood or life satisfaction.
