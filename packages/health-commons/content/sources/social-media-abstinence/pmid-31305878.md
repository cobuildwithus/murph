- Population: A cohort of 3,826 adolescents in 31 schools in the Greater Montreal area; mean age 12.7 years, 47% girls; annual surveys across adolescence.
- Intervention: No abstinence intervention. Annual self-reported screen-time exposure was tracked for social media, television, video gaming, and computer use.
- Comparator: Between-person mean screen-time differences over 4 years and within-person year-specific deviations from each adolescent's own mean screen time.
- Duration: Four-year longitudinal observational assessment; not a 24-hour, 72-hour, or 7-day fasting protocol.
- Effect direction: adjacent_risk_association_mixed_by_modality: social media and television time-varying increases were associated with higher depressive symptoms in adolescents; video gaming was null and other modalities were mixed; no abstinence effect was tested.

## Claim-Safe Summary

Use for context that social media is not interchangeable with generic screen time and that adolescent depression associations vary by screen modality. Not safe for claims that social media fasting improves depressive symptoms.
