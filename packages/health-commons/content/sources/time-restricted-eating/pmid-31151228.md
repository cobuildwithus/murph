This source is included for **Direct 18:6 and six-hour TRE evidence**.

## Findings
- **24-hour glucose and glycemic excursions.**: Early time-restricted feeding lowered mean 24-hour glucose and reduced glycemic excursions compared with the 08:00-20:00 control schedule in this acute controlled-feeding setting.
- **Circadian-clock, aging, autophagy, hormone, and lipid markers.**: The study reported mixed changes in markers related to circadian biology, aging, autophagy, hormones, and lipids; these signals are mechanistic and should not be converted into clinical outcome claims.
- **Generalizability to a free-living 18:6 protocol.**: Because the intervention lasted only four days with provided/matched intake, the source is strongest for mechanism and weakest for long-term weight, adherence, or safety claims.

## Why it matters
This is one of the closest mechanistic human tests of an 18:6-style early eating window and gives source-level support for glucose and circadian-mechanism hypotheses.

## Potential experiment signals
24-hour glucose, glucose excursions, fasting insulin, insulin resistance, circadian and autophagy markers

## Protocol takeaway
A short supervised 08:00-14:00 eating window improved 24-hour glucose metrics, but the study is acute, controlled-feeding evidence rather than free-living adherence or long-term efficacy evidence.
