This source is included for **Behavioral tolerability, eating behavior, and adherence**.

**Findings:** Interviews identified facilitators across personal, behavioral, psychological, social, and environmental levels, including perceived physical-health or energy benefits, alignment with diet/exercise/sleep goals, self-monitoring, positive psychological effects, social support, and busy or regular schedules. Reported barriers included negative physical effects, hunger or sluggishness, skipped routines or inadequate diet, mismatch with activity, self-monitoring burden, negative feelings, social situations, and irregular or idle schedules.

**Why it matters:** This qualitative source captures the everyday reasons people can or cannot sustain TRE, including hunger, energy, social meals, schedule regularity, self-monitoring, and psychological response. It is directly useful for starter-window support language, off-ramps, and social-burden warnings.

**Potential experiment signals:** adherence facilitators, adherence barriers, hunger, energy, social situations, psychological effects, daily schedules.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as adjacent-variant behavioral evidence: TRE adherence depends on schedule fit, social context, hunger/energy response, support, and whether monitoring feels helpful rather than burdensome.
