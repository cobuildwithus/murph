This source is included for **Graded windows and adjacent TRE variants**.

**Findings:**
- Participants showed prolonged and irregular eating patterns in real-world logging; many eating episodes occurred late in the day and habitual eating duration often exceeded common TRE windows.
- In a very small uncontrolled subset, reducing the eating interval to 10–12 h was feasible and was associated with reduced body weight and improvements in sleep, energy, and bedtime hunger.

**Why it matters:** This is foundational real-world feasibility evidence for detecting long eating windows and starting with a less aggressive window before narrower TRE variants.

**Potential experiment signals:** eating-window duration, meal timing variability, body weight, sleep quality, energy level, bedtime hunger, self-reported adherence.

**Protocol takeaway:** Useful adjacent evidence for graded starter-window feasibility, but the intervention was 10–12 h, not 18:6, and the treated subset was uncontrolled and very small.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

**Extraction caveat:** The intervention subset had only eight participants and no randomized comparator.
