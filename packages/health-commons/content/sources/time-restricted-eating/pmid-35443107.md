This source is included for **Graded windows and adjacent TRE variants**.

**Findings:**
- At 12 months, mean weight loss was -8.0 kg with TRE plus calorie restriction and -6.3 kg with daily calorie restriction alone; the between-group difference was -1.8 kg (95% CI, -4.0 to 0.4; P=0.11). Waist, BMI, fat mass, lean mass, blood pressure, and metabolic-risk analyses were consistent with no clear added TRE advantage.
- Available trial reporting found no substantial difference in adverse-event counts between groups. Reported mild events included fatigue, dizziness, headache, decreased appetite, upper abdominal pain, dyspepsia, and constipation; deaths or serious adverse events were not reported in the extracted summary.

**Why it matters:** This trial directly addresses whether TRE adds efficacy beyond a calorie prescription, a central confound for interpreting weight-loss claims around shorter eating windows.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- Body weight
- Waist circumference
- Body fat mass
- Lean mass
- Blood pressure
- Metabolic risk markers
- Adverse events

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as a context-only calorie-restriction boundary: adding an early 8-hour eating window to calorie restriction did not clearly improve weight, body fat, or metabolic risk outcomes over calorie restriction alone.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
