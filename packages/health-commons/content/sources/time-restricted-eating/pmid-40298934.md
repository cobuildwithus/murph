This source is included for **Reviews and evidence maps**.

**Findings:** In 30 studies with 1341 participants, the isocaloric-control analysis reported reductions in body weight (MD -1.46 kg; 95% CI -2.65 to -0.26), fat mass (MD -1.50 kg; 95% CI -2.77 to -0.24), and fat-free mass (MD -0.41 kg; 95% CI -0.79 to -0.03). In non-isocaloric control studies, the TRE group showed larger reductions in body weight (MD -2.82 kg; 95% CI -3.49 to -2.15), fat mass (MD -1.36 kg; 95% CI -2.09 to -0.63), and fat-free mass (MD -0.86 kg; 95% CI -1.23 to -0.49). The review’s design supports separating energy-intake effects from timing effects and flags fat-free-mass loss as a body-composition outcome to monitor.

**Why it matters:** This source is central for calorie-confounding language: it distinguishes weight change driven by energy deficit from possible timing-specific effects.

**Potential experiment signals:** body weight, fat mass, fat-free mass, calorie restriction interpretation.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use to keep 18:6 claims calorie-aware and body-composition-aware; do not imply that all weight change is independent of intake.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
