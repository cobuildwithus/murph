# 2023 International Olympic Committee's (IOC) consensus statement on Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport (REDs)
This source is included for **Guidelines and external safety context**.
## Findings
- `finding:pmid-37752011-01` — The IOC consensus frames REDs as a syndrome of adverse health and performance outcomes associated with problematic low energy availability, making it a safety boundary for fasting-window experiments in athletes.
- `finding:pmid-37752011-02` — The source supports caution that a compressed eating window must not displace training fuel, recovery meals, or total energy intake in athletes or heavy exercisers.

## Why it matters
A daily 18:6 window can unintentionally reduce energy availability when layered on high training load. This guideline is not TRE evidence, but it anchors the athlete contraindication and referral boundary.

## Potential experiment signals
- training load
- energy availability
- menstrual status
- injury or illness recurrence
- recovery and performance complaints

## Protocol takeaway
For athletes, heavy exercisers, or anyone with RED-S risk, do not present 18:6 as a default self-directed protocol; require adequate fueling and clinical/sports-dietitian oversight.
