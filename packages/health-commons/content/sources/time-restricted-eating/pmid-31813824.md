This source is included for **Graded windows and adjacent TRE variants**.

**Findings:** Nineteen participants with metabolic syndrome completed a 12-week self-selected 10-hour TRE intervention; most were already using statin and/or antihypertensive therapy. The study reported reductions in weight, waist circumference, percent body fat, visceral fat, blood pressure, atherogenic lipids, and HbA1c during the 10-hour TRE intervention. Because the study was single-arm and included many participants on cardiometabolic medications, it should be treated as clinical-supervised adjacent evidence rather than direct causal proof for unsupervised 18:6.

**Why it matters:** Useful for cardiometabolic endpoint selection and clinical-supervised boundaries, not as direct strict 18:6 evidence.

**Potential experiment signals:** body weight, waist circumference, body fat, visceral fat, blood pressure, atherogenic lipids, HbA1c, sleep timing.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use to justify tracking cardiometabolic signals during TRE, with a clear caveat that a supervised metabolic-syndrome pilot is not a home 18:6 efficacy proof.
