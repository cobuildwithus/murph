This source is included for a narrow **five-minute future-task-list** experiment.

The study randomized 57 healthy adults age 18–30 to write either tasks they needed to complete over the next few days or activities they had recently completed. The future-task-list group fell asleep faster on the single laboratory night measured by polysomnography.

Use the result cautiously. It was one night, in a young healthy sample, with another writing task as the comparator. It does not establish a chronic-insomnia treatment, prove that writing is better than doing nothing, or show that longer and more emotional journaling is better. A Murph protocol should keep the list brief, concrete, bounded, and easy to stop when it increases arousal.
