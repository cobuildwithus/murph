This source is included for **pharmacology_individual_differences**.

**Findings:** Daily caffeine intake above 300 mg/day was associated with shorter total sleep time and higher odds of sleep complaints and insomnia regardless of ADORA2A polymorphism, with side effects increasing at higher intake. Among lower caffeine consumers, ADORA2A polymorphisms influenced total sleep time and risk of sleep complaints or insomnia, supporting receptor-genotype sensitivity at lower exposure levels.

**Why it matters:** This is a practical bridge between dose, receptor genotype, and sleep complaints: both high total dose and individual receptor variation can matter.

**Potential experiment signals:** total sleep time, sleep-onset latency, sleep efficiency, caffeine side-effect log.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as same-mechanism support for the dose-reset component and sensitivity personalization, but avoid treating it as direct curfew efficacy evidence.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.
