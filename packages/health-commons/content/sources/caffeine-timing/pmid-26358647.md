This source is included for **clinical_safety_boundaries**.

**Findings:**
- `finding:pmid-26358647-dose-normalized-caffeine-increased`: Dose-normalized caffeine concentrations in serum and urine increased between first and third trimester, consistent with higher caffeine exposure later in pregnancy.
- `finding:pmid-26358647-metabolite-patterns`: The study reported trimester-related changes for theophylline and urine/serum ratios, while normalized paraxanthine and theobromine changes were not significant in the accessible summary.

**Why it matters:** A fixed caffeine cutoff may not protect against prolonged exposure in pregnancy because pharmacokinetics shift across trimesters.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- biomarker:caffeine-clearance
- biomarker:caffeine-concentration
- safety:pregnancy

**Protocol takeaway:** Pregnancy should trigger more conservative caffeine guidance and clinician alignment; ordinary curfew windows may not be sufficient.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.
