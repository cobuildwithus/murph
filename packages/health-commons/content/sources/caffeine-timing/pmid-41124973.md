## Extracted evidence fields

**Population:** Participants in controlled crossover caffeine-sleep trials, analyzed by age and dose categories; sample count not available from accessible artifacts.

**Intervention or exposure:** Caffeine exposure in controlled crossover trials, with subgrouping by age and caffeine dose.

**Comparator or control:** Placebo or control condition in crossover trial designs.

**Duration or follow-up:** Subsequent sleep after controlled caffeine exposure; exact dosing-to-bed intervals were not available in the extracted abstract snippets.

**Endpoints:**
  - total sleep time
  - sleep efficiency
  - sleep-onset latency
  - slow-wave sleep percentage
  - REM sleep percentage

**Effect estimates or direction:** Accessible abstract snippet reported total sleep time MD -34.67 minutes, sleep efficiency MD -4.74%, slow-wave sleep percentage MD -1.01%, and sleep-onset latency +8.35 minutes; REM percentage was not significant. Both high and low dose subgroups reduced total sleep time/sleep efficiency and increased sleep-onset latency; high dose additionally reduced slow-wave sleep percentage. Between-group differences and age/dose meta-regressions were not statistically significant in the snippet.

**Adverse events or safety notes:** Sleep disruption is the relevant safety/tolerability endpoint; no separate adverse-event data were extracted.

**Limitations:** Only abstract/search-result level data were accessible; exact participant count, trial count, and protocol details should be verified from the full article when available.

**Population mismatch:** Direct for caffeine-sleep physiology, but it is not an exact 14-day curfew or dose-reset behavior trial.

**Artifact candidates and rights status:** Publisher article appears paywalled/non-redistributable.

**Findings:**
- `finding:pmid-41124973-controlled-crossover-sleep-effects` — Accessible abstract data reported caffeine-associated reductions in total sleep time, sleep efficiency, and slow-wave sleep percentage and an increase in sleep-onset latency; REM percentage was not significantly changed.
- `finding:pmid-41124973-age-dose-subgroup-cautions` — Subgroup snippets indicated parameter-level changes in younger and high-dose groups, but between-group differences and age/dose meta-regressions were not statistically significant; use this as personalization context rather than proof of a sharp age/dose threshold.

**Why it matters:** This source adds recent age- and dose-specific context for why a universal caffeine curfew may need personalization.

**Potential experiment signals:** total sleep time, sleep efficiency, sleep-onset latency, slow-wave sleep / deep sleep, REM sleep percentage.

**Protocol takeaway:** Caffeine appears to disrupt multiple sleep parameters in controlled crossover trials; younger adults and higher doses may show stronger parameter-level changes, but subgroup differences were not clearly statistically separable in the accessible abstract.
