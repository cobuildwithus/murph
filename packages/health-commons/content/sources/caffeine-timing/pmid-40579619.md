This source is included for **systematic_reviews_meta_analyses**.

## Extracted evidence fields

**Population:** Athletes and sports participants using caffeine to enhance performance.

**Intervention or exposure:** Caffeine intake for performance, including cases where intake occurs near evening training or competition.

**Comparator or control:** Not a direct comparator trial; narrative review discusses performance versus sleep-protection tradeoffs.

**Duration or follow-up:** Varies across cited sports studies; no single intervention duration.

**Endpoints:**
  - sports performance
  - activation/arousal
  - sleep quality
  - insomnia risk
  - next-day performance/recovery

**Effect estimates or direction:** Narrative review notes that some studies showing ergogenic effects also report increased activation or reduced sleep quality in the hours afterward; no pooled sleep effect was extracted.

**Adverse events or safety notes:** Insomnia or reduced subsequent-day performance are highlighted as possible tradeoffs when caffeine is used later in the day.

**Limitations:** Narrative review; athlete-specific and not direct protocol efficacy evidence.

**Population mismatch:** Adjacent population/context: performance-oriented caffeine differs from routine caffeine curfew for sleep improvement.

**Artifact candidates and rights status:** Open-access PMC/Springer article candidate; verify license before storing copies.

**Findings:**
- `finding:pmid-40579619-performance-sleep-conflict` — The narrative review frames a conflict in which caffeine can support acute sport performance while also increasing activation or reducing sleep quality afterward in some contexts.
- `finding:pmid-40579619-late-competition-exception-boundary` — The review supports treating late-day performance caffeine as an explicit exception with potential sleep and next-day recovery costs rather than as routine caffeine timing.

**Why it matters:** This source helps articulate when users may knowingly trade sleep protection for acute performance, which should be a boundary rather than a contradiction of the curfew protocol.

**Potential experiment signals:** subjective sleep quality, sleep duration, daytime alertness, training or competition performance, next-day recovery.

**Protocol takeaway:** For late-day sports performance, caffeine timing advice may need modification, but the sleep-quality cost should be made explicit and tracked.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
