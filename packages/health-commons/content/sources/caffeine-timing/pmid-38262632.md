This source is included for **caffeine_source_dose_audit**.

**Findings:** In a large Norwegian cross-sectional study of college and university students aged 18-35, energy drink consumption was associated with negative sleep outcomes, including poorer sleep even at low consumption levels in accessible summaries; the design does not establish causality.

**Why it matters:** Large recent observational evidence helps frame energy drinks as a higher-risk source category for sleep outcomes, without implying causality.

**Potential experiment signals:** energy drink caffeine dose, sleep duration, insomnia symptoms, sleep timing.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as energy-drink sleep-risk context only; do not use to claim the protocol causally improves sleep.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
