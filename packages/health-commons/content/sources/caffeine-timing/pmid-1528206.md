This source is included for **withdrawal_tolerance_dose_reset**.

**Findings:** The study concluded that low to moderate caffeine consumers can experience a withdrawal syndrome after daily caffeine is stopped.

**Why it matters:** Foundational controlled evidence that abrupt caffeine cessation can cause clinically noticeable symptoms.

**Potential experiment signals:** headache, fatigue, mood, sleepiness, protocol adherence.

**Protocol takeaway:** A reset can feel worse before sleep benefits appear; include withdrawal cautions and an offramp.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** People consuming low to moderate caffeine amounts in a double-blind cessation study; exact count was not extracted in this batch.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Double-blind cessation of caffeine consumption.
- **Comparator/control:** Continued caffeine or placebo substitution as structured in the cessation design.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Withdrawal assessed after cessation; exact observation window not extracted here.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** Withdrawal syndrome after cessation is the central adverse/safety concern for abrupt dose resets.
- **Limitations:** Classic controlled cessation study; details beyond abstract-level conclusion and sample count were not extracted here.
- **Population mismatch/directness:** Cessation study, not a timing-only curfew intervention.
