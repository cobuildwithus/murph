This source is included for **systematic_reviews_meta_analyses**.

## Extracted evidence fields

**Population:** Normal and pathological sleep populations across HRV literature.

**Intervention or exposure:** Sleep stages and sleep disorders as contexts for autonomic modulation; not caffeine exposure.

**Comparator or control:** Different sleep stages and normal versus pathological sleep contexts.

**Duration or follow-up:** Not applicable; narrative physiological review.

**Endpoints:**
  - HRV linear metrics
  - HRV nonlinear metrics
  - sympathetic/vagal balance
  - REM versus NREM autonomic patterns
  - insomnia-related autonomic patterns

**Effect estimates or direction:** Review describes REM sleep as involving sympathetic activity and vagal withdrawal, whereas NREM sleep shows the opposite trend; insomnia is described as sympathetic predominance and reduced vagal modulation.

**Adverse events or safety notes:** No caffeine-specific safety data; relevance is measurement interpretation.

**Limitations:** Measurement-context source only, not evidence that caffeine curfew changes HRV.

**Population mismatch:** No caffeine intervention; included for interpreting HRV/RHR signals in sleep experiments.

**Artifact candidates and rights status:** Open-access Frontiers article; verify license before storing copies.

**Findings:**
- `finding:pmid-24137133-sleep-stage-hrv-context` — The review describes sleep-stage-dependent autonomic patterns: REM sleep tends toward sympathetic activation and vagal withdrawal, while NREM sleep tends toward the opposite pattern.
- `finding:pmid-24137133-insomnia-autonomic-context` — The review describes insomnia as involving sympathetic predominance and reduced vagal modulation, which can confound interpretation of wearable HRV during sleep experiments.

**Why it matters:** Caffeine-curfew users may watch HRV or resting heart rate; this source warns that nocturnal HRV is sleep-stage and disorder dependent, not a simple caffeine-specific biomarker.

**Potential experiment signals:** HRV RMSSD, resting heart rate, sleep-stage distribution, deep sleep minutes, sleep fragmentation context.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use HRV/RHR as supportive context only; changes should be interpreted with sleep stages, insomnia symptoms, illness, stress, and measurement noise.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
