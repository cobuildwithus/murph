This source is included for **daytime_function_performance**.

**Findings:** On game night, players went to bed about 3 hours later and slept about 1.5 hours less than on pre-game nights; four players did not sleep after the game. Post-game salivary caffeine was higher than pre-game in most players; increases were moderately associated with longer sleep latency, lower sleep efficiency, and a trend toward shorter sleep duration.

**Why it matters:** Useful real-world athlete evidence that competition-day caffeine exposure can align with post-game sleep disruption, while preserving observational limits.

**Potential experiment signals:** salivary caffeine, bedtime, sleep onset latency, sleep efficiency, total sleep time.

**Protocol takeaway:** Context-only: reinforces caution about evening caffeine near bedtime, but cannot isolate caffeine causality.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
