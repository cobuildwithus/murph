## Extracted evidence fields

**Population:** 128 athletes across 10 studies, mostly male; combat, endurance, and team-sport contexts.

**Intervention or exposure:** Caffeine 3–6 mg/kg before late-afternoon or evening training/competition, with timing in extracted article examples from roughly 16:00 to 19:45.

**Comparator or control:** Placebo comparators in randomized crossover studies and high/low caffeine exposure comparisons in quasi-experimental designs.

**Duration or follow-up:** Overnight sleep after the late-day training or competition caffeine exposure.

**Endpoints:**
  - total sleep time
  - sleep efficiency
  - subjective sleep quality
  - sleep-onset latency
  - wake after sleep onset
  - actigraphy or PSG-derived sleep when available

**Effect estimates or direction:** Sleep efficiency meta-analysis: mean difference -4.87%, 95% CI -7.45 to -2.29, p=0.04, but not robust in leave-one-out sensitivity. Total sleep time: mean difference -32.47 minutes, 95% CI -69.93 to 4.99, p=0.08, I2=0%. Subjective sleep impairment was reported with 3–6 mg/kg caffeine.

**Adverse events or safety notes:** Sleep impairment and recovery tradeoff are the main safety/tolerability issues; no separate non-sleep adverse-event extraction was used.

**Limitations:** Small evidence base, mostly male athletes, heterogeneous sports/doses/timing, and GRADE certainty described as low to very low for some outcomes.

**Population mismatch:** Athletes taking high-dose caffeine for late training/competition differ from general Murph users doing a morning-only curfew reset.

**Artifact candidates and rights status:** Open-access article; source metadata can be included and full-text candidate can be listed with open-access rights.

**Findings:**
- `finding:pmid-41003623-athlete-evening-caffeine-se-tst` — The meta-analysis reported sleep efficiency lower by 4.87 percentage points, but the finding was not robust in leave-one-out sensitivity; total sleep time trended lower by 32.47 minutes but did not reach statistical significance.
- `finding:pmid-41003623-subjective-objective-disconnect` — The review noted subjective sleep impairment with 3–6 mg/kg caffeine even when objective sleep endpoints were mixed, suggesting user-reported recovery should be tracked alongside wearable or PSG-derived metrics.
- `finding:pmid-41003623-athlete-population-limitations` — The source is most applicable to performance-caffeine exceptions and less directly applicable to a general 14-day morning-curfew dose reset because participants, doses, and timing were sport-specific.

**Why it matters:** This source captures the tradeoff case where caffeine is intentionally taken late for performance, which is a key boundary for a sleep-protective caffeine curfew.

**Potential experiment signals:** sleep efficiency, total sleep time, subjective sleep quality, sleep-onset latency, next-day recovery perception.

**Protocol takeaway:** Late-day 3–6 mg/kg caffeine in athlete settings can impair subjective sleep and may reduce sleep efficiency, but objective effects are mixed and certainty is low; this supports treating late performance caffeine as a protocol exception rather than routine.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.
