This source is included for **clinical_safety_boundaries**.

**Findings:**
- `finding:pmid-20844077-preterm-birth-no-important-association`: The meta-analysis concluded there was no important association between caffeine intake during pregnancy and preterm birth in the included cohort and case-control evidence.
- `finding:pmid-20844077-pregnancy-endpoint-specificity`: This preterm-birth meta-analysis should be kept separate from fetal-growth and pregnancy-loss evidence so pregnancy safety language does not overstate uniform risk across outcomes.

**Why it matters:** Preterm birth evidence is more null than fetal-growth or pregnancy-loss evidence, preventing overbroad pregnancy caffeine claims.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- safety:pregnancy
- safety:preterm-birth

**Protocol takeaway:** Pregnancy language should be endpoint-specific and avoid claiming all pregnancy endpoints show the same risk pattern.
