## Extracted evidence fields

**Population:** Athletes in nine randomized controlled trials; exact participant count not available from accessible artifacts.

**Intervention or exposure:** Caffeine supplementation in athlete studies, with variation in dose and timing relative to exercise and sleep.

**Comparator or control:** Placebo/control conditions in randomized controlled trials.

**Duration or follow-up:** Varied across trials; sleep measured after supplementation periods or sessions.

**Endpoints:**
  - sleep quality
  - sleep quantity/duration
  - sleep efficiency
  - awakenings
  - sleep-onset latency
  - insomnia symptoms

**Effect estimates or direction:** The review reported varying results across sleep quality, quantity, efficiency, awakenings, and sleep-onset latency; factors included timing, exercise timing, habitual caffeine intake, and dose.

**Adverse events or safety notes:** Potential harm to sleep and recovery despite performance benefits; individual response emphasized.

**Limitations:** Athlete-only population, heterogeneous interventions/outcomes, and no single pooled effect estimate in the accessible abstract.

**Population mismatch:** Athletes and sport-performance caffeine use are adjacent rather than direct evidence for general caffeine-curfew users.

**Artifact candidates and rights status:** Clinical Nutrition ESPEN article appears open access/CC BY; verify before storing copies.

**Findings:**
- `finding:pmid-39551351-athlete-rct-heterogeneous-sleep-effects` — The systematic review found varied sleep effects across nine athlete RCTs, with heterogeneity linked to caffeine timing, exercise timing, habitual caffeine use, dose, and measurement method.
- `finding:pmid-39551351-performance-recovery-tradeoff` — The review frames caffeine as potentially performance-enhancing while also capable of harming sleep and recovery, supporting individualized rather than blanket advice in athlete contexts.

**Why it matters:** This population-mismatch review shows why performance-oriented caffeine supplementation should not be automatically generalized to everyday sleep-improvement protocols.

**Potential experiment signals:** sleep quality, sleep duration, sleep efficiency, sleep latency, night awakenings, recovery perception.

**Protocol takeaway:** Athlete trials are heterogeneous; timing relative to both exercise and sleep, dose, habitual caffeine use, and measurement method matter.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
