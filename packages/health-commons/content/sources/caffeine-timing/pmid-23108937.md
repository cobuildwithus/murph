This source is included for **withdrawal_tolerance_dose_reset**.

**Findings:** Withdrawal was associated with greater sleepiness, lower alertness, and poorer reaction-time/memory performance; caffeine reversed several deficits in medium/high consumers, but the extracted summary indicates little net mental benefit apart from sleepiness and motor speed effects.

**Why it matters:** Helps prevent overinterpreting caffeine’s daytime benefits as pure enhancement rather than withdrawal reversal.

**Potential experiment signals:** sleepiness, alertness, reaction time, memory, motor speed.

**Protocol takeaway:** During reset, analyze daytime performance separately from sleep outcomes and withdrawal relief.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Habitual caffeine consumers grouped by consumption level; exact sample size was not extracted.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Caffeine administration and caffeine withdrawal in relation to alertness and performance.
- **Comparator/control:** Caffeine versus placebo/withdrawal conditions and consumer groups.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Laboratory test day with morning and afternoon assessments; exact schedule not fully extracted.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** Withdrawal-related impairment can affect daytime function.
- **Limitations:** Performance-focused study, not a sleep-curfew protocol; no participant count extracted.
- **Population mismatch/directness:** Adjacent withdrawal-reversal/performance evidence.
