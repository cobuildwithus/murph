This source is included for **daytime_function_performance**.

**Findings:** Caffeinated beverages maintained critical flicker fusion threshold and sustained performance compared with water. Caffeinated beverage consumption had dose-dependent negative effects on sleep onset, sleep time, and sleep quality; lower-caffeine tea was described as less likely to disrupt sleep.

**Why it matters:** Naturalistic beverage evidence preserves the alertness benefit/sleep-cost tradeoff and cautions against treating tea, coffee, and caffeine capsules as identical.

**Potential experiment signals:** alertness, critical flicker fusion, sleep onset, sleep time, sleep quality, actigraphy.

**Protocol takeaway:** Context-only: supports the logic of avoiding day-long/evening caffeine when sleep is prioritized, while noting beverage and dose differences.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
