This source is included for **withdrawal_tolerance_dose_reset**.

**Findings:** Withdrawal severity increased with higher maintenance dose; 100 mg/day produced significant withdrawal; a substantial dose reduction was needed after 300 mg/day; withdrawal occurred after as little as 3 days of 300 mg/day and was somewhat greater after 7 or 14 days. A once-daily 300 mg schedule did not differ clearly from 100 mg three times daily for withdrawal.

**Why it matters:** It directly shows that the dose being reset can shape withdrawal during a curfew/dose-reset protocol.

**Potential experiment signals:** headache, fatigue, sleepiness, mood, protocol adherence.

**Protocol takeaway:** Warn participants that even modest habitual doses can produce withdrawal when intake is reduced; tapering or a symptom-tracking offramp may be needed.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Healthy adults in four independent double-blind experiments; habitual caffeine background was relevant to withdrawal induction.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Caffeine maintenance doses and dosing schedules followed by placebo substitution or dose reduction, including 100, 300, and 600 mg/day conditions.
- **Comparator/control:** Placebo substitution, lower caffeine doses, divided versus single daily dosing, and different maintenance durations.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Maintenance periods ranged from short exposure to 14 days before withdrawal assessment.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** Withdrawal symptoms are the relevant adverse effect when caffeine is stopped or sharply reduced.
- **Limitations:** Parametric withdrawal model rather than a sleep-curfew trial; exact sample sizes per experiment were not extracted in this batch.
- **Population mismatch/directness:** Adjacent dose-reduction/withdrawal evidence, not direct evidence for no caffeine after 10–11 a.m. or within 8 hours of bedtime.
