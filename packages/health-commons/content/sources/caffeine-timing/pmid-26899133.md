## Extracted evidence fields

**Population:** General adults and other human populations represented in epidemiological studies and RCTs; specific participant count was not available in the accessible artifacts.

**Intervention or exposure:** Coffee or caffeine exposure, including acute caffeine administration and habitual consumption patterns.

**Comparator or control:** Placebo, lower/no caffeine exposure, or epidemiologic comparisons by caffeine/coffee intake.

**Duration or follow-up:** Variable; includes acute pre-sleep caffeine trials and longer observational exposure windows.

**Endpoints:**
  - sleep-onset latency
  - total sleep time
  - sleep efficiency
  - perceived sleep quality
  - sleep architecture in experimental studies

**Effect estimates or direction:** Narrative systematic synthesis reported caffeine typically prolonged sleep latency, reduced total sleep time and sleep efficiency, and worsened perceived sleep quality; extracted artifacts did not provide a pooled effect estimate.

**Adverse events or safety notes:** Sleep disruption is the main safety/tolerability concern for this protocol context; no separate adverse-event table was extracted.

**Limitations:** Mixed evidence sources, heterogeneous populations, doses, and timing; review does not directly test the exact 14-day curfew protocol.

**Population mismatch:** Mostly generalizable to caffeine timing, but not a Murph-specific N-of-1 protocol and not limited to morning-only caffeine.

**Artifact candidates and rights status:** Publisher article/paywalled; ZORA metadata and accepted manuscript information exist but should be handled conservatively.

**Findings:**
- `finding:pmid-26899133-typical-sleep-disruption` — The systematic review reported that caffeine typically prolonged sleep latency, reduced total sleep time and sleep efficiency, and worsened perceived sleep quality across the reviewed human literature.
- `finding:pmid-26899133-heterogeneous-dose-timing-context` — The evidence base includes heterogeneous doses, exposure windows, and study designs, so it supports the general caffeine-timing rationale more strongly than it defines one universal cutoff time.

**Why it matters:** This review is a broad backbone source for the central claim that caffeine exposure can worsen sleep timing and continuity outcomes.

**Potential experiment signals:** sleep-onset latency, total sleep time, sleep efficiency, subjective sleep quality, daytime sleepiness when reported.

**Protocol takeaway:** Reducing late caffeine exposure is biologically and clinically plausible because caffeine typically worsened latency, duration, efficiency, and perceived sleep quality across the reviewed literature.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.
