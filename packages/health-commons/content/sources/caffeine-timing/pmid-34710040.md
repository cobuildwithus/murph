This source is included for **adjacent_curfew_morning_evening_context**.

**Findings:** In adolescents, higher daily caffeine was associated with longer SOL, shorter TST, lower sleep efficiency, and less REM, with effects driven by afternoon/evening caffeine timing.

**Why it matters:** It is one of the stronger adjacent timing sources because it combines daily caffeine timing with objective EEG sleep in adolescents.

**Potential experiment signals:** Sleep-onset latency; Total sleep time; Sleep efficiency; REM sleep.

**Protocol takeaway:** Adjacent/population-mismatch evidence: supports concern about afternoon/evening caffeine but cannot directly generalize to adults or a 10-11am/8-hour 14-day protocol.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
