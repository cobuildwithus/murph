This source is included for **same_mechanism_context**.

**Findings:** Caffeine increased awake/drowsy sleep activity and reduced total sleep time and slow-wave sleep, with subjective impaired sleep reported across doses in an older pharmacology study.

**Why it matters:** It provides foundational human evidence that caffeine can disrupt sleep architecture and subjective sleep.

**Potential experiment signals:** Total sleep time; Slow-wave sleep; REM sleep duration; Subjective sleep impairment.

**Protocol takeaway:** Mechanistic/foundational context only; not a lifestyle curfew or 14-day intervention.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
