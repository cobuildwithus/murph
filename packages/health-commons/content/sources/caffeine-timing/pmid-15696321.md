This source is included for **withdrawal_tolerance_dose_reset**.

**Findings:** Psychostimulant performance/mood effects appeared reliably only after 8 hours of abstinence; caffeine increased blood pressure after 8 hours and had some adverse psychomotor/task-demand effects after 4 hours.

**Why it matters:** It makes the protocol's “within 8 hours” rule plausible as a meaningful abstinence interval, while also showing that effects can be mixed.

**Potential experiment signals:** alertness, mood, blood pressure, hand steadiness, daytime performance.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as adjacent evidence: an 8-hour caffeine gap can change performance/mood and physiology, but it does not prove a bedtime-curfew sleep benefit.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Forty-nine moderate to moderate-high caffeine consumers, including 27 women, with mean daily intake around 370 mg/day.
- **Intervention/exposure:** A caffeine dose approximately equivalent to a second cup after 4, 6, or 8 hours of abstinence; pre-dose caffeine was given earlier in the day.
- **Comparator/control:** Placebo under the same 4-, 6-, or 8-hour abstinence intervals.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Single-day laboratory testing after overnight abstinence and same-day dosing intervals.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** Pressor response after 8-hour abstinence and hand-steadiness/task-demand effects at shorter intervals were reported.
- **Limitations:** Same-day lab dosing, not bedtime sleep outcomes or a 14-day curfew; moderate/high consumers may not represent low consumers.
- **Population mismatch/directness:** Directly adjacent to an 8-hour interval rule but focused on daytime performance and pressor effects, not sleep-protection after bedtime.
