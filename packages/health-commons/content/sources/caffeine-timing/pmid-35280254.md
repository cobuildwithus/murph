This source is included for **pharmacology_individual_differences**.

**Findings:** The systematic analysis assembled data from 141 publications, 500 groups, and 4,714 individuals and documented substantial inter-individual caffeine pharmacokinetic variability relevant to bedtime residual exposure. Across the pharmacokinetic synthesis, smoking consistently increased caffeine clearance/decreased half-life, while oral contraceptive use showed the opposite pattern; caffeine dose had no clear clearance or half-life effect over a wide dose range.

**Why it matters:** It anchors the 8-hour rule in pharmacokinetic variability: some users will still have meaningful caffeine exposure near bedtime because clearance differs by smoking, hormones, disease, medication, and other factors.

**Potential experiment signals:** caffeine cutoff adherence, sleep-onset latency, sleep efficiency, caffeine side-effect log.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as high-priority pharmacology context for individualizing the curfew window and flagging confounders; it is not a direct protocol trial.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.
