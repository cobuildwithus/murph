This source is included for **direct_protocol_and_dose_timing**.

**Findings:** A 100 mg dose did not significantly affect subsequent sleep when consumed up to four hours before bedtime, while 400 mg delayed sleep initiation and altered architecture within 12 hours and increased fragmentation within 8 hours; subjective sleep-quality effects were clearest at four hours. Accessible full-text summaries report 400 mg caffeine exceeded clinically meaningful thresholds for sleep-onset latency within 12 hours and wake-after-sleep-onset/sleep-efficiency within 8 hours; total-sleep-time reduction was most clearly significant at four hours.

**Why it matters:** Directly maps caffeine dose and timing to sleep outcomes, including the protocol’s 8-hour bedtime boundary and dose-reset logic.

**Potential experiment signals:** total sleep time, sleep onset latency, wake after sleep onset, sleep efficiency, N3 sleep, REM sleep, subjective sleep quality, presleep alertness

**Protocol takeaway:** A 100 mg dose may be tolerated closer to bed in this young male sample, but a 400 mg dose should be avoided within 12 hours; an 8-hour curfew is best framed as a cautious, dose-sensitive boundary rather than universal truth.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.
