This source is included for **caffeine_source_dose_audit**.

**Findings:** This analytical study reported that caffeine content in tested dietary supplements was 4%-35% higher than declared in most cases, supporting caution around supplement label accuracy; product count and full distribution were not extracted here.

**Why it matters:** Recent supplement analysis supports conservative handling of declared caffeine amounts when supplement labels may understate actual caffeine.

**Potential experiment signals:** supplement caffeine dose, source fidelity, adverse caffeine symptoms.

**Protocol takeaway:** When supplement caffeine is declared, note that analytical studies can find label discrepancies; consider conservative uncertainty for supplements.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.
