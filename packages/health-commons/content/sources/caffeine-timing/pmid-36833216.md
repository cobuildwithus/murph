This source is included for **clinical_safety_boundaries**.

**Findings:**
- `finding:pmid-36833216-caffeine-dose-child-sleep-duration`: Higher daily caffeine dose was associated with lower odds of children reporting more than nine hours of sleep, preserving a pediatric dose-sleep signal.
- `finding:pmid-36833216-genotype-null`: The study did not detect associations for the studied ADORA2A/CYP1A variants or genotype-by-caffeine interactions with sleep outcomes, preserving a null genotype-moderation result.

**Why it matters:** Provides a pediatric boundary: caffeine dose may affect sleep duration, but adult curfew guidance should not be automatically applied to children.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- endpoint:sleep-duration
- endpoint:sleep-quality
- population:pediatric
- context:genotype-moderation

**Protocol takeaway:** Do not treat the adult caffeine curfew protocol as pediatric advice; child caffeine use needs separate guardian/clinician guidance.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
