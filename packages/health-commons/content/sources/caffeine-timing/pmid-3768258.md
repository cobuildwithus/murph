This source is included for **clinical_safety_boundaries**.

**Findings:** On average, 56.8% of the administered caffeine dose was recovered in urine in both groups; pregnant subjects produced smaller amounts of 1-methylxanthine and 1-methyluric acid and tended toward greater excretion of some other metabolites.

**Why it matters:** Pregnancy alters caffeine metabolism and requires stricter safety guidance than a general adult curfew.

**Potential experiment signals:** Urinary caffeine dose recovery; Caffeine metabolite profile; Pregnancy-related metabolism differences.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use only to support pregnancy as a special-population caution; rely on current guidelines for dose limits.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.
