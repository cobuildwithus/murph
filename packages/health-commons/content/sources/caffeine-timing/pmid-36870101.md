## Extracted evidence fields

**Population:** Human participants from 24 studies of caffeine exposure before subsequent sleep; detailed participant count not available in accessible artifacts.

**Intervention or exposure:** Caffeine intake before sleep, including coffee-like doses and higher-dose pre-workout caffeine.

**Comparator or control:** Placebo or no-caffeine control conditions in included experimental studies.

**Duration or follow-up:** Subsequent nighttime sleep after caffeine exposure; timing modeled relative to bedtime.

**Endpoints:**
  - total sleep time
  - sleep efficiency
  - sleep-onset latency
  - wake after sleep onset
  - N1/light sleep
  - deep sleep / slow-wave sleep

**Effect estimates or direction:** Meta-analysis reported total sleep time -45 minutes, sleep efficiency -7%, sleep-onset latency +9 minutes, wake after sleep onset +12 minutes, N1 +6.1 minutes/+1.7%, and deep sleep -11.4 minutes/-1.4%. Dose/timing modeling suggested 107 mg coffee at least 8.8 hours before bedtime and 217.5 mg pre-workout at least 13.2 hours before bedtime to avoid total-sleep-time reductions.

**Adverse events or safety notes:** Sleep loss and sleep-architecture disruption are the relevant safety/tolerability outcomes; no broader adverse-event synthesis was extracted.

**Limitations:** Timing estimates are modeled from available studies and do not prove that every individual needs the same cutoff; exact 14-day behavior-change protocols were not tested.

**Population mismatch:** Direct for caffeine timing and sleep endpoints, but still not a Murph-specific N-of-1 curfew/dose-reset trial.

**Artifact candidates and rights status:** Publisher PDF/full text appears paywalled; keep as metadata/abstract candidate only.

**Findings:**
- `finding:pmid-36870101-quantified-sleep-disruption` — The meta-analysis reported worse subsequent sleep after caffeine: total sleep time decreased by about 45 minutes, sleep efficiency by about 7%, sleep-onset latency increased by about 9 minutes, wake after sleep onset increased by about 12 minutes, N1/light sleep increased, and deep sleep decreased.
- `finding:pmid-36870101-dose-timing-buffer-estimates` — Dose/timing modeling suggested that a 107 mg coffee dose should be consumed at least 8.8 hours before bedtime and a 217.5 mg pre-workout dose at least 13.2 hours before bedtime to avoid total-sleep-time reductions.
- `finding:pmid-36870101-individualization-limits` — The synthesis strongly supports a timing rationale but its modeled cutoffs should be individualized because the included studies varied in dose, caffeine source, population, and sleep measurement.

**Why it matters:** This is the most directly useful quantitative synthesis for choosing an 8-hour-or-longer bedtime buffer and explaining why higher caffeine doses may need earlier cutoffs.

**Potential experiment signals:** total sleep time, sleep efficiency, sleep-onset latency, wake after sleep onset, deep sleep minutes, light sleep / N1 minutes.

**Protocol takeaway:** An 8-hour bedtime buffer is plausible for lower caffeine doses, but higher doses or pre-workout caffeine may require substantially earlier curfews; the 10–11am option is a conservative way to operationalize that for many bedtimes.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.
