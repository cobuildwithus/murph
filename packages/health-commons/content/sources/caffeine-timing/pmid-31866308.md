This source is included for **withdrawal_tolerance_dose_reset**.

**Findings:** The study examined adaptation of sleep-wake regulation after repeated daytime caffeine intake and withdrawal; extracted metadata supports adaptation/withdrawal context but not a direct curfew effect estimate.

**Why it matters:** Relevant to the 14-day reset idea because repeated intake can lead to adaptation.

**Potential experiment signals:** sleep-wake regulation, adaptation, withdrawal symptoms, circadian context.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as mechanistic context for adaptation over days, with population limits.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Twenty young male habitual caffeine consumers.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Repeated daytime caffeine, 150 mg three times daily for 10 days, placebo for 10 days, and withdrawal condition.
- **Comparator/control:** Placebo and withdrawal conditions in a double-blind within-subject design.
- **Duration/follow-up:** 10-day repeated-intake periods plus withdrawal condition.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** Repeated caffeine and withdrawal may alter sleep-wake regulation in ways that complicate reset interpretation.
- **Limitations:** Young male sample; repeated 3-dose daytime regimen, not a morning-only curfew; detailed results not extracted.
- **Population mismatch/directness:** Mechanistic repeated-intake adaptation evidence, not direct protocol evidence.
