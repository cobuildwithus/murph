This source is included for **withdrawal_tolerance_dose_reset**.

**Findings:** Most treatment seekers had prior serious attempts to modify caffeine use, many had medical advice to reduce, and large proportions endorsed withdrawal, unsuccessful reduction, and use despite problems; headache and functional impairment from withdrawal were common in the extracted summary.

**Why it matters:** Identifies users for whom dose reset may be hard or clinically relevant.

**Potential experiment signals:** problematic caffeine use, withdrawal impairment, headache, failed attempts to reduce, medical advice to reduce.

**Protocol takeaway:** Add a safety boundary for users with severe withdrawal, repeated failed attempts, or medical reasons to reduce caffeine.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Ninety-four individuals seeking treatment for caffeine dependence; mean age about 41 years, 55% women, average intake about 548 mg/day.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Problematic caffeine use and attempts to reduce/quit.
- **Comparator/control:** No intervention comparator; treatment-seeker characterization.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Baseline characterization; not an intervention follow-up.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** This population may need clinical support rather than self-guided curfew alone.
- **Limitations:** Treatment-seeking sample is not representative of ordinary wellness participants; observational baseline characterization.
- **Population mismatch/directness:** Clinical boundary source, not direct curfew efficacy evidence.
