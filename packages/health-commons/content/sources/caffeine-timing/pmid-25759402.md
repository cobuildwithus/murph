This source is included for **direct_protocol_and_dose_timing**.

**Findings:** Compared with young adults, middle-aged adults were generally more sensitive to high-dose caffeine effects on sleep quantity and quality; caffeine produced dose-dependent changes in lighter sleep, slow-wave sleep, and REM sleep.

**Why it matters:** Direct safety/boundary evidence that older/middle-aged users may be more sensitive to high caffeine doses.

**Potential experiment signals:** total sleep time, sleep latency, sleep efficiency, stage 1 sleep, slow-wave sleep, REM sleep, quantitative EEG

**Protocol takeaway:** Dose and age should be part of the curfew interpretation; a one-size cutoff may be insufficient for high-dose middle-aged users.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.
