This source is included for **clinical_safety_boundaries**.

**Findings:**
- `finding:pmid-11503005-pregnancy-cyp1a2-reduced`: CYP1A2 activity ratios were significantly lower during early, middle, and late pregnancy than after delivery, indicating pregnancy-associated reduction in CYP1A2 caffeine metabolism.
- `finding:pmid-11503005-nat2-change-xo-null`: NAT2 activity showed a pregnancy-stage difference, while xanthine oxidase activity did not differ significantly; caffeine-related pregnancy changes were not uniform across all measured metabolic pathways.

**Why it matters:** Pregnancy can slow caffeine metabolism, so ordinary timing and dose assumptions may be unsafe or misleading during pregnancy.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- biomarker:caffeine-clearance
- safety:pregnancy
- biomarker:cyp1a2-activity

**Protocol takeaway:** Treat pregnancy as a safety boundary requiring clinician guidance and more conservative caffeine decisions rather than ordinary self-experimentation.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.
