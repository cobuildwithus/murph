This source is included for **pharmacology_individual_differences**.

**Findings:** The study assessed 185 healthy Caucasian nonsmokers and 51 smokers with caffeine phenotyping and reported genotype-related differences in caffeine metabolic ratios among smokers, supporting CYP1A2 genotype plus smoking status as metabolism modifiers.

**Why it matters:** CYP1A2 activity is a plausible reason two people using the same caffeine curfew can have different residual caffeine exposure near bedtime.

**Potential experiment signals:** caffeine metabolic ratio, sleep-onset latency, sleep efficiency.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context for individual differences in caffeine metabolism and smoking/genotype interactions, not as direct evidence that a 14-day curfew improves sleep.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
