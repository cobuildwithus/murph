This source is included for **daytime_function_performance**.

**Findings:** Before recovery sleep, caffeine increased core body temperature, widened distal-proximal gradient, and increased subjective alertness and clear-headedness. Caffeine disturbed daytime recovery sleep 5 hours later, with signals including longer sleep latency/WASO and lower sleep efficiency, slow-wave sleep, and total sleep time.

**Why it matters:** Useful for shift-work caveats and physiologic plausibility that caffeine can remain sleep-disruptive even several hours before a recovery sleep episode.

**Potential experiment signals:** core body temperature, skin temperature, distal-proximal gradient, alertness, sleep onset latency, sleep efficiency, wake after sleep onset, slow-wave sleep.

**Protocol takeaway:** Context-only/safety-boundary: supports using a relative-to-sleep caffeine cutoff for recovery sleep, not just a fixed clock cutoff.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
