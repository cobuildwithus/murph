This source is included for **daytime_function_performance**.

**Findings:** Caffeine maintained objective alertness compared with placebo across the first 3 sleep-restriction days, but the effect was no longer evident by the fourth day. The study title and abstracted interpretation identify recovery costs after repeated caffeine during sleep restriction, including slower return to baseline alertness/wakefulness during recovery.

**Why it matters:** Shows that daytime caffeine benefits may fade across chronic sleep restriction and may carry recovery tradeoffs.

**Potential experiment signals:** psychomotor vigilance, maintenance of wakefulness, daytime sleepiness, deep sleep, recovery sleep.

**Protocol takeaway:** Context-only: supports not relying on caffeine to compensate for accumulating sleep debt; does not evaluate stopping caffeine after late morning.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
