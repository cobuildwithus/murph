This source is included for **withdrawal_tolerance_dose_reset**.

**Findings:** Caffeine withdrawal increased cerebral blood flow velocity/changed vascular indices, increased EEG theta, and was associated with subjective heaviness and decreased ability to concentrate; caffeine and baseline were more similar in the extracted summary.

**Why it matters:** Provides a plausible physiology link for subjective reset symptoms.

**Potential experiment signals:** concentration, headache proxy, fatigue/heaviness, EEG, cerebral blood flow.

**Protocol takeaway:** Explain that early concentration/fatigue symptoms may reflect acute withdrawal physiology.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Ten volunteers with mean habitual caffeine intake around 333 mg/day.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Cessation of daily caffeine with placebo substitution, and caffeine administration comparison.
- **Comparator/control:** Baseline/caffeine condition compared with placebo withdrawal condition.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Acute withdrawal assessment after caffeine cessation; exact hours not extracted here.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** Physiological withdrawal changes may underlie headache, concentration problems, and fatigue-like symptoms.
- **Limitations:** Small mechanistic sample, not a sleep-curfew trial.
- **Population mismatch/directness:** Mechanistic withdrawal physiology only.
