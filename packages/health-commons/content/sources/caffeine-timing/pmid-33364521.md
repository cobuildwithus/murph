This source is included for **withdrawal_tolerance_dose_reset**.

**Findings:** Caffeine maintained some vigilance/attention and motor outcomes during prolonged sleep deprivation, but performance still declined relative to baseline and some advantages were time-limited in the extracted summary.

**Why it matters:** Provides a countervailing context: caffeine can be performance-supportive in acute sleep-loss states, but this is not a sleep-protection argument.

**Potential experiment signals:** PVT lapses, vigilance, attention, manual dexterity, sleep deprivation.

**Protocol takeaway:** For sleep-deprived or safety-critical users, curfew advice may need context-specific caution rather than blanket caffeine avoidance.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Participants undergoing 77 hours of total sleep deprivation; exact sample size not extracted.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Multiple caffeine doses across prolonged total sleep deprivation.
- **Comparator/control:** Placebo/control condition during sleep deprivation.
- **Duration/follow-up:** 77 hours of total sleep deprivation.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** Extreme sleep deprivation is a special operational context; caffeine benefits do not replace sleep.
- **Limitations:** Prolonged total sleep deprivation, not ordinary sleep hygiene or curfew use.
- **Population mismatch/directness:** Adjacent daytime-function evidence only.
