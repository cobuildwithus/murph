## Extracted evidence fields

**Population:** Adults or mixed participants in included caffeine-abstinence sleep studies; exact participant count not available from accessible artifacts.

**Intervention or exposure:** Caffeine abstinence, caffeine reduction, or decaffeinated days.

**Comparator or control:** Usual caffeine intake, caffeine-containing days, or non-abstinence comparison periods depending on the included study.

**Duration or follow-up:** Varied across the two qualifying studies; not enough detail available to standardize duration.

**Endpoints:**
  - sleep quality
  - sleep duration / total sleep time
  - difficulty falling asleep / sleep onset
  - sleep efficiency when measured

**Effect estimates or direction:** Accessible extraction reported one study with longer sleep duration (p<0.01) and better sleep quality (p<0.05) with abstinence, and another with less difficulty falling asleep on decaffeinated days (p<0.05); no pooled effect estimate was available.

**Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse-event signal was extracted from the accessible abstract-level artifacts.

**Limitations:** Only two qualifying studies were described in the accessible extraction, power was limited, and robust evidence was not established.

**Population mismatch:** Direct-adjacent for a curfew/reset protocol because the evidence concerns broader abstinence rather than a 10–11am cutoff, an 8-hour bedtime buffer, or a 14-day N-of-1 protocol.

**Artifact candidates and rights status:** paywalled; no copyrighted PDF should be committed.

**Findings:**
- `finding:pmid-19120728-caffeine-abstinence-sleep-quality` — The review found small-study evidence that caffeine abstinence or decaffeinated days were associated with longer sleep duration, better sleep quality, or less difficulty falling asleep in the included studies, but no reliable pooled estimate was available.
- `finding:pmid-19120728-limited-review-base` — The accessible extraction indicates only two qualifying higher-quality studies, so the review supports plausibility of a reset approach but should not be used as high-certainty proof of a specific curfew timing rule.

**Why it matters:** This is an abstinence-focused systematic review that directly informs the dose-reset part of a caffeine-curfew experiment, while also showing how thin the older trial base was.

**Potential experiment signals:** sleep-onset latency or difficulty falling asleep, total sleep time / sleep duration, subjective sleep quality, sleep efficiency when available.

**Protocol takeaway:** A caffeine pause or whole-day abstinence could improve subjective sleep quality or sleep duration in some small studies, but the review should be treated as supportive and limited rather than definitive evidence for a specific 10–11am or 8-hour curfew.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.
