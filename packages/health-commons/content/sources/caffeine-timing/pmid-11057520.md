This source is included for **withdrawal_tolerance_dose_reset**.

**Findings:** Low caffeine doses affected cognitive performance and mood after overnight abstinence, with effects more marked in higher habitual consumers; caffeine increased thirst only in low consumers in the extracted summary.

**Why it matters:** Helps interpret whether smaller morning doses still matter during a reset.

**Potential experiment signals:** mood, cognitive performance, thirst, habitual intake subgroup.

**Protocol takeaway:** Even low doses can affect mood/performance after abstinence; log dose amount, not just timing.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Twenty-three low and higher habitual caffeine consumers after overnight abstinence.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Low doses of caffeine after overnight abstinence.
- **Comparator/control:** Placebo and different low caffeine doses; low versus higher consumer groups.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Single testing after overnight abstinence.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** Thirst and subgroup-specific responses show that low-dose effects are not uniform.
- **Limitations:** Overnight-abstinence performance/mood study; not a sleep-curfew trial.
- **Population mismatch/directness:** Adjacent dose-reset/subgroup context.
