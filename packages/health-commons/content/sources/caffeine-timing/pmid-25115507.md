This source is included for **daytime_function_performance**.

**Findings:** Caffeine improved cycling time-trial performance (19.7 ± 3.3 vs 20.5 ± 3.5 min; p=0.006) and lowered rating of perceived exertion (p=0.004). Caffeine increased sleep latency (51.1 ± 34.7 vs 10.2 ± 4.2 min; p=0.028) and reduced sleep efficiency, REM sleep, and total sleep time versus placebo.

**Why it matters:** Directly illustrates a performance-versus-sleep tradeoff after late-day caffeine in athletes.

**Potential experiment signals:** exercise performance, sleep onset latency, sleep efficiency, REM sleep, total sleep time, rating of perceived exertion.

**Protocol takeaway:** Context-only: adjacent evidence that late-day caffeine can improve performance while impairing sleep, but not a test of the Murph caffeine curfew.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
