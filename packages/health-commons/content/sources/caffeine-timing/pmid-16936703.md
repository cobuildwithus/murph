This source is included for **daytime_function_performance**.

**Findings:** Caffeine lengthened sleep latency, increased stage 1, and reduced stage 2 and slow-wave sleep in both nocturnal and daytime recovery sleep groups. Caffeine effects were more marked on daytime recovery sleep, including stronger sleep-efficiency reduction and decreases in sleep duration and REM sleep.

**Why it matters:** Boundary evidence that circadian timing and recovery-sleep state may change sensitivity to caffeine before sleep.

**Potential experiment signals:** sleep onset latency, sleep efficiency, total sleep time, REM sleep, slow-wave sleep, sleep stages.

**Protocol takeaway:** Context-only/safety-boundary: supports avoiding caffeine close to any intended sleep episode, especially daytime recovery sleep.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
