This source is included for **systematic_reviews_meta_analyses**.

## Extracted evidence fields

**Population:** General populations in caffeine, sleep, and daytime sleepiness studies; includes adults and younger consumers.

**Intervention or exposure:** Daily dietary caffeine and acute caffeine intake.

**Comparator or control:** Lower/no caffeine or comparisons across intake levels depending on cited studies.

**Duration or follow-up:** Varied; narrative review rather than a single intervention period.

**Endpoints:**
  - disturbed sleep
  - daytime sleepiness
  - alertness
  - sleep quality
  - sleep duration

**Effect estimates or direction:** Narrative synthesis reported that daily dietary caffeine intake is associated with disturbed sleep and daytime sleepiness; no pooled effect estimate was extracted.

**Adverse events or safety notes:** Daytime sleepiness and disturbed sleep are the main tolerability concerns for the curfew/reset context.

**Limitations:** Narrative review; not a direct test of a morning caffeine curfew.

**Population mismatch:** Same-mechanism context only; it does not provide direct protocol effect estimates.

**Artifact candidates and rights status:** Publisher full text appears paywalled/non-redistributable.

**Findings:**
- `finding:pmid-17950009-sleep-sleepiness-loop` — The review describes a loop in which caffeine is used to counter daytime sleepiness while regular intake is also associated with disturbed sleep and daytime sleepiness.
- `finding:pmid-17950009-track-daytime-sleepiness` — For a caffeine-curfew reset, daytime sleepiness should be treated as an important outcome and safety/adherence signal, not simply an expected inconvenience.

**Why it matters:** The protocol trades off daytime alertness from caffeine against nighttime sleep protection; this source frames that bidirectional loop.

**Potential experiment signals:** daytime sleepiness, sleep quality, sleep-onset latency, total sleep time, morning alertness, caffeine craving.

**Protocol takeaway:** Caffeine can be both a response to sleepiness and a contributor to disturbed sleep, so the protocol should measure daytime sleepiness as a secondary outcome rather than assuming sleep gains are symptom-free.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
