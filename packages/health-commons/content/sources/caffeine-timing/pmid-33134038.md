This source is included for **systematic_reviews_meta_analyses**.

**Findings:** This systematic review found seven eligible studies using commercially available sleep trackers after screening 842 records, indicating a small evidence base and supporting cautious interpretation of consumer sleep metrics.

**Why it matters:** Supports careful interpretation of consumer wearable sleep metrics used in a 14-day caffeine-curfew self-experiment.

**Potential experiment signals:** Sleep duration, sleep efficiency estimate, sleep-stage estimates.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as wearable-method context: consumer sleep data are useful for self-tracking but not equivalent to polysomnography.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
