This source is included for **withdrawal_tolerance_dose_reset**.

**Findings:** Caffeine improved sustained attention and mental alertness when participants were caffeine-deprived, but effects were not observed when participants were no longer deprived.

**Why it matters:** It shows that the apparent benefit of caffeine can depend on whether the person is in withdrawal.

**Potential experiment signals:** alertness, sustained attention, fatigue, withdrawal symptoms.

**Protocol takeaway:** Separate sleep improvements from changes in daytime alertness caused by removal or reversal of withdrawal.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Moderate caffeine consumers tested under different abstinence levels; exact sample size was not extracted in this batch.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Caffeine administration after varying levels of caffeine abstinence/deprivation.
- **Comparator/control:** Caffeine versus placebo under deprived and non-deprived states.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Abstinence level varied before testing; exact durations were not fully extracted in this batch.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No specific adverse event signal was extracted; withdrawal-reversal confounding is the main caution.
- **Limitations:** Performance/mood study rather than sleep-curfew trial; exact abstinence schedule and sample size not extracted here.
- **Population mismatch/directness:** Adjacent abstinence-duration evidence; does not test no caffeine after morning or before bedtime.
