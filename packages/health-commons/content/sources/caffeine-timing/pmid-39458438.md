This source is included for **withdrawal_tolerance_dose_reset**.

**Findings:** Caffeine reduced recovery total sleep time by about 30.2 ± 8.2 minutes and N3 by about 35.6 ± 23.2 minutes, and altered sleep continuity, stability, organization, and NREM delta power in the extracted summary.

**Why it matters:** Relevant to “within 8 hours of bedtime” because caffeine about 6.5 hours before recovery sleep still changed sleep outcomes in a vulnerable state.

**Potential experiment signals:** total sleep time, deep sleep/N3, sleep continuity, long awakenings, NREM delta power.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as boundary evidence supporting caution for sleep-deprived users; do not generalize the exact effect size to ordinary nights.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Participants studied after total sleep deprivation/recovery sleep; exact sample size not extracted.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Approximately 350 mg/day caffeine intake, including caffeine about 6.5 hours before bedtime, during a total sleep deprivation/recovery-sleep protocol.
- **Comparator/control:** Placebo/control condition during recovery sleep.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Recovery sleep after approximately 38 hours of continuous wakefulness.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** Caffeine before recovery sleep after deprivation can impair recovery sleep architecture.
- **Limitations:** Sleep-deprived recovery-sleep state, not ordinary nightly curfew; sample count not extracted.
- **Population mismatch/directness:** Boundary evidence for sleep-deprived recovery sleep rather than normal home sleep.
