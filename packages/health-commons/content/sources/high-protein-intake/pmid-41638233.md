## Extracted findings

- **finding:pmid-41638233-metabolome-stability** — The high-protein arm showed modest metabolite shifts, with two metabolites increased and 98 reduced versus baseline, but the authors did not find disruption of global metabolic homeostasis.
- **finding:pmid-41638233-renal-liver-marker-boundary** — Urea increased as expected with higher amino acid intake but remained within the reference range; creatinine, uric acid, and bilirubin-related interpretation did not indicate impaired kidney or liver function in this trial.
- **finding:pmid-41638233-target-achievement-context** — The high-protein group increased intake to 1.6 g/kg/day; this supports feasibility of a protein floor but also shows that a nominal 2.0 g/kg/day target may be difficult to fully achieve with food-based implementation.

## Protocol appraisal

- **evidence_appraisal:protein-floor-high-protein-intake:pmid-41638233** — Older adults achieved about 1.6 g/kg/day with modest metabolomic shifts and no renal/liver disruption signal. Implication: Supports using renal/liver labs and metabolomic or cardiometabolic markers as safety monitors in a protein-floor experiment.
