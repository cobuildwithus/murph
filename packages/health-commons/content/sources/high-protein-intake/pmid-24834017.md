## Extracted findings

- **finding:pmid-24834017-extreme-protein-body-composition-null** — The high-protein group consumed about 4.4 g/kg/day versus 1.8 g/kg/day in control, with no significant changes over time or between groups in body weight, fat mass, fat-free mass, or percent body fat.
- **finding:pmid-24834017-training-volume-maintained** — Training volume did not change in either group, supporting interpretation that the body-composition null result reflects added protein/calories without a training-volume cointervention.
- **finding:pmid-24834017-safety-lab-gap** — Because the study emphasized body composition, food intake, and training volume rather than renal, cardiometabolic, or adverse-event endpoints, it should not be used as comprehensive safety proof for extreme protein intake.

## Protocol appraisal

- **evidence_appraisal:protein-floor-high-protein-intake:pmid-24834017** — Extreme 4.4 g/kg/day protein did not improve body composition in young resistance-trained adults. Implication: Useful to avoid overselling protein beyond the floor; very high intake is not evidence of added benefit or comprehensive safety.
