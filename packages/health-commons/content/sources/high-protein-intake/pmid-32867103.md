## Extracted findings

- **finding:pmid-32867103-rt-protein-no-added-strength** — Across 657 participants, adding protein supplementation to resistance training did not produce statistically significant advantages for upper-limb strength (SMD 0.56; 95% CI -0.09 to 1.21), lower-limb strength (SMD 0.00; 95% CI -0.18 to 0.18), handgrip strength (SMD 0.03; 95% CI -0.26 to 0.32), or physical-performance outcomes compared with resistance training alone or placebo.

## Protocol appraisal

- **evidence_appraisal:pmid-32867103-protein-floor-resistance_training_athlete_body_composition_adjacent_2** — Protein supplementation did not add clear strength or performance gains on top of resistance training in healthy older adults. Implication: Supports separating protein-floor adequacy from claims that supplementation necessarily amplifies resistance-training outcomes.
