## Extracted findings

- **finding:pmid-32232404-lbm-benefit** — Across 65 studies with 2907 participants, protein supplementation improved lean body mass in adults (mean difference 0.62 kg; 95% CI 0.36 to 0.88) and older adults (0.46 kg; 95% CI 0.23 to 0.70).
- **finding:pmid-32232404-strength-timing-unclear** — Pooled handgrip and leg-press effects were not clearly beneficial, muscle-synthesis data were scarce and inconclusive, and subgroup analyses did not show a beneficial effect of specific protein timing on lean body mass or strength.

## Protocol appraisal

- **evidence_appraisal:pmid-32232404-protein-floor-resistance_training_athlete_body_composition_adjacent_2** — Protein supplementation improved lean body mass, but timing and strength outcomes were not clearly confirmed. Implication: Supports a cautious daily-protein-first framing for body composition while keeping timing claims and strength promises bounded.
