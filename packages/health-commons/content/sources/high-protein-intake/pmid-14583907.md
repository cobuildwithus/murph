## Extracted findings

- **finding:pmid-14583907-high-protein-pregnancy-sga-safety** — High-protein supplementation produced no fetal-growth benefit signal and was associated with significantly increased small-for-gestational-age birth, with nonsignificant lower mean birthweight and nonsignificant higher neonatal death.
- **finding:pmid-14583907-balanced-energy-protein-context** — Balanced energy/protein supplementation was associated with modest increases in maternal weight gain and birthweight, substantial reduction in small-for-gestational-age birth, and reduced stillbirth/neonatal-death risks, without a detected preterm-birth effect.
- **finding:pmid-14583907-protein-alone-not-beneficial** — The review concluded that high-protein or balanced-protein supplementation alone was not beneficial and may be harmful to the infant.

## Protocol appraisal

- **evidence_appraisal:protein-floor-high-protein-intake:pmid-14583907** — Older Cochrane pregnancy review flags high-protein supplementation as no-benefit and potentially harmful. Implication: Pregnancy should be a protocol safety exclusion or clinician-guidance path, not a subgroup for self-directed high-protein targeting.
