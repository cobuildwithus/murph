## Extracted findings

- **finding:pmid-35179185-nondairy-animal-protein-stone-risk** — The meta-analysis found positive associations for nondairy animal protein (RR 1.11, 95% CI 1.03–1.20), total meat/meat products (RR 1.22, 95% CI 1.09–1.38), processed meat (RR 1.29, 95% CI 1.10–1.51), and each 100 g/day red-meat increment (RR 1.39, 95% CI 1.13–1.71).
- **finding:pmid-35179185-dairy-protein-inverse-low-certainty** — Higher dairy-protein intake was inversely associated with kidney-stone risk (RR 0.91, 95% CI 0.84–0.99), but credibility for most exposures was low and some results were sensitive to individual studies.

## Protocol appraisal

- **evidence_appraisal:protein-floor-high-protein-intake:pmid-35179185** — Observational evidence separates nondairy animal protein from dairy protein for stone risk. Implication: Protocol implementations should capture protein source and avoid a source-agnostic safety claim, especially in stone-prone users.
