## Extracted findings

- **finding:pmid-26947338-acute-fullness-meta-analysis** — Five studies met all criteria for quantitative meta-analysis; higher-protein preloads increased fullness versus lower-protein preloads with an overall effect estimate of 2,435.74 mm·240 min (95% CI 1,375.18 to 3,496.31; P<0.0001).
- **finding:pmid-26947338-fullness-extrapolation-limits** — The directional analysis also favored higher protein for fullness (P<0.01), but the authors emphasize that extrapolation to common eating conditions, ingestive behavior, body weight, or health outcomes must be cautious.

## Protocol appraisal

- **evidence_appraisal:protein-floor-high-protein-intake:pmid-26947338** — Higher-protein preloads acutely increased fullness, but the source cautions against extrapolating to weight outcomes. Implication: Supports appetite/fullness measurement in a protein-floor experiment without becoming direct efficacy evidence.
