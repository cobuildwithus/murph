This source is included for **direct_protocol_and_dose_evidence**.

## Findings

- **intervention_result: Functional capacity and cardiovascular risk markers** — In 106 sedentary adults, a 12-week home walking trial found evidence of benefit even below current recommended levels; the 3-day/week 30-minute arm was associated with improvements in blood pressure, waist-to-hip ratio, and functional capacity.

## Why it matters

This is among the closest source matches for frequency, modality simplicity, and home implementation.

## Potential experiment signals

- weekly walking minutes
- functional capacity / walk test
- morning blood pressure
- waist-to-hip or waist circumference
- adherence

## Protocol takeaway

Use as closest walking evidence that 3 days/week of brisk walking can improve functional capacity and some cardiometabolic signals over 12 weeks.

## Claim use

`supports-protocol`.

## Directness and limitations

Sessions were 30 minutes and follow-up was 12 weeks, so this still exceeds the 4-week target duration and is slightly shorter than the 35–60 minute protocol window.
