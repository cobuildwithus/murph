This source is included for **Travel comfort, flight edema, and VTE boundary**.

**Findings:** Population/count: Low- to moderate-risk long-distance air travelers aged 18–70 in the New Zealand Air Traveller Thrombosis study. Participant count: 878 (completed_follow_up; 1000_recruited). Intervention/exposure: Long-distance air travel of at least 10 hours. Comparator/control: No device comparator; cohort follow-up. Duration/follow-up: Long-distance flights with pre/post D-dimer and imaging follow-up when indicated. Endpoints: VTE frequency, DVT, pulmonary embolism, D-dimer-triggered imaging. Effect/direction: VTE frequency was reported as 1% (9/878) among completed low- to moderate-risk long-distance air travelers. Safety/adverse events: Travel-related VTE was uncommon but present in low- to moderate-risk travelers. Limitations: Cohort context only; no pneumatic or stocking intervention.; Event ascertainment depended on screening workflow.; Low- to moderate-risk population does not cover high-risk medical travelers. Population mismatch: Travel cohort without pneumatic pants.

**Why it matters:** Defines background incidence for travel-associated VTE without implying device benefit.

**Potential experiment signals:** VTE frequency, DVT, pulmonary embolism, D-dimer-triggered imaging.

**Protocol takeaway:** Context only: travel VTE exists but this cohort does not support pneumatic-pants prevention claims.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
