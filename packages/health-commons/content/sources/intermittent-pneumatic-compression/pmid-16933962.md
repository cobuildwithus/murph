This source is included for **Travel comfort, flight edema, and VTE boundary**.

**Findings:** Population/count: MEGA population-based case-control study participants younger than 70 with first venous thrombosis and controls/partners. Participant count: 1906 (cases_plus_controls_case_control_study_context). Intervention/exposure: Travel longer than 4 hours, including air, car, bus, or train travel. Comparator/control: No travel exposure in the risk window. Duration/follow-up: Travel exposure within 8 weeks before the index date. Endpoints: venous thrombosis, travel mode, risk-factor interactions. Effect/direction: Travel was associated with venous thrombosis with OR 2.1; risk was highest in the first week. Risk was higher in subgroups including factor V Leiden, obesity, tall or short stature, and oral contraceptive use. Safety/adverse events: Risk-factor interactions are central; oral contraceptive use and thrombophilia markedly increased estimated risk with travel. Limitations: Case-control design; no causality for prevention methods.; No pneumatic-pants intervention.; Subgroup estimates may be imprecise. Population mismatch: Travel-exposure epidemiology, not protocol efficacy.

**Why it matters:** Shows why travel VTE risk is not only about flight length but also individual risk factors.

**Potential experiment signals:** venous thrombosis, travel mode, risk-factor interactions.

**Protocol takeaway:** Safety context: screen for risk factors and avoid DVT-prevention claims for consumer pants.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
