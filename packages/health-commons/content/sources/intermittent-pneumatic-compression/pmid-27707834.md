This source is included for **Clinical DVT prophylaxis and hospital boundary**.

**Findings:** The feasibility study enrolled 7/142 potentially eligible participants (5%, 95% CI 2 to 9.9). Device use averaged 4.1 hours/day (range 1.9 to 10.2), and none of the 7 participants had adequate adherence. Three of 7 participants had adverse events, including one DVT, two dorsal foot ulcers, and one skin maceration.

**Why it matters:** The source provides concrete safety and adherence concerns in immobilized lower-limb users, including foot ulcers and skin maceration.

**Potential experiment signals:** Adherence / daily use hours; Skin maceration; Pressure/ulcer symptoms; DVT or new leg symptoms requiring medical care.

**Protocol takeaway:** Within-cast IPC/JIT had poor feasibility and adverse events; consumer protocols should include skin checks, comfort limits, and stop criteria.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Population mismatch:** Lower-limb cast immobilization with a within-cast device is a high-risk clinical scenario, not routine recovery use.

**Limitations:** Very small feasibility cohort with no control group.; Designed to assess feasibility for a future RCT, not efficacy.; Within-cast IPC/JIT is an adjacent variant, not consumer pants.; Adverse-event proportions are unstable with n=7..
