This source is included for **Travel comfort, flight edema, and VTE boundary**.

**Findings:** Population/count: Low- to medium-risk Concorde/long-flight travelers in two LONFLIT4 components, plus a microangiopathy sub-study context. Participant count: 284 (randomized; 276_completed). Intervention/exposure: Kendall Travel Socks providing about 20–30 mmHg at the ankle. Comparator/control: No stockings. Duration/follow-up: Part I 7–8 hour flights; Part II 11–12 hour flights. Endpoints: DVT, SVT, edema score, microangiopathy score, tolerability. Effect/direction: No DVTs were reported in stocking groups. Part II controls had 2 popliteal DVT and 2 SVT events; edema scores were lower with stockings in both parts. Safety/adverse events: Stockings were described as well tolerated in the extracted abstract. Limitations: Static stocking study, not pneumatic compression pants.; Small travel cohorts and asymptomatic ultrasound endpoints.; Not designed for consumer recovery-pants use. Population mismatch: Travel socks differ in mechanism and pressure profile from IPC pants.

**Why it matters:** Adds flight edema context and reinforces that travel-prophylaxis evidence is stocking-based.

**Potential experiment signals:** DVT, SVT, edema score, microangiopathy score, tolerability.

**Protocol takeaway:** Adjacent variant only: static travel socks are not evidence that pneumatic pants prevent flight VTE.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
