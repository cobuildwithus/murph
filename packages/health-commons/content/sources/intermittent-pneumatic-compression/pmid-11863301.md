This source is included for **Travel comfort, flight edema, and VTE boundary**.

**Findings:** Population/count: High-risk long-haul travelers randomized to no prophylaxis, aspirin, or LMWH. Participant count: 300 (randomized; 249_completed). Intervention/exposure: Aspirin or low-molecular-weight heparin before/during air travel. Comparator/control: No prophylaxis; aspirin versus LMWH. Duration/follow-up: Long-haul flights; medication started before travel; post-flight vascular assessment. Endpoints: DVT, superficial thrombosis, drug side effects. Effect/direction: Among completers, DVT occurred in 4.82% of control subjects and 3.6% of aspirin subjects; no DVT occurred in LMWH subjects, with one SVT reported in the LMWH group. Safety/adverse events: Mild gastrointestinal symptoms occurred in about 13% of aspirin users; drug-interaction or contraindicated participants were excluded. Limitations: Drug prophylaxis trial, not a mechanical or pants intervention.; High-risk travelers and medical prophylaxis context.; Dropout rate about 17%; asymptomatic outcomes included. Population mismatch: Pharmacologic prevention in high-risk travelers, not consumer compression pants.

**Why it matters:** Shows that high-risk travel VTE prevention may require medical intervention rather than consumer recovery products.

**Potential experiment signals:** DVT, superficial thrombosis, drug side effects.

**Protocol takeaway:** Safety boundary: pneumatic pants should not replace clinician-directed medication decisions for high-risk travelers.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
