This source is included for **Direct post-exercise recovery evidence: reviews and evidence maps**.

**Findings:** The review retrieved 354 studies and included six after applying inclusion/exclusion criteria and full-text review. It found no significant difference indicating superiority or inferiority of compression boots compared with other recovery methods.

**Why it matters:** This is direct consumer-relevant evidence because it addresses compression boots as post-exercise recovery devices.

**Potential experiment signals:** Perceived recovery, muscle fatigue, soreness, neuromuscular performance, blood lactate, heart rate, and comparator method.

**Protocol takeaway:** The protocol should avoid superiority claims. Use IPC as an optional recovery experiment with uncertain comparative benefit.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`, specifically as direct no-clear-advantage evidence.
