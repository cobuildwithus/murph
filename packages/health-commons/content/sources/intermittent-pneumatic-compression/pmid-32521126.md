This source is included for **Clinical edema, lymphedema, venous, PAD, and wound-care boundary**.

**Findings:** The consensus describes IPC as a two-phase lymphedema program: sequential gradient pumping followed by form-fitting low-stretch garments or sleeves. It warns to observe for proximal or genital edema and fibrosclerotic rings at the limb root.

**Why it matters:** The source names safety phenomena that matter for self-directed lower-limb compression: proximal displacement, genital swelling, and fibrosclerotic rings.

**Potential experiment signals:** Proximal swelling, Genital swelling, Maintenance garment use, Known lymphedema, Specialist oversight.

**Protocol takeaway:** Known lymphedema should be treated as clinician-supervised context, and users should stop or seek care if swelling shifts proximally.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
