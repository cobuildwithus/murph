This source is included for **Clinical DVT prophylaxis and hospital boundary**.

**Findings:** The accessible abstract reports 150 critically ill adult ICU patients at elevated hemorrhage risk randomized to IPC or standard care. VTE and DVT incidence were lower in the IPC group, mean and peak femoral vein blood-flow velocities were higher, and proximal DVT, pulmonary embolism, fatal pulmonary embolism, leg skin lesions, severe/major hemorrhage, and deaths did not differ between groups. Exact event counts and effect estimates were not available from accessible abstract snippets in this extraction.

**Why it matters:** This 2026 trial updates the ICU boundary and reinforces the need to keep VTE-prevention claims separate from recovery pants claims.

**Potential experiment signals:** Leg skin lesions; Major bleeding only in medical prophylaxis context; Femoral vein blood-flow velocity as mechanism-only; No exercise recovery endpoint measured.

**Protocol takeaway:** Recent ICU evidence may support supervised thromboprophylaxis in high-bleeding-risk patients, but exact estimates were not available and directness to consumer recovery is low.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Population mismatch:** Critically ill ICU adults at elevated hemorrhage risk differ substantially from consumer recovery-pants users.

**Limitations:** Full article was not accessible in this extraction; exact event counts, effect estimates, and treatment duration are not extracted.; ICU high-hemorrhage-risk population, not recovery users.; Standard-care details were not extracted from accessible abstract snippets..
