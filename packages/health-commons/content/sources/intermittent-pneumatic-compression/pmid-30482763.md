This source is included for **Clinical DVT prophylaxis and hospital boundary**.

**Findings:** ASH conditionally recommends against routine prophylaxis in chronically ill medical patients such as nursing-home residents or outpatients with minor provoking risk factors; in acutely or critically ill patients receiving mechanical prophylaxis, pneumatic compression devices or GCS are options. Safety/adverse-event notes: Bleeding risk and low/very-low certainty evidence influence recommendations. Limitations: Guideline evidence is for medical-patient VTE prophylaxis and does not support healthy consumer clot-prevention claims.

**Why it matters:** It defines the supervised clinical boundary for IPC use and helps prevent over-claiming consumer benefits.

**Potential experiment signals:** Skin irritation or skin breaks; Discomfort/tolerance; Falls or tubing/device hazards; Adherence or wear time; DVT/PE only as clinician-managed safety context.

**Protocol takeaway:** Consumers should not use pneumatic pants as a substitute for medical VTE prophylaxis decisions.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
