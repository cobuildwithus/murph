This source is included for **Clinical DVT prophylaxis and hospital boundary**.

**Findings:** Thirty-four patients (68 limbs) undergoing knee and spine operations were randomized to alternate sequential compression or simultaneous sequential compression. DVT developed in 7/34 patients (20.6%), all asymptomatic isolated calf DVTs: 2 in the ASCD group (11.8%) and 5 in the SSCD group (29.4%), with no significant difference (p=0.331). Baseline peak velocity, mean velocity, peak volume flow, and total volume flow increased significantly in both groups (p<0.001), while degrees of flow and velocity enhancement did not significantly differ between groups.

**Why it matters:** This source separates hemodynamic mechanism signals from clinical outcomes and prevents over-reading device-comparison data as direct recovery evidence.

**Potential experiment signals:** Venous-flow velocity if using lab equipment; Skin/discomfort observations; Adherence / device tolerability; No soreness or performance endpoint measured.

**Protocol takeaway:** IPC device settings can measurably increase venous flow in supervised postoperative patients, but this study does not show a clear clinical advantage between device modes.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Population mismatch:** Postoperative knee and spine surgery patients are a clinical thromboprophylaxis population, not consumer recovery users.

**Limitations:** Very small postoperative orthopedic sample.; Active-device comparison without a no-IPC control group.; Short postoperative follow-up.; Clinical outcomes were asymptomatic isolated calf DVTs, not recovery endpoints..
