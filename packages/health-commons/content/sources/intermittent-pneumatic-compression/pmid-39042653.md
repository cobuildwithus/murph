This source is included for **Clinical DVT prophylaxis and hospital boundary**.

**Findings:** The pooled DVT estimate was not clearly significant (OR 0.81, 95% CI 0.59 to 1.11). Benefit appeared in the no-prophylaxis subgroup (OR 0.41, 95% CI 0.26 to 0.65), while pharmacologic-prophylaxis and combined-prophylaxis subgroup estimates did not show DVT prevention benefit. PE and bleeding findings require cautious interpretation because comparators differed; IPC had lower bleeding when compared with pharmacologic/combined controls. Safety/adverse-event notes: Bleeding was lower in IPC comparisons involving pharmacologic or combined prophylaxis controls (OR 0.17, 95% CI 0.08 to 0.36), reflecting anticoagulant bleeding-risk context rather than a universal IPC safety claim. Limitations: Surgical RCT review with subgroup-dependent, mixed results; participant count not re-extracted; not consumer evidence.

**Why it matters:** It defines the supervised clinical boundary for IPC use and helps prevent over-claiming consumer benefits.

**Potential experiment signals:** Skin irritation or skin breaks; Discomfort/tolerance; Falls or tubing/device hazards; Adherence or wear time; DVT/PE only as clinician-managed safety context.

**Protocol takeaway:** Do not state that IPC always prevents DVT in surgical settings; comparator matters.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
