This source is included for **Travel comfort, flight edema, and VTE boundary**.

**Findings:** Population/count: Low- to medium-risk long-haul flight passengers in two LONFLIT4-Concorde cohorts. Participant count: 657 (randomized_across_two_parts; 629_completed). Intervention/exposure: Scholl Flight Socks providing about 14–17 mmHg ankle compression. Comparator/control: No prophylaxis. Duration/follow-up: Part I 7–8 hour flights; Part II 11–12 hour flights. Endpoints: DVT, SVT, edema score, tolerability. Effect/direction: No DVT/SVT occurred in stocking groups; controls had thrombotic events in both parts. Edema scores were lower with stockings. Tolerability was described as very good, with no complaints or side effects reported. Safety/adverse events: No complaints or side effects reported for the stockings in the extracted abstract; thrombotic events were asymptomatic and occurred in controls. Limitations: Stocking intervention, not pneumatic pants.; Travel-specific edema and asymptomatic thrombosis endpoints.; Limited generalizability to unsupervised consumer pants. Population mismatch: Elastic flight socks and travel microangiopathy; not IPC pants.

**Why it matters:** This source is relevant for flight edema but only as adjacent static-compression evidence.

**Potential experiment signals:** DVT, SVT, edema score, tolerability.

**Protocol takeaway:** Adjacent variant: lower edema with stockings during flights does not establish pneumatic-pants benefit.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
