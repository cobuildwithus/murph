This source is included for **Clinical DVT prophylaxis and hospital boundary**.

**Findings:** Older meta-analysis reported lower DVT with IPC versus placebo (11% vs 29%, p<0.001), GCS (8% vs 15%, p=0.04), and heparin (7% vs 14%, p=0.01), but no significant PE difference. Sleeve-height and compression-method data were sparse and conflicting, with no compelling evidence that one IPC method was superior. Safety/adverse-event notes: Adverse events not fully extracted; older studies and prophylaxis comparators require cautious interpretation. Limitations: Older meta-analysis; participant count not re-extracted; clinical hospitalized populations; no consumer pants evidence.

**Why it matters:** It defines the supervised clinical boundary for IPC use and helps prevent over-claiming consumer benefits.

**Potential experiment signals:** Skin irritation or skin breaks; Discomfort/tolerance; Falls or tubing/device hazards; Adherence or wear time; DVT/PE only as clinician-managed safety context.

**Protocol takeaway:** Do not use older DVT meta-analysis results as consumer clot-prevention support.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
