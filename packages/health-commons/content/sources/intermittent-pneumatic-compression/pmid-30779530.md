This source is included for **Clinical DVT prophylaxis and hospital boundary**.

**Findings:** In PREVENT, 2003 ICU patients were randomized to IPC plus pharmacologic thromboprophylaxis (991) or pharmacologic thromboprophylaxis alone (1012). IPC was applied for a median 22 hours/day for 7 days. Incident proximal lower-limb DVT occurred in 37/957 (3.9%) versus 41/985 (4.2%) (RR 0.93, 95% CI 0.60-1.44; p=0.74). VTE was 103/991 (10.4%) versus 95/1012 (9.4%) (RR 1.11, 95% CI 0.85-1.44), and 90-day death was 258/990 (26.1%) versus 270/1011 (26.7%) (RR 0.98, 95% CI 0.84-1.13).

**Why it matters:** PREVENT is a large modern RCT showing no additive DVT advantage in a clinical ICU setting despite high achieved IPC adherence.

**Potential experiment signals:** Skin injury or ischemia; Adherence / daily hours; Medical contraindications and anticoagulation context; No recovery endpoint measured.

**Protocol takeaway:** Adding IPC to pharmacologic prophylaxis did not lower proximal DVT in ICU patients; this is context-only and argues against broad claims of clinical prevention benefit.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Population mismatch:** Critically ill anticoagulated ICU patients are not representative of consumer recovery-pants users.

**Limitations:** Adjunctive design on top of pharmacologic thromboprophylaxis.; ICU population with medical, surgical, and trauma patients.; Unblinded device use; sonography schedule and care delivery were clinical-trial procedures.; Control-event rate was lower than expected in some interpretations, affecting power..
