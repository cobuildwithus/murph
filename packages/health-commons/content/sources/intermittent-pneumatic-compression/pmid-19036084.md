This source is included for **Travel comfort, flight edema, and VTE boundary**.

**Findings:** Population/count: MEGA study participants, including a subset of patients and controls with recent air travel longer than 4 hours. Participant count: 11033 (parent_case_control_participants; 188_recent_air_travel_subset). Intervention/exposure: Flight-related behaviors such as window/aisle seating, sleeping, exercise, alcohol, nonalcoholic drinks, business class, and stockings. Comparator/control: Alternative behaviors or seating choices among recent air travelers. Duration/follow-up: Air travel longer than 4 hours within 8 weeks before index date. Endpoints: venous thrombosis, flight-related behavior, seat location, stocking use. Effect/direction: Window seat was associated with higher VTE risk than aisle seat (OR about 2.2). The observational analysis did not find a protective effect for exercising, drinking nonalcoholic drinks, or wearing stockings. Safety/adverse events: Window-seat risk was more pronounced among travelers with BMI over 30, though estimates were imprecise. Limitations: Observational behavior subanalysis; behavior reports can be confounded.; Not a randomized stocking or IPC trial.; Stocking-use null finding should not be treated as proof of stocking inefficacy. Population mismatch: Flight behavior epidemiology; no pneumatic pants intervention.

**Why it matters:** Important because common advice like exercise/hydration is often overinterpreted as proven VTE prevention.

**Potential experiment signals:** venous thrombosis, flight-related behavior, seat location, stocking use.

**Protocol takeaway:** Context only: observational flight-behavior findings should not support direct protocol claims.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
