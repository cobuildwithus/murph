This source is included for **Travel comfort, flight edema, and VTE boundary**.

**Findings:** Population/count: Long-distance travelers, especially high-risk travelers, addressed by Saudi clinical practice guideline. Participant count: 0 (guideline_not_applicable). Intervention/exposure: Ambulation, calf exercises, aisle seats, anticoagulants, and graduated compression stockings recommendations. Comparator/control: No prophylaxis or alternative prophylaxis strategies. Duration/follow-up: Long-distance travel longer than 8 hours. Endpoints: VTE prophylaxis recommendations, GCS recommendation, anticoagulants, ambulation/calf exercises. Effect/direction: The guideline conditionally recommends frequent ambulation, calf exercises, aisle seat, and anticoagulants for high-risk long-distance travelers, but suggests against GCS in the Saudi context despite evidence because feasibility/cost/acceptability were concerns. Safety/adverse events: Anticoagulants are clinician-directed; guideline evidence quality was very low for several recommendations. Limitations: Guideline context and local feasibility judgments.; Not a pneumatic-pants source.; Very-low-quality evidence for several travel-behavior recommendations. Population mismatch: Clinical guideline, not consumer IPC pants.

**Why it matters:** Useful because guidelines differ on stockings and prophylaxis, reinforcing conservative claim boundaries.

**Potential experiment signals:** VTE prophylaxis recommendations, GCS recommendation, anticoagulants, ambulation/calf exercises.

**Protocol takeaway:** Safety/context only: travel prophylaxis is guideline- and risk-dependent, not a consumer-pants claim.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
