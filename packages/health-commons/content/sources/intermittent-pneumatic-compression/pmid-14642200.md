This source is included for **Travel comfort, flight edema, and VTE boundary**.

**Findings:** Population/count: Healthy and moderate-risk participants on actual long-haul flights between Vienna and Washington. Participant count: 20 (participants). Intervention/exposure: Actual 9-hour long-haul flight exposure. Comparator/control: Pre-flight baseline and repeated post-flight measurements. Duration/follow-up: Measurements 48 hours before flight, during flight, immediately after return, and 1 and 3 days after. Endpoints: lower-extremity fluid accumulation, leg/thigh tissue thickness, edema formation. Effect/direction: Long-haul flights induced significant fluid accumulation in lower extremities involving lower leg and thigh; tissue thickness persisted for several days after flights. Safety/adverse events: Physiologic edema context; no IPC safety data. Limitations: Small acute physiology study.; No intervention tested.; Flight edema physiology does not establish pants benefit. Population mismatch: Travel physiology without pneumatic compression.

**Why it matters:** Relevant for why travelers perceive swelling and may seek compression interventions.

**Potential experiment signals:** lower-extremity fluid accumulation, leg/thigh tissue thickness, edema formation.

**Protocol takeaway:** Background only: flight edema exists, but no pneumatic-pants effect was tested.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
