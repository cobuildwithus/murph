This source is included for **Travel comfort, flight edema, and VTE boundary**.

**Findings:** Population/count: Young healthy seated volunteers in a prolonged-immobility physiology study. Participant count: 0 (not_extracted_from_abstract). Intervention/exposure: Leg and foot exercise regimens, including foot exercises against increased resistance. Comparator/control: Seated immobility and motionless sitting conditions. Duration/follow-up: Prolonged seated immobility simulation. Endpoints: popliteal venous blood flow, blood volume flow, mechanistic VTE prevention rationale. Effect/direction: Seated immobility reduced popliteal venous blood flow by almost 40%, and foot exercises against increased resistance enhanced flow. Safety/adverse events: No clinical VTE outcomes or adverse-event data were extracted. Limitations: Mechanistic physiology study; no clinical VTE outcomes.; Young healthy participants, not high-risk travelers.; No pneumatic pants intervention. Population mismatch: Exercise physiology, not IPC pants.

**Why it matters:** Relevant to the mechanism boundary: moving the legs can improve venous outflow without a device.
