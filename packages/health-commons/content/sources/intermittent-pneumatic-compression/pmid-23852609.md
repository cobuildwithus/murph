This source is included for **Clinical DVT prophylaxis and hospital boundary**.

**Findings:** The review concluded IPC was effective for reducing VTE in hospitalized patients and that combining pharmacological thromboprophylaxis with IPC was more effective than IPC alone. Safety/adverse-event notes: Bleeding and medication-related safety are comparator issues; specific adverse-event extraction not completed. Limitations: Dense clinical review; exact included participant count and subgroup effect estimates were not re-extracted in this batch. Hospital-only evidence cannot be generalized to consumer pants.

**Why it matters:** It defines the supervised clinical boundary for IPC use and helps prevent over-claiming consumer benefits.

**Potential experiment signals:** Skin irritation or skin breaks; Discomfort/tolerance; Falls or tubing/device hazards; Adherence or wear time; DVT/PE only as clinician-managed safety context.

**Protocol takeaway:** Hospital IPC efficacy is a boundary: it should not become a wellness claim.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
