This source is included for **Clinical DVT prophylaxis and hospital boundary**.

**Findings:** ESO guideline recommends IPC for immobile acute ischaemic stroke patients based on moderate evidence; contraindication/caution contexts include open leg wounds, existing DVT, heart failure, severe peripheral vascular disease, and confusion or unsupervised mobilization increasing falls risk. Safety/adverse-event notes: Leg wounds, DVT, heart failure, severe peripheral vascular disease, and falls/confusion are caution or contraindication boundaries. Limitations: Stroke guideline, not consumer protocol; acute stroke patients differ fundamentally from healthy recovery users.

**Why it matters:** It defines the supervised clinical boundary for IPC use and helps prevent over-claiming consumer benefits.

**Potential experiment signals:** Skin irritation or skin breaks; Discomfort/tolerance; Falls or tubing/device hazards; Adherence or wear time; DVT/PE only as clinician-managed safety context.

**Protocol takeaway:** Stroke or suspected DVT is outside self-experiment scope.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
