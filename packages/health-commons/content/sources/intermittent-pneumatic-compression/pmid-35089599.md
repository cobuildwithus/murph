This source is included for **Clinical DVT prophylaxis and hospital boundary**.

**Findings:** Compared with IPC alone, combined prophylaxis reduced symptomatic PE (1.34% vs 0.65%; OR 0.51, 95% CI 0.29 to 0.91) and DVT (3.81% vs 2.03%; OR 0.51, 95% CI 0.36 to 0.72) but increased any bleeding (0.95% vs 5.88%; OR 6.02, 95% CI 3.88 to 9.35) and major bleeding (0.34% vs 2.21%; OR 5.77, 95% CI 2.81 to 11.83). Compared with pharmacological prophylaxis alone, adding IPC reduced PE (OR 0.46, 95% CI 0.30 to 0.71) and DVT (OR 0.38, 95% CI 0.21 to 0.70) without an observed bleeding increase from IPC itself. Safety/adverse-event notes: Bleeding increased when pharmacologic prophylaxis was added to IPC alone; adding IPC to drugs did not itself increase bleeding in the reported comparison. Limitations: Evidence certainty varied from very low to high; population was hospitalized trauma/surgery patients, not consumers.

**Why it matters:** It defines the supervised clinical boundary for IPC use and helps prevent over-claiming consumer benefits.

**Potential experiment signals:** Skin irritation or skin breaks; Discomfort/tolerance; Falls or tubing/device hazards; Adherence or wear time; DVT/PE only as clinician-managed safety context.

**Protocol takeaway:** Avoid clot-prevention efficacy claims and emphasize clinician-led anticoagulation/IPC decisions.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
