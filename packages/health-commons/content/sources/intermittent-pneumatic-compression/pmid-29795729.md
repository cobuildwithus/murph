This source is included for **Adjacent post-exercise compression evidence: upper-limb DOMS model**.

**Findings:** Daily PCD treatments reduced upper-arm swelling (1.7 vs 2.0 cm, p=0.012), ROM disturbance (peak reduction -9.04 vs -17.25 degrees, p<0.05), and peak pain (27.5 vs 45.2 mm, p<0.05) compared with a continuously worn compression sleeve.

**Why it matters:** This provides a positive compression-device DOMS signal but with a clear population/anatomy mismatch.

**Potential experiment signals:** Upper-arm circumference, ROM disturbance, Peak pain, Recovery time from DOMS.

**Protocol takeaway:** Daily pneumatic compression may reduce DOMS-related pain and ROM loss in an adjacent upper-limb model, but it should be labeled as population-mismatched for pants.

**Safety and limitations:** No adverse events were recovered in accessible extraction. Key limitations: Upper-limb DOMS model, not lower-limb pants.; Very small n=8 sample.; No adverse-event detail recovered..

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
