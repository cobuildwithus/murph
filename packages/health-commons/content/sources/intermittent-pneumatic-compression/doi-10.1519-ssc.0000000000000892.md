This source is included for **Direct post-exercise recovery evidence: reviews and evidence maps**.

**Findings:** The indexed abstract reports 27 studies including 554 healthy adults who used IPC after exercise-induced muscle damage. IPC was associated with small statistically significant effects for soreness/pain and creatine kinase values. Other recovery variables were not statistically significant, and the authors characterize the overall evidence as limited.

**Why it matters:** This review is directly relevant to pneumatic compression pants because it evaluates intermittent pneumatic compression as a post-exercise recovery modality rather than clinical IPC for thrombosis, edema, or lymphedema.

**Potential experiment signals:** Soreness, pain, perceived fatigue, jump/strength recovery, and creatine kinase are plausible signals to track. Biomarkers should not be treated as required consumer endpoints.

**Protocol takeaway:** Support only cautious claims: IPC may help perceived soreness/pain, while broader recovery and performance effects remain uncertain.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`, with mixed-result boundaries.
