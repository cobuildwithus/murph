This source is included for **Clinical DVT prophylaxis and hospital boundary**.

**Findings:** Guideline indicates low-risk surgical patients may use mechanical prophylaxis such as IPC; moderate-risk patients may use LMWH/UFH or mechanical IPC; high-risk patients may add mechanical prophylaxis to drugs, while high bleeding-risk patients may use mechanical prophylaxis, preferably IPC, until bleeding risk falls. Safety/adverse-event notes: Bleeding risk determines whether mechanical prophylaxis replaces or delays pharmacologic prophylaxis. Limitations: Perioperative surgical guideline; no consumer population or direct pneumatic-pants efficacy.

**Why it matters:** It defines the supervised clinical boundary for IPC use and helps prevent over-claiming consumer benefits.

**Potential experiment signals:** Skin irritation or skin breaks; Discomfort/tolerance; Falls or tubing/device hazards; Adherence or wear time; DVT/PE only as clinician-managed safety context.

**Protocol takeaway:** Recent surgery is a clinician-guided use case; consumer pants are not a substitute for prescribed prophylaxis.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
