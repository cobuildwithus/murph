This source is included for **Clinical DVT prophylaxis and hospital boundary**.

**Findings:** In 2876 CLOTS 3 participants, secondary analysis found no significant differences in disability (OHS 0-2 vs 3-6 adjusted OR 0.98, 95% CI 0.80-1.19; p=0.83), living circumstances (institutional care adjusted OR 1.11, 95% CI 0.89-1.37; p=0.358), or EQ-5D utility (median 0.26 with IPC vs 0.27 with no IPC; p=0.952). Hospital costs increased by £451 with IPC because of longer hospital stay, while quality-adjusted survival gain was nonsignificant (0.9 days; 95% CI -2.1 to 3.9).

**Why it matters:** This source keeps the CLOTS 3 clinical benefit in proportion: DVT reduction did not translate into clear functional or quality-adjusted survival gains.

**Potential experiment signals:** Quality-of-life score only as clinical context; Functional status only as clinical context; Adherence and skin safety; No exercise recovery endpoint measured.

**Protocol takeaway:** Even when IPC reduces DVT in a clinical population, downstream functional and quality-of-life benefits may be absent or small.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Population mismatch:** Immobile stroke patients in hospital are not representative of healthy users using pneumatic pants for recovery.

**Limitations:** Secondary outcomes from a stroke thromboprophylaxis trial, not a recovery study.; Postal questionnaires were completed by unmasked patients/carers.; Hospital cost and length-of-stay results are setting-specific..
