This source is included for **Direct post-exercise recovery evidence: completed trials and dose studies**.

**Findings:** No statistically significant time-by-recovery-modality interactions were reported for any CMJ force-time metric; 13 of 20 metrics declined post-recovery versus pre-practice regardless of intervention.

**Why it matters:** This recent direct trial evaluates a field-relevant force-plate endpoint in athletes.

**Potential experiment signals:** CMJ height, CMJ force-time metrics, Post-practice neuromuscular fatigue.

**Protocol takeaway:** For acute post-practice jump performance, IPC should not be claimed to prevent neuromuscular decline versus passive recovery.

**Safety and limitations:** No adverse events were identified in accessible extraction. Key limitations: Small adolescent athlete sample.; Acute 45-60 minute post-practice window.; Intervention dose details for IPC were not fully recovered in accessible extraction..

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.
