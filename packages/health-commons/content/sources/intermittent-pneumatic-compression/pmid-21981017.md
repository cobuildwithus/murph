This source is included for **Travel comfort, flight edema, and VTE boundary**.

**Findings:** Population/count: UK general practice case-control participants with VTE cases and age/gender-matched controls. Participant count: 2521 (550_cases_1971_controls). Intervention/exposure: Cumulative flying time and single flight legs in previous weeks. Comparator/control: No or lower recent flying exposure. Duration/follow-up: Previous 4 weeks and 12-week follow-up/risk window comparisons. Endpoints: VTE, cumulative flying time, single leg flight duration. Effect/direction: Cumulative flying time over 12 hours in the previous 4 weeks was associated with OR 2.75; a single flight leg over 4 hours in the previous 4 weeks had OR 2.20. Risks were no longer evident by 12 weeks. Safety/adverse events: Recent cumulative flying exposure is a safety context; not a device outcome. Limitations: Case-control design and self-reported exposure.; No prevention or compression device tested.; VTE risk depends on broader clinical factors. Population mismatch: Exposure epidemiology, not IPC pants.

**Why it matters:** Adds time-window and cumulative exposure context for travel VTE.

**Potential experiment signals:** VTE, cumulative flying time, single leg flight duration.

**Protocol takeaway:** Context only: flying exposure risk does not prove prevention by pneumatic pants.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
