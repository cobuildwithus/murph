This source is included for **Travel comfort, flight edema, and VTE boundary**.

**Findings:** Population/count: Male Dutch commercial airline pilots. Participant count: 2630 (cohort_members). Intervention/exposure: Occupational cumulative flight exposure. Comparator/control: General Dutch population and international employee cohort comparisons. Duration/follow-up: 20,420 person-years of follow-up; flight hours/year assessed. Endpoints: venous thromboembolism incidence, standardized morbidity ratio, flight hours. Effect/direction: Six venous thrombosis events occurred; incidence was 0.3 per 1000 person-years. Standardized morbidity ratios were not increased versus comparison populations, and incidence did not increase with flight hours/year. Safety/adverse events: Null risk finding in pilots does not remove risk for passengers with different risk profiles. Limitations: Healthy-worker/occupational pilot population may not represent passengers.; Male cohort.; No prevention intervention or IPC pants. Population mismatch: Occupational cohort, not consumer IPC use.

**Why it matters:** Important null/context evidence in the travel-VTE boundary.

**Potential experiment signals:** venous thromboembolism incidence, standardized morbidity ratio, flight hours.

**Protocol takeaway:** Context only: cumulative flight exposure in pilots is not evidence for or against pneumatic pants.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
