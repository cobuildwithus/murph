This source is included for **Clinical edema, lymphedema, venous, PAD, and wound-care boundary**.

**Findings:** At 5 months, initial and absolute claudication distances and resting/postexercise ABI increased in the IPC group by reported medians of about 197%, 212%, 17%, and 64%; control changes were small, and benefits persisted after treatment cessation. Popliteal flow differences were small/non-significant.

**Why it matters:** Important arterial-boundary source: IPC may be used in PAD under supervision, while PAD is a screening concern for consumer use.

**Potential experiment signals:** Walking distance, ABI or postexercise ABI, Quality of life, Compliance, Adverse symptoms.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use only as clinical arterial/PAD context and safety-boundary evidence.

**Claim use:** `context-only`. This is not direct unsupervised consumer-recovery evidence.

**Safety and boundary notes:** Accessible summary described the method as complication-free with high compliance (>82% in month 1 and >85% at months 3 and 5).

**Limitations:** PAD/claudication medical population.; Foot-and-calf arterial-assist regimen differs from consumer recovery pants.; Some details came from accessible summaries because full PMC text was blocked by reCAPTCHA during extraction..
