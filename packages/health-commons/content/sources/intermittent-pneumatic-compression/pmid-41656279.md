This source is included for **Direct post-exercise recovery evidence: completed trials and dose studies**.

**Findings:** Across 42 post-match observations, IPC did not enhance CMJ, knee-extensor low-frequency fatigue, CK, fatigue, or soreness versus placebo across 30-minute, 24-hour, and 48-hour recovery windows.

**Why it matters:** This is a direct, recent, open-access sports-recovery trial that preserves a clear null result.

**Potential experiment signals:** Countermovement jump, Low-frequency fatigue, Creatine kinase, Fatigue score, Muscle soreness, Sleep and match-load covariates.

**Protocol takeaway:** A 30-minute high-pressure IPC session after soccer match play should not be claimed to improve neuromuscular, biochemical, or perceptual recovery.

**Safety and limitations:** No adverse events were identified in accessible extraction. Key limitations: Youth elite male soccer population.; Repeated observations mean participant count and observation count differ.; Post-match load and sleep are important covariates..

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.
