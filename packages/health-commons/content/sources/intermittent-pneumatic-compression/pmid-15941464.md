This source is included for **Travel comfort, flight edema, and VTE boundary**.

**Findings:** Population/count: RCTs of airline passengers using graduated compression stockings for flight-related venous thrombosis prevention. Participant count: 2482 (systematic_review_trial_participants_for_DVT_denominator). Intervention/exposure: Medium-compression below-knee graduated compression stockings. Comparator/control: No stockings. Duration/follow-up: Long-haul flights across included randomized trials. Endpoints: DVT, SVT, intention-to-treat risk, stocking prophylaxis. Effect/direction: Across nine RCTs, DVT occurred in 2/1237 treatment participants versus 46/1245 controls; fixed RR 0.08. SVT reduction was not statistically clear, with 4/826 versus 7/823 and RR 0.67. Safety/adverse events: Review did not establish a clear SVT benefit; detailed adverse events were not extracted here. Limitations: Stocking review, not pneumatic pants.; Authors called for larger, more diverse studies, better blinding, prolonged observation, and optimal pressure clarification.; Flight-related asymptomatic thrombosis endpoints dominate. Population mismatch: Static stockings differ from intermittent pneumatic pants.

**Why it matters:** Frequently cited stocking review that needs to be separated from IPC-pants evidence.

**Potential experiment signals:** DVT, SVT, intention-to-treat risk, stocking prophylaxis.

**Protocol takeaway:** Adjacent stocking evidence may reduce flight-DVT risk, but pneumatic pants remain untested for this boundary.
