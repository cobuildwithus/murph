This source is included for **Clinical DVT prophylaxis and hospital boundary**.

**Findings:** Guidance cites CLOTS 3 as showing about a 30% relative DVT reduction and a survival signal in immobile stroke patients, under hospital supervision. Safety/adverse-event notes: Contraindications include severe congestive heart failure, severe leg skin problems, and severe peripheral vascular disease; guidance calls for skin checks two to three times daily and attention to falls risk from trailing tubing. Limitations: Implementation guidance is based on CLOTS 3 and specifically on one thigh-length hospital IPC system; it is not a consumer protocol and not a post-discharge home recommendation.

**Why it matters:** It defines the supervised clinical boundary for IPC use and helps prevent over-claiming consumer benefits.

**Potential experiment signals:** Skin checks during use; Falls/tripping hazard from pump tubing; Discomfort/tolerance; Clinician assessment of CHF, leg skin disease, and peripheral vascular disease.

**Protocol takeaway:** Keep clot-prevention language out of the consumer protocol; refer possible DVT-risk use to supervised medical care.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
