This source is included for **Travel comfort, flight edema, and VTE boundary**.

**Findings:** Population/count: Adults aged 18–60 without a medical indication for compression stockings on a 3-hour flight. Participant count: 34 (completed_paired_randomized_trial). Intervention/exposure: Compression stocking worn on one randomized leg during flight. Comparator/control: Contralateral control leg without stocking. Duration/follow-up: Single 3-hour flight with pre/post circumference and symptom measures. Endpoints: ankle edema, calf edema, pain, discomfort, adverse events. Effect/direction: Stocking legs had reduced ankle and calf swelling relative to control legs. Pain and discomfort did not differ between legs. Safety/adverse events: No adverse events were reported. Limitations: Paired stocking trial, not pneumatic pants.; Short 3-hour flight and small sample.; Edema circumference endpoint; no VTE outcomes. Population mismatch: Static stocking on one leg, not full-leg IPC pants.

**Why it matters:** Most directly relevant to flight edema/comfort, but still stockings and a short flight.

**Potential experiment signals:** ankle edema, calf edema, pain, discomfort, adverse events.

**Protocol takeaway:** Adjacent variant: stockings may reduce edema on short flights, but comfort benefit was not shown and pants remain untested.
