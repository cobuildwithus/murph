This source is included for **Prolonged standing/sitting and workday leg swelling sibling variant**.

**Findings:** Thirteen non-obese normotensive men with sleep apnea completed active and sham calf-stimulation sitting visits. Active stimulation reduced leg-fluid accumulation compared with sham (51.9 ± 8.8 mL versus 91.5 ± 8.9 mL, p<0.001), with important sex and disease-population limitations.

**Why it matters:** It supports the general idea that lower-leg fluid accumulation during sitting can be modified by active calf-pump mechanisms, while staying outside direct IPC evidence.

**Potential experiment signals:** biomarker / leg-fluid-accumulation, biomarker / calf-circumference.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as same-problem/adjacent-mechanism context only.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
