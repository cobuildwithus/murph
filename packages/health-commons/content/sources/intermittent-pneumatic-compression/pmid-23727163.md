This source is included for **Clinical DVT prophylaxis and hospital boundary**.

**Findings:** Among 2876 immobile acute stroke patients, proximal DVT within 30 days occurred in 122/1438 (8.5%) assigned to IPC versus 174/1438 (12.1%) assigned to no IPC. Excluding patients who died or lacked screening compression duplex ultrasound, the adjusted odds ratio was 0.65 (95% CI 0.51-0.84; p=0.001). Skin breaks were more frequent with IPC (44/1438, 3%) than no IPC (20/1438, 1%; p=0.002); falls with injury were 33 (2%) versus 24 (2%; p=0.221).

**Why it matters:** CLOTS 3 is a large primary trial that anchors the hospital/stroke boundary and provides concrete safety signals for skin breaks and falls monitoring.

**Potential experiment signals:** Skin breaks or irritation; Falls/tripping with device use; Adherence / daily wear time; No post-exercise recovery endpoint measured.

**Protocol takeaway:** Clinical stroke data should not be promoted as recovery evidence; it is useful for safety cautions and context around DVT prophylaxis under supervision.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Population mismatch:** Immobile acute stroke patients using thigh-length sleeves under hospital supervision do not match consumer recovery-pants users.

**Limitations:** Hospitalized immobile stroke population, not healthy users.; Clinical exclusions included some conditions relevant to compression safety, including severe edema, severe peripheral vascular disease, dermatitis, leg ulcers, and congestive cardiac failure.; Outcome assessors for imaging were masked, but patients/care teams could not be blinded to sleeves..
