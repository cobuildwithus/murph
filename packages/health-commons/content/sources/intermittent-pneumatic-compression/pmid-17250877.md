This source is included for **Travel comfort, flight edema, and VTE boundary**.

**Findings:** Population/count: Healthy volunteers seated in aircraft chairs under simulated long-haul flight conditions. Participant count: 12 (healthy_volunteers). Intervention/exposure: 10 hours sitting in modern aircraft chairs under normobaric hypoxia. Comparator/control: Baseline earlier in the simulated flight. Duration/follow-up: 10-hour simulated long-haul flight. Endpoints: lower leg volume, leg edema formation, venous blood flow velocity. Effect/direction: Lower-leg volume increased significantly after 4 hours by about 109 mL and reached about 145 mL after 10 hours. Safety/adverse events: Physiologic edema and venous-flow context only. Limitations: Small simulation study.; No compression intervention.; Healthy volunteers and surrogate outcomes. Population mismatch: Simulated flight physiology, not IPC pants.

**Why it matters:** Useful for choosing edema/time-window endpoints in travel-adjacent self-experiments.

**Potential experiment signals:** lower leg volume, leg edema formation, venous blood flow velocity.

**Protocol takeaway:** Background only: prolonged seated immobility can increase leg volume; no pants effect tested.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
