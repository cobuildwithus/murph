This source is included for **Clinical edema, lymphedema, venous, PAD, and wound-care boundary**.

**Findings:** The 2023 consensus describes IPC/pneumomassage as a two-phase program and warns about proximal or genital fluid displacement and fibrosclerotic rings. It also emphasizes thorough medical evaluation for confounding conditions such as venous insufficiency and congestive heart failure.

**Why it matters:** The document names confounders such as venous insufficiency and congestive heart failure that matter when screening lower-limb swelling before self-directed compression.

**Potential experiment signals:** Known or suspected lymphedema, Unexplained swelling, Proximal or genital swelling, CHF or venous disease history, Clinician evaluation.

**Protocol takeaway:** Known lymphedema or unexplained swelling should be treated as a clinical boundary, especially if swelling shifts upward or involves the groin/genitals.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
