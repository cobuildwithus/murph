This source is included for **Dose, hemodynamics, and wearable-relevant mechanisms**.

**Findings:** All IPC treatments increased ankle dorsiflexion ROM and pressure-to-pain threshold versus sham immediately and at 20 minutes. BOOST80 and BOOST135 increased popliteal artery blood-flow change at Post-0 versus sham and SQUEE80. Muscle hardness and HRV did not differ significantly between trials; higher pressure/frequency did not add ROM benefit.

**Why it matters:** It compares dose and mode, preserving both positive ROM/blood-flow findings and null HRV/hardness findings.

**Potential experiment signals:** Weight-bearing lunge test ankle dorsiflexion ROM, pressure-to-pain threshold, popliteal artery blood flow, muscle hardness, HRV, perceived relaxation.

**Protocol takeaway:** Treat pressure/mode as a variable to test; avoid assuming higher pressure improves all endpoints.

**Safety and limitations:** No adverse events were extracted from accessible materials unless noted above. Key limitations: Recovery effects on ankle ROM were not tested after exercise-induced fatigue.; Blood flow was not measured during IPC because sleeves covered the site.; Could not test sequential 135 mmHg because the device lacked that pre-programmed mode.; Funded by a compression-device company, although the article reports no funder role in study conduct..
