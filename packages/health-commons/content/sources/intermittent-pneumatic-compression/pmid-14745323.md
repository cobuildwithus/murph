This source is included for **Clinical DVT prophylaxis and hospital boundary**.

**Findings:** Review states major types of intermittent compression systems are successful in emptying deep veins and preventing stasis; it does not identify clear superiority for foot compression, graded sequential compression, or one IPC method based on available evidence. Safety/adverse-event notes: Safety not the central extracted signal; device choice/cost and evidence limitations are key. Limitations: Older evidence review; broad clinical/mechanistic scope; not a direct consumer pants protocol.

**Why it matters:** It defines the supervised clinical boundary for IPC use and helps prevent over-claiming consumer benefits.

**Potential experiment signals:** Skin irritation or skin breaks; Discomfort/tolerance; Falls or tubing/device hazards; Adherence or wear time; DVT/PE only as clinician-managed safety context.

**Protocol takeaway:** Avoid device-superiority claims for pneumatic pants based on older compression literature.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
