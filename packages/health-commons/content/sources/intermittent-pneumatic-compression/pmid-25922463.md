This source is included for **Clinical DVT prophylaxis and hospital boundary**.

**Findings:** One hundred fifty patients were randomized after Achilles tendon surgery. IPC was used under an orthosis 6 hours/day for 2 weeks in the intervention group, while controls received usual plaster-cast treatment without IPC. At 2 weeks, DVT was detected in 21% of IPC-treated patients versus 37% of controls (p=0.042). At 6 weeks, after IPC had been discontinued and all patients remained immobilized in orthoses, DVT was 52% versus 48% (OR 0.94, 95% CI 0.49-1.83).

**Why it matters:** This trial shows a time-dependent clinical DVT prophylaxis effect and preserves the null follow-up after stopping IPC.

**Potential experiment signals:** Adherence / session duration; Skin irritation and comfort; Leg pain or swelling requiring medical review; No soreness/performance recovery endpoint measured.

**Protocol takeaway:** IPC may reduce DVT during active postoperative immobilization use, but this is clinical thromboprophylaxis evidence and the benefit did not persist after discontinuation.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Population mismatch:** Postoperative immobilized Achilles tendon rupture patients do not match healthy consumer recovery-pants users.

**Limitations:** Clinical postoperative Achilles rupture population, not recovery users.; DVT endpoint, not soreness or performance.; Two-week benefit did not persist after IPC stopped.; Exact safety/adverse-event details were not available in the indexed abstract beyond DVT outcomes..
