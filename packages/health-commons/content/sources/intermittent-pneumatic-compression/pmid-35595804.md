This source is included for **Clinical DVT prophylaxis and hospital boundary**.

**Findings:** Among 275 PREVENT participants with heart failure, 18 (6.5%) had prevalent proximal DVT on trial days 1-3. Among 257 without prevalent DVT, incident proximal DVT occurred in 11/125 (8.8%) assigned to IPC versus 6/132 (4.5%) controls (RR 1.94, 95% CI 0.74-5.08; p=0.17). Ventilator-free days did not significantly differ (median 21 vs 25 days; p=0.17), and VTE, pulmonary embolism, mechanical ventilation duration, mortality, 28-day DVT freedom, and 90-day survival were similar.

**Why it matters:** This source explicitly examines a population where IPC could theoretically worsen venous return and heart-failure status, while also showing no DVT advantage in the subgroup.

**Potential experiment signals:** Breathlessness or worsening edema stop criteria; Skin inspection every session; Adherence / daily hours; No recovery endpoint measured.

**Protocol takeaway:** Heart-failure subgroup data did not show clear DVT benefit or ventilation-free-day harm, but this remains supervised ICU evidence with limited power.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Population mismatch:** Critically ill heart-failure patients receiving pharmacologic prophylaxis do not match consumer recovery users.

**Limitations:** Subgroup/secondary analysis with relatively small sample size and limited power.; Heart failure determined by treating teams and local practices.; Direct measures of HF decompensation such as pulmonary edema, BNP, or worsening hypoxia were not evaluated.; Does not address IPC without pharmacologic prophylaxis..
