This source is included for **Travel comfort, flight edema, and VTE boundary**.

**Findings:** Population/count: Employees of international organizations followed for symptomatic venous thrombosis after long-distance flights. Participant count: 8755 (cohort_members). Intervention/exposure: Flights longer than 4 hours. Comparator/control: Non-exposed time without recent long-distance flight. Duration/follow-up: Risk assessed up to 8 weeks after flights; highest risk in first 2 weeks. Endpoints: symptomatic venous thrombosis, absolute risk, dose-response by flight duration/number. Effect/direction: The absolute risk of symptomatic venous thrombosis within 8 weeks after flights ≥4 hours was about 1 per 4,500 flights; rate ratio was about 3.2 and risk increased with duration and number of flights. Safety/adverse events: Risk was higher in subgroups including women using oral contraceptives and shorter passengers. Limitations: Cohort response and healthy-worker selection limitations.; No prevention intervention.; Does not evaluate compression pants. Population mismatch: Travel epidemiology, not IPC efficacy.

**Why it matters:** Helps explain both sides: nonzero risk but low absolute risk in many travelers.

**Potential experiment signals:** symptomatic venous thrombosis, absolute risk, dose-response by flight duration/number.

**Protocol takeaway:** Context only: travel risk is not equivalent to evidence that pants prevent clots.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
