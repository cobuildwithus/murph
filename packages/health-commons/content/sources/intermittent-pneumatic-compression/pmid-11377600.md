This source is included for **Travel comfort, flight edema, and VTE boundary**.

**Findings:** Population/count: Airline passengers older than 50 years with no previous thromboembolic episode, travelling on long-haul flights longer than 8 hours per flight. Participant count: 231 (randomized). Intervention/exposure: Below-knee elastic compression stockings during long-haul flights. Comparator/control: No stockings. Duration/follow-up: Outbound and return long-haul travel; return within 6 weeks; duplex ultrasound before and after travel. Endpoints: symptomless calf DVT, superficial thrombophlebitis, edema/swelling context. Effect/direction: Symptomless calf DVT occurred in 12/116 no-stocking passengers and 0 stocking passengers. Four stocking-assigned passengers with varicose veins developed superficial thrombophlebitis. Safety/adverse events: Superficial thrombophlebitis occurred in four stocking users with varicose veins; DVT events were symptomless. Limitations: Graduated stocking trial, not pneumatic compression pants.; Older long-haul travelers; results should not be generalized to low-risk consumer recovery use.; Endpoints focused on asymptomatic ultrasound DVT, not comfort or recovery. Population mismatch: Elastic stockings in supervised travel research; not consumer IPC pants.

**Why it matters:** This is a landmark travel-stocking RCT often invoked in flight-DVT discussions; it should be kept separate from pants efficacy.

**Potential experiment signals:** symptomless calf DVT, superficial thrombophlebitis, edema/swelling context.

**Protocol takeaway:** Context only: stocking RCTs do not establish pneumatic compression pants as flight-DVT prophylaxis.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
