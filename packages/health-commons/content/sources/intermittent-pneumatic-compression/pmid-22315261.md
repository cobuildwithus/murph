This source is included for **Clinical DVT prophylaxis and hospital boundary**.

**Findings:** For acutely ill hospitalized medical patients at increased thrombosis risk, guideline recommends anticoagulant prophylaxis; for bleeding or high bleeding risk it suggests mechanical prophylaxis with GCS or IPC; for low-risk patients it recommends against pharmacologic or mechanical prophylaxis. Safety/adverse-event notes: Bleeding risk is central; mechanical prophylaxis is positioned partly when anticoagulant bleeding risk is unacceptable. Limitations: Guideline evidence applies to selected medical populations and travel/immobility contexts, not healthy consumer recovery use.

**Why it matters:** It defines the supervised clinical boundary for IPC use and helps prevent over-claiming consumer benefits.

**Potential experiment signals:** Skin irritation or skin breaks; Discomfort/tolerance; Falls or tubing/device hazards; Adherence or wear time; DVT/PE only as clinician-managed safety context.

**Protocol takeaway:** Do not suggest pneumatic pants for VTE prevention in low-risk consumers; clinical risk assessment comes first.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
