This source is included for **Travel comfort, flight edema, and VTE boundary**.

**Findings:** Population/count: Healthy volunteers tested on the ground and a subset tested during flight. Participant count: 17 (healthy_volunteers_ground; 8_in_flight_subset). Intervention/exposure: Two battery-operated IPC devices and three foot/calf pump-facilitating devices. Comparator/control: Active foot movements. Duration/follow-up: Acute testing on ground and during flight. Endpoints: lower-limb hemodynamics, flow changes, mechanical device comparison. Effect/direction: PFD devices were not significantly different from active foot movements; IPC devices had significantly less hemodynamic effect than active foot movements. In-flight and ground values were not significantly different. Safety/adverse events: No clinical VTE outcomes or adverse-event data extracted. Limitations: Small acute physiology comparison.; Healthy volunteers; no clinical outcomes.; Devices differ from consumer pneumatic pants. Population mismatch: Same mechanism but not pants and not clinical outcome evidence.

**Why it matters:** Important boundary against overclaiming mechanical devices for travel thrombosis prevention.

**Potential experiment signals:** lower-limb hemodynamics, flow changes, mechanical device comparison.

**Protocol takeaway:** Mechanism context: movement may match or exceed some mechanical-device hemodynamic effects.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
