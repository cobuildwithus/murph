This source is included for **Travel comfort, flight edema, and VTE boundary**.

**Findings:** Population/count: Studies of travel and VTE, largely from Western-country evidence bases. Participant count: 4055 (cases_reported_in_meta_analysis_context). Intervention/exposure: Travel exposure by any mode and air travel duration. Comparator/control: No or lower travel exposure. Duration/follow-up: Dose-response per 2-hour travel increments; post-travel windows varied. Endpoints: VTE risk, travel duration dose-response, air travel dose-response. Effect/direction: Pooled relative risk was reported around 2.8 after excluding referred-control studies; VTE risk increased by about 18% per 2-hour increase in any travel and 26% per 2-hour increase in air travel. Safety/adverse events: Travel duration matters, but absolute risk and individual risk factors remain important. Limitations: Meta-analysis of observational studies; control selection influenced heterogeneity.; Evidence mainly from Western countries.; No prevention intervention or compression pants. Population mismatch: Travel-risk meta-analysis, not protocol evidence.

**Why it matters:** Useful for the VTE boundary and dose-response context.

**Potential experiment signals:** VTE risk, travel duration dose-response, air travel dose-response.

**Protocol takeaway:** Context only: travel duration can increase VTE risk, but pneumatic-pants prevention remains untested.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
