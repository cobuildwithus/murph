This source is included for **Comparators and adjacent modalities**.

**Findings:** Upper-body ISPC arm sleeves showed perceptual recovery effect-size signals but no statistically significant performance or perceptual differences.

**Why it matters:** Shows ISPC can change perceived fatigue/soreness signals without clear performance benefit in an adjacent population.

**Potential experiment signals:** grip strength, pressure-to-pain threshold, medicine ball throw, wheelchair sprints, repeated sprints, muscle soreness, fatigue.

**Protocol takeaway:** Context-only: perceptual effects in upper-body wheelchair athletes should not be promoted as lower-limb pants efficacy.

**Safety and limitations:** No adverse events were recovered; financial disclosure reported no conflicts in accessible PubMed extraction. Key limitations: Small sample of 11.; Upper-body sleeves and disability-sport population.; Effect-size signals were not accompanied by statistically significant between-trial differences..

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
