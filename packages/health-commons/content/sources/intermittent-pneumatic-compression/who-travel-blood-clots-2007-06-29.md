This source is included for **Travel comfort, flight edema, and VTE boundary**.

**Findings:** Population/count: Travelers and public-health audience for WHO WRIGHT project findings. Participant count: 0 (not_applicable). Intervention/exposure: Travel longer than 4 hours and public-health preventive advice. Comparator/control: No or shorter travel context. Duration/follow-up: Travel of 4 hours or more; VTE risk remains elevated for about 4 weeks after flights. Endpoints: travel-associated VTE risk, absolute risk, risk factors, public-health advice. Effect/direction: WHO reported that VTE risk approximately doubles after travel of 4 hours or more, with an absolute risk around 1 in 6000 for people seated and immobile for more than 4 hours. Safety/adverse events: DVT/PE symptoms can be serious and untreated VTE can be fatal; risk factors include obesity, extreme height/short stature, oral contraceptive use, and inherited blood disorders. Limitations: WHO release did not investigate preventive measures directly.; Public-health summary, not trial evidence.; No pneumatic-pants intervention. Population mismatch: Public-health travel VTE context, not consumer IPC pants.

**Why it matters:** Useful public-health statement for travel-risk magnitude and risk factors.
