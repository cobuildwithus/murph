This source is included for **Clinical edema, lymphedema, venous, PAD, and wound-care boundary**.

**Findings:** Treatment reduced left leg volume (p<0.043), fluid in left and right legs (p=0.0018 and p=0.0476), extracellular/intracellular fluid ratios, subcutaneous adipose tissue depth, swelling, tenderness/pain, and more SF-36 domains than control. Controls did not show comparable fluid/volume changes.

**Why it matters:** The garment is lower-body and pant-like, but the trial tests medical edema/lipedema outcomes rather than recovery outcomes.

**Potential experiment signals:** Leg volume, Bioimpedance fluid, Skin/subcutaneous thickness, Pain/tenderness, SF-36 domains.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as supervised clinical context for edema-related endpoints and contraindication screening, not as direct efficacy support for consumer recovery.

**Claim use:** `context-only`. This is not direct unsupervised consumer-recovery evidence.

**Safety and boundary notes:** The study excluded serious arterial insufficiency, decompensated heart-failure edema, active phlebitis, active DVT, localized wound infection, cellulitis, significant lymphedema signs, and several unstable medical conditions; no adverse-event signal was located in accessible extraction.

**Limitations:** Industry-funded device study.; Short 30-day treatment period.; Lipedema population and medical-device screening differ from consumer recovery use.; Control condition was not a sham device..
