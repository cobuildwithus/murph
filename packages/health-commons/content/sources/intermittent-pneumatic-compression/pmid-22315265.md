This source is included for **Clinical DVT prophylaxis and hospital boundary**.

**Findings:** Guideline includes IPCD among recommended options for major orthopedic surgery and suggests adding IPCD during hospitalization when pharmacologic prophylaxis is used; for increased bleeding risk it suggests IPCD or no prophylaxis. Safety/adverse-event notes: Bleeding risk changes prophylaxis selection; mechanical device burden and compliance are implied clinical issues. Limitations: Orthopedic surgery patients are not healthy recovery users; durations are clinical prophylaxis durations, not consumer wellness schedules.

**Why it matters:** It defines the supervised clinical boundary for IPC use and helps prevent over-claiming consumer benefits.

**Potential experiment signals:** Skin irritation or skin breaks; Discomfort/tolerance; Falls or tubing/device hazards; Adherence or wear time; DVT/PE only as clinician-managed safety context.

**Protocol takeaway:** Post-op use should be governed by the surgeon or anticoagulation/VTE plan, not a general Murph protocol.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
