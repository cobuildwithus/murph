This source is included for **Clinical DVT prophylaxis and hospital boundary**.

**Findings:** Adjunctive IPC was associated with decreased VTE (RR 0.53, 15 trials, 95% CI 0.35 to 0.81) and DVT (RR 0.52, 14 trials, 95% CI 0.33 to 0.81), but not PE (RR 0.73, 7 trials, 95% CI 0.32 to 1.68). Safety/adverse-event notes: Safety/adverse effects not fully re-extracted; source emphasized low-quality evidence due to risk of bias and inconsistency. Limitations: Evidence quality low; hospital adults receiving pharmacologic prophylaxis; not consumer setting.

**Why it matters:** It defines the supervised clinical boundary for IPC use and helps prevent over-claiming consumer benefits.

**Potential experiment signals:** Skin irritation or skin breaks; Discomfort/tolerance; Falls or tubing/device hazards; Adherence or wear time; DVT/PE only as clinician-managed safety context.

**Protocol takeaway:** Hospital adjunctive-use evidence should remain context-only.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
