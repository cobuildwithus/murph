This source is included for **Clinical DVT prophylaxis and hospital boundary**.

**Findings:** Primary outcome occurred in 122/1438 (8.5%) IPC patients vs 174/1438 (12.1%) controls; absolute risk reduction 3.6% (95% CI 1.4 to 5.8), adjusted OR 0.65 (95% CI 0.51 to 0.84; p=0.001). Six-month survival HR was 0.86 (95% CI 0.73 to 0.99; p=0.042), while disability did not improve. Safety/adverse-event notes: Skin breaks were more frequent with IPC: 44/1438 (3.1%) vs 20/1438 (1.4%), p=0.002. Falls were 33 (2.3%) vs 24 (1.7%), p=0.221. Limitations: Acute immobile stroke population in hospitals; clinical staff applied/monitored IPC; not a consumer or at-home protocol.

**Why it matters:** It defines the supervised clinical boundary for IPC use and helps prevent over-claiming consumer benefits.

**Potential experiment signals:** Skin breaks/irritation; Falls/tripping hazards; Clinician-monitored DVT/PE outcomes only; Adherence/wear-time feasibility.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use CLOTS 3 only to explain why DVT-risk users belong under medical care; do not turn it into a consumer DVT-prevention claim.
