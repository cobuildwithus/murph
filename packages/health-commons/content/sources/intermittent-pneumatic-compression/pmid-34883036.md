This source is included for **Clinical edema, lymphedema, venous, PAD, and wound-care boundary**.

**Findings:** LymphAssist sequencing reduced distal leg volume more than sequential mode (230±135 mL vs 140±84 mL, p=0.01). Volume rebounded during washout, and QoL improved (mean score reduction 10±11, p<0.001).

**Why it matters:** Shows sequence design can matter, and that benefits may be transient without regular use.

**Potential experiment signals:** Distal leg volume, QoL score, Washout rebound, Adherence.

**Protocol takeaway:** Context-only; useful for dose sequencing and durability endpoints.

**Claim use:** `context-only`. This is not direct unsupervised consumer-recovery evidence.

**Safety and boundary notes:** No major adverse-event signal was located in accessible extraction; rebound indicates effects may require ongoing treatment.

**Limitations:** Pilot crossover study.; Lymphedema population and medical device.; Treatment sequencing, not consumer recovery, was the main contrast..
