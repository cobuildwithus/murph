This source is included for **Clinical DVT prophylaxis and hospital boundary**.

**Findings:** The review reported IPC was better than GCS and similar to LMWH for VTE prevention in critically ill patients. IPC versus LMWH was not significantly different (RR 1.26, 95% CI 0.72 to 2.22; p=0.41). Adjunctive IPC plus pharmacologic prophylaxis did not significantly further reduce VTE incidence versus pharmacologic prophylaxis alone (RR 0.55, 95% CI 0.24 to 1.27; p=0.16). Safety/adverse-event notes: LMWH was associated with more bleeding in the review context; exact bleeding effect size not re-extracted. Limitations: Critical-care evidence; participant count not re-extracted; adjunctive benefit was not statistically significant.

**Why it matters:** It defines the supervised clinical boundary for IPC use and helps prevent over-claiming consumer benefits.

**Potential experiment signals:** Skin irritation or skin breaks; Discomfort/tolerance; Falls or tubing/device hazards; Adherence or wear time; DVT/PE only as clinician-managed safety context.

**Protocol takeaway:** Do not claim “more compression is always better.”

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
