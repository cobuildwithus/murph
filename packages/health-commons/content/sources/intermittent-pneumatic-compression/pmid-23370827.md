This source is included for **Clinical DVT prophylaxis and hospital boundary**.

**Findings:** In 407 randomized high-bleeding-risk ICU patients, IPC plus graduated compression stockings was compared with stockings alone for 6 days. The day-6 primary VTE endpoint was assessed in 363 patients and occurred in 5.6% of IPC+GCS patients versus 9.2% of GCS-only patients (RR 0.60, 95% CI 0.28-1.28; p=0.19). Poor tolerance was reported in 12 patients (6.0%), and mortality did not differ between groups.

**Why it matters:** This RCT is useful for defining the clinical thromboprophylaxis boundary and for preserving a null/mixed result rather than treating all IPC evidence as uniformly positive.

**Potential experiment signals:** Skin irritation or intolerance; Adherence / time in device; New unilateral leg swelling or pain requiring medical assessment; No post-exercise recovery endpoint measured.

**Protocol takeaway:** Do not generalize ICU thromboprophylaxis findings to unsupervised recovery pants; the short-term VTE effect was directionally favorable but not statistically significant.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Population mismatch:** Critically ill hospitalized adults with high bleeding risk are clinically and behaviorally far from unsupervised consumer recovery pants users.

**Limitations:** High-bleeding-risk ICU population, not healthy or athletic users.; Comparator was stockings alone, not no treatment or a recovery routine.; Primary result was not statistically significant and trial power was limited.; Short six-day supervised treatment window..
