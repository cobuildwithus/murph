This source is included for **Travel comfort, flight edema, and VTE boundary**.

**Findings:** Population/count: Passengers after flights of at least 8 hours and nontraveling controls; people using anticoagulants or compression stockings were excluded. Participant count: 2177 (analyzed_964_passengers_1213_controls). Intervention/exposure: Long-haul flight exposure. Comparator/control: Nontraveling control group. Duration/follow-up: Assessment after flights; 4-week follow-up for new events. Endpoints: calf muscle venous thrombosis, deep venous thrombosis, pulmonary embolism, mortality. Effect/direction: Thrombotic events occurred in 27/964 passengers versus 12/1213 controls; relative risk 2.83. Deep VT was 7/964 versus 2/1213; one symptomatic PE occurred among passengers. Safety/adverse events: All passengers with thrombosis had at least one risk factor; one symptomatic PE was observed; no mortality. Limitations: Observational cohort, not an intervention study.; Baseline differences and selection bias were noted.; Not general population evidence for consumer compression devices. Population mismatch: Travel exposure cohort; no pneumatic pants intervention.

**Why it matters:** Helps quantify why travel-related clot claims should be handled as medical safety context.

**Potential experiment signals:** calf muscle venous thrombosis, deep venous thrombosis, pulmonary embolism, mortality.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as VTE boundary context only; do not claim pneumatic pants prevent flight-related thrombosis.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
