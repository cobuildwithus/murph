This source is included for **Travel comfort, flight edema, and VTE boundary**.

**Findings:** Population/count: Healthy volunteers in simulated 12-hour long-distance flight sessions. Participant count: 12 (healthy_volunteers). Intervention/exposure: Simulated 12-hour sitting sessions with repeated leg exercise in some sessions. Comparator/control: Constant sitting during simulated flights. Duration/follow-up: Four simulated 12-hour flights including day/night conditions. Endpoints: lower leg edema, fluid balance, rheology, effect of leg exercise. Effect/direction: Lower-leg volume significantly increased but not pathologically; periodic leg exercising showed no measurable preventive effect. Changes were considered within physiologic variation for healthy passengers but potentially relevant to predisposed passengers. Safety/adverse events: Could intensify risk in predisposed passengers despite physiologic range in healthy volunteers. Limitations: Small simulated-flight physiology study.; Healthy volunteers; no clinical VTE outcomes.; Leg exercise, not pneumatic pants. Population mismatch: Simulation and exercise physiology, not IPC pants.

**Why it matters:** Important background for economy-class swelling and physiological limits.

**Potential experiment signals:** lower leg edema, fluid balance, rheology, effect of leg exercise.

**Protocol takeaway:** Background/null result only: swelling can occur during long sitting, and not all movement protocols prevent edema.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
