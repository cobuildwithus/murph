This source is included for **Clinical edema, lymphedema, venous, PAD, and wound-care boundary**.

**Findings:** All three pressures significantly increased foot skin blood flow during IPC in healthy subjects, while only 90 and 120 mmHg significantly improved foot skin blood flow in diabetic subjects; 120 mmHg appeared most suitable for diabetic subjects.

**Why it matters:** Useful for dose/pressure mechanism and diabetes/PAD screening boundaries.

**Potential experiment signals:** Foot skin blood flow, Pressure response, Diabetes status, Exclusion-screening variables.

**Protocol takeaway:** Context-only mechanism; do not infer recovery efficacy.

**Claim use:** `context-only`. This is not direct unsupervised consumer-recovery evidence.

**Safety and boundary notes:** The study excluded people with PAD, prior foot ulcer/amputation, inflammation/lesions, severe neuropathy, renal/retinal/liver disease, cancer, coronary heart disease, or vascular surgery history.

**Limitations:** Acute physiology only; no clinical outcomes.; Small sample; exact group split not extracted.; Exclusion criteria limit generalization to higher-risk diabetic users..
