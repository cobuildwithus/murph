This source is included for **Travel comfort, flight edema, and VTE boundary**.

**Findings:** Population/count: Travelers, including higher-risk travelers, addressed in travel-related venous thrombosis guidelines. Participant count: 0 (guideline_not_applicable). Intervention/exposure: Mobility, compression hosiery, and pharmacologic prophylaxis recommendations. Comparator/control: No universal prophylaxis. Duration/follow-up: Journeys longer than 3 hours; risk attribution up to 8 weeks after travel. Endpoints: guideline recommendations, travel-related VTE risk, compression hosiery, pharmacologic prophylaxis. Effect/direction: Guideline states long-duration travel is a weak VTE risk factor; global use of compression stockings or anticoagulants is not indicated, while highest-risk travelers on journeys longer than 3 hours may use well-fitted below-knee compression hosiery and consider pharmacologic prophylaxis. Safety/adverse events: Severe symptomatic PE is extremely rare on shorter journeys; travel-related VTE may be attributed up to 8 weeks after travel. Limitations: Guideline, not trial evidence.; Addresses hosiery and medications, not consumer pneumatic pants.; Recommendations depend on risk stratification. Population mismatch: Clinical travel guideline rather than IPC pants.

**Why it matters:** Guideline-level source for the boundary between comfort products and medical thromboprophylaxis.

**Potential experiment signals:** guideline recommendations, travel-related VTE risk, compression hosiery, pharmacologic prophylaxis.

**Protocol takeaway:** Do not advise universal pneumatic-pants use for DVT prevention; high-risk travelers need clinician-directed guidance.
