This source is included for **Travel comfort, flight edema, and VTE boundary**.

**Findings:** Population/count: Older healthy volunteers without VTE or heart failure in a seated immobilization physiology study. Participant count: 6 (volunteers). Intervention/exposure: AviaFit intermittent mechanical compression device on both calves, about 45 mmHg for 7 seconds once per minute. Comparator/control: Seated immobilization without the device. Duration/follow-up: 4 hours of seated immobilization. Endpoints: popliteal venous flow velocity, contraction peak flow velocity, travellers thrombosis mechanism. Effect/direction: Reported popliteal venous flow velocity increased almost twofold versus no device after 4 hours of seated immobilization; baseline-adjusted absolute increase was imprecise. Contraction peak flow was much higher during compression pulses. Safety/adverse events: No clinical outcomes or adverse-event data extracted; authors called for further experimental/clinical evaluation. Limitations: Very small acute physiology study.; Surrogate venous-flow endpoint only.; Device was calf mechanical compression, not consumer pants. Population mismatch: Same mechanism but not protocol form factor and no clinical endpoint.

**Why it matters:** Closest same-mechanism travel source, but extremely small and surrogate-only.

**Potential experiment signals:** popliteal venous flow velocity, contraction peak flow velocity, travellers thrombosis mechanism.

**Protocol takeaway:** Mechanism only: IPC may move venous blood during seated immobility, but this does not prove clot prevention or travel comfort for pants.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
