This source is included for **Lens dose and implementation guardrail**.

**Findings:** The study reports that spectral sensitivity for melatonin suppression and phase resetting changes with exposure duration; cone contribution is more prominent early, while melanopsin contribution becomes more prominent during later sustained exposure.

**Why it matters:** Strengthens the mechanistic rationale that exposure duration changes which photoreceptor pathways dominate melatonin suppression and phase resetting.

**Potential experiment signals:** melatonin suppression, circadian phase resetting, spectral sensitivity, photoreceptor contribution over exposure duration

**Protocol takeaway:** Evening glasses are most plausibly useful when they reduce sustained melanopic/short-wavelength exposure, especially over the pre-bed window.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

## Extraction notes

- **Study design:** Controlled inpatient mechanism/dose-response study.
- **Population or measurement target:** Healthy young adults in a nine-day inpatient protocol.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Nine-day inpatient protocol with 6.5-hour light exposures.
- **Directness to Red Light Glasses Before Bed:** `same_mechanism`.
- **Population mismatch:** Healthy young adults in inpatient protocol; not broader home-use population.
- **Safety/adverse-event notes:** None extracted.

## Limitations

- No glasses intervention.
- Inpatient monochromatic exposures differ from consumer environments.
- Re-used data from earlier light-exposure work are included in the analysis.
