This source is included for **Lens dose and implementation guardrail**.

**Findings:** The study found that circadian spectral response depends on irradiance and exposure duration, with cone contribution more prominent early/at lower irradiance and melanopsin contribution becoming more prominent with longer or higher-irradiance exposure.

**Why it matters:** Supports the mechanism that short-wavelength exposure is especially relevant after sustained evening/night exposure, while dose and duration determine the response.

**Potential experiment signals:** melatonin suppression, circadian spectral sensitivity, irradiance-response, duration-dependent photoreceptor contribution

**Protocol takeaway:** Frame glasses as a dose-reduction tool for sustained evening exposure, not as a binary blue/no-blue switch.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

## Extraction notes

- **Study design:** Controlled laboratory spectral-response study.
- **Population or measurement target:** Healthy individuals in controlled laboratory light exposure conditions.
- **Duration/follow-up:** 6.5-hour equal-photon-density monochromatic light exposure.
- **Directness to Red Light Glasses Before Bed:** `same_mechanism`.
- **Population mismatch:** Controlled laboratory light exposure in healthy people; not routine home glasses use.
- **Safety/adverse-event notes:** None extracted.

## Limitations

- No red-light glasses intervention.
- Small laboratory sample.
- Monochromatic exposures differ from screens and room lighting.
