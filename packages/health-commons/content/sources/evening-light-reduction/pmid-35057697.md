This source is included for **Safety, vision, or eye-health claim boundary**.

**Findings:** In 23 healthy young adults, commercial blue-blocking filters did not reduce orbicularis oculi muscle activity or visual symptomatology during 30-minute screen-reading tasks.

**Why it matters:** Adds null adjacent evidence for digital-eye-strain relief claims.

**Potential experiment signals:** orbicularis oculi muscle activity, visual symptomatology, digital eye strain signs

**Protocol takeaway:** Do not promise eye-strain symptom relief from the bedtime protocol.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Study design:** Controlled acute screen-reading study.
- **Population or measurement target:** Healthy young adults performing screen-reading tasks.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Commercial blue-blocking filters.
- **Comparator/control:** Control/no blue-blocking filter condition.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Two 30-minute screen-reading tasks.
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Commercial blue-blocking filters were ineffective in reducing orbicularis oculi activity and visual symptoms during the 30-minute reading tasks.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No clinical adverse-event rate was extracted.
- **Limitations:** Small healthy young adult sample.; Filter/screen task is adjacent to spectacle-based bedtime use.
- **Directness to Red Light Glasses Before Bed:** `measurement_context`.
- **Population mismatch:** Screen-reading task in young adults, not red glasses before sleep.
