This source is included for **Lens dose and implementation guardrail**.

**Findings:** Commercial lenses reduced 400-500 nm blue light by about 6% to 43%, circadian sensitivity by about 4% to 27%, and also affected scotopic sensitivity and blue perception, indicating wide product variability and visibility tradeoffs.

**Why it matters:** Demonstrates that blue-blocking products are not interchangeable and may also affect visual functions.

**Potential experiment signals:** 400-500 nm transmission reduction, circadian sensitivity modelling, scotopic sensitivity modelling, blue perception

**Protocol takeaway:** Do not treat commercial blue-blocking labels as equivalent; lens choice must balance melanopic reduction with visibility and task safety.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

## Extraction notes

- **Study design:** Bench spectral modelling study.
- **Population or measurement target:** Seven blue-blocking lens types from six manufacturers plus controls.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Bench measurement and modelling; no follow-up.
- **Directness to Red Light Glasses Before Bed:** `measurement_context`.
- **Population mismatch:** No human participants; optical modelling only.
- **Safety/adverse-event notes:** Modelled reductions in scotopic sensitivity and blue perception indicate a task-visibility boundary for stronger filters.

## Limitations

- Bench modelling rather than clinical outcome study.
- Only seven lens types tested.
- Visual-function impacts are modelled, not adverse-event rates.
