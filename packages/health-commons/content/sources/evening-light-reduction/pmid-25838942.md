This source is included for **Safety, vision, or eye-health claim boundary**.

**Findings:** A randomized crossover study in 154 bilaterally pseudophakic patients reported improved photostress recovery and glare-disability measures with a short-wave absorbing filter, but the population is a major mismatch to consumer bedtime use.

**Why it matters:** Shows that some clinical visual outcomes can improve in special populations, preventing a one-sided safety story while preserving mismatch.

**Potential experiment signals:** photostress recovery time, glare disability threshold

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as clinical-supervised context only; do not apply to general bedtime protocol efficacy.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Study design:** Patient-masked randomized crossover study in pseudophakic patients.
- **Population or measurement target:** Bilaterally pseudophakic patients at multiple clinical sites.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Short-wave absorbing/blue-light filtering clip-on or glasses filter.
- **Comparator/control:** Control or non-short-wave absorbing condition.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Crossover visual testing during study visits.
- **Effect estimates or direction:** In pseudophakic patients, the short-wave absorbing filter improved photostress recovery and glare-disability measures in the extracted summary.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse-event rate was extracted; the main safety relevance is clinical glare/photostress context.
- **Limitations:** Special pseudophakic clinical population.; Glare/photostress outcomes do not establish bedtime sleep efficacy.
- **Directness to Red Light Glasses Before Bed:** `measurement_context`.
- **Population mismatch:** Pseudophakic patients, not healthy home users.
