This source is included for **Lens dose and implementation guardrail**.

**Findings:** Mean 400-455 nm transmission was reported as 58.76 ± 3.01% with blue-light-blocking coating versus 83.10 ± 1.71% without coating, showing partial attenuation but substantial residual transmission.

**Why it matters:** Shows that common coatings can attenuate some blue light but may still transmit a large fraction of short-wavelength light.

**Potential experiment signals:** 400-455 nm blue-light transmission, coated versus uncoated lens comparison

**Protocol takeaway:** Blue-light-blocking coatings are not automatically equivalent to bedtime red/amber light-reduction glasses.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Study design:** Bench optical measurement report.
- **Population or measurement target:** Spectacle lenses with and without blue-light-blocking coating.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Bench measurement; no follow-up.
- **Directness to Red Light Glasses Before Bed:** `measurement_context`.
- **Population mismatch:** No human participants; spectacle-lens testing only.
- **Safety/adverse-event notes:** No adverse events measured.

## Limitations

- Short report.
- No clinical outcomes or sleep endpoints.
- Terminology focuses on blue-light hazard, not circadian timing.
