This source is included for **Melanopic/evening-light mechanism**.

**Findings:**

- Both bright evening light conditions increased subjective alertness and reduced salivary melatonin compared with dim light, supporting the mechanism that evening light dose can influence melatonin and alertness.

- Solid angle primarily affected visual comfort in this setup, providing an implementation guardrail that corneal illuminance or lens color alone may not capture user experience.

**Why it matters:** Directly supports the biological premise that evening light characteristics can change melatonin and alertness, while separating light-exposure mechanism from glasses efficacy.

**Potential experiment signals:** salivary melatonin, subjective alertness, cognitive performance, visual comfort

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as same-mechanism support for reducing bright evening light; do not present it as a red-glasses trial.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

## Extraction notes

- **Study design:** Within-subject acute physiology study
- **Population or measurement target:** Adult participants in a laboratory automotive-light setting
- **Intervention/exposure or measurement:** bright versus dim evening light exposure in an automotive setting
- **Comparator/control:** Dim-light condition, plus comparison between bright-light conditions with different solid angles.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Three-hour evening light exposure sessions
- **Directness to Red Light Glasses Before Bed:** `same_mechanism`.
- **Population mismatch:** Automotive laboratory light exposure rather than at-home bedtime glasses.
- **Safety/adverse-event notes:** No protocol adverse-event finding extracted; visual comfort was measured as an implementation-relevant endpoint.

## Limitations

- Small acute laboratory study.
- Tests bright light exposure, not a filtering intervention.
- Automotive setting may not generalize to home bedroom routines.
