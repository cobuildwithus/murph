This source is included for **Safety, vision, or eye-health claim boundary**.

**Findings:** In a double-masked RCT of 120 adults during extended screen time, blue-blocking lenses did not show a clear visual-fatigue or critical flicker-fusion advantage over clear lenses in the extracted review data.

**Why it matters:** A comparatively large RCT limiting claims about blue blockers reducing eye strain from screen exposure.

**Potential experiment signals:** visual fatigue score, critical flicker-fusion frequency

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as adjacent null evidence for digital-eye-strain claims.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Study design:** Double-masked randomized controlled trial during extended screen time.
- **Population or measurement target:** Adults completing extended screen-time tasks.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Blue-blocking spectacle lenses.
- **Comparator/control:** Clear or non-blue-blocking spectacle lenses.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Acute extended screen-time exposure.
- **Effect estimates or direction:** The extracted review data showed no clear advantage: visual fatigue mean difference was imprecise and critical flicker-fusion frequency did not clearly favor blue-blocking lenses.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No distinct adverse-event rate was extracted for this source in the batch.
- **Limitations:** Screen-time eye-strain trial, not bedtime sleep trial.; Effect estimates are for visual fatigue/CFF, not sleep.
- **Directness to Red Light Glasses Before Bed:** `measurement_context`.
- **Population mismatch:** Extended screen-time task participants rather than bedtime glasses users.
