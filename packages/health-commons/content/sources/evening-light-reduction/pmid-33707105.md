This source is included for **Healthy-adult mixed/null direct trial**.

**Findings:** `finding:pmid-33707105-healthy-adult-null`: Blue-light blocking glasses did not improve objective sleep time or quality in healthy adults; total sleep time trended shorter in the active condition for subjective TST (468 ± 45 vs 480 ± 48 min; p=.066) and objective TST (433 ± 40 vs 449 ± 39 min; p=.075).; `finding:pmid-33707105-subgroup-signals`: The authors reported some significant improvements in subjective awakenings/sleep onset latency and objective awakenings among self-reported poor sleepers, but these signals were interpreted cautiously given no overall objective duration or quality benefit.

**Why it matters:** Important direct null trial for preventing overclaims, especially in low-symptom or healthy users.

**Potential experiment signals:** actigraphy total sleep time, subjective total sleep time, objective sleep quality, subjective sleep onset latency, awakenings

**Protocol takeaway:** Healthy adults may see little or no objective benefit from evening blue-light blocking glasses, even if some subjective subgroup signals appear.

**Limitations and mismatch:** Healthy adult sample; small n; some subgroup/self-report signals require cautious interpretation; not a symptomatic-insomnia trial. Population mismatch: Healthy adults with no diagnosed sleep disorder; may not represent high screen-exposure or insomnia users.

**Safety notes:** No adverse-event data extracted from accessible abstract metadata.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
