This source is included for **Safety, vision, or eye-health claim boundary**.

**Findings:** A Cochrane review of 51 RCTs of blue-light filtering intraocular lenses, involving more than 5000 eyes in the extracted summary, did not support strong macular-health protection claims; the surgical IOL population is not comparable to bedtime glasses users.

**Why it matters:** Important boundary for macular-health claims, especially when lens marketing cites retinal protection.

**Potential experiment signals:** macular health, visual performance, post-cataract outcomes, retinal-protection claims

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as eye-health claim boundary only.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Study design:** Cochrane systematic review of blue-light filtering intraocular lenses.
- **Population or measurement target:** People undergoing cataract surgery or receiving intraocular lenses.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Blue-light filtering intraocular lenses.
- **Comparator/control:** Non-blue-light filtering intraocular lenses.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Included study follow-up ranged from about one month to five years.
- **Effect estimates or direction:** The review covered 51 RCTs and more than 5000 eyes; extracted conclusions did not support strong claims that blue-light filtering IOLs protect macular health.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No reusable adverse-event rate was extracted in this batch.
- **Limitations:** IOL surgical population is a major mismatch to consumer glasses.; Participant count was reported as eyes in the extracted summary, so participantCount is omitted.
- **Directness to Red Light Glasses Before Bed:** `measurement_context`.
- **Population mismatch:** Cataract/IOL surgical population rather than healthy adults wearing glasses before bed.
