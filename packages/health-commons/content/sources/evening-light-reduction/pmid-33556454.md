This source is included for **Clinical or special-population supervised variant**.

**Findings:**

- This child and adolescent psychiatry review frames circadian rhythm sleep disorders as clinically important and transdiagnostic, but it is not a bedtime-glasses efficacy trial and should be used only for pediatric/psychiatric boundaries.

**Why it matters:** Identifies pediatric and psychiatric circadian contexts that require clinical framing rather than wellness self-experiment claims.

**Potential experiment signals:** sleep timing, circadian phase, mood stability, pediatric safety boundary

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as a special-population boundary; do not cite it as evidence that red-light glasses improve sleep in adults.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Study design:** Narrative clinical review
- **Population or measurement target:** Children and adolescents with psychiatric conditions
- **Intervention/exposure or measurement:** clinical circadian rhythm disorder assessment and management
- **Comparator/control:** Not applicable; narrative clinical review.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Not applicable
- **Directness to Red Light Glasses Before Bed:** `clinical_supervised`.
- **Population mismatch:** Pediatric/adolescent psychiatric populations differ from ordinary adult bedtime protocol users.
- **Safety/adverse-event notes:** Pediatric and psychiatric circadian interventions should be clinician-supervised.

## Limitations

- Narrative review rather than trial or meta-analysis.
- Population and clinical goals differ from general adult home use.
- Does not isolate red or blue-blocking glasses before bed.
