This source is included for **Safety, vision, or eye-health claim boundary**.

**Findings:** In optical and clinical testing of blue-light filtering spectacle lenses, modeled blue-light reductions did not translate into significant measured losses in contrast sensitivity or color discrimination in the extracted clinical endpoints, but scotopic-sensitivity modeling signals a low-light visibility boundary.

**Why it matters:** Links lens spectral filtering to potential visual-performance tradeoffs and implementation fidelity.

**Potential experiment signals:** phototoxicity reduction estimate, scotopic sensitivity, melatonin-suppression model, contrast sensitivity, color discrimination, subjective optical changes

**Protocol takeaway:** Use for lens-dose and visibility safety context, not as direct bedtime red-glasses sleep evidence.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Study design:** Optical laboratory study plus pseudo-randomized controlled lens-wear study.
- **Population or measurement target:** Young and middle-aged adult computer users in optical/clinical performance testing.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Commercial blue-light filtering spectacle lenses with measured spectral transmittance.
- **Comparator/control:** Regular clear lenses or lower-filtering lenses.
- **Duration/follow-up:** One month of lens wear in the clinical component.
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Five tested lenses showed modeled reductions in phototoxicity, scotopic sensitivity, and melatonin suppression; the clinical component reported no significant reduction in contrast sensitivity or color discrimination and most participants could not detect optical changes.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No serious adverse-event signal was extracted; modeled scotopic-sensitivity reduction is a task-visibility boundary.
- **Limitations:** Includes modeled optical outcomes rather than bedtime sleep outcomes.; Sponsor/manufacturer involvement may affect interpretation.; Blue-filtering lens dose is not equivalent to red lenses before bed.
- **Directness to Red Light Glasses Before Bed:** `measurement_context`.
- **Population mismatch:** Computer-user optical-performance context, not a bedtime home sleep experiment.
