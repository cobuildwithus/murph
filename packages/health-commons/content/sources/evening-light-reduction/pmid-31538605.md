This source is included for **Measurement and wearable endpoint context**.

**Findings:**

- In adolescents, consumer and research wearables showed useful sleep/wake measurement capacity but systematic biases versus polysomnography, including underestimation of sleep duration and limitations in sleep-stage interpretation.

**Why it matters:** Helps prevent overinterpretation of consumer sleep-tracker changes during Murph self-experiments, especially sleep stages and small total-sleep-time shifts.

**Potential experiment signals:** total sleep time, wake after sleep onset, sleep stages, consumer wearable validity

**Protocol takeaway:** Use to qualify wearable-derived outcomes; do not treat consumer sleep-stage changes as definitive protocol effects.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Study design:** Wearable and actigraphy validation study with PSG comparator
- **Population or measurement target:** Healthy adolescents aged 15 to 19 years
- **Intervention/exposure or measurement:** consumer sleep wearable and research actigraphy measurement
- **Comparator/control:** Polysomnography reference standard, with research-grade actigraphy as an additional comparator to the consumer wearable.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Nine laboratory PSG nights across restricted and extended sleep opportunities
- **Directness to Red Light Glasses Before Bed:** `measurement_context`.
- **Population mismatch:** Adolescent laboratory sample, not adult home protocol users.
- **Safety/adverse-event notes:** No protocol adverse-event finding; measurement-only source.

## Limitations

- Population is adolescents under manipulated sleep opportunity conditions.
- Measurement bias can vary by device, algorithm, and firmware.
- Findings support measurement guardrails, not protocol efficacy.
