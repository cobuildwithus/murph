This source is included for **Lens dose and implementation guardrail**.

**Findings:** The review/product analysis reports wide variation in commercial glasses and argues that color or marketing label alone is insufficient; products need adequate melanopic filtering and correct use to justify sleep/circadian claims.

**Why it matters:** Shows why lens color and marketing labels are insufficient; spectral filtering strength, fit, edge leakage, timing, and use conditions determine circadian relevance.

**Potential experiment signals:** melanopic daylight filtering density, spectral transmittance, sleep and circadian outcome interpretation, implementation fidelity

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as the product-quality guardrail and avoid brand recommendations unless spectral data is available.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Study design:** Narrative review with product spectral characterization.
- **Population or measurement target:** Blue-blocking spectacle literature and 26 commercial glasses characterized by melanopic daylight filtering density.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Review-level source; no single intervention duration.
- **Directness to Red Light Glasses Before Bed:** `same_mechanism`.
- **Population mismatch:** Review/product characterization rather than a single user cohort.
- **Safety/adverse-event notes:** Strong filters may affect visibility or color perception depending on lens design and environment; implementation needs a task-visibility boundary.

## Limitations

- Narrative review rather than systematic pooled estimate.
- Commercial product results may change as products change.
- Efficacy depends on actual spectral filtering and use conditions.
