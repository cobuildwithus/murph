This source is included for **Safety, vision, or eye-health claim boundary**.

**Findings:** Blue-blocking lenses reduced colour contrast sensitivity for blue colours in acute testing, particularly at low contrasts/luminance and with stronger filtering lenses.

**Why it matters:** Relevant to safety labeling for tinted lenses in dim home environments.

**Potential experiment signals:** colour contrast sensitivity, blue colour discrimination, low-contrast/luminance performance

**Protocol takeaway:** Do not claim no visual tradeoff; caution users about color/contrast changes.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Study design:** Acute visual-performance study.
- **Population or measurement target:** Participants in color contrast-sensitivity testing.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Blue-blocking lenses with differing transmittance profiles.
- **Comparator/control:** Control or higher-transmittance lens conditions.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Acute lens-testing sessions.
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Blue-blocking lenses impaired colour contrast sensitivity for blue colours, especially at low contrast or luminance and with lower-transmittance lenses.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No clinical adverse-event rate was extracted; the safety issue is color/contrast visibility reduction.
- **Limitations:** Acute visual task, not sleep protocol.; Participant count was not extracted in this batch.
- **Directness to Red Light Glasses Before Bed:** `measurement_context`.
- **Population mismatch:** Visual psychophysics setting rather than bedtime self-experiment.
