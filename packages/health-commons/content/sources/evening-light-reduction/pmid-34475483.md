This source is included for **Safety, vision, or eye-health claim boundary**.

**Findings:** In acute testing, blue-light filtering lenses reduced perceived speed of chromatic and achromatic stimuli by about 6% to 20%, indicating a motion-perception safety boundary.

**Why it matters:** Supports caution about moving around or performing tasks while wearing strong filters in dim conditions.

**Potential experiment signals:** perceived speed, motion perception, visual performance

**Protocol takeaway:** Do not claim tinted lenses are visually neutral; include safety caution for mobility/tasks.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Study design:** Acute visual psychophysics study.
- **Population or measurement target:** Participants in speed-perception testing.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Blue-light filtering lenses.
- **Comparator/control:** Control or less-filtering lens conditions.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Acute lens-testing sessions.
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Blue-light filtering lenses reduced perceived speed of chromatic and achromatic stimuli by roughly 6% to 20%, with stronger filtering producing larger reductions.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No clinical adverse-event rate was extracted; the safety boundary is altered motion perception.
- **Limitations:** Acute laboratory perception task.; Not a bedtime or sleep outcome.
- **Directness to Red Light Glasses Before Bed:** `measurement_context`.
- **Population mismatch:** Visual perception task population, not bedtime experiment users.
