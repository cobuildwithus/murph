This source is included for **Lens dose and implementation guardrail**.

**Findings:** The filter set showed large diversity in visual and non-visual properties; filters could attenuate melanopsin more than luminance, but color and gamut tradeoffs must be considered.

**Why it matters:** Provides a general measurement framework for distinguishing melanopsin reduction from luminance loss and color/visual side effects.

**Potential experiment signals:** melanopsin attenuation, luminance attenuation, color shift, gamut reduction

**Protocol takeaway:** Lens selection should consider melanopsin attenuation relative to luminance and color distortion, not only tint color.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

## Extraction notes

- **Study design:** Bench filter-characterization study.
- **Population or measurement target:** 120 filters designed to manipulate short-wavelength light.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Bench measurement; no follow-up.
- **Directness to Red Light Glasses Before Bed:** `measurement_context`.
- **Population mismatch:** No human participants; filters measured optically.
- **Safety/adverse-event notes:** Color shift and luminance attenuation can matter for task visibility and adherence.

## Limitations

- No direct user outcomes.
- Filters may not correspond exactly to consumer red-light glasses.
- Findings are optical/measurement rather than clinical.
