This source is included for **Clinical supervised insomnia adjunct**.

**Findings:** `finding:pmid-31752544-cbti-adjunct-sleep-symptoms`: Active glasses produced a greater Beck Anxiety Inventory reduction than placebo (4.33 ± 4.58 vs -0.92 ± 3.68; F=6.389, p=.019, d=1.26); within the active group, subjective sleep latency decreased (t=2.65, p=.021, d=0.73) and subjective total sleep time increased (t=-2.73, p=.018, d=-0.76).; `finding:pmid-31752544-objective-boundary`: Objective sleep duration did not increase in the active-glasses group and was significantly shortened in the placebo group; because all participants received CBT-I, this is adjunct evidence rather than standalone eyewear evidence.

**Why it matters:** Clinical supervised insomnia-adjunct evidence, but the intervention is bundled with CBT-I and should not be used as standalone-glasses causality.

**Potential experiment signals:** anxiety symptoms, subjective sleep onset latency, subjective total sleep time, objective sleep duration, depression symptoms, hyperarousal

**Protocol takeaway:** Active blue-light filtering glasses added to CBT-I improved some subjective sleep and symptom outcomes, but standalone eyewear effects cannot be isolated from CBT-I context.

**Limitations and mismatch:** Glasses were added to CBT-I; small sample; objective sleep duration did not increase in the active group; not a standalone protocol trial. Population mismatch: Adults in insomnia treatment, not unstructured self-experimenters.

**Safety notes:** No adverse-event counts extracted from accessible abstract metadata.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
