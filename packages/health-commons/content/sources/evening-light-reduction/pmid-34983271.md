This source is included for **Lens dose and implementation guardrail**.

**Findings:** Red-tinted lenses had the lowest circadian-proficient light transmission, while orange lenses showed high transmission specificity by limiting biologically active light while transmitting more non-circadian-proficient light under ordinary conditions.

**Why it matters:** Shows that lens performance depends on both lens type and the light source encountered in real evenings.

**Potential experiment signals:** absolute irradiance 380-780 nm, circadian-proficient light transmission, non-circadian-proficient light transmission, transmission specificity

**Protocol takeaway:** Pair lens-selection language with room/screen-light context; red/orange lenses may differ in specificity and tolerability.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Study design:** Bench spectrophotometric study.
- **Population or measurement target:** 50 commercial blue-blocking lenses measured under five lighting conditions.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Bench measurement; no follow-up.
- **Directness to Red Light Glasses Before Bed:** `measurement_context`.
- **Population mismatch:** No human participants; lens measurements only.
- **Safety/adverse-event notes:** Transmission tradeoffs may affect perceived visibility and adherence but adverse events were not measured.

## Limitations

- No biological or sleep outcomes.
- Commercial product landscape can change.
- Bench measurements do not account for fit, edge leakage, or adherence.
