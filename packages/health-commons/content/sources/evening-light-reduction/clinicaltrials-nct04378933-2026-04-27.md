This source is included for **Melanopic/evening-light mechanism**.

**Findings:** The GLAD registry/protocol record describes an adolescent DSWPD glasses trial with circadian-phase and objective sleep endpoints; it is a pediatric clinical context source and does not provide an extracted efficacy result here.

**Why it matters:** Captures adolescent circadian-phase trial design using amber glasses, actigraphy, subjective sleepiness, and salivary DLMO endpoints.

**Potential experiment signals:** actigraphic total sleep time, sleep onset, daytime sleepiness, salivary DLMO, adherence

**Protocol takeaway:** Use for adolescent circadian-phase and safety-boundary context, not as adult consumer efficacy evidence.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Study design:** Clinical trial registry/protocol record for a randomized adolescent field study.
- **Population or measurement target:** Adolescents aged 14 to 17 with delayed sleep-wake phase disorder.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Three-week field study structure with baseline and intervention phases.
- **Directness to Red Light Glasses Before Bed:** `clinical_supervised`.
- **Population mismatch:** Adolescents with delayed sleep-wake phase disorder; comparator and wake-time instructions may not match the target protocol.
- **Safety/adverse-event notes:** No adverse events were extracted from the registry/protocol record in this batch; pediatric use should be treated as clinician- or parent-supervised.

## Limitations

- No source-owned outcome result is extracted from this registry/protocol record.
- Adolescent circadian disorder population differs from adult self-experiment users.
- Stable wake-time and scheduling components may confound eyewear-specific interpretation.
