This source is included for **Lens dose and implementation guardrail**.

**Findings:** The review argues that blue-light retinal-hazard evidence is based on abnormally intense exposures and should not be generalized to ordinary ambient light; blue light also supports scotopic/mesopic vision and other health-related functions.; The review notes that filtering blue light can have visual-function consequences in dim environments, supporting a practical safety boundary against using strong filters for navigation, driving, or color-critical tasks.

**Why it matters:** Prevents the bedtime protocol from drifting into unsupported retinal-hazard marketing or all-day blue-light avoidance.

**Potential experiment signals:** retinal blue-light hazard claim boundary, scotopic and mesopic vision, blue-filtering lens claims, fall/visibility context

**Protocol takeaway:** Use glasses for evening circadian-light reduction only; avoid retinal-hazard claims and warn about dim-environment visibility when strong filters are used.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Study design:** Narrative review / perspective.
- **Population or measurement target:** Blue-light retinal-hazard and blue-filtering lens claims in ordinary environments and ophthalmic contexts.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Review-level source; no single follow-up duration.
- **Directness to Red Light Glasses Before Bed:** `safety_boundary`.
- **Population mismatch:** Ophthalmic and retinal-hazard framing differs from healthy adult bedtime self-experimentation.
- **Safety/adverse-event notes:** Suppressing blue light can matter for scotopic/mesopic vision; strong filters should not be used for driving, stair navigation, or tasks needing accurate color/night vision.

## Limitations

- Narrative review/perspective.
- No protocol-specific intervention or sleep outcomes.
- Does not quantify fall risk or adverse-event rates for red-light glasses.
