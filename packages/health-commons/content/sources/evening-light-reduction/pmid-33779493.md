This source is included for **Lens dose and implementation guardrail**.

**Findings:** The modelling found large differences among brands; many lenses continued to transmit blue light that could suppress nocturnal melatonin, so some products may not improve sleep quality through circadian light reduction.

**Why it matters:** Directly challenges generic product claims by showing modeled circadian effects vary by brand and light source.

**Potential experiment signals:** modeled circadian stimulus, modeled melatonin-suppression potential, blue-light transmission

**Protocol takeaway:** Avoid claiming that all blue-blocking lenses are useful before bed; require sufficient short-wavelength/melanopic reduction.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

## Extraction notes

- **Study design:** Bench spectral modelling study.
- **Population or measurement target:** Commercial blue-blocking lenses modeled against human circadian photoreception.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Bench measurement and modelling; no follow-up.
- **Directness to Red Light Glasses Before Bed:** `measurement_context`.
- **Population mismatch:** No human participants; optical modelling only.
- **Safety/adverse-event notes:** No adverse events measured; continuing blue-light transmission is an efficacy boundary rather than a safety event.

## Limitations

- Model-based source rather than observed user outcomes.
- Commercial products and light sources are not exhaustive.
- Does not determine adherence or safety.
