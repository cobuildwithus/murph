This source is included for **Measurement and wearable endpoint context**.

**Findings:** `finding:pmid-17969470-measurement-context`: In 15 healthy subjects, actigraphy wake specificity was very low across algorithms and conditions; this casts doubt on using actigraphy alone to judge sleep quality when sleep is fragmented or quiet wake is common.

**Why it matters:** Directly addresses a central measurement caveat: actigraphy tends to struggle with wake detection during sleep, especially relevant to quiet wakefulness.

**Potential experiment signals:** wake detection, WASO, sleep efficiency, quiet wakefulness

**Protocol takeaway:** Treat wearable sleep quality, WASO, and efficiency as noisy trend signals when quiet wakefulness is possible.

**Limitations and mismatch:** Small healthy-subject validation; not a red-light-glasses intervention and not a consumer-wearable study. Population mismatch: Measurement-method or device-validation source, not a direct bedtime red-light-glasses trial; population, device, or clinical context may not match ordinary self-experimenters.

**Safety notes:** No adverse events relevant to red-light glasses were assessed.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
