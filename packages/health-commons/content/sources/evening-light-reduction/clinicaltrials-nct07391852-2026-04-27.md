This source is included for **Melanopic/evening-light mechanism**.

**Findings:** The registry describes a pediatric home-light trial comparing amber glasses, clear glasses, and smart lightbulbs; it is a future-results and safety-boundary source, not adult protocol evidence.

**Why it matters:** Provides pediatric home-light and eyewear design context, including amber-glasses, clear-glasses, and smart-lightbulb arms with sleep and circadian endpoints.

**Potential experiment signals:** sleep onset latency, ambient light exposure, DLMO, parent-reported sleep, cognition

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as pediatric and home-light context only; not evidence that adult bedtime red-light glasses improve sleep.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Study design:** Clinical trial registry record for a randomized pediatric home-light study.
- **Population or measurement target:** Children aged 5 to 6 years with parent-reported sleep-onset difficulties.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Five-week study structure with baseline assessment and two-week randomized home-light intervention.
- **Directness to Red Light Glasses Before Bed:** `clinical_supervised`.
- **Population mismatch:** Very young children with parent-reported sleep-onset difficulties; parent-led home intervention.
- **Safety/adverse-event notes:** No adverse-event result is extracted; pediatric evening light interventions should be treated as parent/clinician-supervised context.

## Limitations

- Not-yet-enrolling/registered source without outcome data.
- Pediatric population and parent implementation create major mismatch with adult self-use.
- Multiple intervention arms include smart lighting and clear glasses, so eyewear-specific claims require future results.
