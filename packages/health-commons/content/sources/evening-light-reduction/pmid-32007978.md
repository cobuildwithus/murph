This source is included for **Safety, vision, or eye-health claim boundary**.

**Findings:** A double-blind test reported increased symptoms after a near-vision task but no significant difference between blue-blocking and control filters, so it does not confirm digital-eye-strain relief from blue blocking.

**Why it matters:** Constrains claims that blue blockers generally prevent digital eye strain.

**Potential experiment signals:** digital eye strain symptoms, near-task symptom change

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as do-not-confirm evidence for eye-strain claims, not as protocol efficacy evidence.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Study design:** Double-blind randomized test of screen filters during a near-vision task.
- **Population or measurement target:** Participants exposed to extended screen/near-vision work.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Blue-blocking filters.
- **Comparator/control:** Control or comparison filters.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Acute near-vision task exposure.
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Symptoms increased after the near-vision task, but there was no significant symptom difference between blue-blocking and control filters in the extracted summary.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** Task-related symptom increase occurred, but no distinct blue-filter adverse-event rate was extracted.
- **Limitations:** Participant count was not extracted in this batch.; Screen-filter study is adjacent to spectacle-lens bedtime use.
- **Directness to Red Light Glasses Before Bed:** `measurement_context`.
- **Population mismatch:** Digital eye-strain screen-task context rather than evening bedtime eyewear.
