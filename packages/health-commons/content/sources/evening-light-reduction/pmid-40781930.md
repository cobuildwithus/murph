This source is included for **Clinical or special-population supervised variant**.

**Findings:**

- The qualitative study indicates non-pharmacological glasses research in mania can be studied with structured supports, but it is feasibility evidence rather than sleep or mania efficacy evidence.

**Why it matters:** Helps define feasibility and safety boundaries for psychiatric glasses research without implying efficacy for general sleep users.

**Potential experiment signals:** feasibility, acceptability, adherence, mania safety boundary

**Protocol takeaway:** Use only for clinical-supervised mania implementation boundaries; do not use for sleep efficacy.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Study design:** Qualitative grounded-theory analysis
- **Population or measurement target:** Hospitalized participants with mania and clinical/research context from the Ottawa Sunglasses at Night study
- **Intervention/exposure or measurement:** blue-blocking versus lightly tinted glasses trial participation in hospitalized mania
- **Comparator/control:** Qualitative feasibility source nested in a mania glasses trial; not an efficacy comparator extraction.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Qualitative interviews nested in a clinical trial context
- **Directness to Red Light Glasses Before Bed:** `clinical_supervised`.
- **Population mismatch:** Hospitalized mania population and qualitative research context differ from general bedtime use.
- **Safety/adverse-event notes:** Mania/bipolar contexts require clinician supervision and separate safety review.

## Limitations

- Qualitative substudy with interview sample, not an efficacy analysis.
- Parent trial context is psychiatric and inpatient.
- Cannot estimate sleep or mood effect size.
