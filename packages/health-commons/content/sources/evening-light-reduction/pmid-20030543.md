This source is included for **Direct adult bedtime eyewear trial**.

**Findings:** `finding:pmid-20030543-subjective-sleep-mood`: After a one-week baseline and two weeks of glasses use, the amber group showed improved overall sleep quality relative to control (sleep quality × condition interaction p<0.001; amber improvement p<0.001) and improved positive affect (p=0.005).; `finding:pmid-20030543-objective-boundary`: The trial relied on subjective sleep diaries and affect scales; the authors called for replication with objective sleep measures and circadian baseline assessment.

**Why it matters:** Early direct amber-lens bedtime trial relevant to subjective sleep and mood signals.

**Potential experiment signals:** overall sleep quality, positive affect, negative affect, sleep diary measures

**Protocol takeaway:** Amber lenses before bed improved subjective sleep quality versus a yellow control in a small trial, but objective sleep and circadian phase were not measured.

**Limitations and mismatch:** Small sample; subjective sleep diaries; no objective sleep or circadian baseline measures; replication requested by authors. Population mismatch: Adult volunteers, not necessarily insomnia patients or wearable-tracked self-experimenters.

**Safety notes:** No adverse-event data extracted from accessible abstract metadata.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.
