This source is included for **Safety, vision, or eye-health claim boundary**.

**Findings:** In 22 adults in simulated night-driving conditions, yellow-lens glasses did not improve pedestrian detection with or without headlight glare, supporting a visibility-safety boundary for tinted lenses.

**Why it matters:** Tinted lenses before bed could be worn while walking in dark rooms; this source constrains night-visibility claims.

**Potential experiment signals:** pedestrian detection response time, headlight glare performance

**Protocol takeaway:** Do not claim tinted lenses improve night safety or visibility; advise removing them when moving in dark environments if visibility is compromised.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Study design:** Within-participant simulated night-driving crossover study.
- **Population or measurement target:** Adults completing simulated night-driving pedestrian-detection tasks.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Commercial yellow-lens night-driving glasses.
- **Comparator/control:** Clear or no yellow-lens condition under glare and no-glare scenarios.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Acute simulated night-driving test sessions.
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Yellow-lens glasses did not improve pedestrian detection in simulated night driving and may reduce confidence in night-visibility claims.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No clinical adverse-event rate was extracted; the safety boundary is impaired or unimproved night-task visibility.
- **Limitations:** Simulated driving study, not a bedtime protocol.; Yellow lenses are an adjacent tinted-lens variant, not necessarily red bedtime lenses.
- **Directness to Red Light Glasses Before Bed:** `measurement_context`.
- **Population mismatch:** Night-driving task population rather than sleep self-experiment participants.
