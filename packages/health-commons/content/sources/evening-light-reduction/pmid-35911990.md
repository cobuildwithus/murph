This source is included for **Safety, vision, or eye-health claim boundary**.

**Findings:** A six-month comparison of 15% and 30% blue-light blocking spectacle lenses versus clear lenses found no significant contrast-sensitivity differences under scotopic, photopic, or glare conditions in the extracted summary.

**Why it matters:** Useful as a visual-performance safety context source with longer follow-up than many acute tasks.

**Potential experiment signals:** contrast sensitivity, scotopic contrast, photopic contrast, glare conditions

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as visual safety context only; no sleep efficacy inference.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Study design:** Longer-term randomized comparison of blue-light blocking spectacle lenses.
- **Population or measurement target:** Adults wearing clear, 15% blue-blocking, or 30% blue-blocking spectacle lenses.
- **Intervention/exposure:** 15% or 30% blue-light blocking spectacle lenses.
- **Comparator/control:** Clear spectacle lenses.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Six months of lens use.
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Across six months of consistent use, the extracted result reported no significant contrast-sensitivity differences under scotopic, photopic, or glare conditions between blue-blocking and clear lenses.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse-event rate was extracted; the result is reassuring for contrast sensitivity under tested conditions.
- **Limitations:** Participant count was not extracted in this batch.; Contrast sensitivity is not a sleep endpoint.; Lens doses differ from red bedtime eyewear.
- **Directness to Red Light Glasses Before Bed:** `measurement_context`.
- **Population mismatch:** Long-term visual-performance lens use, not bedtime sleep protocol.
