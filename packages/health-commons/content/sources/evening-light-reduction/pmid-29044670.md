This source is included for **Safety, vision, or eye-health claim boundary**.

**Findings:** A 2017 systematic review of three randomized studies with 136 participants found limited, low-certainty evidence for blue-light blocking spectacle lenses; visual/eye-strain effects were not consistently confirmed, one small insomnia study suggested subjective sleep benefit, and macular-health evidence was absent.

**Why it matters:** Earlier systematic review supports caution around visual, sleep, and macular-health marketing claims.

**Potential experiment signals:** critical flicker-fusion frequency, eye strain, subjective sleep quality, macular health

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as a mixed/low-certainty safety boundary, not as direct efficacy evidence.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Study design:** Systematic review of randomized controlled studies.
- **Population or measurement target:** Participants in randomized studies of blue-light blocking spectacle lenses.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Blue-light blocking spectacle lenses.
- **Comparator/control:** Low-blue-blocking or standard spectacle lenses.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Short-term visual-performance and sleep-wake outcomes varied across included studies.
- **Effect estimates or direction:** The review found limited low-certainty evidence; eye-strain and visual-performance effects were not consistently demonstrated, one small insomnia study reported subjective sleep-quality improvement, and no macular structure/function studies were found.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No reusable adverse-event rate was extracted.
- **Limitations:** Only three included studies and low or very-low certainty evidence.; Subjective sleep signal was small and population-specific.; No macular-health study data.
- **Directness to Red Light Glasses Before Bed:** `measurement_context`.
- **Population mismatch:** Broad blue-blocking spectacle-lens review rather than red lenses before bed in a general home protocol.
