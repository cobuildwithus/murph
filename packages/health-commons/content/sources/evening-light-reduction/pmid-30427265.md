This source is included for **Direct adult bedtime eyewear trial**.

**Findings:** `finding:pmid-30427265-subjective-positive-objective-null`: Amber-lens light restriction shortened subjective sleep onset latency by about 7 minutes, improved subjective sleep quality by 0.6 on a 1-10 scale, and increased morning alertness, while actigraphy-based sleep estimates showed no significant differences.

**Why it matters:** Direct at-home evening eyewear pilot with both diary and actigraphy outcomes in an active young-adult population.

**Potential experiment signals:** subjective sleep onset latency, subjective sleep quality, morning alertness, actigraphy sleep estimates, sleep hygiene practices

**Protocol takeaway:** Amber lenses improved subjective sleep onset, subjective sleep quality, and morning alertness in athletes, but did not produce significant actigraphy changes.

**Limitations and mismatch:** Pilot sample; athlete population; short condition windows; objective actigraphy estimates were null despite subjective improvements. Population mismatch: Recreational athletes with sleep initiation/maintenance challenges and sufficient sleep opportunity; not general insomnia or low-symptom users.

**Safety notes:** No adverse-event data extracted from accessible abstract metadata.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.
