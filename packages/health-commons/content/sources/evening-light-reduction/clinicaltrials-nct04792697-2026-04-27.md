This source is included for **Adjacent non-eyewear or population-mismatch context**.

**Findings:** The registry describes an adolescent multi-component circadian manipulation in which blue-blocker goggles are only one component; it should not be used to infer isolated glasses efficacy.

**Why it matters:** Highlights a multi-component circadian intervention where eyewear is embedded with sleep scheduling and morning light, making it unsuitable for isolated eyewear claims.

**Potential experiment signals:** sleep timing, actigraphy, circadian regulation, reward function, inflammation

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as adjacent mechanistic context only; do not attribute outcomes to glasses because the intervention is multi-component.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Study design:** Clinical trial registry record for a randomized mechanistic adolescent study.
- **Population or measurement target:** Adolescents, including teens with late sleep timing in a mechanistic protocol.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Approximately two-week manipulation context with home sleep scheduling and light instructions.
- **Directness to Red Light Glasses Before Bed:** `adjacent_variant`.
- **Population mismatch:** Adolescents and a mechanistic risk protocol, not adult consumer use.
- **Safety/adverse-event notes:** No adverse-event result is extracted from the registry record in this batch; adolescent circadian manipulation should be treated as supervised research context.

## Limitations

- The eyewear component is not isolated from bedtime scheduling and morning bright light.
- Registry source is not extracted as an outcome result.
- Adolescent population and reward/inflammation endpoints are not direct adult sleep-protocol endpoints.
