This source is included for **Safety, vision, or eye-health claim boundary**.

**Findings:** In 12 younger normal-vision participants, photostress recovery with blue-blocking lenses depended on target color and contrast; the extracted result does not support broad visual-performance claims.

**Why it matters:** Boundary evidence for glare/photostress claims that could be confused with night visibility or retinal protection.

**Potential experiment signals:** photostress recovery time, target color, target contrast, visual performance

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as visual-safety context only.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Study design:** Acute photostress recovery study.
- **Population or measurement target:** Younger adults with normal vision in photostress recovery testing.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Four brands of blue-blocking lenses.
- **Comparator/control:** Control lens.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Acute lens-testing sessions.
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Photostress recovery results depended on target color/contrast; the extracted summary did not support a broad practical-performance claim from blue-blocking lenses.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse-event rate was extracted; glare/photostress is a visual-safety context.
- **Limitations:** Small acute visual-performance study.; Participant age and normal-vision sample may not generalize.
- **Directness to Red Light Glasses Before Bed:** `measurement_context`.
- **Population mismatch:** Photostress testing, not sleep protocol.
