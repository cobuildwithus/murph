This source is included for **Clinical or special-population supervised variant**.

**Findings:**

- This case report describes blue-blocking glasses in a single elderly inpatient with mania and polysomnographic observations, making it a clinical boundary source rather than reusable adult sleep-protocol evidence.

**Why it matters:** Shows that psychiatric dark-therapy variants exist but should not be merged with general consumer sleep-hygiene protocols.

**Potential experiment signals:** mania symptoms, polysomnography, sleep architecture, psychiatric safety boundary

**Protocol takeaway:** Use only as a clinician-supervised psychiatric boundary; do not cite as healthy-adult sleep evidence.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Study design:** Single-patient case report
- **Population or measurement target:** Elderly hospitalized patient with mania
- **Intervention/exposure or measurement:** blue-blocking glasses as adjunctive inpatient mania treatment
- **Comparator/control:** No formal comparator; single-patient case report in a supervised inpatient treatment context.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Hospitalization case course; reported hospital context was about 20 days
- **Directness to Red Light Glasses Before Bed:** `clinical_supervised`.
- **Population mismatch:** Elderly hospitalized mania case; not general adult bedtime self-experiment.
- **Safety/adverse-event notes:** Mania/bipolar symptoms are high-stakes; any dark-therapy or blue-blocking intervention should be clinician-supervised.

## Limitations

- Single case report with no control group.
- Psychiatric treatment setting and medication context are not equivalent to consumer use.
- No generalizable sleep efficacy estimate.
