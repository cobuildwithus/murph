This source is included for **Clinical or special-population supervised variant**.

**Findings:** The BATCAT-pilot registry describes a pediatric psychiatry trial of blue-blocking glasses or real darkness as adjunctive care; it is a supervised safety-boundary source with no extracted outcome results.

**Why it matters:** Marks a pediatric psychiatric boundary where eyewear/darkness is studied with treatment-as-usual and dark-mode controls, not as standalone wellness evidence.

**Potential experiment signals:** sleep timing, mood stability, feasibility, hospital light conditions, safety

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as future-results and safety-boundary context only; no efficacy claims are supported by the registry record.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Study design:** Clinical trial registry record for a randomized pediatric psychiatric pilot.
- **Population or measurement target:** Children and adolescents aged 12 to 18 in psychiatric inpatient or outpatient care with insomnia or delayed sleep phase disorder.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Evening/night intervention window from about 21:00 to desired wake time within a pilot treatment period.
- **Directness to Red Light Glasses Before Bed:** `clinical_supervised`.
- **Population mismatch:** Children/adolescents in psychiatric care with insomnia or delayed sleep phase disorder.
- **Safety/adverse-event notes:** No adverse-event result is extracted; the registered population and setting are supervised pediatric psychiatry contexts.

## Limitations

- Not-yet-enrolling registry source without outcomes.
- Intervention can include real darkness and dark-mode co-intervention, not only glasses.
- Psychiatric care setting and pediatric population limit generalizability.
