This source is included for **Implementation guardrail only**.

**Findings:**

- Filtered and unfiltered monocular light conditions produced greater normalized melatonin AUC than unfiltered binocular light, while filtering one eye did not worsen NVT, GNG, or KSS outcomes relative to unfiltered binocular light.

- The study is an implementation guardrail: partial-field or one-eye filtering can change melanopic response, so eyewear fit, field coverage, and binocular exposure matter when translating red-glasses claims.

**Why it matters:** Informs field coverage and partial-filtering guardrails: how much of the retina and both eyes are exposed matters for melatonin and performance outcomes.

**Potential experiment signals:** melatonin AUC, visual performance, subjective sleepiness, pupil area, lens coverage

**Protocol takeaway:** Use for implementation guardrails about full-field/binocular filtering and dose; do not claim bedtime red glasses improve sleep from this night-work study.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Study design:** Within-subject crossover night-light exposure study
- **Population or measurement target:** Adult participants in a night-worker or simulated night-work light-at-night context
- **Intervention/exposure or measurement:** blue-blocking filter over one eye and monocular/binocular light conditions
- **Comparator/control:** Unfiltered binocular light and other monocular/filtered light-condition combinations.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Six Friday-night light-condition sessions
- **Directness to Red Light Glasses Before Bed:** `same_mechanism`.
- **Population mismatch:** Night-worker simulation and monocular filtering differ from home bedtime glasses.
- **Safety/adverse-event notes:** Performance and sleepiness tasks did not show impairment from filtering one eye in this context, but this does not establish driving or occupational safety for consumer use.

## Limitations

- Not a home bedtime protocol.
- Monocular or partial-field conditions differ from red/amber glasses worn over both eyes.
- Participant count represents eligible scheduled subjects; complete-session analytic counts should be checked in the article before meta-analysis.
