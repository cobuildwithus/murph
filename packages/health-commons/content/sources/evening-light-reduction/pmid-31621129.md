This source is included for **Measurement and wearable endpoint context**.

**Findings:** `finding:pmid-31621129-measurement-context`: Clinical and consumer wrist devices can mis-estimate sleep measures with wide individual-level limits, and accuracy varies with patient- and device-specific factors.

**Why it matters:** Useful caveat paper emphasizing that wrist-derived movement/physiology is not a direct brain-state measure of sleep or sleep stages.

**Potential experiment signals:** sleep duration, sleep efficiency, WASO, sleep stages, device caveats

**Protocol takeaway:** Use wrist-device data as trend context, not a direct readout of brain sleep state.

**Limitations and mismatch:** Specific devices and clinical sample; algorithms may not match current devices; not a red-light-glasses study. Population mismatch: Measurement-method or device-validation source, not a direct bedtime red-light-glasses trial; population, device, or clinical context may not match ordinary self-experimenters.

**Safety notes:** No eyewear adverse events were assessed; safety relevance is avoiding clinical decisions based solely on tracker outputs.
