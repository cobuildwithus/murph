This source is included for **Direct adult bedtime eyewear trial**.

**Findings:** `finding:pmid-29101797-insomnia-sleep`: Amber lenses improved PIRS total and Quality of Life, Distress, and Sleep Parameter subscales versus clear lenses (p<0.05), increased subjective total sleep time/quality/soundness (p<0.05), and increased actigraphy total sleep time (358.80 ± 66.29 vs 330.33 ± 66.01 min; p=0.035).; `finding:pmid-29101797-implementation-boundary`: The abstract describes amber lenses as safe, affordable, and easily implemented, but this extraction did not identify adverse-event counts; short duration and small sample limit safety inference.

**Why it matters:** One of the closest direct clinical tests of amber/blue-blocking lenses before bed in adults with insomnia symptoms.

**Potential experiment signals:** Pittsburgh Insomnia Rating Scale, subjective total sleep time, subjective sleep quality, actigraphy total sleep time, sleep onset latency, wake after sleep onset

**Protocol takeaway:** In adults with insomnia symptoms, amber lenses worn before bedtime improved insomnia ratings and increased actigraphy total sleep time versus clear placebo lenses.

**Limitations and mismatch:** Small sample; short 7-night periods; actigraphy improvements were not broad across every sleep metric; participant population had insomnia symptoms. Population mismatch: More applicable to users with insomnia symptoms than already-stable sleepers.

**Safety notes:** The article abstract described amber lenses as safe, affordable, and easily implemented; no specific adverse-event counts were extracted here.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.
