This source is included for **Adjacent non-eyewear or population-mismatch context**.

**Findings:**

- In the Grade 7 teenage subgroup, blue-light-blocking glasses were associated with a shorter time to fall asleep during evening screen-time nights, including a reported 62% reduction and paired t-test p=0.0207.

- In the adult subgroup, the same small experiment reported less than a one-minute difference and a nonsignificant 4% reduction in time to fall asleep (paired t-test p=0.2969), so the adult-relevant signal was null.

**Why it matters:** Provides a claim-audit example with a youth signal but adult null finding, underscoring why small uncontrolled mixed-age experiments cannot support broad adult protocol claims.

**Potential experiment signals:** sleep-onset latency, screen time, age subgroup, consumer sleep trackers

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as low-quality adjacent context only; preserve the adult null result and avoid generalizing the teenage signal to adults.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Study design:** Small uncontrolled student-led sequential pre-post experiment
- **Population or measurement target:** 10 teenagers aged 12 to 13 and 8 adults aged 21 to 35
- **Intervention/exposure or measurement:** blue-light-blocking glasses during two hours of evening screen time
- **Comparator/control:** Three no-glasses control nights before three blue-light-blocking-glasses nights; adult and teenage subgroups reported separately.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Six consecutive nights: three without glasses followed by three with glasses, each with two hours of evening screen time
- **Directness to Red Light Glasses Before Bed:** `adjacent_variant`.
- **Population mismatch:** Teenage subgroup is age-mismatched, adult subgroup is small and null; measurement relied on participant-chosen sleep trackers.
- **Safety/adverse-event notes:** No adverse-event finding extracted; use as low-priority context only.

## Limitations

- No randomization, blinding, or counterbalanced order.
- Small sample with mixed teenage and adult groups.
- Screen devices, content, routines, stress, activity load, and sleep-tracking methods were not standardized.
- Sequential design may confound intervention with time/order effects.
