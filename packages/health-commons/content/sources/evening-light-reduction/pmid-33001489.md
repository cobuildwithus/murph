This source is included for **Safety, vision, or eye-health claim boundary**.

**Findings:** In 19 healthy young adults completing two 30-minute reading tasks, a blue-blocking screen filter did not improve accommodative accuracy, pupil dynamics, or visual discomfort, although reading speed increased in the extracted result.

**Why it matters:** Clear adjacent null result for visual discomfort claims while preserving the reading-speed finding.

**Potential experiment signals:** accommodative accuracy, accommodation variability, reading speed, pupil dynamics, visual discomfort

**Protocol takeaway:** Do not claim blue-blocking filters improve visual discomfort.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Study design:** Short visual-task crossover study of a blue-blocking screen filter.
- **Population or measurement target:** Healthy young adults performing reading tasks.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Commercial blue-blocking screen filter.
- **Comparator/control:** No/filter-control screen condition.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Two 30-minute reading tasks on different days.
- **Effect estimates or direction:** The filter did not improve accommodative accuracy/variability, pupil dynamics, or visual discomfort; reading speed increased in the extracted result, but the study did not support prescribing blue-blocking filters for visual symptoms/signs in healthy young adults.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse-event rate was extracted.
- **Limitations:** Small young adult sample.; Screen filter rather than spectacle lenses.; No sleep endpoint.
- **Directness to Red Light Glasses Before Bed:** `measurement_context`.
- **Population mismatch:** Screen reading in healthy young adults, not red glasses before bed.
