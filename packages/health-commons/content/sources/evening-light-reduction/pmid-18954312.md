This source is included for **Safety, vision, or eye-health claim boundary**.

**Findings:** In 22 bilateral pseudophakic participants with early AMD, blue-light blocking filters affected low-light visual-function tasks, including an extracted average 13% longer block-manipulation time with the filter.

**Why it matters:** Important safety boundary for low-light mobility and special visual populations.

**Potential experiment signals:** mobility/obstacle course, object manipulation, dark-adapted full-field flash, color discrimination

**Protocol takeaway:** Do not generalize to healthy users; flag visual impairment/low-light tasks as safety cautions.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Study design:** Clinical visual-function study in AMD with blue-light blocking filters.
- **Population or measurement target:** Bilateral pseudophakic participants with early age-related macular degeneration.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Blue-attenuating/blue-light blocking filter.
- **Comparator/control:** No filter condition.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Acute visual-function tasks with and without filter.
- **Effect estimates or direction:** In this AMD/pseudophakia population, blue-light blocking filters affected low-light visual-function tasks; the extracted detail included an average 13% increase in time on a block manipulation task with the filter.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No clinical adverse-event rate was extracted; the safety boundary is task slowing/low-light visual performance.
- **Limitations:** Special clinical population with AMD and pseudophakia.; Acute task outcomes, not sleep.
- **Directness to Red Light Glasses Before Bed:** `measurement_context`.
- **Population mismatch:** AMD/pseudophakia clinical sample is a major mismatch to healthy adult bedtime use.
