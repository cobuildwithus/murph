This source is included for **Adjacent non-eyewear or population-mismatch context**.

**Findings:**

- The narrative review reports no evidence that normal domestic LED or screen-device use is retinotoxic to the human eye, while noting uncertainty about long-term cumulative dose-response.

- The review states that evidence does not support blue-blocking lenses for prevention of eye diseases such as AMD, so protocol pages should avoid retinal-protection claims.

**Why it matters:** Provides explicit boundaries against eye-disease-prevention claims for blue-blocking lenses while noting uncertainty about cumulative exposure.

**Potential experiment signals:** retinal safety, eye disease prevention, AMD, digital-device exposure

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as safety boundary; do not claim red-light glasses protect the retina or prevent AMD.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

- **Comparator/control:** Not applicable; narrative ocular-hazard and prevention review.

## Limitations

- Narrative review, not a protocol trial.
- Long-term cumulative exposure remains uncertain.
- Does not evaluate red-light glasses for sleep endpoints.
