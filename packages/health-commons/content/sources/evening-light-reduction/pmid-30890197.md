This source is included for **Direct adult bedtime eyewear trial**.

**Findings:** `finding:pmid-30890197-neuropsych-sleep`: List Sorting Working Memory improved after amber lenses versus baseline (p=.002, d=0.66); insomnia severity was lower with amber versus clear lenses (PIRS total 72.64 ± 28.14 vs 88.93 ± 33.19; p=.023) and actigraphy TST was higher (358.80 ± 66.29 vs 330.33 ± 66.01 min; p=.035), while SOL, SE, and WASO were not significant.; `finding:pmid-30890197-pilot-limitations`: The study was a small pilot with no healthy control group, possible practice effects on neuropsychological tests, and no correction for multiple comparisons; it likely overlaps the Shechter insomnia trial cohort and should not be counted as a fully independent sleep RCT.

**Why it matters:** Adds cognitive and insomnia-severity outcomes to the nocturnal blue-light blockage evidence base, but overlaps with the Shechter insomnia cohort.

**Potential experiment signals:** working memory, processing speed, Pittsburgh Insomnia Rating Scale, actigraphy total sleep time, wake time, sleep onset latency, sleep efficiency, wake after sleep onset

**Protocol takeaway:** Amber lenses before bed improved working-memory performance versus baseline and were associated with lower insomnia severity and higher actigraphy total sleep time versus clear lenses in a small insomnia-symptom cohort.

**Limitations and mismatch:** Small pilot; likely overlapping parent sleep cohort; practice effects possible; no healthy control group; no correction for multiple comparisons. Population mismatch: Adults with insomnia symptoms and multiple exclusions; not general low-symptom users.

**Safety notes:** No adverse-event data extracted; exclusion criteria limit generalizability.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.
