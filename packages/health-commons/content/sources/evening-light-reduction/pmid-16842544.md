This source is included for **Lens dose and implementation guardrail**.

**Findings:** Orange blue-blocker lenses impeded light-induced melatonin suppression; the reported melatonin change with orange lenses was non-significant, while grey lenses produced a significant approximately 46% reduction in melatonin.

**Why it matters:** Directly demonstrates that lens spectral filtering can change melatonin suppression during bright nocturnal light exposure.

**Potential experiment signals:** salivary melatonin, light-induced melatonin suppression, lens spectral filtering

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as mechanistic support for high-filtering amber/orange lenses, not as proof of improved sleep in ordinary users.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

## Extraction notes

- **Study design:** Acute controlled laboratory melatonin-suppression study.
- **Population or measurement target:** Healthy adults exposed to a nighttime bright-light pulse.
- **Duration/follow-up:** 60-minute 1300-lux light pulse from 01:00 to 02:00 with orange versus grey lenses.
- **Directness to Red Light Glasses Before Bed:** `same_mechanism`.
- **Population mismatch:** Healthy adult laboratory participants exposed to an acute bright-light pulse; not routine home bedtime users.
- **Safety/adverse-event notes:** Commentary associated with the article notes that safety considerations are relevant when blocking blue light during night work or travel.

## Limitations

- Acute melatonin-suppression endpoint rather than sleep architecture or next-day function.
- Laboratory bright-light exposure may exceed ordinary household exposure.
- Small sample.
