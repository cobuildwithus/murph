This source is included for **Safety, vision, or eye-health claim boundary**.

**Findings:** In a small tinted-lens visual-performance study, filters could enhance some contrast sensitivity while reducing chromatic discrimination, supporting a visual tradeoff boundary.

**Why it matters:** Foundational context that tinted filters can shift visual performance rather than acting neutrally.

**Potential experiment signals:** contrast sensitivity, chromatic discrimination, visual performance

**Protocol takeaway:** Use for visual safety boundary only.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Study design:** Acute chromatic-filter visual-performance study.
- **Population or measurement target:** Observers in tinted-lens visual-performance testing.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Tinted/chromatic lenses.
- **Comparator/control:** No-filter or alternative filter conditions.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Acute filter-testing sessions.
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Tinted lenses may enhance some contrast-sensitivity performance while reducing chromatic discrimination, indicating a tradeoff rather than a uniformly beneficial visual effect.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No clinical adverse-event rate was extracted; the safety issue is color-discrimination loss.
- **Limitations:** Small older visual-performance study.; Not specific to bedtime red glasses or sleep outcomes.
- **Directness to Red Light Glasses Before Bed:** `measurement_context`.
- **Population mismatch:** Laboratory visual-performance observers, not sleep-protocol users.
