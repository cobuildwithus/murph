This source is included for **Safety, vision, or eye-health claim boundary**.

**Findings:** This narrative review provides retinal-protection hypothesis context but does not establish that blue-light filters or bedtime red glasses prevent age-related macular degeneration in humans.

**Why it matters:** Helps prevent overclaiming mechanistic retinal-protection arguments as protocol benefits.

**Potential experiment signals:** age-related macular degeneration hypothesis, retinal phototoxicity, claim guardrails

**Protocol takeaway:** Use context-only; do not claim red glasses prevent AMD.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Study design:** Narrative review of blue-light filters and age-related macular degeneration hypothesis.
- **Population or measurement target:** People considered in retinal phototoxicity and age-related macular degeneration discussions.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Blue-light filtering or short-wavelength attenuation concepts.
- **Comparator/control:** No filter or standard optical conditions in reviewed context.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Review source; no intervention follow-up.
- **Effect estimates or direction:** The review is mechanistic/theoretical context for retinal protection, not direct human evidence that consumer glasses prevent age-related macular degeneration.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No source-owned adverse-event rate was extracted.
- **Limitations:** Narrative/theoretical review.; Older retinal-protection context and not a bedtime intervention.
- **Directness to Red Light Glasses Before Bed:** `measurement_context`.
- **Population mismatch:** AMD/retinal-protection context rather than healthy adult bedtime use.
