This source is included for **Safety, vision, or eye-health claim boundary**.

**Findings:** In a 10-resident crossover pilot, blue-light filtering glasses showed directional reductions in many computer-vision and fatigue symptoms, but no individual symptom reached statistical significance.

**Why it matters:** Preserves a small mixed adjacent occupational source without overstating it.

**Potential experiment signals:** computer vision syndrome symptoms, SOFI fatigue symptoms, daily symptom reports

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as mixed adjacent context only.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Study design:** Prospective crossover pilot study in radiology residents.
- **Population or measurement target:** Radiology residents doing clinical display work.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Blue light filtering glasses during clinical work.
- **Comparator/control:** Sham glasses.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Two five-day work periods/crossover exposures.
- **Effect estimates or direction:** No individual symptom reached statistical significance; directional reductions were observed for many CVS-Q and SOFI symptoms with blue-light filtering glasses, and larger studies were recommended.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse-event rate was extracted.
- **Limitations:** Pilot sample of 10 residents.; Occupational clinical-display context, not bedtime use.; Directional symptom reductions were mostly not statistically significant.
- **Directness to Red Light Glasses Before Bed:** `measurement_context`.
- **Population mismatch:** Radiology residents at work rather than general bedtime users.
