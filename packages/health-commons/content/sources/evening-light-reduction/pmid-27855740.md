This source is included for **Safety, vision, or eye-health claim boundary**.

**Findings:** A three-patient orthosomnia case series described excessive focus on sleep-tracker data and behavior such as spending too much time in bed to improve sleep scores, which may worsen insomnia or anxiety.

**Why it matters:** Murph experiments may use sleep wearables; this source protects against overinterpreting noisy sleep metrics.

**Potential experiment signals:** insomnia symptoms, time in bed, sleep-related anxiety, tracker-driven behavior

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as measurement-safety context: discourage obsessive score chasing and excessive time in bed.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Study design:** Case series/commentary on sleep-tracker-driven insomnia behavior.
- **Population or measurement target:** Patients with sleep concerns and excessive focus on sleep-tracker data.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Overinterpretation of consumer sleep-tracker metrics and attempts to optimize sleep scores.
- **Comparator/control:** Not applicable.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Clinical case observations.
- **Effect estimates or direction:** The case series described patients spending excessive time in bed or becoming preoccupied with tracker sleep scores, a behavioral safety risk for self-experimentation.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** Potential worsening of insomnia or anxiety through obsessive tracker optimization was the safety concern.
- **Limitations:** Case series, not an eyewear intervention.; No causal estimate for red glasses.
- **Directness to Red Light Glasses Before Bed:** `measurement_context`.
- **Population mismatch:** Behavioral sleep-tracker safety context, not optical intervention evidence.
