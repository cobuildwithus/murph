This source is included for **Clinical or special-population supervised variant**.

**Findings:**

- A hospital-room blue-depleted evening light environment produced favorable circadian and sleep-related signals versus standard lighting in a small crossover study, but the intervention was room lighting rather than eyewear.

- The study reported no serious side-effect signal in the small supervised lighting intervention, but it is underpowered for general consumer safety conclusions.

**Why it matters:** Shows that reducing short-wavelength evening light in a real room environment can move circadian and sleep endpoints, while remaining a supervised room-lighting variant.

**Potential experiment signals:** melatonin, circadian phase, REM sleep, sleepiness, side effects

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as adjacent environmental-light support for mechanism; do not translate directly into glasses efficacy.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Study design:** Small randomized or balanced crossover light-environment study
- **Population or measurement target:** Healthy participants living in a hospital-like environment
- **Intervention/exposure or measurement:** blue-depleted evening hospital lighting
- **Comparator/control:** Standard or normal hospital evening light environment.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Five days in each evening light environment in a crossover design
- **Directness to Red Light Glasses Before Bed:** `clinical_supervised`.
- **Population mismatch:** Hospital-like supervised setting and room-light intervention rather than home red-light glasses.
- **Safety/adverse-event notes:** No serious side-effect signal was extracted, but the study is small and supervised.

## Limitations

- Small sample size.
- Room-light spectral intervention cannot isolate glasses fit, lens transmittance, or adherence.
- Healthy/hospital-environment participants may not generalize to ordinary home users.
