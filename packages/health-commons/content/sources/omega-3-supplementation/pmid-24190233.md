This source is included for **dry_eye_treatment_prevention**.

**Findings:** Population: Typical dry-eye syndrome subjects selected from 43 candidates; fish-oil n=15 and placebo n=12. Intervention/exposure: Fish oil providing EPA 1245 mg/day and DHA 540 mg/day. Comparator/control: Placebo without EPA/DHA. Duration/follow-up: 12 weeks of supplementation plus 4-week washout. Endpoints: VAS dry-eye symptoms, eye pain, Schirmer I test, TBUT, fluorescein staining, rose bengal staining. Effect/direction: Eye pain, TBUT, and rose bengal staining improved significantly after 8-12 weeks and/or at 4-week washout compared with placebo. Safety/adverse events: No adverse-event signal extracted from accessible abstract.

**Why it matters:** This direct fish-oil RCT helps preserve real-world adjunct-care context for dry-eye evidence.

**Potential experiment signals:** eye pain VAS, TBUT, rose bengal staining, Schirmer I test.

**Protocol takeaway:** Context-only dry-eye treatment evidence requiring clinical boundary language.

**Limitations and population mismatch:** Very small sample.; Adjunct-to-usual-care context may not isolate real-world monotherapy effects.; Short treatment and washout windows. Population mismatch: Small dry-eye treatment sample; not prevention or general wellness.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
