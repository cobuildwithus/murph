This source is included for **dry_eye_treatment_prevention**.

**Findings:** Population: US men aged 50+ and women aged 55+ without previous dry-eye diagnosis or severe dry-eye symptoms at baseline. Intervention/exposure: Marine omega-3 fatty acids, 1 g/day. Comparator/control: Placebo within a double-blind 2x2 factorial VITAL design. Duration/follow-up: Median 5.3 years of treatment/follow-up (range 3.8-6.1 years). Endpoints: incident clinically diagnosed dry eye disease, incident dry-eye diagnosis plus severe dry-eye symptoms. Effect/direction: Primary endpoint: 232/11,757 omega-3 vs 240/11,766 placebo; HR 0.97 (95% CI 0.81-1.16; p=0.72). Secondary endpoint: HR 0.97 (95% CI 0.89-1.06; p=0.49). Safety/adverse events: This extraction focused on dry-eye incidence; no distinct ocular safety signal was extracted.

**Why it matters:** It prevents converting dry-eye treatment hypotheses into prevention claims for healthy adults.

**Potential experiment signals:** incident dry-eye diagnosis, severe dry-eye symptoms, dry-eye medical-record confirmation.

**Protocol takeaway:** Do not claim that 1 g/day marine omega-3 prevents dry eye in generally healthy older adults.

**Limitations and population mismatch:** Prevention cohort differs from dry-eye treatment trials.; Dose was 1 g/day marine omega-3, lower than some dry-eye treatment trials.; Does not address high-dose EPA/DHA treatment in diagnosed DED or MGD. Population mismatch: Primary prevention in generally healthy older adults; not treatment of established dry eye.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
