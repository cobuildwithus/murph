This source is included for **lipids_triglycerides_dose_response**.

**Findings:** In final analysis (48 supplement, 47 fish), both groups reduced total cholesterol, non-HDL-C, TG, and TC/HDL ratio, but fresh fish had more pronounced effects. Supplement group TG change −12.7±35.48 versus fish −66.55±30.79 mg/dL; supplement LDL-C increased +18.7±24.97 versus fish −22.75±27.28 mg/dL; HDL increased in both groups with greater increase from fish.

**Why it matters:** Food-versus-supplement comparison for lipid-profile outcomes; open-label design lowers directness.

**Potential experiment signals:** triglycerides, LDL-C, HDL-C, dietary fish intake, supplement dose

**Protocol takeaway:** Fresh fish may affect lipids differently from capsules; do not use food-comparator results as direct supplement efficacy evidence.

**Population mismatch:** Food-versus-supplement comparison and hyperlipidemia population; context-only for the supplement protocol.

**Safety notes:** Five supplement-group participants were excluded due to gastrointestinal oil intolerance and/or new need for lipid-lowering drugs in the flow description; exact attribution split was not extracted.

**Limitations:**
- Open-label design.
- Fresh fish is a food-pattern comparator, not an inert control.
- Baseline lipid differences required covariance analysis.
- EPA/DHA amounts were close but not exactly equal between groups.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
