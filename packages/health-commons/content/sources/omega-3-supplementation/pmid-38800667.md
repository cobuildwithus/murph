This source is included for **safety_adverse_events**.

**Findings:** Among people without known CVD, regular fish oil use was associated with transition from healthy status to AF (HR 1.13, 95% CI 1.10-1.17) and to stroke (HR 1.05, 95% CI 1.00-1.11). Among participants with known CVD, fish oil use was associated with lower transition risk from AF to MACE (HR 0.92, 95% CI 0.87-0.98), AF to MI (HR 0.85, 95% CI 0.76-0.96), and heart failure to death (HR 0.91, 95% CI 0.84-0.99).

**Why it matters:** Observational supplement-use context; should not be treated as causal protocol evidence.

**Potential experiment signals:** adverse_event:atrial-fibrillation, adverse_event:stroke, outcome:major-adverse-cardiovascular-events, outcome:myocardial-infarction.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as safety-only evidence only: Among people without known CVD, regular fish oil use was associated with transition from healthy status to AF (HR 1.13, 95% CI 1.10-1.17) and to stroke (HR 1.05, 95% CI 1.00-1.11). Among participants with known CVD, fish oil use was associated with lower transition risk from AF to MACE (HR 0.92, 95% CI 0.87-0.98), AF to MI (HR 0.85, 95% CI 0.76-0.96), and heart failure to death (HR 0.91, 95% CI 0.84-0.99).

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

**Population mismatch:** Observational exposure rather than randomized oral EPA/DHA protocol; not causal.

**Limitations:** Observational design, residual confounding, self-reported fish-oil use, no dose/formulation detail, UK Biobank generalizability limitations.
