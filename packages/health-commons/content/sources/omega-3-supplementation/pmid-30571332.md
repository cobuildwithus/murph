This source is included for **safety_adverse_events**.

**Findings:** In 1,516 cardiac-surgery patients, perioperative fish oil did not increase major BARC bleeding. The fish-oil arm had fewer blood transfusion units, and higher achieved omega-3 levels were associated with lower bleeding risk.

**Why it matters:** Most direct randomized perioperative fish-oil bleeding evidence in this batch.

**Potential experiment signals:** BARC type 4 or 5 bleeding, blood transfusion units, perioperative disclosure, EPA+DHA dose, surgery status.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as direct perioperative safety evidence: high perioperative fish-oil dosing did not increase major cardiac-surgery bleeding and reduced transfusion units, but this is a surgical secondary analysis.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

**Directness:** `safety_boundary` for `protocol_variant:omega-3-supplementation/oral-epa-dha-supplementation`.

**Limitations and population mismatch:** Secondary safety analysis of OPERA; not primarily designed for bleeding, limited to cardiac surgery, and acute perioperative loading may not reflect long-term community supplementation.
