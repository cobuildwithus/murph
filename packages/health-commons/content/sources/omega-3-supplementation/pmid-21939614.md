This source is included for **mood_cognition**.

**Findings:** Supplements with EPA >=60% showed benefit on standardized mean depression scores (effect size 0.532; 95% CI 0.277 to 0.733; P<.001), while supplements with EPA <60% were ineffective.

**Why it matters:** Important ingredient-specific boundary source.

**Potential experiment signals:** depression scores.

**Protocol takeaway:** EPA-specific meta-analysis suggested benefit when EPA was at least 60% of EPA+DHA. Do not infer DHA is ineffective for all endpoints; this is depression-specific.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Clinical trials of depression involving EPA-containing omega-3 supplements
- **Intervention/exposure:** EPA-containing omega-3 supplements, analyzed by EPA proportion
- **Comparator/control:** Placebo/control
- **Duration/follow-up:** Varied across trials
- **Endpoints:** depression scores
- **Effect estimate or direction:** Supplements with EPA >=60% showed benefit on standardized mean depression scores (effect size 0.532; 95% CI 0.277 to 0.733; P<.001), while supplements with EPA <60% were ineffective.
- **Adverse events / safety notes:** Adverse-event details were not extracted in this batch.
- **Population mismatch:** EPA-specific clinical depression context, not general EPA/DHA wellness supplementation.
- **Artifact rights status:** open_access

## Limitations

- Clinical depression trial synthesis.
- EPA proportion analysis may be vulnerable to trial differences.
- Older meta-analysis.
