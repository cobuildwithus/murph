This source is included for **dry_eye_treatment_prevention**.

**Findings:** At baseline, 81% had low omega-3 index; the omega-3 group showed significant omega-3 index increase, symptom improvement, lower osmolarity, and higher Schirmer, TBUT, and goblet-cell density; low omega-3 index (<4%) subgroup improved more (p<0.001).

**Why it matters:** Recent large VDT-user trial introduces omega-3 index as response predictor.

**Potential experiment signals:** omega-3 index, DESS/symptoms, TBUT, Schirmer, tear osmolarity.

**Protocol takeaway:** Useful screen-user subgroup context and biomarker-response hypothesis, not general dry-eye proof.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Population mismatch / boundary:** Screen-use dry-eye subgroup; adjacent to wellness users with heavy device exposure but still clinical dry-eye treatment evidence.

**Safety notes:** No specific adverse-event signal extracted from abstract-level material.

**Limitations:** Unit of randomization/count is ambiguous in abstract snippets.; Specific VDT-user population.; High capsule burden may limit practicality..
