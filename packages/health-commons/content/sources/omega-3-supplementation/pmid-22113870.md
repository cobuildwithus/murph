This source is included for **lipids_triglycerides_dose_response**.

**Findings:** Pooled algal DHA lowered triglycerides by −0.20 mmol/L (95% CI −0.27 to −0.14), increased LDL-C by 0.23 mmol/L (95% CI 0.16 to 0.30), and increased HDL-C by 0.07 mmol/L (95% CI 0.05 to 0.10).

**Why it matters:** Important DHA-specific meta-analysis for lipid effects and LDL/HDL tradeoffs.

**Potential experiment signals:** fasting triglycerides, LDL-C, HDL-C, lipid-panel before/after

**Protocol takeaway:** Algal DHA can lower triglycerides, but LDL-C may rise; lipid tracking should include LDL-C and HDL-C, not just triglycerides.

**Population mismatch:** Mostly healthy/non-CHD participants rather than clinician-managed hypertriglyceridemia or cardiovascular disease populations.

**Safety notes:** No adverse-event extraction available in the abstract-level record used here.

**Limitations:**
- Algal DHA only rather than combined EPA+DHA fish oil.
- Participants were persons without coronary heart disease, limiting clinical hypertriglyceridemia generalization.
- LDL-C increase must be preserved alongside triglyceride lowering.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.
