This source is included for **mood_cognition**.

**Findings:** No significant differences between fish oil and placebo at any assessed time point despite large reductions in depression; red-blood-cell fatty acids indicated compliance and participants did not show initial omega-3 deficiency.

**Why it matters:** Adjacent clinical null source.

**Potential experiment signals:** major depression symptoms, fatty acid status.

**Protocol takeaway:** Adjunctive fish-oil MDD trial found no additional benefit over placebo. Not a healthy-adult trial.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Outpatients with major depression receiving conventional treatment
- **Intervention/exposure:** Tuna fish oil supplementation adjunctive to conventional treatment
- **Comparator/control:** Placebo adjunct
- **Duration/follow-up:** 4 months
- **Endpoints:** major depression symptoms, fatty acid status
- **Effect estimate or direction:** No significant differences between fish oil and placebo at any assessed time point despite large reductions in depression; red-blood-cell fatty acids indicated compliance and participants did not show initial omega-3 deficiency.
- **Adverse events / safety notes:** Adverse-event details were not extracted.
- **Population mismatch:** Clinical disease-context trial, not general protocol evidence.
- **Artifact rights status:** permission_required

## Limitations

- Clinical MDD adjunctive treatment context.
- Conventional treatment in both arms.
- No initial omega-3 deficiency may limit effect.
