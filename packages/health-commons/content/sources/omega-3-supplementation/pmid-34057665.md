This source is included for **safety_adverse_events**.

**Findings:** AF occurred more often with omega-3 fatty acids than control (4.0% vs 3.3%; RR 1.24, 95% CI 1.11-1.38). The signal was stronger in trials using >1 g/day (RR 1.51, 95% CI 1.26-1.80) than ≤1 g/day (RR 1.12, 95% CI 1.04-1.21); stroke was not increased (RR 1.04, 95% CI 0.90-1.20).

**Why it matters:** Primary safety relevance is the dose-related AF signal; no stroke increase was detected in the pooled analysis.

**Potential experiment signals:** adverse_event:atrial-fibrillation, adverse_event:stroke, dose:epa-dha-grams-per-day.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as safety-only evidence only: AF occurred more often with omega-3 fatty acids than control (4.0% vs 3.3%; RR 1.24, 95% CI 1.11-1.38). The signal was stronger in trials using >1 g/day (RR 1.51, 95% CI 1.26-1.80) than ≤1 g/day (RR 1.12, 95% CI 1.04-1.21); stroke was not increased (RR 1.04, 95% CI 0.90-1.20).

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

**Population mismatch:** Mostly cardiovascular outcome trials, not self-experiment consumer supplementation in generally healthy adults.

**Limitations:** Trial-level meta-analysis; cardiovascular outcome trial populations do not map cleanly to healthy adults using ordinary consumer EPA/DHA supplements; AF ascertainment differed across trials.
