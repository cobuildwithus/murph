This source is included for **dry_eye_treatment_prevention**.

**Findings:** Reported conclusions: both forms reduced tear osmolarity and increased tear stability over 3 months; krill oil may have conferred additional symptom improvement and lower tear IL-17A versus placebo.

**Why it matters:** Useful for product-formulation boundaries in dry-eye claims.

**Potential experiment signals:** tear osmolarity, tear break-up time, OSDI, tear cytokines.

**Protocol takeaway:** Formulation may matter, but this small direct dry-eye trial should be treated as formulation-specific context rather than a broad oral EPA/DHA claim.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Population mismatch / boundary:** Direct dry-eye treatment evidence, but not a simple generic fish-oil-only comparison.

**Safety notes:** No specific adverse-event signal extracted from available abstract-level material.

**Limitations:** Small three-arm design.; Formulations were not equivalent except for broadly similar EPA/DHA exposure.; No source-level adverse-event extraction available from abstract snippets..
