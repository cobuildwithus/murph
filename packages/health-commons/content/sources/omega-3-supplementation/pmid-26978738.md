This source is included for **mood_cognition**.

**Findings:** After accounting for potential publication bias, meta-analysis showed an overall beneficial effect in MDD (SMD 0.398; 95% CI 0.114 to 0.682; P=0.006), with heterogeneity (I2=73.36%). Higher EPA dose and antidepressant use were associated with better outcomes in meta-regression.

**Why it matters:** Supports EPA-dose and adjunctive-treatment hypotheses only.

**Potential experiment signals:** depressive symptoms.

**Protocol takeaway:** MDD meta-regression found a benefit signal linked to EPA dose and antidepressant use. High heterogeneity and population mismatch limit protocol claims.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Adults with DSM-confirmed major depressive disorder in 13 studies
- **Intervention/exposure:** Omega-3 PUFA supplementation, often adjunctive; EPA dose varied
- **Comparator/control:** Placebo/control
- **Duration/follow-up:** Varied across included trials
- **Endpoints:** depressive symptoms
- **Effect estimate or direction:** After accounting for potential publication bias, meta-analysis showed an overall beneficial effect in MDD (SMD 0.398; 95% CI 0.114 to 0.682; P=0.006), with heterogeneity (I2=73.36%). Higher EPA dose and antidepressant use were associated with better outcomes in meta-regression.
- **Adverse events / safety notes:** Adverse-event details were not extracted in this batch.
- **Population mismatch:** Clinical supervised/adjunctive evidence, not direct healthy adult supplementation evidence.
- **Artifact rights status:** unknown

## Limitations

- High heterogeneity.
- Clinical MDD and often adjunctive treatment context.
- Meta-regression is observational across trials.
