This source is included for **mood_cognition**.

**Findings:** Pooled anxiety-symptom improvement favored omega-3 PUFA treatment (Hedges g 0.374; 95% CI 0.081 to 0.666; P=.01). Effects were stronger in clinical populations; the nonclinical subgroup was not significant (g -0.008; 95% CI -0.266 to 0.250).

**Why it matters:** May justify tracking anxiety symptoms, but not a blanket mood-improvement claim for healthy adults.

**Potential experiment signals:** anxiety symptoms.

**Protocol takeaway:** Anxiety meta-analysis found a pooled benefit, mainly in clinical populations. Nonclinical subgroup was null; included studies were heterogeneous.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** 19 clinical trials including participants with anxiety symptoms across clinical and nonclinical populations
- **Intervention/exposure:** Omega-3 PUFA supplementation, mean dose about 1606 mg/day; EPA/DHA formulations varied by trial
- **Comparator/control:** Placebo or non-placebo control groups
- **Duration/follow-up:** Varied across included trials
- **Endpoints:** anxiety symptoms
- **Effect estimate or direction:** Pooled anxiety-symptom improvement favored omega-3 PUFA treatment (Hedges g 0.374; 95% CI 0.081 to 0.666; P=.01). Effects were stronger in clinical populations; the nonclinical subgroup was not significant (g -0.008; 95% CI -0.266 to 0.250).
- **Adverse events / safety notes:** Adverse-event details were not extracted in this batch.
- **Population mismatch:** Clinical-anxiety findings may not generalize to healthy adults seeking wellness mood tracking.
- **Artifact rights status:** open_access

## Limitations

- Mixed clinical and nonclinical populations.
- Included placebo-controlled and non-placebo-controlled trials.
- Subgroup and dose findings should not be treated as definitive self-experiment dose rules.
- Canonical ledger DOI appeared to contain a digit transposition; source page uses the PubMed/PMC DOI.
