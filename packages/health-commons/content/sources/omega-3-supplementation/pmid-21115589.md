This source is included for **cardiovascular_outcomes_boundary**.

**Findings:**
- **null_result / major vascular events:** SU.FOL.OM3 shows biochemical exposure without clinical event benefit in secondary prevention. Effect/direction: Omega-3 increased plasma omega-3 concentrations by 37% but major vascular events were 81 vs 76; HR 1.08, 95% CI 0.79-1.47; P=0.64.

**Why it matters:** Shows biomarker exposure can rise without clear clinical-event benefit.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- major vascular events
- cardiovascular disease prevention
- plasma omega-3 concentrations

**Protocol takeaway:** Increased omega-3 status does not automatically imply event reduction.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Source kind:** journal_article
- **Study design:** rct
- **Participants:** 2501 (reported)
- **Population:** patients with history of myocardial infarction, unstable angina, or ischemic stroke
- **Intervention/exposure:** omega-3 fatty acids and/or B vitamins
- **Comparator/control:** placebo in factorial design
- **Duration/follow-up:** median follow-up about 4.7 years
- **Endpoints:** major vascular events, cardiovascular disease prevention, plasma omega-3 concentrations
- **Effect or direction:** Omega-3 increased plasma omega-3 concentrations by 37% versus placebo but had no significant effect on major vascular events (81 vs 76 patients; HR 1.08, 95% CI 0.79-1.47; P=0.64).
- **Adverse events/safety:** No specific safety result extracted beyond lack of routine-use support.
- **Population mismatch:** History of ischemic cardiovascular/cerebrovascular disease.
- **Directness:** `adjacent_variant`
- **Artifact rights status:** `open_access`

## Limitations

- Secondary-prevention and co-supplement/factorial design.
- CVD disease population.
- Not a general EPA/DHA wellness trial.
