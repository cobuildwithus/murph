This source is included for **cardiovascular_outcomes_boundary**.

**Findings:**
- **mixed_result / cardiac mortality, MACE, MI:** Formulation, dose, and baseline risk appear to matter; effects should not be generalized to consumer EPA+DHA. Effect/direction: Reported lower cardiac mortality OR 0.91, MACE OR 0.90, and MI OR 0.83, with subgroup emphasis on secondary prevention, >1 g/day, and EPA-only contexts.

**Why it matters:** Prevents overgeneralization from EPA-only or high-dose clinical settings to ordinary oral EPA/DHA supplementation.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- cardiac mortality
- major adverse cardiovascular events
- myocardial infarction
- formulation
- dose

**Protocol takeaway:** Formulation and baseline risk matter; do not promote adjacent high-risk/EPA-only effects as direct protocol benefit.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Source kind:** review
- **Study design:** meta_analysis
- **Participants:** 81073 (included_participants)
- **Population:** participants in cardiovascular-outcome RCTs across baseline risk strata
- **Intervention/exposure:** omega-3 PUFA supplementation, subgrouped by formulation, dose, and baseline cardiovascular risk
- **Comparator/control:** placebo or control
- **Duration/follow-up:** varied across 16 RCTs
- **Endpoints:** cardiac mortality, major adverse cardiovascular events, myocardial infarction, formulation, dose
- **Effect or direction:** Updated meta-analysis reported associations with lower cardiac mortality (OR 0.91, 95% CI 0.85-0.98), MACE (OR 0.90, 95% CI 0.82-0.99), and MI (OR 0.83, 95% CI 0.71-0.98); subgroup interpretations emphasized secondary prevention, doses above 1 g/day, and EPA-only formulations.
- **Adverse events/safety:** No batch-specific adverse-event extraction.
- **Population mismatch:** Includes higher-risk and secondary-prevention populations plus EPA-only/prescription-like formulations.

## Limitations

- EPA-only findings are adjacent to an EPA+DHA protocol.
- Subgroup findings can be unstable and confounded by trial design.
- Clinical-risk context, not general wellness.
