This source is included for **cardiovascular_outcomes_boundary**.

**Findings:**
- **null_result / major cardiovascular events after MI:** Alpha Omega is adjacent, with food-spread delivery and secondary-prevention mismatch. Effect/direction: Low-dose EPA-DHA or ALA in margarine did not significantly reduce major cardiovascular events among post-MI patients.

**Why it matters:** Separates dietary-spread secondary prevention from oral EPA/DHA supplementation.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- major cardiovascular events
- cardiovascular death
- myocardial infarction
- stroke

**Protocol takeaway:** Alpha Omega is not direct support for a supplement protocol.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Source kind:** journal_article
- **Study design:** rct
- **Participants:** 4837 (reported)
- **Population:** patients aged 60-80 years after myocardial infarction receiving contemporary therapy
- **Intervention/exposure:** low-dose EPA-DHA (400 mg/day), ALA (2 g/day), or both delivered in margarine spread
- **Comparator/control:** placebo margarine
- **Duration/follow-up:** about 40 months
- **Endpoints:** major cardiovascular events, cardiovascular death, myocardial infarction, stroke
- **Effect or direction:** Alpha Omega Trial concluded low-dose EPA-DHA or ALA did not significantly reduce major cardiovascular events among post-MI patients.
- **Adverse events/safety:** No batch-specific adverse-event extraction.
- **Population mismatch:** Older post-MI adults using enriched margarine, not general oral supplement users.
- **Directness:** `adjacent_variant`
- **Artifact rights status:** `permission_required`

## Limitations

- Food/spread delivery and ALA arm; not a standard capsule-only EPA/DHA protocol.
- Post-MI secondary-prevention population.
- Low-dose intervention.
