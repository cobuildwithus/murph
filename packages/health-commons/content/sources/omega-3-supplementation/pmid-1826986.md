This source is included for **lipids_triglycerides_dose_response**.

**Findings:** Fish and fish oil lowered plasma TG by 20% and 28% and VLDL TG by 42% and 52%, respectively; HDL-C increased 10% and 9%, with HDL2 particle proportion up 34% and 32%; total cholesterol, LDL-C, apoB, and blood pressure changes were not significantly different from control. Fish, but not fish oil, improved hemostatic factors (fibrinogen −15.7%, thromboxane −10.5%, bleeding time +10.8%).

**Why it matters:** Older fish-versus-fish-oil comparison for product/food-form context.

**Potential experiment signals:** triglycerides, VLDL triglycerides, HDL-C, LDL-C, bleeding time/hemostasis

**Protocol takeaway:** Fish oil can resemble fish for TG/HDL changes in this context, but food-specific hemostatic effects and design limits make it context-only.

**Population mismatch:** Mildly hyperlipidemic men and food-versus-supplement context; same-mechanism context-only for the oral supplement protocol.

**Safety notes:** Hemostatic direction noted for fish: increased bleeding time; safety-event rates were not extracted.

**Limitations:**
- Older small male study.
- Fish-versus-fish-oil product/food comparison, not a clean supplement-placebo trial.
- Control group was small.
- Hemostatic changes are food-specific in this report.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
