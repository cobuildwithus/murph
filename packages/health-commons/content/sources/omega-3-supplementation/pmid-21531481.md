This source is included for **mood_cognition**.

**Findings:** DHA supplementation significantly raised plasma DHA and total omega-3 and lowered total omega-6, but no significant effects on cognitive functioning were found. Corrected-vision subgroup had better right-eye visual acuity post-treatment.

**Why it matters:** Use only as cognition/population-boundary context.

**Potential experiment signals:** cognitive function, visual acuity, plasma DHA, plasma omega-3 and omega-6.

**Protocol takeaway:** Healthy-aging DHA trial found no cognitive benefit over 90 days. Not affective-outcome evidence despite batch placement.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Healthy adults aged 45–77 years in a healthy-aging cognition/visual-acuity trial
- **Intervention/exposure:** HiDHA tuna oil 1000 mg/day containing 252 mg DHA, 60 mg EPA, and 10 mg vitamin E
- **Comparator/control:** 1000 mg soybean oil placebo
- **Duration/follow-up:** 90 days
- **Endpoints:** cognitive function, visual acuity, plasma DHA, plasma omega-3 and omega-6
- **Effect estimate or direction:** DHA supplementation significantly raised plasma DHA and total omega-3 and lowered total omega-6, but no significant effects on cognitive functioning were found. Corrected-vision subgroup had better right-eye visual acuity post-treatment.
- **Adverse events / safety notes:** Adverse-event details were not extracted.
- **Population mismatch:** Not mood evidence; direct oral DHA exposure but cognition/vision healthy-aging context.
- **Artifact rights status:** permission_required

## Limitations

- Healthy-aging cognition/vision endpoint, not mood/depression/anxiety.
- Short follow-up and low DHA dose; source title in canonical ledger appears mismatched and was corrected against PubMed/publisher metadata.
