This source is included for **mood_cognition**.

**Findings:** Review concluded omega-3 fatty acids, particularly DHA supplementation, may alleviate depressive symptoms in patients with MCI. EPA dose subgroup findings were mixed: high and medium EPA doses were not significant, while low-dose EPA was significant; notable improvements were reported in MCI.

**Why it matters:** Boundary source for geriatric/neurocognitive mood claims.

**Potential experiment signals:** depressive symptoms in elderly/dementia/MCI populations.

**Protocol takeaway:** Recent elderly/dementia review suggests possible MCI depression benefit but has strong population mismatch. Do not generalize to healthy adults.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Elderly adults and patients with dementia or mild cognitive impairment in RCT/meta-analysis context
- **Intervention/exposure:** Omega-3 fatty acids, including EPA/DHA dose subgroups
- **Comparator/control:** Placebo/control
- **Duration/follow-up:** Varied across included trials
- **Endpoints:** depressive symptoms in elderly/dementia/MCI populations
- **Effect estimate or direction:** Review concluded omega-3 fatty acids, particularly DHA supplementation, may alleviate depressive symptoms in patients with MCI. EPA dose subgroup findings were mixed: high and medium EPA doses were not significant, while low-dose EPA was significant; notable improvements were reported in MCI.
- **Adverse events / safety notes:** Safety details were not extracted here.
- **Population mismatch:** Strong population mismatch for general wellness users; useful age/neurocognitive boundary only.
- **Artifact rights status:** open_access

## Limitations

- Population restricted to elderly/dementia/MCI contexts.
- Limited sample size noted by authors.
- Not general healthy-adult mood evidence.
