This source is included for **exercise_recovery_soreness**.

**Findings:** Fish-oil supplementation showed a dose-related recovery pattern: the 6 g/day arm had the clearest lower-soreness and faster vertical-jump recovery signal after eccentric exercise, while lower doses were less clearly separated from placebo.

**Why it matters:** Useful for dose-boundary context because it suggests recovery signals may require higher EPA/DHA exposure than many general wellness regimens use.

**Potential experiment signals:** delayed-onset muscle soreness, vertical jump recovery, muscle-damage biomarkers, post-exercise recovery time course.

**Protocol takeaway:** Treat as a small direct dose-response recovery trial: promising at the highest dose, but not enough to promise soreness relief for all users.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness:** `direct_protocol`.

**Limitations and caveats:** Small sample with approximately eight participants per arm. Laboratory eccentric-exercise model rather than normal training. Sex composition and omega-3 tissue-status reporting were not sufficient to remove population and adherence uncertainty.
