This source is included for **safety_adverse_events**.

**Findings:** The review included 70 randomized or quasi-randomized trials involving 19,927 women. Omega-3 addition during pregnancy reduced preterm and early preterm birth, but probably increased post-term pregnancies, so timing, gestational status, and obstetric monitoring matter.

**Why it matters:** Core pregnancy evidence base; pregnancy use should be treated as a special-population pathway rather than ordinary self-experimentation.

**Potential experiment signals:** pregnancy status, gestational age, preterm birth, early preterm birth, post-term pregnancy, maternal GI effects.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as pregnancy boundary evidence: benefits and risks are pregnancy-specific and should be routed through obstetric guidance, not generalized to all adult users.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

**Directness:** `safety_boundary` for `protocol_variant:omega-3-supplementation/oral-epa-dha-supplementation`.

**Limitations and population mismatch:** Pregnancy-specific interventions varied in form, dose, timing, and baseline diet; findings should not be promoted as direct evidence for non-pregnant adult supplementation.
