This source is included for **dry_eye_treatment_prevention**.

**Findings:** Population: Patients with dry eye disease in placebo-controlled omega-3 RCTs published from 2013-2023. Intervention/exposure: Omega-3 intake/supplementation; doses, EPA percentage, duration, and formulations varied. Comparator/control: Placebo or non-omega-3 oil control. Duration/follow-up: Varied across RCTs; subgroup analyses examined duration up to 12 months in extracted text. Endpoints: dry-eye symptoms, TBUT, Schirmer test, corneal fluorescein staining, tear osmolarity. Effect/direction: Reported significant improvements versus placebo for symptoms (Hedges g -1.047; p<0.001), TBUT (SMD -0.939; p<0.001), Schirmer test (SMD -0.372; p<0.001), corneal fluorescein staining (SMD -0.299; p=0.037), and tear osmolarity (SMD -0.721; p<0.001). Meta-regression linked effect direction to dose, duration, and EPA percentage. Safety/adverse events: No prominent adverse-event signal extracted from accessible summary; safety was not the primary extracted focus.

**Why it matters:** It preserves the recent positive meta-analytic signal while identifying why precision dosing and subgroup prediction remain uncertain.

**Potential experiment signals:** OSDI or symptom score, TBUT, Schirmer test, corneal fluorescein staining, tear osmolarity.

**Protocol takeaway:** Keep dry-eye benefits as context-only and paired with Cochrane/DREAM/VITAL null or mixed anchors.

**Limitations and population mismatch:** High heterogeneity was reported, including I² around 96% for some endpoints.; Patient characteristics and trial protocols differed widely.; The authors cautioned about extrapolation above studied doses, beyond 12 months, and without dietary intake data. Population mismatch: Dry-eye treatment population; does not test primary prevention or general adult supplementation.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
