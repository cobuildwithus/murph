This source is included for **mood_cognition**.

**Findings:** Plasma EPA+DHA increased substantially, but neither dose improved mental well-being after 13 or 26 weeks compared with placebo; CES-D changes were similar across high-dose, low-dose, and placebo groups (P=0.87).

**Why it matters:** Direct null evidence for general older-adult mood claims.

**Potential experiment signals:** mental well-being, CES-D, MADRS, GDS-15, HADS-A.

**Protocol takeaway:** Older-adult fish-oil RCT found no mental well-being benefit. Not an MDD treatment trial.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Independent-living adults aged 65 years or older
- **Intervention/exposure:** Fish-oil capsules providing 1800 mg/day or 400 mg/day EPA+DHA
- **Comparator/control:** Placebo
- **Duration/follow-up:** 26 weeks
- **Endpoints:** mental well-being, CES-D, MADRS, GDS-15, HADS-A
- **Effect estimate or direction:** Plasma EPA+DHA increased substantially, but neither dose improved mental well-being after 13 or 26 weeks compared with placebo; CES-D changes were similar across high-dose, low-dose, and placebo groups (P=0.87).
- **Adverse events / safety notes:** Adverse-event details were not extracted in this batch.
- **Population mismatch:** Direct oral EPA/DHA but population is older adults with low baseline depressive symptoms.
- **Artifact rights status:** unknown

## Limitations

- Older-adult population with low baseline depressive symptom scores.
- May be underpowered for clinical depression treatment effects.
