This source is included for **mood_cognition**.

**Findings:** Primary analyses suggested a small-to-modest effect versus placebo that may not be clinically beneficial; evidence was low or very low quality and heterogeneous.

**Why it matters:** Major caution source for mood claims.

**Potential experiment signals:** depressive symptomology, adverse events, non-completion.

**Protocol takeaway:** Cochrane review found only low-certainty, likely small/non-clinical depression benefit. Evidence is heterogeneous and imprecise.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Adults with depression or depressive symptoms
- **Intervention/exposure:** Omega-3 PUFA supplementation, including EPA/DHA formulations
- **Comparator/control:** Placebo or other comparators
- **Duration/follow-up:** Varied across included trials
- **Endpoints:** depressive symptomology, adverse events, non-completion
- **Effect estimate or direction:** Primary analyses suggested a small-to-modest effect versus placebo that may not be clinically beneficial; evidence was low or very low quality and heterogeneous.
- **Adverse events / safety notes:** Rates of adverse events and non-completion were similar between omega-3 and placebo, but estimates were imprecise.
- **Population mismatch:** Adjacent clinical evidence, not a healthy-adult mood protocol claim.
- **Artifact rights status:** open_access

## Limitations

- Low/very-low certainty.
- Heterogeneity and bias could make true effect smaller.
- Clinical depression context.
