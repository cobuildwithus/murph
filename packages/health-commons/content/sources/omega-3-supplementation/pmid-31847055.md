This source is included for **dry_eye_treatment_prevention**.

**Findings:** Population: Adults with dry eye disease enrolled in randomized controlled trials across 13 countries. Intervention/exposure: Omega-3 and/or omega-6 PUFA supplements, including long-chain EPA/DHA in some comparisons. Comparator/control: Placebo/control supplement, artificial tears, no treatment, omega-6, or conventional therapy depending on comparison. Duration/follow-up: Varied across included RCTs. Endpoints: dry-eye symptoms, Schirmer test, tear osmolarity, TBUT, ocular surface staining, gastrointestinal adverse events. Effect/direction: Long-chain omega-3 versus placebo showed low-certainty little-to-no symptom reduction (4 studies, n=677; MD -2.47 OSDI units; 95% CI -5.14 to 0.19). Schirmer tear production had moderate-certainty probable benefit (6 studies, n=1704; MD 0.68 mm/5 min; 95% CI 0.26 to 1.09), judged not clinically meaningful by the review. Safety/adverse events: Adverse effects were difficult to pool; temporary gastrointestinal problems were the most common side effect described in extracted review summaries.

**Why it matters:** This source prevents overstatement by retaining null, mixed, low-certainty, and adverse-event uncertainty findings.

**Potential experiment signals:** OSDI or symptom score, Schirmer test, tear osmolarity, TBUT, gastrointestinal symptoms.

**Protocol takeaway:** Dry-eye content should remain context-only unless treated as a separate clinically supervised variant.

**Limitations and population mismatch:** Evidence certainty was often low or moderate.; Substantial heterogeneity prevented pooling TBUT and adverse-effect data in key comparisons.; Interventions included omega-3, omega-6, and combinations, making EPA/DHA-specific interpretation limited. Population mismatch: Dry-eye treatment population with heterogeneous interventions and comparators; not general prevention evidence.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
