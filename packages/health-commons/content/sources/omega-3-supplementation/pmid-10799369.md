This source is included for **lipids_triglycerides_dose_response**.

**Findings:** Relative to olive oil, TG fell by 0.45±0.15 mmol/L (~20%) with DHA and 0.37±0.14 mmol/L (~18%) with EPA; DHA increased LDL-C by 8% and LDL particle size by 0.25±0.08 nm, increased HDL2-C by ~29%, and both EPA and DHA increased fasting insulin; EPA tended to increase fasting glucose but not significantly.

**Why it matters:** Foundational head-to-head EPA versus DHA lipid-panel RCT.

**Potential experiment signals:** triglycerides, LDL-C, LDL particle size, HDL2-C, fasting glucose, fasting insulin

**Protocol takeaway:** Both EPA and DHA can lower TG; DHA may raise LDL-C while glucose/insulin signals should not be ignored in susceptible users.

**Population mismatch:** Overweight mildly hyperlipidemic male population differs from general users and from mixed EPA+DHA products.

**Safety notes:** No adverse-event extraction available; metabolic watch item: fasting insulin increased with both EPA and DHA, and EPA tended to increase fasting glucose.

**Limitations:**
- Men only.
- Mild hyperlipidemia and overweight population.
- High purified EPA/DHA doses.
- Short duration.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.
