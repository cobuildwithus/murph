This source is included for **safety_adverse_events**.

**Findings:** The review reports no evidence of bleeding with fish oil independent of anticoagulants and states that fish oil, ginseng, and saw palmetto are not associated with bleeding. It still emphasizes that clinicians should know about dietary supplement use.

**Why it matters:** Places fish oil among other supplements that clinicians ask about before surgery or anticoagulant treatment.

**Potential experiment signals:** supplement disclosure, bleeding symptoms, surgery planning, anticoagulant use.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as supplement-bleeding context: fish oil was not associated with bleeding in this review, but supplement disclosure remains important.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

**Directness:** `safety_boundary` for `protocol_variant:omega-3-supplementation/oral-epa-dha-supplementation`.

**Limitations and population mismatch:** Narrative review across heterogeneous supplements; direct fish-oil evidence should be drawn from dedicated bleeding reviews and trials where possible.
