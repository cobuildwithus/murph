This source is included for **mood_cognition**.

**Findings:** Network meta-analysis reported both high-dose (SMD 0.908 ± 0.331; 95% CI 0.262 to 1.581) and low-dose (SMD 0.601 ± 0.286) n-3 PUFAs superior to placebo, and concluded high dose might be superior in early therapy.

**Why it matters:** Dose-boundary context only.

**Potential experiment signals:** depressive symptoms.

**Protocol takeaway:** MDD network meta-analysis suggests high-dose may outperform low-dose early, but evidence is indirect. Do not use as a standalone dose recommendation.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Patients with major depressive disorder in a network meta-analysis of omega-3 dose comparisons
- **Intervention/exposure:** High-dose and low-dose n-3 PUFA supplementation
- **Comparator/control:** Placebo and indirect dose comparisons
- **Duration/follow-up:** Early therapy period; underlying trials varied
- **Endpoints:** depressive symptoms
- **Effect estimate or direction:** Network meta-analysis reported both high-dose (SMD 0.908 ± 0.331; 95% CI 0.262 to 1.581) and low-dose (SMD 0.601 ± 0.286) n-3 PUFAs superior to placebo, and concluded high dose might be superior in early therapy.
- **Adverse events / safety notes:** Adverse-event details were not extracted in this batch.
- **Population mismatch:** Adjacent clinical MDD evidence, not direct general-adult protocol evidence.
- **Artifact rights status:** open_access

## Limitations

- Network meta-analysis relies on indirect comparisons.
- MDD clinical-treatment context.
- Authors call for direct head-to-head trials.
