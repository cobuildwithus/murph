This source is included for **mood_cognition**.

**Findings:** Long-term marine omega-3 did not prevent depression or improve mood scores; trial reported a small statistically significant increase in risk of depression/clinically relevant symptoms but no difference in mood-score trajectory.

**Why it matters:** Strong direct guardrail against prevention claims.

**Potential experiment signals:** incident depression or clinically relevant depressive symptoms, mood scores.

**Protocol takeaway:** Large VITAL-DEP RCT found no depression-prevention or mood-score benefit. Dose and prevention population differ from clinical depression treatment trials.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Adults aged 50 years or older without clinically relevant depressive symptoms at baseline
- **Intervention/exposure:** Long-term marine omega-3 fatty acids, about 465 mg EPA plus 375 mg DHA daily
- **Comparator/control:** Placebo
- **Duration/follow-up:** Median follow-up 5.3 years
- **Endpoints:** incident depression or clinically relevant depressive symptoms, mood scores
- **Effect estimate or direction:** Long-term marine omega-3 did not prevent depression or improve mood scores; trial reported a small statistically significant increase in risk of depression/clinically relevant symptoms but no difference in mood-score trajectory.
- **Adverse events / safety notes:** Detailed adverse-event extraction was not performed here; public summaries reported no overall mood benefit.
- **Population mismatch:** Highly relevant to prevention/wellness claims in older adults; not treatment evidence.
- **Artifact rights status:** permission_required

## Limitations

- Prevention trial in older adults; low-to-moderate daily EPA/DHA dose.
- Not designed as a treatment trial for diagnosed depression.
