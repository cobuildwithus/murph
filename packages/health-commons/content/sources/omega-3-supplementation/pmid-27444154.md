This source is included for **clinical_cardiovascular_lipid_boundary**.

**Findings:**
- The review identifies three prescription omega-3 fatty-acid formulation classes approved in the US for adults with severe hypertriglyceridemia: omega-3 ethyl esters containing EPA+DHA, icosapent ethyl as EPA-only, and omega-3 carboxylic acids containing EPA, DHA, and DPA.
- It discusses triglyceride lowering at prescription doses and highlights that DHA-containing products can raise LDL-C in some severe hypertriglyceridemia contexts, while EPA-only icosapent ethyl is described as not raising LDL-C in those trials.
- The source is a formulation and clinical-use review, not primary direct protocol evidence.

**Why it matters:** Provides product-class distinctions that prevent inappropriate generalization from clinical products to consumer supplements.

**Potential experiment signals:** triglycerides; LDL-C; non-HDL-C; ApoB; formulation; dose; GI tolerability.

**Protocol takeaway:** Formulation and regulatory status matter; prescription omega-3 evidence should not be treated as identical to supplement evidence.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
