This source is included for **dry_eye_treatment_prevention**.

**Findings:** Population: Human studies in dry eye disease, contact-lens discomfort, and corneal collagen crosslinking plus animal ocular-surface models. Intervention/exposure: Topical omega-3 fatty acid eyedrops. Comparator/control: Varied controls across human and animal studies. Duration/follow-up: Varied across included studies. Endpoints: ocular surface staining, TBUT, symptoms, MGD, tear production, inflammatory cytokines, monocyte infiltration. Effect/direction: Review included five human and five animal studies. Human evidence was promising for ocular surface staining and TBUT, equivocal for symptoms/MGD, and showed no effect on tear production; animal models suggested decreased inflammatory cytokines and monocyte infiltration. Safety/adverse events: Topical route review; no oral-supplement safety inference should be made.

**Why it matters:** It prevents topical omega-3 ocular findings from being misused as oral supplementation evidence.

**Potential experiment signals:** ocular surface staining, TBUT, tear production, ocular inflammatory markers.

**Protocol takeaway:** Adjacent-variant context only.

**Limitations and population mismatch:** Adjacent topical route.; Small evidence base with heterogeneous indications.; Animal mechanisms do not establish oral clinical efficacy. Population mismatch: Topical eyedrop route and mixed ocular-surface indications; not oral EPA/DHA supplementation.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
