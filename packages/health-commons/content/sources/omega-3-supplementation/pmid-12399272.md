This source is included for **lipids_triglycerides_dose_response**.

**Findings:** In 51 completers, EPA and DHA reduced triglycerides by 19% and 15%, respectively; no significant total, LDL, or HDL cholesterol effects were reported, but HDL2 increased 16% with EPA and 12% with DHA and HDL3 decreased 11% with EPA. Fasting glucose increased with EPA (+1.40±0.29 mmol/L) and DHA (+0.98±0.29 mmol/L) versus olive oil; no significant glycated hemoglobin, insulin/C-peptide, insulin sensitivity/secretion, or blood pressure effects were reported.

**Why it matters:** Population-specific RCT useful for preserving diabetic/hypertensive subgroup evidence and mismatches.

**Potential experiment signals:** triglycerides, fasting glucose, HbA1c, blood pressure, LDL-C, HDL-C

**Protocol takeaway:** Users with diabetes or glycemic instability should not treat high-dose EPA/DHA as purely lipid-beneficial without glucose monitoring/clinician context.

**Population mismatch:** Type 2 diabetes with treated hypertension is a clinically specific population.

**Safety notes:** Metabolic safety watch: short-term fasting glucose increased with EPA and DHA in this type 2 diabetes/treated hypertension population.

**Limitations:**
- Population has type 2 diabetes and treated hypertension.
- Short duration.
- High-dose purified EPA/DHA rather than mixed supplement.
- Glycemic signal may not generalize but matters for diabetic users.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.
