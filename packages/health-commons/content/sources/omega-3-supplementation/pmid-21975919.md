This source is included for **lipids_triglycerides_dose_response**.

**Findings:** Extracted review summary: EPA monotherapy trials n=10, DHA monotherapy trials n=17, and EPA-versus-DHA head-to-head trials n=6. DHA raised LDL-C by 7.23 mg/dL versus placebo and 4.63 mg/dL more than EPA in direct comparisons; both EPA and DHA reduced triglycerides, with greater triglyceride reduction by DHA in head-to-head trials; DHA raised HDL-C by 4.49 mg/dL versus placebo while EPA did not.

**Why it matters:** Key evidence for separating EPA and DHA effects on TG, LDL-C, and HDL-C.

**Potential experiment signals:** fasting triglycerides, LDL-C, HDL-C, EPA dose, DHA dose

**Protocol takeaway:** Component mix matters: DHA may have stronger HDL/LDL effects, while both EPA and DHA can lower TG.

**Population mismatch:** EPA-only/DHA-only monotherapy evidence is component-specific rather than a direct mixed EPA+DHA product test.

**Safety notes:** Not a safety-focused extraction.

**Limitations:**
- Participant count was not extracted from accessible review summary.
- Included trials and dosing varied.
- EPA-only and DHA-only findings should not be collapsed into one EPA+DHA claim without caveats.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.
