This source is included for **mood_cognition**.

**Findings:** Across 58 RCTs, each 2000 mg/day increment was associated with significant improvements in several domains, including attention, perceptual speed, language, primary memory, visuospatial function, and global cognition; executive function was not statistically significant, and dose-response shapes were non-linear for some domains. Safety note: Adverse events were not extracted as a main finding from accessible summaries.

**Why it matters:** This is the newest large randomized-trial dose-response synthesis in the batch and helps bound cognitive expectations.

**Potential experiment signals:** attention, perceptual speed, language, primary memory, visuospatial function, global cognition, executive function, episodic memory.

**Protocol takeaway:** Omega-3 cognition evidence is domain- and population-dependent; executive-function and long-term generalization remain uncertain.

**Population mismatch:** Some included trials involved dementia, MCI, or risk-factor populations, so findings should not be promoted as a blanket benefit for healthy adults.

**Limitations:** Aggregate includes heterogeneous adult populations and cognitive statuses.; GRADE certainty varied by domain and was low for several outcomes.; High heterogeneity was noted for executive-function estimates.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
