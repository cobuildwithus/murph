This source is included for **mood_cognition**.

**Findings:** Each 1 g/day omega-3 decreased anxiety symptoms (SMD -0.70; 95% CI -1.17 to -0.22; low-certainty). Nonlinear dose-response suggested the greatest effect at 2 g/day (SMD -0.93; 95% CI -1.85 to -0.01); doses below 2 g/day did not affect symptoms.

**Why it matters:** Useful for anxiety tracking and safety context, with uncertainty.

**Potential experiment signals:** anxiety symptoms, adverse events.

**Protocol takeaway:** Anxiety dose-response review found low-certainty symptom reduction and no pooled AE increase. Certainty is low and clinical/nonclinical populations may differ.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Participants in intervention studies assessing anxiety symptoms
- **Intervention/exposure:** Omega-3 fatty acid supplementation at varying doses
- **Comparator/control:** Placebo or control
- **Duration/follow-up:** Varied across included studies
- **Endpoints:** anxiety symptoms, adverse events
- **Effect estimate or direction:** Each 1 g/day omega-3 decreased anxiety symptoms (SMD -0.70; 95% CI -1.17 to -0.22; low-certainty). Nonlinear dose-response suggested the greatest effect at 2 g/day (SMD -0.93; 95% CI -1.85 to -0.01); doses below 2 g/day did not affect symptoms.
- **Adverse events / safety notes:** Omega-3 did not increase adverse events in pooled analysis (OR 1.20; 95% CI 0.89 to 1.61; moderate-certainty).
- **Population mismatch:** Anxiety-symptom treatment evidence may not generalize to healthy wellness users.
- **Artifact rights status:** open_access

## Limitations

- Certainty for efficacy was low/very low.
- Dose-response estimates are review-level and may be unstable.
- Population mix may not match healthy adults.
