This source is included for **lipids_triglycerides_dose_response**.

**Findings:** The review summary extracted here reports that both EPA and DHA lowered fasting triglycerides by roughly 12–26% across direct comparison studies; DHA appeared to increase HDL-C more and also increased LDL-C and LDL particle size more than EPA in some trials.

**Why it matters:** Systematic comparison of EPA and DHA differences across lipid and related endpoints.

**Potential experiment signals:** fasting triglycerides, LDL-C, HDL-C, LDL particle size, fasting glucose/insulin context

**Protocol takeaway:** Use EPA/DHA composition as an interpretation variable; triglycerides may fall while LDL-C can rise, especially with DHA-heavy products.

**Population mismatch:** Component-specific trials may not match mixed EPA+DHA consumer products.

**Safety notes:** Not a safety-focused extraction.

**Limitations:**
- Systematic review but not always pooled quantitatively.
- Few direct EPA-versus-DHA trials for some endpoints.
- Glycemic and other non-lipid outcomes were limited or inconclusive.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.
