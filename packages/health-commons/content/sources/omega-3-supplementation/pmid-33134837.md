This source is included for **dry_eye_treatment_prevention**.

**Findings:** Population: Patients with meibomian gland dysfunction or MGD-associated dry eye in included trials/clinical-trial records. Intervention/exposure: Oral omega-3 supplementation; dose and formulation varied across included records. Comparator/control: Placebo or control groups in included records where applicable. Duration/follow-up: Varied across included trials; no single pooled duration extracted. Endpoints: NaFl tear breakup time (TBUT), non-invasive TBUT, Schirmer I test, OSDI, tear osmolarity, meibomian gland dysfunction signs. Effect/direction: Review reported a favorable association for invasive sodium-fluorescein TBUT versus placebo (OR 8.72; 95% CI 4.73-16.09; p<0.001); no publication-bias signal was reported by Egger test (p=0.2944). Safety/adverse events: No batch-extracted adverse-event signal was available from the accessible abstract/summary.

**Why it matters:** MGD is a common evaporative dry-eye driver, so this review helps preserve the endpoint-specific treatment signal without merging it into general omega-3 wellness claims.

**Potential experiment signals:** MGD symptom burden, TBUT/NITBUT, OSDI, Schirmer test, tear osmolarity.

**Protocol takeaway:** Keep as context-only dry-eye/MGD evidence; do not present as a general prevention or wellness benefit.

**Limitations and population mismatch:** MGD-specific evidence, not broad dry-eye prevention evidence.; Only a small evidence base was available, including one RCT and six clinical-trial records in the accessible summary.; Doses, formulations, follow-up, and outcome measurement methods varied. Population mismatch: Dry-eye/MGD treatment population; does not represent general adult wellness supplementation or primary prevention.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
