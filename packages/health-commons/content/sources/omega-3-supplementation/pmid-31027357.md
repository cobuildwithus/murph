This source is included for **dry_eye_treatment_prevention**.

**Findings:** Population: Patients with nonspecific typical dry eye disease in RCTs before March 2019. Intervention/exposure: Polyunsaturated fatty acid supplementation, with subgroup emphasis on PUFAs without concurrent eye medications. Comparator/control: Placebo or control groups in RCTs. Duration/follow-up: Varied; extracted summary indicates short-term benefits and duration-related attenuation for some endpoints. Endpoints: TBUT, Schirmer test, tear osmolarity, OSDI. Effect/direction: For PUFAs without other eye medications, reported improvements versus placebo included TBUT (MD 1.80; p=0.001), Schirmer (MD 0.50; p<0.001), tear osmolarity (MD -15.95; p<0.001), and OSDI (MD -10.19; p<0.001). TBUT and OSDI effects weakened with treatment duration. Safety/adverse events: Accessible summary described relatively few adverse events but did not provide a batch-extracted effect estimate.

**Why it matters:** It helps separate oral EPA/DHA from broader PUFA and mixed omega-3/omega-6 products used in ocular nutrition.

**Potential experiment signals:** TBUT, Schirmer test, tear osmolarity, OSDI, concurrent eye-medication use.

**Protocol takeaway:** Context-only; do not generalize PUFA findings directly to the oral EPA/DHA starter protocol.

**Limitations and population mismatch:** Broader PUFA class can include omega-3/omega-6 formulations.; Best signal appears tied to short-term use without other eye medications.; Duration may attenuate some observed effects. Population mismatch: Dry-eye treatment population; includes broader PUFA interventions and not necessarily EPA/DHA alone.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
