This source is included for **mood_cognition**.

**Findings:** Long-chain omega-3 probably had little or no effect on risk of depression symptoms (RR 1.01; 95% CI 0.92 to 1.10). Anxiety symptom analysis also did not support benefit (SMD 0.15; 95% CI 0.05 to 0.26). Evidence for existing depression severity/remission was very low quality.

**Why it matters:** Strong guardrail against mood-prevention claims in general adult users.

**Potential experiment signals:** new depression, depression symptoms, anxiety symptoms.

**Protocol takeaway:** Prevention meta-analysis found little or no support for omega-3 preventing depression or anxiety symptoms. Does not rule out clinician-managed treatment effects in diagnosed depression.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction details

- **Population:** Adults with or without existing depression/anxiety in randomized prevention trials of at least 24 weeks
- **Intervention/exposure:** Increased long-chain omega-3, ALA, total PUFA, or omega-6 intake; median LC omega-3 dose around 0.95 g/day for depression-risk analysis
- **Comparator/control:** Usual or lower intake/placebo controls
- **Duration/follow-up:** Median 12 months for depression-risk analysis; median 6 months for anxiety-symptom analysis
- **Endpoints:** new depression, depression symptoms, anxiety symptoms
- **Effect estimate or direction:** Long-chain omega-3 probably had little or no effect on risk of depression symptoms (RR 1.01; 95% CI 0.92 to 1.10). Anxiety symptom analysis also did not support benefit (SMD 0.15; 95% CI 0.05 to 0.26). Evidence for existing depression severity/remission was very low quality.
- **Adverse events / safety notes:** Safety details were not extracted in this batch.
- **Population mismatch:** Highly relevant to general prevention claims; less direct for treating diagnosed depression.
- **Artifact rights status:** unknown

## Limitations

- Prevention-focused trials may dilute treatment effects in diagnosed depression.
- Existing-depression outcomes were very low-certainty.
