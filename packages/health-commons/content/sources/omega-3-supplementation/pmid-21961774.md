This source is included for **safety_adverse_events**.

**Findings:** Ten trials involving 699 children were included. Omega-3 supplementation showed a small ADHD symptom effect, and EPA dose correlated with efficacy; for this batch, the main use is to flag pediatric population mismatch and the need for separate pediatric safeguards.

**Why it matters:** Pediatric trial synthesis provides a child-population boundary; children should not be treated as smaller adults in supplement protocols.

**Potential experiment signals:** ADHD symptoms, EPA dose, pediatric tolerability, dropouts, GI symptoms.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as pediatric boundary/context only: omega-3 trials in children show modest ADHD symptom effects, but pediatric use needs separate dosing and safety framing.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

**Directness:** `safety_boundary` for `protocol_variant:omega-3-supplementation/oral-epa-dha-supplementation`.

**Limitations and population mismatch:** Pediatric ADHD populations, variable doses/forms, and limited accessible adverse-event detail; not direct evidence for a general adult oral EPA/DHA protocol.
