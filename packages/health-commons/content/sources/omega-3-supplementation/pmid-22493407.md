This source is included for **cardiovascular_outcomes_boundary**.

**Findings:**
- **null_result / secondary prevention overall cardiovascular events:** Secondary-prevention EPA+DHA meta-analysis does not support broad clinical event claims. Effect/direction: 14 RCTs / 20,485 patients: overall cardiovascular events RR 0.99, 95% CI 0.89-1.09; no clear benefit for all-cause mortality, sudden death, MI, CHF, or TIA/stroke.
- **safety / gastrointestinal adverse events:** Safety data were limited and did not show a clear GI excess in this extraction. Effect/direction: GI troubles RR 1.19, 95% CI 0.95-1.49; GI bleeding evidence sparse.

**Why it matters:** Separates secondary-prevention disease evidence from general oral EPA/DHA wellness claims.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- overall cardiovascular events
- all-cause mortality
- sudden cardiac death
- myocardial infarction
- stroke
- gastrointestinal adverse events

**Protocol takeaway:** Do not generalize secondary-prevention meta-analysis to wellness benefits.

## Extraction details

- **Source kind:** review
- **Study design:** meta_analysis

- **Population:** patients with a history of cardiovascular disease in secondary-prevention trials
- **Intervention/exposure:** EPA+DHA omega-3 fatty acid supplements
- **Comparator/control:** placebo
- **Duration/follow-up:** varied across 14 randomized double-blind placebo-controlled trials
- **Endpoints:** overall cardiovascular events, all-cause mortality, sudden cardiac death, myocardial infarction, stroke, gastrointestinal adverse events
- **Effect or direction:** Meta-analysis found insufficient evidence for secondary prevention: overall cardiovascular events RR 0.99 (95% CI 0.89-1.09); no reduction in all-cause mortality, sudden cardiac death, MI, CHF, or TIA/stroke.
- **Adverse events/safety:** Reported GI troubles RR 1.19 (95% CI 0.95-1.49) and sparse GI bleeding data; no clear extracted safety excess.
- **Population mismatch:** History of CVD; not a general adult supplement population.

## Limitations

- Secondary-prevention population with existing CVD.
- Trial heterogeneity and older studies.
- Not primary wellness evidence.
