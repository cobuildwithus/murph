This source is included for **exercise_recovery_soreness**.

**Findings:** Eight weeks of 2.5 g/day DHA plus 0.5 g/day EPA increased plasma EPA/DHA-derived oxylipins; strength-recovery signals improved, but soreness did not clearly differ from placebo.

**Why it matters:** Useful for mechanism and endpoint selection because it connects oxylipins with post-exercise recovery rather than relying only on symptom ratings.

**Potential experiment signals:** omega-3-derived oxylipins, maximal voluntary contraction recovery, muscle soreness, body-fat and oxylipin predictors.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as mechanistic context for strength-recovery tracking; do not present it as proof that omega-3 reliably reduces soreness.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness:** `same_mechanism`.

**Limitations and caveats:** Small sample of healthy men. Mechanistic and recovery endpoints rather than a broad consumer-outcome trial. Open-access status appears to use a non-commercial/no-derivatives license, so PDF redistribution should be handled conservatively.
