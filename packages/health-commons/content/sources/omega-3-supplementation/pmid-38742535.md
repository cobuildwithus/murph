This source is included for **safety_adverse_events**.

**Findings:** Across randomized trials, omega-3 PUFAs were not associated with higher overall bleeding, hemorrhagic stroke, intracranial bleeding, gastrointestinal bleeding, or fatal bleeding. A subgroup receiving high-dose purified EPA showed a relative bleeding increase with a small absolute increase, making dose/formulation separation essential.

**Why it matters:** Most current batch backbone synthesis for bleeding risk, with a separate high-dose purified EPA signal that should not be generalized to low-dose mixed EPA/DHA supplements.

**Potential experiment signals:** bleeding events, bruising or nosebleeds, anticoagulant or antiplatelet use, EPA dose boundary, atrial fibrillation/flutter safety watch.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as a bleeding-safety boundary: randomized evidence does not show a broad bleeding increase, but high-dose purified EPA appears to require separate caution and monitoring.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

**Directness:** `safety_boundary` for `protocol_variant:omega-3-supplementation/oral-epa-dha-supplementation`.

**Limitations and population mismatch:** Bleeding definitions varied across trials, analyses were trial-level rather than individual-participant data, and many included populations were clinical cardiovascular-risk cohorts or prescription-product cohorts rather than healthy supplement users.
