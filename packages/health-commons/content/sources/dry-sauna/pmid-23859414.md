This source is included for **acute mechanistic**.

## Why it matters

Supports plausibility for repeated dry-sauna autonomic and respiratory physiology under clinical supervision.

## Findings captured

- At six weeks, the sauna group had higher LF/HF (2.5 vs 0.9), higher peak nasal inspiratory flow (161.9 vs 103.0), and higher FEV1 (95.6 vs 80.1) versus controls; FVC and FEV1/FVC were not significantly different. Outcomes were measured immediately after the intervention window.
- The trial excluded asthma, chronic respiratory disease, cardiovascular disease, hypertension, diabetes, and inability to tolerate heat/cold; the authors also noted the small sample, lack of symptom outcomes, and absence of 24-hour HRV monitoring.

## Protocol takeaway

Six-week 3x/week dry sauna improved HRV-frequency and respiratory measures in allergic-rhinitis patients.

## Important limits

Young allergic-rhinitis population, small sample, exclusions, and immediate measurements limit direct general-population Murph claims.
