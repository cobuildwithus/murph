## Why it matters

Supports stop rules and modality-disambiguation language for heat exposure.

## Findings captured

- The abstract reports that at thermal tolerance cerebral blood flow decreased by about 30% in the sauna condition while remaining unchanged in spa and exercise conditions; non-invasive intracranial pressure increased about 18% across modes. This is a heat-load and modality safety source, not dry-sauna efficacy evidence.

## Protocol takeaway

Heating mode changed cerebral blood-flow response at thermal tolerance.

## Important limits

The sauna condition was hot humid air and heating was to tolerance or core 40 °C, beyond ordinary conservative self-experiment dosing.
