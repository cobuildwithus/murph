This source is included for **Measurement artifacts: core temperature, wearables, sweat, and hydration**.

**Findings:** The 2025 study reported that pills ingested 0.5 or 3 hours pre-exercise produced significantly lower gastrointestinal temperatures than pills ingested 12 hours before exercise when tepid water was ingested, indicating that even 3 hours may be insufficient to avoid fluid-interference artifacts. Effect/estimate: Direction only in extracted batch: 0.5-hour and 3-hour pills read lower than 12-hour pills under tepid-water conditions.

**Why it matters:** Recent caveat that shorter pre-ingestion windows plus fluid intake can confound gastrointestinal temperature measurements.

**Potential experiment signals:** core body temperature, measurement caveats, fluid loss

**Protocol takeaway:** For sauna GI-pill experiments, require longer pre-ingestion or strict fluid-ingestion logs; 3 hours may not be enough if drinking continues.

**Safety/adverse-event notes:** Measurement-safety boundary: normal hydration timing can artifactually lower pill temperature and obscure heat strain.

**Limitations and population mismatch:** Exercise-heat and tepid-water protocol, not sauna; participant count and full estimates were not extracted here. Population mismatch: Exercise-heat participants rather than sauna users.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
