This source is included for **Long-term Finnish cohorts and observational outcome context**.

**Findings:** During follow-up with 155 incident strokes reported, 4-7 sauna sessions/week was associated with lower stroke risk than once-weekly sauna use; age- and sex-adjusted hazard ratio was reported as 0.39.

**Why it matters:** Primary Finnish cohort for stroke-incidence context; important boundary source because it is not an acute self-test outcome.

**Potential experiment signals:** stroke context, cardiovascular-risk context.

**Protocol takeaway:** Adds long-term vascular outcome context while remaining noncausal and not directly testable in Murph.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Study design:** Prospective cohort of Finnish men and women.
- **Population:** Finnish men and women aged approximately 53-74 years without a known prior stroke.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Self-reported sauna bathing frequency.
- **Comparator/control:** Once-weekly sauna bathing.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Median follow-up about 14.9 years.
- **Endpoints:** incident stroke.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No acute sauna-session adverse-event reporting for high-heat daily use.
- **Limitations:** Observational; older Finnish population; residual confounding possible; exposure was habitual frequency rather than assigned dose.
- **Population mismatch:** Not the Bryan Johnson routine and not a short-term self-experiment endpoint.
- **Directness to Bryan Johnson Sauna:** same_mechanism; this is not direct evidence for daily 20 minutes at 93 C / 200 F after workouts.
