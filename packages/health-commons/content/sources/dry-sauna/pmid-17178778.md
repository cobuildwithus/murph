This source is included for **Measurement artifacts: core temperature, wearables, sweat, and hydration**.

**Findings:** Review evidence supports ingestible telemetric sensors as a field-friendly index of core temperature when calibration, data correction, ingestion timing, and electromagnetic-interference controls are handled carefully; the review also notes intestinal temperature may lag esophageal temperature and differ from rectal temperature. Effect/estimate: Reported review-level agreement examples include intestinal-esophageal systematic bias below 0.1 °C with limits of agreement within about ±0.4 °C; intestinal-rectal comparisons were more directionally biased.

**Why it matters:** Foundational review for ingestible-pill core-temperature measurement and practical use caveats in exercise heat monitoring.

**Potential experiment signals:** core body temperature, measurement caveats

**Protocol takeaway:** Use ingestible-pill data as measurement-context evidence for core-temperature logging, not as proof that the sauna protocol improves outcomes.

**Safety/adverse-event notes:** Measurement caveat rather than safety outcome: poor ingestion timing, electromagnetic interference, or uncorrected sensor bias can misclassify heat strain.

**Limitations and population mismatch:** Narrative review; evidence is from exercise/occupational heat settings, not dry sauna, and includes device/protocol heterogeneity. Population mismatch: Exercise and occupational monitoring populations; not Bryan Johnson dry-sauna users.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
