This source is included for **Measurement artifacts: core temperature, wearables, sweat, and hydration**.

**Findings:** A water-bath validation of 119 ingestible sensors found systematic device bias: 13 sensors (10.9%) exceeded ±0.1 °C bias, 4 (3.3%) exceeded ±0.5 °C, and 71 (59.7%) exceeded the reference thermometer accuracy of ±0.06 °C; average bias was 0.06 ± 0.24 °C. Effect/estimate: 13/119 sensors had bias greater than ±0.1 °C; 4/119 greater than ±0.5 °C; 71/119 greater than the reference thermometer accuracy; average bias 0.06 ± 0.24 °C.

**Why it matters:** Important correction/bias source before using pill-sensor readouts as precise biological-dose endpoints.

**Potential experiment signals:** core body temperature, measurement caveats

**Protocol takeaway:** Calibrate or correct ingestible sensors before interpreting small core-temperature changes.

**Safety/adverse-event notes:** Measurement-safety boundary: uncorrected pill bias may create false reassurance or false alarm in heat monitoring.

**Limitations and population mismatch:** Bench water-bath study, not human sauna; temperature dynamics and GI physiology are not represented. Population mismatch: No human population; device-bias evidence only.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
