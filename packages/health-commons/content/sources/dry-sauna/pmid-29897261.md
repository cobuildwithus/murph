This source is included for **Long-term Finnish cohorts and observational outcome context**.

**Findings:** Frequent sauna sessions were reported to reduce or associate with lower inflammatory markers at baseline and 11-year follow-up, while extracted records did not show a corresponding effect on the oxidative-stress proxy gamma-glutamyltransferase.

**Why it matters:** Longitudinal biomarker context for plausible pathways behind cohort associations; still not a short-term outcome study.

**Potential experiment signals:** inflammation context, oxidative stress context.

**Protocol takeaway:** Preserve the mixed biomarker finding: inflammation associations, but not a clear oxidative-stress signal.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Study design:** KIHD prospective cohort biomarker analysis.
- **Population:** Middle-aged Finnish men in the KIHD cohort.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Sauna bathing frequency.
- **Comparator/control:** Lower sauna frequency categories.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Baseline and 11-year follow-up biomarker context.
- **Endpoints:** inflammatory markers, oxidative stress proxy, C-reactive protein, fibrinogen, leukocyte count, gamma-glutamyltransferase.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No acute high-temperature daily sauna safety data.
- **Limitations:** Observational biomarker study; oxidative-stress proxy interpretation is indirect; men-only; not a randomized dose test.
- **Population mismatch:** Biomarker context does not prove Bryan Johnson protocol effects on wearable endpoints.
- **Directness to Bryan Johnson Sauna:** same_mechanism; this is not direct evidence for daily 20 minutes at 93 C / 200 F after workouts.
