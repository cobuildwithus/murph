This source is included for **Core-temperature threshold mechanisms and heat-stress biology**.

**Findings:** The study reported 1,423 differentially expressed genes after heat exposure, with an amplified response at 1 hour and HSPA1A upregulated immediately after exposure. The authors reported no statistically significant group increase in core temperature or vital signs, although one male participant reached 39 °C.; Eleven of fifteen participants completed the 15-minute exposure; four female participants stopped early at 10, 10, 13, and 14 minutes. Seven participants reported headache, dizziness, or nausea immediately after exposure, and one male reached a core temperature of 39 °C.

**Why it matters:** Closest source in this batch to sauna exposure and includes both molecular heat-stress response and tolerability boundary data, but it is shorter and cooler than the Bryan Johnson target.

**Potential experiment signals:** core body temperature, heart rate, blood pressure, heat-stress symptoms, PBMC transcriptome, HSPA1A transcript.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as adjacent passive-heat mechanism and safety context: a short 75.7 °C exposure changed the PBMC transcriptome and caused early stops/symptoms in some participants, but it did not reproduce the Bryan Johnson 93 °C dose.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
