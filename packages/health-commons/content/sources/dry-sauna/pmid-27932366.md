This source is included for **Long-term Finnish cohorts and observational outcome context**.

**Findings:** Compared with once-weekly sauna bathing, reported hazard ratios for dementia were 0.78 for 2-3 sessions/week and 0.34 for 4-7 sessions/week; corresponding Alzheimer disease hazard ratios were 0.80 and 0.35.

**Why it matters:** Commonly cited non-cardiovascular Finnish cohort outcome; useful as adjacent observational context rather than protocol evidence.

**Potential experiment signals:** cognitive outcome context, long-term observational context.

**Protocol takeaway:** Interesting long-term context, but not a short-term self-experiment claim.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Study design:** KIHD prospective cohort.
- **Population:** Middle-aged Finnish men in the KIHD cohort.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Self-reported sauna bathing frequency.
- **Comparator/control:** Once-weekly sauna bathing.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Median follow-up about 20.7 years.
- **Endpoints:** incident dementia, Alzheimer disease.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No acute safety data for high-temperature daily sauna.
- **Limitations:** Observational men-only cohort; dementia associations may be influenced by lifestyle, vascular risk, and healthy-user confounding.
- **Population mismatch:** Long-term dementia outcome is not a Murph protocol endpoint and does not test Bryan Johnson dosing.
- **Directness to Bryan Johnson Sauna:** same_mechanism; this is not direct evidence for daily 20 minutes at 93 C / 200 F after workouts.
