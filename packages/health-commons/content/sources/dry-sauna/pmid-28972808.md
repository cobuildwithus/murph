This source is included for **Long-term Finnish cohorts and observational outcome context**.

**Findings:** The study reported that frequent sauna bathing and higher cardiorespiratory fitness were jointly associated with lower cardiovascular and all-cause mortality risk than lower-exposure categories.

**Why it matters:** Useful for separating sauna associations from fitness/lifestyle context and for avoiding overclaiming causality.

**Potential experiment signals:** fitness confounding, mortality context.

**Protocol takeaway:** Useful for showing exercise/fitness can modify sauna associations, which matters because the Bryan Johnson protocol is post-workout.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Study design:** Long-term prospective cohort examining sauna plus cardiorespiratory fitness.
- **Population:** Middle-aged Finnish men in the KIHD context.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Joint categories of sauna bathing frequency and objectively assessed cardiorespiratory fitness.
- **Comparator/control:** Lower sauna frequency and/or lower cardiorespiratory fitness categories.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Long-term mortality follow-up.
- **Endpoints:** cardiovascular mortality, all-cause mortality, fitness interaction context.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No acute safety or tolerability outcomes for high-temperature daily post-workout sauna.
- **Limitations:** Observational joint-exposure design; fitness and sauna use are both lifestyle variables, so residual confounding and selection effects are plausible.
- **Population mismatch:** Does not assign a sauna dose and does not isolate sauna from cardiorespiratory fitness or training load.
- **Directness to Bryan Johnson Sauna:** same_mechanism; this is not direct evidence for daily 20 minutes at 93 C / 200 F after workouts.
