This source is included for **Long-term Finnish cohorts and observational outcome context**.

**Findings:** In 13,994 participants with 1,805 dementia cases reported, 9-12 sauna sessions/month versus 0-4/month had reported hazard ratios of 0.47 in the first 20 years and 0.81 over the whole follow-up.

**Why it matters:** Independent Finnish cohort context for dementia claims, useful for triangulating or limiting dementia extrapolation from KIHD.

**Potential experiment signals:** dementia context, long-term observational context.

**Protocol takeaway:** Context only; do not promote dementia prevention claims.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Study design:** Finnish Mobile Clinic cohort.
- **Population:** Finnish men and women aged approximately 30-69 years in the Finnish Mobile Clinic follow-up survey.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Self-reported sauna bathing frequency per month.
- **Comparator/control:** Lower sauna frequency, especially 0-4 times/month.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Up to about 39 years of follow-up.
- **Endpoints:** dementia.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No acute safety information for high-temperature daily sauna.
- **Limitations:** Observational; exposure measured as monthly sauna frequency; dementia outcome strongly confounded by age, vascular risk, and health behaviors.
- **Population mismatch:** Monthly sauna frequency and decades-long dementia follow-up do not map to Bryan Johnson protocol endpoints.
- **Directness to Bryan Johnson Sauna:** same_mechanism; this is not direct evidence for daily 20 minutes at 93 C / 200 F after workouts.
- **Artifact and rights:** `open_access`. No copyrighted PDF is stored in Git; this draft only references a manifest candidate.
