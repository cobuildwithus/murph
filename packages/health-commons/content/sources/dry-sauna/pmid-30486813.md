This source is included for **Long-term Finnish cohorts and observational outcome context**.

**Findings:** The study reported 181 fatal cardiovascular events and lower cardiovascular mortality with higher sauna frequency. In fully adjusted models, 4-7 sessions/week versus once weekly had a reported hazard ratio of 0.23 for cardiovascular mortality; average sauna temperature in the cohort was about 75.9 C.

**Why it matters:** Extends mortality/risk-prediction context beyond men-only cohorts and is useful for population-boundary language.

**Potential experiment signals:** cardiovascular mortality context, risk prediction context, temperature mismatch.

**Protocol takeaway:** High-value cohort context, but explicitly note the lower average exposure temperature and nonrandomized design.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Study design:** Prospective cohort of Finnish men and women.
- **Population:** Finnish men and women, mean age about 63 years, from the KIHD cohort extension.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Self-reported sauna bathing frequency, duration, and temperature context.
- **Comparator/control:** Once-weekly sauna bathing.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Median follow-up about 15 years.
- **Endpoints:** cardiovascular mortality, risk prediction.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No daily 93 C post-workout adverse-event surveillance; cohort exposure temperature was generally lower than the Blueprint target.
- **Limitations:** Observational; exposure self-reported; sex-subgroup estimates were less certain; residual confounding remains possible.
- **Population mismatch:** The observed sauna temperature averaged below 93 C and the design was long-term habitual exposure, not a 20-minute daily post-workout self-test.
- **Directness to Bryan Johnson Sauna:** same_mechanism; this is not direct evidence for daily 20 minutes at 93 C / 200 F after workouts.
- **Artifact and rights:** `open_access`. No copyrighted PDF is stored in Git; this draft only references a manifest candidate.
