This source is included for **Measurement artifacts: core temperature, wearables, sweat, and hydration**.

**Findings:** The meta-analysis is a measurement guardrail for ear/aural thermometry in hyperthermic or exercising people; it should be used to caution against treating aural readings as interchangeable with rectal core temperature. Effect/estimate: No pooled numerical estimate extracted in this batch; direction is cautionary rather than supportive of aural substitution.

**Why it matters:** Directly addresses ear/aural temperature caveats when interpreting sauna or heat-exposure body-temperature data.

**Potential experiment signals:** core body temperature, measurement caveats

**Protocol takeaway:** Do not use ear temperature as a primary core-temperature endpoint for Bryan Johnson Sauna.

**Safety/adverse-event notes:** Safety boundary: ear thermometry can be inadequate for detecting clinically important hyperthermia.

**Limitations and population mismatch:** Exercise/hyperthermia literature, not dry sauna; batch did not extract device-specific pooled effects. Population mismatch: Hyperthermic/exercising settings, not controlled sauna self-experiment.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
