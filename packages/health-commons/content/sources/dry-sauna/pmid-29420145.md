This source is included for **Measurement artifacts: core temperature, wearables, sweat, and hydration**.

**Findings:** In 26 recreational athletes cycling for 90 minutes during moderate exercise-heat stress, regional sweat rate and regional sweat sodium correlated with whole-body values, but regression slopes/intercepts often differed from direct equivalence, meaning patch location matters. Effect/estimate: Extracted text reported correlations of regional sweat rate with whole-body sweating rate of r=0.58–0.83 and regional sweat sodium with whole-body sweat sodium of r=0.74–0.88.

**Why it matters:** Supports caveats about where a sweat patch is placed and whether regional values generalize to whole-body fluid/electrolyte loss.

**Potential experiment signals:** fluid loss, measurement caveats

**Protocol takeaway:** If a sauna protocol uses sweat patches, specify body site and avoid equating any one region with whole-body loss.

**Safety/adverse-event notes:** Hydration context: patch placement affects individualized fluid/electrolyte interpretation.

**Limitations and population mismatch:** Exercise-heat cycling, not sauna; equations may not generalize across conditions. Population mismatch: Recreational exercise athletes, not passive sauna users.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
