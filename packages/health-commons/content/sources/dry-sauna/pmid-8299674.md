This source is included for **Safety, heat illness, medications, pregnancy, alcohol, older-adult risk**.

**Findings:** In six healthy young women, sauna exposure changed the timing of midazolam and ephedrine absorption and associated effects, supporting medication/substance caution rather than efficacy claims.

**Why it matters:** Direct sauna source for sedative and stimulant pharmacology boundaries.

**Potential experiment signals:** midazolam absorption, ephedrine absorption, psychomotor effects, visual effects, subjective alertness.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as safety-only evidence that sauna can alter drug absorption/effects for sedative and stimulant-like exposures.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Healthy young women
- **Exposure/intervention:** Single midazolam 15 mg or ephedrine 50 mg doses with sauna exposure: three 10-minute stays at 80-100 C
- **Comparator/control:** Control sessions without sauna and placebo-controlled comparisons
- **Duration/follow-up:** Single acute drug-sauna sessions
- **Endpoints:** midazolam absorption, ephedrine absorption, psychomotor effects, visual effects, subjective alertness
- **Effect/direction:** Sauna retarded midazolam absorption and made ephedrine peak earlier; pharmacodynamic effects differed across early and later time points.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** Supports caution around sedatives and stimulants during high heat, especially because altered timing of effects can change subjective risk.
- **Limitations:** Only six healthy young women; single doses; not powered for safety events.
- **Population mismatch:** Direct sauna exposure but not Bryan dose, not repeated use, and not vulnerable medication users.
- **Directness to Bryan Johnson Sauna:** direct_protocol
