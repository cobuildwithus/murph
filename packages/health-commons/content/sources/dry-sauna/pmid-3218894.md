This source is included for **reviews meta guidelines**.

## Why it matters

Can support protocol monitoring language around heat strain, sweat loss, hydration, and temperature response.

## Findings captured

- Finnish sauna exposure presents a heat load of roughly 300–600 W/m², raises mean skin temperature to about 40–41 °C, and depends on sweating for heat loss; the abstract reports sweating around 0.6–1 kg/h, rectal temperature rising about 0.9 °C after 30 min at 80 °C in adults, and about 1.5 °C after 10 min at 70 °C in children.

## Protocol takeaway

Direct Finnish sauna thermoregulation context defines acute heat strain.

## Important limits

Does not evaluate the Murph 3x/week protocol or long-term health effects.
