This source is included for **Long-term Finnish cohorts and observational outcome context**.

**Findings:** Two 10-minute Finnish sauna bouts increased IL-6 and IL-1 receptor antagonist in acute sampling; one 10-minute sauna bout did not show the same IL-6 increase in the extracted record.

**Why it matters:** Near-duration dry/Finnish sauna crossover source for inflammation and acute tolerability, below the target temperature.

**Potential experiment signals:** inflammation context, acute heat-load context.

**Protocol takeaway:** Useful mechanistic signal that sauna can acutely perturb inflammatory markers, but not efficacy evidence for a wearable endpoint.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Study design:** Acute crossover sauna biomarker study.
- **Population:** Healthy middle-aged and older adults.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Finnish sauna around 80 C, comparing one 10-minute session and two 10-minute sessions with room-temperature control.
- **Comparator/control:** Room-temperature control and shorter sauna exposure.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Acute pre/post session measurement.
- **Endpoints:** IL-6, IL-1 receptor antagonist, circulating inflammatory markers.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** Designed as an acute controlled exposure in healthy adults; no long-term safety inference for daily high-temperature sauna.
- **Limitations:** Acute biomarker study; small-sample details were not available in the extracted batch metadata; temperature was about 80 C, not 93 C; clinical outcomes were not measured.
- **Population mismatch:** Short acute 80 C exposure in healthy adults, not daily 20-minute 93 C post-workout use.
- **Directness to Bryan Johnson Sauna:** same_mechanism; this is not direct evidence for daily 20 minutes at 93 C / 200 F after workouts.
