This source is included for **Near-direct dry-sauna interventions and acute dry-sauna dose evidence**.

**Findings:** `finding:pmid-14648127-passive-heating-rpe-face-cooling` — Sauna-induced hyperthermia increased overall RPE at the end of exercise (14.5 vs 17.2, P<0.001), leg RPE (P<0.01), and prolactin (1598 vs 225 mU/L, P<0.001); in the six-person substudy, face cooling reduced overall RPE after sauna to a level not different from control, while leg RPE and heart rate remained higher.

**Why it matters:** Closest located source for face-cooling plausibility after sauna-induced hyperthermia, especially for comfort and perceived exertion boundaries.

**Potential experiment signals:** perceived-exertion, thermal-comfort, rectal-temperature, heart-rate, prolactin.

**Protocol takeaway:** Face cooling may improve perceived exertion during heat stress, but this is an adjacent comfort modifier rather than protocol efficacy evidence.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
