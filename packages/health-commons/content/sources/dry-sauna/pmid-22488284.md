This source is included for **Core-temperature threshold mechanisms and heat-stress biology**.

**Findings:** Heat exposure increased rectal temperature by about 0.8 °C and heart rate to 131.4 ± 22.4 bpm, decreased systolic and diastolic blood pressure, and increased norepinephrine, prolactin, and extracellular HSP72 in the blood-marker subset; the control condition did not show these changes.; The heat exposure produced a large heart-rate rise and reduced blood pressure, identifying cardiovascular and orthostatic-tolerance signals that should be treated as safety endpoints when extrapolating to hotter or longer sauna protocols.

**Why it matters:** Provides passive dry-heat physiology closer to sauna than exercise-heat sources, including cardiovascular strain and extracellular HSP72 response.

**Potential experiment signals:** core body temperature, heart rate, blood pressure, extracellular HSP72, heat perception.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as adjacent passive-heat context and safety context: short 73 °C heat exposure raised heart rate, lowered blood pressure, and increased HSP72 in a subset, but it is not the Bryan Johnson Sauna dose.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
