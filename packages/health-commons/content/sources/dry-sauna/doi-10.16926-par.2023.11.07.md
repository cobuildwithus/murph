This source is included for **acute mechanistic**.

## Why it matters

Supports boundary language around cold immersion, long sessions, and fluid-shift-prone body-composition readings.

## Findings captured

- The abstract reports increased forehead temperature, decreased diastolic blood pressure, and decreases in body mass and several body-composition estimates after the 72-minute alternating thermal-stress exposure. Because cold immersion and acute fluid shifts are built into the intervention, the body-composition findings should be treated as adjacent, not durable dry-sauna efficacy evidence.

## Protocol takeaway

Young-women hot/cold thermal-stress study is adjacent, not ordinary dry-sauna evidence.

## Important limits

No non-heat control, cold-water exposure is part of the intervention, and acute body-composition estimates should not be treated as durable fat-loss evidence.
