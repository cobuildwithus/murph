This source is included for **Measurement artifacts: core temperature, wearables, sweat, and hydration**.

**Findings:** In one 29-year-old Caucasian male over 24 hours, Apple Watch 3 and Fitbit Charge 2 heart-rate outputs were compared with ambulatory ECG over 102,740 heartbeats; extracted values showed Apple Watch mean difference −1.80 bpm, MAPE 5.86%, agreement 95%, and Fitbit mean difference −3.47 bpm, MAPE 5.96%, agreement 91%. Effect/estimate: Apple Watch 3: −1.80 bpm mean difference, 5.86% MAPE, 95% agreement; Fitbit Charge 2: −3.47 bpm mean difference, 5.96% MAPE, 91% agreement in extracted text.

**Why it matters:** RHR/self-tracking caveat source for daylong wearable HR data outside controlled laboratory settings.

**Potential experiment signals:** resting heart rate, measurement caveats

**Protocol takeaway:** Consumer HR can be acceptable for within-person context in stable conditions, but N-of-1 wearable validation cannot support broad sauna claims.

**Safety/adverse-event notes:** Measurement note: single-person accuracy does not establish broad safety-critical HR validity.

**Limitations and population mismatch:** N-of-1, single demographic profile, older devices, not heat or sauna. Population mismatch: Single young adult male in daily life, not sauna users.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
