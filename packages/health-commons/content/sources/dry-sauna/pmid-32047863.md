This source is included for **Measurement artifacts: core temperature, wearables, sweat, and hydration**.

**Findings:** In a diverse validation sample, optical heart-rate error was driven by device and activity context; the article identified skin type, motion artifacts, and signal crossover as major sources of inaccuracy, found no statistically significant accuracy difference by skin tone in this dataset, and reported activity absolute error about 30% higher than rest. Effect/estimate: Extracted article text reported average error differences by device/activity; activity error was about 30% higher than rest, with consumer-device MAE around 7.2 ± 5.4 bpm at rest and 10.2 ± 7.5 bpm during activity.

**Why it matters:** High-quality source for optical HR caveats such as individual/device factors that could matter when sweating or heat changes skin perfusion.

**Potential experiment signals:** resting heart rate, measurement caveats

**Protocol takeaway:** Prefer resting, stable-condition HR readings and device consistency; treat moving/sweaty wrist HR as noisier context.

**Safety/adverse-event notes:** Measurement note: wrist PPG during post-sauna movement or high sweating may be less reliable than resting measurements.

**Limitations and population mismatch:** Wearable validation outside sauna; 56 recruited with 53 analyzed; sweat/heat-specific sauna conditions were not the exposure. Population mismatch: General wearable-validation adults, not sauna users.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
