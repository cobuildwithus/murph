This source is included for **Modality-boundary and adjacent passive-heat context**.

**Findings:** Hot-water immersion produced the largest reported core-temperature and cardiac-output increases and was the only modality reported to elicit measurable IL-6 and immune-cell changes; traditional sauna produced smaller heat strain and far-infrared sauna the least. This is a modality-boundary finding, not direct proof for the Bryan Johnson dry-sauna routine.

**Why it matters:** Directly compares passive-heat modalities and shows that hot-water, traditional dry sauna, and far-infrared exposures should not be collapsed into one interchangeable heat dose.

**Potential experiment signals:** core/ear temperature measurement context, resting heart rate, morning blood pressure, inflammatory response context, tolerability symptoms.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use to label hot-water and infrared findings as adjacent variants when interpreting Bryan Johnson’s high-temperature dry-sauna protocol.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
