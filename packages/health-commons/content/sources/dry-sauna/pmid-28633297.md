This source is included for **Long-term Finnish cohorts and observational outcome context**.

**Findings:** Compared with once-weekly sauna bathing, adjusted hazard ratios were reported as 0.83 for 2-3 sessions/week and 0.53 for 4-7 sessions/week after further adjustment, suggesting lower hypertension incidence with higher frequency.

**Why it matters:** Core prospective hypertension incidence paper for BP-risk context, while remaining observational and long-term.

**Potential experiment signals:** blood pressure context, cardiovascular-risk context.

**Protocol takeaway:** Supports blood-pressure risk context, but cannot prove a short Murph test will lower blood pressure or resting heart rate.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Study design:** KIHD prospective cohort without baseline hypertension.
- **Population:** Middle-aged Eastern Finnish men without baseline hypertension in the KIHD cohort.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Self-reported weekly sauna bathing frequency.
- **Comparator/control:** Once-weekly sauna bathing.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Long-term follow-up for incident physician-diagnosed hypertension.
- **Endpoints:** incident hypertension.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No protocol-specific adverse-event surveillance for daily high-heat post-workout sauna.
- **Limitations:** Observational men-only cohort; residual confounding by fitness, diet, alcohol, socioeconomic status, and health behaviors remains possible.
- **Population mismatch:** Habitual Finnish sauna frequency is adjacent to, not the same as, a daily 20-minute 93 C post-workout routine.
- **Directness to Bryan Johnson Sauna:** same_mechanism; this is not direct evidence for daily 20 minutes at 93 C / 200 F after workouts.
