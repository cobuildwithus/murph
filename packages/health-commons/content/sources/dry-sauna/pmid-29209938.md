This source is included for **Long-term Finnish cohorts and observational outcome context**.

**Findings:** Sauna bathing frequency was inversely associated with serum C-reactive protein in the extracted cohort record.

**Why it matters:** Mechanistic epidemiology bridge for inflammation hypotheses; should not be used as direct protocol efficacy evidence.

**Potential experiment signals:** inflammation context.

**Protocol takeaway:** Mechanistic context only; useful for background, not endpoint claims.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Study design:** KIHD cohort analysis of systemic inflammation.
- **Population:** Middle-aged Finnish men without acute or chronic inflammation at baseline in the extracted record.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Sauna bathing frequency.
- **Comparator/control:** Lower sauna frequency categories.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Baseline observational inflammation analysis in cohort context.
- **Endpoints:** C-reactive protein, systemic inflammation.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No protocol-level adverse-event data.
- **Limitations:** Observational biomarker association; cannot establish that sauna lowers inflammation; men-only population.
- **Population mismatch:** Inflammatory biomarker context, not direct RHR/HRV efficacy evidence for a daily 93 C routine.
- **Directness to Bryan Johnson Sauna:** same_mechanism; this is not direct evidence for daily 20 minutes at 93 C / 200 F after workouts.
