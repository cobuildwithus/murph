This source is included for **Safety, heat illness, medications, pregnancy, alcohol, older-adult risk**.

**Findings:** The 2024 Wilderness Medical Society guideline updates heat-illness classification, prevention, recognition, hydration, and field/hospital management recommendations; it is safety guidance for heat exposure, not efficacy evidence for a dry-sauna protocol.

**Why it matters:** Current heat-illness guideline useful for stop conditions, hydration/cooling boundaries, medication-risk screening, and distinguishing physiologic heat load from heat illness.

**Potential experiment signals:** heat illness recognition, heat stroke, heat exhaustion, hydration, cooling, medication-related heat risk.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as safety boundary evidence for heat illness; do not cite it as proof that the Bryan Johnson Sauna improves health outcomes.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** People at risk for heat illness in wilderness, occupational, athletic, and environmental heat settings
- **Exposure/intervention:** Environmental or exertional heat exposure; heat-illness prevention, recognition, and treatment
- **Comparator/control:** Not a comparative intervention trial; guideline recommendations compare prevention and treatment modalities where evidence permits
- **Duration/follow-up:** Guideline update based on evidence available through the update process; not a participant follow-up study
- **Endpoints:** heat illness recognition, heat stroke, heat exhaustion, hydration, cooling, medication-related heat risk
- **Effect/direction:** Guideline source; emphasizes heat-illness classification, risk screening, hydration, rapid cooling for severe heat illness, and graded recommendations rather than Bryan-protocol efficacy.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** Defines safety boundaries for hyperthermia, heat exhaustion, heat stroke, dehydration, altered mental status, and delayed cooling.
- **Limitations:** Not dry-sauna-specific and not an efficacy test of a daily 93 C post-workout protocol.
- **Population mismatch:** General wilderness/field/hospital heat-illness populations rather than healthy adults following Bryan Johnson Sauna.
