This source is included for **Long-term Finnish cohorts and observational outcome context**.

**Findings:** Among 2,575 men with 1,618 deaths, high sauna frequency was associated with lower all-cause mortality versus low frequency (reported HR 0.86). The high-hsCRP/low-sauna group had higher mortality risk, while high-hsCRP/high-sauna was not clearly elevated in the extracted abstract record.

**Why it matters:** Later cohort analysis useful for interaction/mediation framing around inflammation and mortality.

**Potential experiment signals:** inflammation context, mortality context.

**Protocol takeaway:** Supports inflammatory-risk context but must be phrased as observational effect modification, not causal protection.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Study design:** KIHD prospective cohort with inflammation interaction.
- **Population:** Middle-aged and older Finnish men aged 42-61 years at baseline.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Joint categories of high-sensitivity C-reactive protein and Finnish sauna bathing frequency.
- **Comparator/control:** Lower sauna frequency and/or lower hsCRP categories.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Median follow-up about 27.8 years for all-cause mortality.
- **Endpoints:** all-cause mortality, hsCRP interaction.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No acute adverse-event assessment for high-temperature daily sauna.
- **Limitations:** Observational interaction analysis; men-only; hsCRP and sauna frequency may reflect broader health status and behaviors.
- **Population mismatch:** Long-term Finnish male cohort and inflammation stratification, not direct Bryan Johnson dose evidence.
- **Directness to Bryan Johnson Sauna:** same_mechanism; this is not direct evidence for daily 20 minutes at 93 C / 200 F after workouts.
