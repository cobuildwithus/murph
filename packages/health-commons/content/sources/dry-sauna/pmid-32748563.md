This source is included for **Measurement artifacts: core temperature, wearables, sweat, and hydration**.

**Findings:** In 49 participants cycling for 90 minutes, published equations improved estimation of whole-body sweat sodium from regional patch measures compared with raw uncorrected regional values, but prediction still required site-specific equations and had limits of agreement. Effect/estimate: Extracted abstract text reports raw regional mean biases from −4 to 23 mmol/L with wide limits of agreement; predicted whole-body estimates reduced mean bias to roughly −2 to 5 mmol/L with limits of agreement around ±12 to ±17 mmol/L depending on site.

**Why it matters:** Important caveat that regional sweat sodium patches require correction/translation before being used as whole-body electrolyte-loss estimates.

**Potential experiment signals:** fluid loss, measurement caveats

**Protocol takeaway:** Use equations or validated local methods for whole-body sodium estimates; do not overread one regional patch.

**Safety/adverse-event notes:** Hydration context: regional patch values should be converted/validated before electrolyte replacement decisions.

**Limitations and population mismatch:** Exercise-cycling heat conditions; validity outside similar environmental ranges remains uncertain. Population mismatch: Cycling exercise heat stress, not dry sauna.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
