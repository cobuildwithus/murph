This source is included for **Measurement artifacts: core temperature, wearables, sweat, and hydration**.

**Findings:** The review emphasizes that accurate internal core-temperature monitoring generally requires invasive or inconvenient methods; oral measurements may be inaccurate, esophageal measures are difficult and uncomfortable, and rectal temperature remains a practical accuracy reference for sports heat illness despite slower response and field inconvenience. Effect/estimate: No pooled effect estimate; this is a methods review defining measurement tradeoffs.

**Why it matters:** Older foundational methods review useful for defining what is and is not a core-temperature endpoint in sauna self-experiments.

**Potential experiment signals:** core body temperature, measurement caveats

**Protocol takeaway:** Anchor core-temperature endpoint definitions around validated internal measures; do not treat oral/skin estimates as interchangeable with core temperature.

**Safety/adverse-event notes:** Safety boundary: inaccurate temperature-site choice can delay recognition of dangerous hyperthermia.

**Limitations and population mismatch:** Older narrative review; not a sauna intervention and not a consumer-wearable validation study. Population mismatch: Sports and heat-illness measurement context, not direct dry-sauna protocol evidence.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
