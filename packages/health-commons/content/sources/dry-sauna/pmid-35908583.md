This source is included for **Long-term Finnish cohorts and observational outcome context**.

**Findings:** Extracted records indicate frequent sauna bathing may offset the increased mortality risk associated with low socioeconomic status; one low-SES/high-sauna joint estimate was reported as HR 1.07 with confidence interval crossing 1.0.

**Why it matters:** Important confounding and effect-modification context for lifestyle and socioeconomic boundaries.

**Potential experiment signals:** socioeconomic confounding, mortality context.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use to illustrate confounding and effect modification, not causal benefit.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Study design:** Cohort analysis of socioeconomic status, sauna, and mortality.
- **Population:** Middle-aged and older Finnish men.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Joint categories of socioeconomic status and sauna bathing frequency.
- **Comparator/control:** Higher socioeconomic status and/or lower sauna-frequency categories.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Long-term all-cause mortality follow-up.
- **Endpoints:** all-cause mortality, socioeconomic-status interaction.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No acute safety data.
- **Limitations:** Observational effect-modification study; socioeconomic status, lifestyle, and health behaviors remain major confounders.
- **Population mismatch:** Social-risk mortality context is not a direct Bryan Johnson protocol endpoint.
- **Directness to Bryan Johnson Sauna:** same_mechanism; this is not direct evidence for daily 20 minutes at 93 C / 200 F after workouts.
