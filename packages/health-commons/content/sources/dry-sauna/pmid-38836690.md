This source is included for **Long-term Finnish cohorts and observational outcome context**.

**Findings:** Extracted records indicate each additional sauna session per week was associated with a baseline cardiorespiratory fitness difference of about +0.30 mL/kg/min, and the authors called for randomized trials to confirm whether sauna can improve cardiorespiratory fitness.

**Why it matters:** Recent KIHD cardiorespiratory-fitness context; useful for separating sauna from fitness and broader lifestyle effects.

**Potential experiment signals:** cardiorespiratory fitness context.

**Protocol takeaway:** Recent CRF context only; do not claim sauna improves fitness without RCT evidence.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Study design:** KIHD prospective cardiorespiratory fitness analysis.
- **Population:** KIHD prospective study participants.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Sauna bathing frequency in relation to cardiorespiratory fitness.
- **Comparator/control:** Lower sauna frequency / per-session frequency contrast.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Baseline and longitudinal cardiorespiratory fitness context; exact follow-up structure depends on source extraction.
- **Endpoints:** cardiorespiratory fitness, VO2 context.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No acute high-temperature daily sauna adverse-event data.
- **Limitations:** Observational association; cardiorespiratory fitness and sauna habits can be confounded by physical activity and health status; article was paywalled in the ledger rights status.
- **Population mismatch:** Does not directly test post-workout daily 93 C sauna or RHR/HRV endpoints.
- **Directness to Bryan Johnson Sauna:** same_mechanism; this is not direct evidence for daily 20 minutes at 93 C / 200 F after workouts.
- **Artifact and rights:** `paywalled`. No copyrighted PDF is stored in Git; this draft only references a manifest candidate.
