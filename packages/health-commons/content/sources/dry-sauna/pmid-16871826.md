This source is included for **safety contraindications**.

## Why it matters

Useful for safety framing around stable chronic disease and medication-related orthostatic hypotension, not as standalone efficacy proof.

## Findings captured

- The review describes common Finnish sauna experimental exposure as 80–90°C for 5–20 min, often repeated 1–3 times, but notes many studies were uncontrolled and involved people accustomed to sauna from childhood.
- The review concludes sauna was generally well tolerated in healthy people and not especially risky for stable hypertension, coronary heart disease, or heart failure, while warning that antihypertensive medication can predispose to orthostatic hypotension after bathing.
- The authors note the evidence base was often uncontrolled and called for stronger designs and inclusion of sauna-unaccustomed participants.

## Protocol takeaway

Narrative review supports general Finnish-sauna tolerability but emphasizes weak study designs.

## Important limits

Many underlying studies were uncontrolled and sauna-accustomed; do not generalize to unstable cardiovascular disease or beginners without caution.
