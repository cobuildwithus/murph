This source is included for **Safety, heat illness, medications, pregnancy, alcohol, older-adult risk**.

**Findings:** This systematic review reports age-related thermoregulatory vulnerabilities in older adults exposed to heat, including reduced sweating/vasodilation and greater dehydration or fatigue risk.

**Why it matters:** Systematic review for older-adult heat vulnerability boundaries.

**Potential experiment signals:** sweating, cutaneous vasodilation, cardiovascular adjustment, hydration status, thermal perception, heat-related outcomes.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use for age-related heat-risk cautions; do not use as direct sauna efficacy evidence.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Older adults exposed to environmental heat in experimental and epidemiological studies
- **Exposure/intervention:** Heat exposure and age-related thermoregulatory vulnerability
- **Comparator/control:** Younger adults or lower-risk groups across included studies where available
- **Duration/follow-up:** Systematic review of 24 original studies
- **Endpoints:** sweating, cutaneous vasodilation, cardiovascular adjustment, hydration status, thermal perception, heat-related outcomes
- **Effect/direction:** Review reports older adults show reduced sweating and cutaneous vasodilation, attenuated cardiovascular/autonomic adjustments, impaired hydration status, altered thermal perception, greater heat storage, faster core-temperature rise, and higher dehydration/fatigue risk compared with younger adults.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** Older-adult heat vulnerability, dehydration, fatigue, and impaired heat tolerance.
- **Limitations:** General environmental heat evidence, not dry-sauna-specific; publication year 2025 source metadata should be rechecked in final repo updates.
- **Population mismatch:** Older-adult environmental heat context rather than healthy adults following Bryan Johnson Sauna.
