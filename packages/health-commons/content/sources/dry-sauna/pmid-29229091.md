This source is included for **Long-term Finnish cohorts and observational outcome context**.

**Findings:** Frequent sauna bathing was associated with lower pneumonia risk. Fully adjusted hazard ratios versus no more than once weekly were reported as 0.69 for 2-3 sessions/week and 0.56 for at least 4 sessions/week.

**Why it matters:** Adjacent respiratory endpoint source; supports broad context inventory but should not drive protocol claims.

**Potential experiment signals:** respiratory context, safety boundary.

**Protocol takeaway:** Respiratory context only; do not imply acute respiratory protection or use during illness.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Study design:** KIHD prospective cohort pneumonia analysis.
- **Population:** Middle-aged Caucasian Finnish men.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Sauna bathing frequency.
- **Comparator/control:** No more than once-weekly sauna bathing.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Long-term follow-up for pneumonia.
- **Endpoints:** pneumonia.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** Pneumonia risk association does not mean sauna is safe during acute respiratory infection; acute illness remains a stop/avoid condition.
- **Limitations:** Observational men-only cohort; pneumonia diagnosis and health behaviors may confound associations.
- **Population mismatch:** Pneumonia incidence is not a direct Murph endpoint or Bryan Johnson protocol outcome.
- **Directness to Bryan Johnson Sauna:** same_mechanism; this is not direct evidence for daily 20 minutes at 93 C / 200 F after workouts.
- **Artifact and rights:** `permission_required`. No copyrighted PDF is stored in Git; this draft only references a manifest candidate.
