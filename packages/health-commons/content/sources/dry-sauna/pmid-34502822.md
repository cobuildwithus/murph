This source is included for **Measurement artifacts: core temperature, wearables, sweat, and hydration**.

**Findings:** In 24 male athletes, the CORE sensor was reported as acceptably reliable, but the extracted review material does not support treating it as a valid measure of core temperature; sweat, skin temperature, and hairy skin were noted as possible confounders. Effect/estimate: Direction only: reliability was acceptable, but validity versus core-temperature reference was not supported in extracted appraisal.

**Why it matters:** Direct CORE-sensor reliability/validity paper useful for caveating non-ingestible core-temperature wearables.

**Potential experiment signals:** core body temperature, measurement caveats

**Protocol takeaway:** Treat CORE as an experimental estimate, not a validated core-temperature endpoint for this protocol.

**Safety/adverse-event notes:** Measurement-safety boundary: a wearable CORE estimate may under- or over-state true core temperature during heat exposure.

**Limitations and population mismatch:** Male cycling cohort; no female cohort and not passive sauna. Population mismatch: Male athletes cycling, not Bryan Johnson sauna users.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
