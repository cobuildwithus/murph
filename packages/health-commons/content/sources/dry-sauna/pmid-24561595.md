This source is included for **Measurement artifacts: core temperature, wearables, sweat, and hydration**.

**Findings:** In 12 physically active males, intestinal temperature from an ingestible telemetry pill showed excellent inter-day reliability during exercise in a hot environment, with near-absent test-retest error reported as 0.01 °C and 95% limits of agreement of about ±0.23 °C. Effect/estimate: Test-retest error 0.01 °C with confidence interval −0.02 to 0.05 °C; 95% limits of agreement approximately ±0.23 °C in the available extraction.

**Why it matters:** Reliability source for repeated-measures self-experiment interpretation of intestinal/core temperature during heat exposure.

**Potential experiment signals:** core body temperature, measurement caveats

**Protocol takeaway:** Telemetry pills can provide repeatable intestinal temperature under controlled conditions, but sauna interpretation still requires timing/fluid logs.

**Safety/adverse-event notes:** Measurement caveat: reliability under controlled exercise heat does not eliminate pill timing or fluid-ingestion confounds.

**Limitations and population mismatch:** Small male-only exercise-heat study; not sauna and not a validity study versus every core-temperature reference. Population mismatch: Physically active males exercising in heat; passive-sauna users and female users not directly represented.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
