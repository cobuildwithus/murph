This source is included for **Measurement artifacts: core temperature, wearables, sweat, and hydration**.

**Findings:** In 10 firefighters, cool-water ingestion and pill timing could perturb gastrointestinal pill temperature; a pill ingested immediately before physical activity was not reliable for measuring core temperature in all individuals. Effect/estimate: Direction only in extracted batch: immediate pre-activity pill ingestion can yield inaccurate GI temperature after cool-water ingestion.

**Why it matters:** Direct caveat for sauna protocols that combine temperature pills with fluid intake before, during, or after heat exposure.

**Potential experiment signals:** core body temperature, measurement caveats, fluid loss

**Protocol takeaway:** When GI pills are used around sauna, log ingestion timing and fluid intake; do not interpret post-water pill readings as clean core-temperature data.

**Safety/adverse-event notes:** Measurement-safety boundary: hydration during or near heat exposure can confound ingestible-pill temperature readings.

**Limitations and population mismatch:** Small operational/exercise sample and firefighter context; water temperature and activity conditions differ from Bryan Johnson dry sauna. Population mismatch: Firefighters and physical activity context rather than passive dry-sauna users.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
