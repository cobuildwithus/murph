This source is included for **Measurement artifacts: core temperature, wearables, sweat, and hydration**.

**Findings:** In 19 elite female field hockey players over four hot-condition sessions, CORE showed poor agreement with BodyCap pill temperature, underestimated core temperature by about −0.06 °C to −0.34 °C, and disagreed by more than ±0.3 °C for 41–60% of data samples in each session. Effect/estimate: Extracted abstract values: mean bias −0.06 °C to −0.34 °C; 95% confidence intervals about ±0.35 °C to ±0.56 °C; >±0.3 °C disagreement for 41–60% of samples.

**Why it matters:** Manufacturer-independent wearable-temperature validation source with a negative caveat for using CORE as a pill substitute in heat conditions.

**Potential experiment signals:** core body temperature, measurement caveats

**Protocol takeaway:** Do not treat CORE as a valid alternative to ingestible-pill core temperature in hot sauna-like conditions without local validation.

**Safety/adverse-event notes:** Safety boundary: CORE may increasingly underestimate as core temperature rises, which can be risky near heat thresholds.

**Limitations and population mismatch:** Elite women in team-sport heat training, not passive sauna; comparator is a pill, not rectal or esophageal temperature. Population mismatch: Elite female field hockey athletes, not Bryan Johnson sauna users.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
