This source is included for **Long-term Finnish cohorts and observational outcome context**.

**Findings:** The study reported 744 cardiovascular deaths. High systolic blood pressure with low sauna frequency had a higher cardiovascular mortality association than the normal-SBP/high-sauna reference, and the authors concluded frequent sauna may offset some risk for high-normal but not clearly elevated SBP categories.

**Why it matters:** Directly relevant to BP-risk context and expectation setting around long-term cardiovascular mortality, not acute BP self-testing.

**Potential experiment signals:** blood pressure context, cardiovascular mortality context.

**Protocol takeaway:** Important BP nuance: sauna context does not remove the need to screen uncontrolled blood pressure.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Study design:** KIHD prospective cohort with systolic blood pressure interaction.
- **Population:** Middle-aged and older Finnish men aged 42-61 years at baseline.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Joint categories of systolic blood pressure and Finnish sauna bathing frequency.
- **Comparator/control:** Normal systolic blood pressure with high sauna frequency and other joint categories.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Median follow-up about 27.8 years.
- **Endpoints:** cardiovascular mortality, systolic blood pressure interaction.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** Not an acute safety study; participants with high blood pressure are a key population for clinician guidance in a high-heat routine.
- **Limitations:** Observational interaction analysis; men-only; SBP categories and sauna habits are not randomized.
- **Population mismatch:** Does not test a daily 93 C protocol, and the findings are about long-term mortality interactions rather than short-term blood pressure response.
- **Directness to Bryan Johnson Sauna:** same_mechanism; this is not direct evidence for daily 20 minutes at 93 C / 200 F after workouts.
