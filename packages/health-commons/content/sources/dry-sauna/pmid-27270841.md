This source is included for **modality disambiguation**.

## Why it matters

Supports blood-pressure and vascular-mechanism plausibility for passive heating while preserving modality boundaries.

## Findings captured

- The heat-therapy group improved flow-mediated dilation from 5.6 ± 0.3% to 10.9 ± 1.0%, increased femoral dynamic arterial compliance from 0.06 ± 0.01 to 0.09 ± 0.01 mm²/mmHg, reduced aortic pulse-wave velocity from 7.1 ± 0.3 to 6.1 ± 0.3 m/s, reduced carotid intima-media thickness from 0.43 ± 0.01 to 0.37 ± 0.01 mm, and reduced mean arterial pressure from 83 ± 1 to 78 ± 2 mmHg; the sham group did not show those changes.

## Protocol takeaway

Hot-water passive heat therapy improved vascular function and mean arterial pressure, but it was not sauna.

## Important limits

Hot-water immersion, higher frequency, and longer thermal dose differ from Finnish dry sauna.
