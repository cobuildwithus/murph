This source is included for **Measurement artifacts: core temperature, wearables, sweat, and hydration**.

**Findings:** Indoor exercise-heat validation recommended rectal or intestinal temperature to assess hyperthermia and found that many alternative instruments were invalid substitutes in hot indoor exercise. Effect/estimate: Direction only: rectal/intestinal measures were favored; many non-core instruments did not validly assess hyperthermia.

**Why it matters:** Indoor heat-validation source closer to sauna-like controlled environments than outdoor field-only studies.

**Potential experiment signals:** core body temperature, measurement caveats

**Protocol takeaway:** For sauna experiments, separate validated core-temperature measures from peripheral or consumer estimates.

**Safety/adverse-event notes:** Safety boundary: invalid indoor heat thermometry can miss heat strain.

**Limitations and population mismatch:** Exercise in heat rather than sauna; device-level estimates were not extracted here. Population mismatch: Exercise-heat participants, not Bryan Johnson sauna users.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
