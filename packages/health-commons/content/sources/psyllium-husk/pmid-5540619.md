This source is included for **Mechanism: viscosity, bile-acid, sterol, and fecal-excretion context**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Animal bile-acid turnover study in normal and hypophysectomized rats (1971).
- **Population/model:** Normal and hypophysectomized rats.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Diet supplemented with 4% Metamucil/psyllium hydrocolloid.
- **Comparator/control:** Unsupplemented control diet.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Not extracted from accessible abstract/metadata.
- **Role in Murph:** Context-only mechanism evidence for the Psyllium Husk For Cholesterol protocol.

## Findings

- **Bile-acid half-life, pool size, and excretion.:** Metamucil reduced cholic and chenodeoxycholic acid half-lives in normal rats and reduced cholic acid half-life by about 40% in hypophysectomized rats, with pool-size changes interpreted as increased bile-acid excretion.

## Why it matters

Adds historical bile-acid turnover context but should be treated cautiously because it is old animal-only evidence.

## Potential experiment signals

- cholic acid half-life

- bile acid excretion

## Protocol takeaway

Use as historical mechanism context for bile-acid turnover; do not use as human LDL efficacy evidence.

## Safety and adverse-event notes

No human adverse-event or tolerability data were extracted from this animal/comparative mechanism source. Safety claims for a human psyllium protocol should come from human clinical trials, guidelines, labels, or safety-specific sources.

## Important limitations

Older animal study; no direct human cholesterol outcomes; accessible extraction did not expose full sample-size or adverse-event details. The source is **not** direct evidence that the target human protocol lowers LDL cholesterol.
