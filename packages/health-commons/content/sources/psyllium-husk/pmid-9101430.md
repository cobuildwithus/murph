This source is included for **Mechanism: viscosity, bile-acid, sterol, and fecal-excretion context**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Animal additive-effect dietary intervention study in hamsters (1997).
- **Population/model:** Hamsters fed basal chow with palm oil and cholesterol.
- **Intervention/exposure:** 5% psyllium, 0.5% cholestyramine, or the 5% psyllium plus 0.5% cholestyramine combination.
- **Comparator/control:** Basal diet with cellulose-matched control.
- **Duration/follow-up:** 21 days
- **Role in Murph:** Context-only mechanism evidence for the Psyllium Husk For Cholesterol protocol.

## Findings

- **Lipoprotein/hepatic cholesterol, fecal bile acid excretion, neutral sterol excretion.:** Psyllium and cholestyramine individually had hypocholesterolemic effects and together produced additive reductions in lipoprotein and hepatic cholesterol; total bile acid excretion increased 26% with psyllium, 57% with cholestyramine, and 79% with the combination, while fecal neutral sterol excretion was not affected.
- **Fecal bile acid profile.:** Psyllium affected only unconjugated bile-acid excretion, while cholestyramine increased conjugated and primary bile acids; the authors concluded that psyllium does not bind bile acids in vivo despite both agents increasing bile-acid excretion.

## Why it matters

Clarifies that psyllium and cholestyramine may both increase bile-acid excretion but with different fecal bile-acid profiles, limiting simple “bile acid binding” claims.

## Protocol takeaway

Use as adjacent mechanism/co-therapy context and to avoid conflating psyllium with cholestyramine.

## Safety and adverse-event notes

No human adverse-event or tolerability data were extracted from this animal/comparative mechanism source. Safety claims for a human psyllium protocol should come from human clinical trials, guidelines, labels, or safety-specific sources.

## Important limitations

Hamster model with cholestyramine combination; not direct human psyllium protocol evidence. The source is **not** direct evidence that the target human protocol lowers LDL cholesterol.
