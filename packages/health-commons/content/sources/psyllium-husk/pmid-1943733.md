This source is included for **Mechanism: viscosity, bile-acid, sterol, and fecal-excretion context**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Animal mechanistic dietary intervention study in hamsters (1991).
- **Population/model:** Hamsters in dietary cholesterol and saturated-triacylglycerol challenge models.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Dietary psyllium mucilloid.
- **Comparator/control:** Control, cholestyramine, and surfomer comparators.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Not extracted from accessible abstract/metadata.
- **Role in Murph:** Context-only mechanism evidence for the Psyllium Husk For Cholesterol protocol.

## Findings

- **Plasma total cholesterol, LDL cholesterol, hepatic synthesis, and intestinal bile acid pool.:** Psyllium, cholestyramine, and surfomer all lowered plasma total and LDL cholesterol in hamsters; psyllium increased hepatic cholesterol synthesis and increased the small-intestinal bile acid pool from 17.9 µmol in controls to 23.0 µmol, without in vitro bile-acid binding.

## Why it matters

Helps frame older mechanism hypotheses and cautions against assuming psyllium works by the same direct binding mechanism as cholestyramine.
