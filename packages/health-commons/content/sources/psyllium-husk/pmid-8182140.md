This source is included for **Mechanism: viscosity, bile-acid, sterol, and fecal-excretion context**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Animal hepatic enzyme and LDL receptor expression study in hamsters (1994).
- **Population/model:** Hamsters fed cholesterol-free or cholesterol/triglyceride-enriched semisynthetic diets.
- **Intervention/exposure:** 7.5% dietary psyllium substituted for cellulose.
- **Comparator/control:** 7.5% cellulose and 1% cholestyramine comparator diets.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Not extracted from accessible abstract/metadata.
- **Role in Murph:** Context-only mechanism evidence for the Psyllium Husk For Cholesterol protocol.

## Findings

- **Hepatic cholesterol 7 alpha-hydroxylase activity and mRNA.:** In hamsters, replacing cellulose with 7.5% psyllium increased hepatic cholesterol 7 alpha-hydroxylase activity and mRNA levels by about 3- to 4-fold, with comparable effects reported for 1% cholestyramine.
- **Hepatic LDL receptor expression and hepatic cholesterol content.:** Psyllium did not increase hepatic LDL receptor expression on a cholesterol-free very-low-fat diet, but increased or restored LDL receptor expression that had been downregulated by dietary cholesterol and triglyceride; hepatic cholesterol content declined despite increased cholesterol synthesis.

## Why it matters

High-value mechanistic support for a bile-acid synthesis explanation and for considering hepatic LDL receptor context.
