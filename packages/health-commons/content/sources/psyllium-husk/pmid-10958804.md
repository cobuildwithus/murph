This source is included for **Mechanism: viscosity, bile-acid, sterol, and fecal-excretion context**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Animal dose-response mechanistic dietary intervention study in rats (2000).
- **Population/model:** Male Wistar rats, 4 groups of 10.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Semipurified diets containing 10% dietary fiber with psyllium at 0%, 3.33%, 6.67%, or 10%; cellulose supplied the remaining fiber.
- **Comparator/control:** 0% psyllium cellulose-fiber diet.
- **Duration/follow-up:** 21 days
- **Role in Murph:** Context-only mechanism evidence for the Psyllium Husk For Cholesterol protocol.

## Findings

- **Liver cholesterol and cholesterol 7 alpha-hydroxylase activity.:** Total liver cholesterol decreased across psyllium dose groups (138, 105, 105, and 93 µmol for 0%, 3.33%, 6.67%, and 10% psyllium; SEM 4.2), while cholesterol 7 alpha-hydroxylase activity increased (6.36, 16.92, 15.28, and 20.37 pmol/min/mg protein; SEM 3.19).
- **Ileal apical sodium-dependent bile acid transporter mRNA, fecal bile acids, fecal total steroids, intestinal bile acids.:** Psyllium was reported to increase ileal apical sodium-dependent bile acid transporter mRNA and produce greater fecal bile acid and total steroid excretion and intestinal bile acid content, supporting a coordinated bile-acid metabolism response in rats.

## Why it matters

Provides mechanistic detail for why fecal bile acid/steroid loss might connect to hepatic cholesterol metabolism and bile-acid transporter adaptation.

Use as animal mechanistic rationale for bile-acid metabolism measures only; human LDL claims require human trials.

## Safety and adverse-event notes

No human adverse-event or tolerability data were extracted from this animal/comparative mechanism source. Safety claims for a human psyllium protocol should come from human clinical trials, guidelines, labels, or safety-specific sources.

## Important limitations

Rat model with high dietary psyllium percentages and semipurified diets; not a human supplement trial. The source is **not** direct evidence that the target human protocol lowers LDL cholesterol.
