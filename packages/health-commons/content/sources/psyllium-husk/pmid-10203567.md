This source is included for **Mechanism: viscosity, bile-acid, sterol, and fecal-excretion context**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Animal mechanistic dietary intervention study in hamsters (1999).
- **Population/model:** Male Golden Syrian hamsters fed high-fat, high-cholesterol lithogenic diets.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Lithogenic diet with 4% or 6% psyllium; comparators included no psyllium and 1% cholestyramine.
- **Comparator/control:** Lithogenic diet control and cholestyramine comparator.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Not extracted from accessible abstract/metadata.
- **Role in Murph:** Context-only mechanism evidence for the Psyllium Husk For Cholesterol protocol.

## Findings

- **Fecal bile acid and neutral sterol excretion.:** Six-percent psyllium increased daily fecal bile acid excretion by about 400% versus control, while neutral sterol excretion was not changed by psyllium; cholestyramine produced a larger bile-acid increase and increased neutral sterols.
- **Plasma lipids, bile acid pool composition, lithogenic index, cholesterol gallstones, hepatic esterified cholesterol.:** Psyllium lowered plasma total cholesterol and triacylglycerol similarly to cholestyramine and normalized lithogenic index/prevented cholesterol gallstones, but unlike cholestyramine did not prevent hepatic esterified cholesterol storage.

## Why it matters

Supports a bile-acid/sterol-loss mechanism seam and identifies fecal bile acid excretion and bile composition as mechanistic signals to separate from human LDL outcome claims.

## Potential experiment signals

- plasma total cholesterol
- triacylglycerol
- hepatic cholesterol storage
- fecal bile acid excretion
- neutral sterol excretion
- bile acid pool composition
- lithogenic index
- cholesterol gallstones

## Protocol takeaway

Use as animal mechanism context for bile-acid excretion and bile-pool changes; do not use as standalone evidence that a human psyllium protocol lowers LDL.

## Safety and adverse-event notes

No human adverse-event or tolerability data were extracted from this animal/comparative mechanism source. Safety claims for a human psyllium protocol should come from human clinical trials, guidelines, labels, or safety-specific sources.

## Important limitations

Hamster lithogenic high-fat/high-cholesterol diet model; duration and sample size were not extracted from accessible abstract/metadata; gallstone endpoints are not direct human LDL outcomes. The source is **not** direct evidence that the target human protocol lowers LDL cholesterol.
