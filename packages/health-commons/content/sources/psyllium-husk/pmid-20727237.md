This source is included for **Direct protocol adult lipid trial evidence**.

## Source extraction notes

**Findings:** The psyllium supplement arm improved total cholesterol, LDL-C, body weight, BMI, and body-fat percentage versus control; healthy-diet arms also improved lipid and metabolic measures. Because diet and weight changed alongside fiber exposure, this source is best used to flag confounding rather than to define the direct cholesterol effect of psyllium alone.

**Why it matters:** This trial is useful for population subgroup and confounding context because diet and weight changes can co-move with lipid outcomes.

**Potential experiment signals:** LDL-C; total cholesterol; triglycerides; insulin; body weight; BMI; body fat percentage.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context that psyllium may influence lipids in overweight/obese adults, but do not make it a primary cholesterol efficacy claim for hypercholesterolemia.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Evidence boundary

- **Directness:** `direct_protocol`.
- **Population mismatch:** High-to-moderate mismatch: overweight/obese population and multi-arm lifestyle comparison, not a direct hypercholesterolemia treatment trial.
- **Limitations:** Small four-arm study with 18 participants per group. Diet and body-weight changes confound lipid interpretation. Comparator structure differs from a simple psyllium-versus-placebo cholesterol protocol.
- **Safety notes:** The article described the psyllium supplement as well tolerated in accessible text, but this batch treats the study as context-only.
