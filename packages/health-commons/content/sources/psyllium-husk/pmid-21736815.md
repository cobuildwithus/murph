This source is included for **Mechanism: viscosity, bile-acid, sterol, and fecal-excretion context**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Randomized crossover controlled feeding study (2011).
- **People studied or addressed:** Apparently healthy adults.
- **Intervention or exposure:** Cereals enriched with low-viscosity wheat bran, medium-viscosity psyllium, or high-viscosity fiber blend.
- **Comparator or control:** Within-participant comparison across fiber-viscosity interventions.
- **Duration or follow-up:** Three 3-week interventions with at least 2-week washouts.
- **Endpoints:** LDL-C and apparent viscosity.
- **Directness:** adjacent_variant.
- **Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Findings

- A high-viscosity fiber blend produced larger LDL-C reductions than psyllium and wheat bran, and LDL-C change was associated with apparent viscosity (reported r=-0.41, P=0.001).

## Why it matters

Important context for why dose in grams alone may not describe cholesterol-lowering potential.

## Potential experiment signals

- LDL cholesterol
- fiber viscosity
- food matrix

## Protocol takeaway

Use as viscosity/formulation context; do not interpret as psyllium monotherapy efficacy in hypercholesterolemia.

## Safety and adverse events

No adverse-event extraction was available.

## Limitations and population fit

Healthy population; mixed fiber matrices; not psyllium monotherapy for hypercholesterolemia.
