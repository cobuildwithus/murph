This source is included for **Mechanism: viscosity, bile-acid, sterol, and fecal-excretion context**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Animal comparative soluble-fiber mechanism study in hamsters (1998).
- **Population/model:** Golden Syrian hamsters fed high-fat, cholesterol-containing semipurified diets.
- **Intervention/exposure:** 8% psyllium, high- or low-esterified pectin, or high- or low-viscous guar gum in diets containing 20% fat and 0.12% cholesterol.
- **Comparator/control:** Other soluble fibers and control diet.
- **Duration/follow-up:** 5 weeks
- **Role in Murph:** Context-only mechanism evidence for the Psyllium Husk For Cholesterol protocol.

## Findings

- **Lipoprotein composition, biliary bile acid composition, and fecal sterol excretion.:** Accessible extraction identified psyllium, but not pectin or guar gum, as altering lipoprotein and biliary bile acid composition and fecal sterol excretion in hamsters; exact group-level estimates were not extracted.

## Why it matters

Important for avoiding overgeneralization between psyllium, pectin, and guar gum.

## Potential experiment signals

- plasma lipoproteins
- biliary bile acid composition
- fecal sterol excretion
- cholesterol metabolism

## Protocol takeaway

Use as comparative animal mechanism context; separate psyllium findings from pectin and guar effects.

## Safety and adverse-event notes

No human adverse-event or tolerability data were extracted from this animal/comparative mechanism source. Safety claims for a human psyllium protocol should come from human clinical trials, guidelines, labels, or safety-specific sources.

## Important limitations

Animal high-fat/cholesterol diet; exact effect estimates were not extracted from accessible snippet; not direct human protocol evidence. The source is **not** direct evidence that the target human protocol lowers LDL cholesterol.
