This source is included for **Direct protocol synthesis and dose-response evidence**.

## Source extraction notes

**Findings:** Psyllium-enriched cereals lowered total cholesterol by -0.31 mmol/L and LDL-C by -0.35 mmol/L, with no HDL-C change in the pooled analysis. The review mentioned gastrointestinal discomfort and nausea but did not report how adverse events were measured, limiting safety interpretation.

**Why it matters:** This source directly supports a food/cereal delivery variant and helps keep formulation-specific evidence separate from generic psyllium powder claims.

**Potential experiment signals:** LDL-C; total cholesterol; HDL-C; GI discomfort.

**Protocol takeaway:** Psyllium-enriched cereals can lower LDL-C and total cholesterol, but this is formulation-specific evidence rather than proof every delivery method performs identically.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

## Evidence boundary

- **Directness:** `direct_protocol`.
- **Population mismatch:** Low mismatch for hypercholesterolemic adults, but delivery format differs from loose husk/powder protocols.
- **Limitations:** DARE noted no formal validity assessment. Included published and unpublished trials with limited methodological detail. Adverse-event measurement was not consistently reported.
- **Safety notes:** GI discomfort and nausea were mentioned but measurement methods were not reported in the DARE summary.
