This source is included for **Direct protocol synthesis and dose-response evidence**.

## Source extraction notes

**Findings:** Compared with placebo, psyllium lowered total cholesterol by about 4% and LDL-C by about 7%; HDL-C and triglycerides were not significantly affected. The review summarized psyllium intake as well tolerated in short-term studies, but this does not resolve choking risk, drug-spacing, or long-term tolerability questions.

**Why it matters:** This older pooled analysis is a direct cholesterol-backbone source because it focuses on hypercholesterolemia and the commonly cited 10.2 g/day psyllium dose.

**Potential experiment signals:** LDL-C; total cholesterol; HDL-C; triglycerides; apolipoprotein B; GI tolerability.

**Protocol takeaway:** A 10.2 g/day psyllium dose adjunctive to diet is directly supported for LDL-C reduction over at least 8 weeks in hypercholesterolemic adults.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

## Evidence boundary

- **Directness:** `direct_protocol`.
- **Population mismatch:** Low mismatch for adults with mild-to-moderate hypercholesterolemia; applicability is weaker for normolipidemic users.
- **Limitations:** Older trials and pooled summary; details on modern risk-of-bias methods are limited. Adjunctive-to-diet design means the independent psyllium effect is tied to diet-therapy context. Safety summary is short-term and not a substitute for contraindication review.
- **Safety notes:** Clinical studies of 6 weeks to 6 months were summarized as well tolerated; still use separate safety guidance for choking risk, medication spacing, and GI intolerance.
