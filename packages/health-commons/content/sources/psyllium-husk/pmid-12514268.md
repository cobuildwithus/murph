This source is included for **Mechanism: viscosity, bile-acid, sterol, and fecal-excretion context**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Randomized crossover human mechanistic study (2003).
- **People studied or addressed:** Men, premenopausal women, and postmenopausal women.
- **Intervention or exposure:** 15 g/day psyllium compared with 0 g/day control period.
- **Comparator or control:** No-psyllium control period.
- **Duration or follow-up:** 30 days per psyllium/control period.
- **Endpoints:** Plasma lipids; monocyte HMG-CoA reductase, LDL receptor, and lipoprotein lipase mRNA.
- **Directness:** direct_protocol.
- **Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Findings

- Accessible text reports LDL-C decreases with psyllium but sex/hormonal-status differences in triglycerides and monocyte gene-expression markers, including increased HMG-CoA reductase mRNA and unchanged LDL receptor mRNA.

## Why it matters

Useful for caveats around heterogeneous lipid responses and sex/menopause subgroup boundaries.

## Potential experiment signals

- LDL cholesterol
- triglycerides
- monocyte gene expression

## Protocol takeaway

Use as direct but context-only evidence that lipid responses may vary by sex and hormonal status.

## Safety and adverse events

No adverse-event extraction was available from accessible text.

## Limitations and population fit

Participant count and full subgroup effect estimates were not available from accessible text; subgroup mechanisms should not be used as mainline efficacy claims.

**Population mismatch:** Subgroup/sex-hormonal mechanism study rather than a clean general adult cholesterol trial.
