This source is included for **Adjacent variants, soluble-fiber comparators, and population mismatch**.

**Findings:** The review reported beta-glucan consumption lowered total cholesterol by about 0.60 mmol/L and LDL-C by about 0.66 mmol/L; it also reported small triglyceride/TAG and HDL-C changes. The authors concluded that 3 g/day oat or barley beta-glucan was sufficient to decrease total cholesterol, while glucose findings were highly heterogeneous and required longer intervention evidence.

**Safety / adverse events:** No adverse-event data were extracted from the accessible article summary.

**Why it matters:** Useful as broad beta-glucan class context and as a guardrail against assuming all soluble fibers behave identically.

**Potential experiment signals:** total cholesterol, LDL-C, HDL-C, triglycerides, blood glucose.

**Limitations / population mismatch:** Combines oat and barley beta-glucan, not psyllium; high heterogeneity was reported for total cholesterol, LDL-C, and blood glucose analyses, so precision and transferability are limited.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use only for adjacent beta-glucan mechanism/class context, not for psyllium efficacy.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
