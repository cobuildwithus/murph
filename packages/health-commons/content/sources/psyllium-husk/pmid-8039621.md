This source is included for **Mechanism: viscosity, bile-acid, sterol, and fecal-excretion context**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Animal mechanism and combination-therapy dietary study in hamsters (1994).
- **Population/model:** Hamsters fed a cholesterol-enriched chow diet.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Psyllium, cholestyramine, surfomer, or psyllium plus low-dose cholestyramine combinations.
- **Comparator/control:** Avicel control and single-agent comparator diets.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Not extracted from accessible abstract/metadata.
- **Role in Murph:** Context-only mechanism evidence for the Psyllium Husk For Cholesterol protocol.

## Findings

- **Cholesterol absorption, plasma/hepatic cholesterol, and sterol balance.:** Psyllium alone lowered plasma and hepatic cholesterol but did not block cholesterol absorption in the accessible abstract; adding psyllium to low-dose cholestyramine augmented cholesterol lowering, interpreted as enhanced net negative sterol balance across the liver.

## Why it matters

Helps separate psyllium-only mechanisms from bile-acid sequestrant co-therapy and suggests sterol balance may matter even when cholesterol absorption is not directly blocked.

## Potential experiment signals

- cholesterol absorption efficiency
- plasma total cholesterol
- hepatic total cholesterol
- net sterol balance across the liver

## Protocol takeaway

Use as adjacent mechanism/co-therapy context only; do not use as standalone psyllium supplementation evidence.

## Safety and adverse-event notes

No human adverse-event or tolerability data were extracted from this animal/comparative mechanism source. Safety claims for a human psyllium protocol should come from human clinical trials, guidelines, labels, or safety-specific sources.

## Important limitations

Hamster cholesterol-enriched diet; combination and comparator-heavy design; not direct human protocol evidence. The source is **not** direct evidence that the target human protocol lowers LDL cholesterol.
