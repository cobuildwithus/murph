This source is included for **Mechanism: viscosity, bile-acid, sterol, and fecal-excretion context**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials (2023).
- **People studied or addressed:** Participants in RCTs of viscous versus non-viscous fiber sources.
- **Intervention or exposure:** Viscous dietary fibers including psyllium, beta-glucan, konjac glucomannan, guar gum, and pectin.
- **Comparator or control:** Non-viscous cereal fibers or controls.
- **Duration or follow-up:** At least 3 weeks; median 6 weeks reported in accessible text.
- **Endpoints:** LDL-C, non-HDL-C, apoB.
- **Directness:** adjacent_variant.
- **Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Findings

- The meta-analysis reported pooled reductions in LDL-C, non-HDL-C, and apoB with viscous fibers, but this is a mixed-fiber estimate that includes psyllium rather than psyllium-only evidence.

## Why it matters

Useful to support the importance of viscosity while avoiding all-fiber generalization.

## Protocol takeaway

Use as adjacent viscous-fiber context; do not use pooled effect as psyllium-only effect.

## Limitations and population fit

Mixed viscous-fiber classes, heterogeneity, and publication-bias concerns; psyllium subgroup should be extracted separately before source-specific claims.

**Population mismatch:** Adjacent pooled viscous-fiber synthesis, not psyllium-only protocol evidence.
