This source is included for **Mechanism: viscosity, bile-acid, sterol, and fecal-excretion context**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Animal fractionation and fermentation mechanistic study in rats (2002).
- **Population/model:** Colectomized rats and in vitro fermentation preparations.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Psyllium seed husk and isolated fractions A, B, and C; fraction B was the gel-forming poorly fermented fraction.
- **Comparator/control:** Cellulose control and non-gel or more fermentable psyllium fractions.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Meal-feeding model; in vitro fermentation assessed over 3 days.
- **Role in Murph:** Context-only mechanism evidence for the Psyllium Husk For Cholesterol protocol.

## Findings

- **Excreta moisture and ileal bile acid excretion.:** Psyllium seed husk and its gel-forming poorly fermented fraction B increased wet weight/moisture of ileal excreta and total bile acids in ileal excreta compared with cellulose or other fractions.

## Why it matters

Useful for separating psyllium’s gel/viscosity mechanism from generalized fermentable fiber claims.

## Potential experiment signals

- ileal excreta wet weight
- ileal excreta moisture
- ileal bile acid excretion
- in vitro fermentation

## Protocol takeaway

Use as mechanism context for gel-forming psyllium effects on bile-acid loss; do not treat as human cholesterol outcome evidence.

## Safety and adverse-event notes

No human adverse-event or tolerability data were extracted from this animal/comparative mechanism source. Safety claims for a human psyllium protocol should come from human clinical trials, guidelines, labels, or safety-specific sources.

## Important limitations

Colectomized rat and in vitro fractionation model; no human lipid outcomes. The source is **not** direct evidence that the target human protocol lowers LDL cholesterol.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
