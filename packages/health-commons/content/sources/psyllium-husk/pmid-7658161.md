This source is included for **Mechanism: viscosity, bile-acid, sterol, and fecal-excretion context**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Animal mechanistic dietary intervention study in guinea pigs (1995).
- **Population/model:** Male Hartley guinea pigs fed low- or high-cholesterol semipurified diets.
- **Intervention/exposure:** 7.5% psyllium replacing cellulose in low-cholesterol or high-cholesterol diets.
- **Comparator/control:** 12.5% cellulose control diets with matching cholesterol levels.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Not extracted from accessible abstract/metadata.
- **Role in Murph:** Context-only mechanism evidence for the Psyllium Husk For Cholesterol protocol.

## Findings

- **Plasma LDL cholesterol and hepatic cholesterol homeostasis.:** Psyllium reduced plasma LDL by about 30% in low-cholesterol diets and 54% in high-cholesterol diets, and accessible abstract text reports lower hepatic free and esterified cholesterol and increased HMG-CoA reductase activity.
- **LDL composition, molecular weight, diameter, and density.:** Psyllium altered LDL composition and physical properties, with lower cholesteryl ester, higher triacylglycerol, lower molecular weight, smaller diameter, and higher peak density reported in the abstract.

## Why it matters

Connects psyllium intake to hepatic cholesterol balance and LDL particle changes in a comparative animal lipid model.

No human adverse-event or tolerability data were extracted from this animal/comparative mechanism source. Safety claims for a human psyllium protocol should come from human clinical trials, guidelines, labels, or safety-specific sources.

## Important limitations

Guinea-pig dietary model with diet-level psyllium; accessible extraction did not provide full trial duration or safety details. The source is **not** direct evidence that the target human protocol lowers LDL cholesterol.
