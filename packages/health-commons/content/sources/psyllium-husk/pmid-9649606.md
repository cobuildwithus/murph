This source is included for **Mechanism: viscosity, bile-acid, sterol, and fecal-excretion context**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Animal bile-acid biosynthesis dietary intervention study in rats (1998).
- **Population/model:** Rats, 4 groups of 10.
- **Intervention/exposure:** 5% psyllium hydrocolloid diet.
- **Comparator/control:** 5% cellulose control, 5% cellulose plus 1% cholic acid, and 5% cellulose plus 2% cholestyramine comparator diets.
- **Duration/follow-up:** 3 weeks
- **Role in Murph:** Context-only mechanism evidence for the Psyllium Husk For Cholesterol protocol.

## Findings

- **Fecal bile acid excretion, total steroid excretion, liver cholesterol, 7 alpha-hydroxylase activity and mRNA.:** Rats fed psyllium had significantly lower liver cholesterol than cellulose controls, significantly greater total fecal steroid and bile acid excretion, and significantly higher 7 alpha-hydroxylase activity and mRNA levels compared with cellulose or cholic-acid diets.

## Why it matters

Directly supports the bile-acid/steroid-loss mechanism seam in an animal model.

Use as animal mechanism context for bile-acid biosynthesis and excretion; not direct human protocol evidence.

## Safety and adverse-event notes

No human adverse-event or tolerability data were extracted from this animal/comparative mechanism source. Safety claims for a human psyllium protocol should come from human clinical trials, guidelines, labels, or safety-specific sources.

## Important limitations

Rat semipurified diet model with 5% psyllium hydrocolloid; not a human supplement trial. The source is **not** direct evidence that the target human protocol lowers LDL cholesterol.
