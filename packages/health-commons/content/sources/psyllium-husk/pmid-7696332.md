This source is included for **Mechanism: viscosity, bile-acid, sterol, and fecal-excretion context**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Animal LDL-production and clearance mechanism study in hamsters (1995).
- **Population/model:** Adult male Golden Syrian hamsters fed a cholesterol- and saturated-fat-enriched diet.
- **Intervention/exposure:** 7.5% psyllium hydrophilic mucilloid in a cereal-based diet containing 0.1% cholesterol and 10% hydrogenated coconut oil for 30 days.
- **Comparator/control:** 7.5% microcrystalline cellulose control diet.
- **Duration/follow-up:** 30 days
- **Role in Murph:** Context-only mechanism evidence for the Psyllium Husk For Cholesterol protocol.

## Findings

- **Plasma cholesterol, LDL cholesterol, hepatic total cholesterol, and hepatic cholesterol synthesis.:** Compared with controls, psyllium-fed hamsters had lower plasma total cholesterol (122.1 ± 4.1 vs 399.4 ± 39.4 mg/dL), lower LDL cholesterol (46.0 ± 2.2 vs 143.5 ± 12.0 mg/dL), lower hepatic total cholesterol (2.6 ± 0.1 vs 16.6 ± 1.1 mg/g), and higher hepatic cholesterol synthesis (165.1 ± 27.1 vs 26.1 ± 1.2 nmol/h/g).
- **LDL cholesterol production and hepatic LDL clearance.:** Psyllium was associated with 44% lower LDL-cholesterol production (167.6 ± 8.1 vs 300.2 ± 16.0 µg/h/100 g body weight) and 2.2-fold higher hepatic LDL clearance (50.1 ± 2.3 vs 22.6 ± 2.1 µL/h/g) in hamsters.

## Why it matters

Provides mechanistic context that animal LDL lowering may involve both lower LDL production and hepatic clearance changes, not just fecal excretion.

## Safety and adverse-event notes

No human adverse-event or tolerability data were extracted from this animal/comparative mechanism source. Safety claims for a human psyllium protocol should come from human clinical trials, guidelines, labels, or safety-specific sources.

## Important limitations

Hamster high-fat/high-cholesterol diet model; no human participants; accessible abstract did not expose sample size. The source is **not** direct evidence that the target human protocol lowers LDL cholesterol.
