This source is included for **Mechanism: viscosity, bile-acid, sterol, and fecal-excretion context**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Randomized double-blind crossover trial (1992).
- **People studied or addressed:** Men with moderate hypercholesterolemia.
- **Intervention or exposure:** 15 g/day psyllium hydrophilic mucilloid.
- **Comparator or control:** Cellulose placebo in crossover sequence.
- **Duration or follow-up:** Two 40-day treatment periods separated by an 11 ± 2 day washout.
- **Endpoints:** LDL cholesterol, cholesterol absorption, bile-acid pool kinetics, bile-acid synthesis.
- **Directness:** direct_protocol.
- **Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

## Findings

- LDL cholesterol averaged 184 mg/dL at baseline, 169 mg/dL during psyllium, and 179 mg/dL during placebo; psyllium differed from baseline (P<0.004) and placebo (P<0.02).
- Psyllium reduced relative cholesterol absorption and increased chenodeoxycholic and cholic acid fractional turnover; LDL responders showed increased bile-acid synthesis versus baseline and placebo.

## Why it matters

This is direct protocol evidence and a key human mechanistic source for the LDL/bile-acid hypothesis.

## Potential experiment signals

- LDL cholesterol
- cholesterol absorption
- bile-acid synthesis
- fecal or kinetic bile-acid signal

## Protocol takeaway

Supports a direct cholesterol-lowering mechanism in which psyllium reduces cholesterol absorption and stimulates bile-acid synthesis, with a small male-only evidence base.

## Safety and adverse events

No adverse-event details were extracted from accessible source text.

## Limitations and population fit

Small male-only trial; short treatment periods; mechanistic measurements may not generalize to all psyllium products or populations.

**Population mismatch:** Male hypercholesterolemic sample; does not directly address women, normolipidemic adults, or long-term outcomes.
