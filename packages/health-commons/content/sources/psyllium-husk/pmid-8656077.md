This source is included for **Mechanism: viscosity, bile-acid, sterol, and fecal-excretion context**.

## Quick read

- **Source type:** Animal comparative soluble-fiber mechanism study in guinea pigs (1995).
- **Population/model:** Guinea pigs fed low- or high-cholesterol diets.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Pectin, guar gum, or psyllium dietary fiber interventions.
- **Comparator/control:** Fiber control and other soluble-fiber comparators.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Not extracted from accessible abstract/metadata.
- **Role in Murph:** Context-only mechanism evidence for the Psyllium Husk For Cholesterol protocol.

## Findings

- **Plasma LDL cholesterol, hepatic cholesterol, cholesterol absorption, and 7 alpha-hydroxylase activity.:** Pectin, guar gum, and psyllium lowered plasma and hepatic cholesterol, but mechanisms differed: pectin and psyllium upregulated 7 alpha-hydroxylase in low-cholesterol conditions, while psyllium did not reduce cholesterol absorption versus control in the accessible extraction.
- **LDL fractional catabolic rate and apoB kinetics.:** In high-cholesterol-fed guinea pigs, dietary fibers produced faster LDL fractional catabolic rate; pectin and psyllium increased LDL fractional catabolic rate, while guar gum effects were described as more limited to apoB fractional rate.

## Why it matters

Useful for avoiding overgeneralization from all viscous fibers and for distinguishing absorption, bile-acid synthesis, and LDL-transport mechanisms.

Use as adjacent comparative animal mechanism evidence; do not merge pectin/guar findings into psyllium claims.

## Safety and adverse-event notes

No human adverse-event or tolerability data were extracted from this animal/comparative mechanism source. Safety claims for a human psyllium protocol should come from human clinical trials, guidelines, labels, or safety-specific sources.

## Important limitations

Animal comparative design; not a human psyllium protocol; different fiber types and cholesterol-level strata limit directness. The source is **not** direct evidence that the target human protocol lowers LDL cholesterol.
