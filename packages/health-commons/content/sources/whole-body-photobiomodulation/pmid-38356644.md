This source is included for **Chronic pain/fibromyalgia whole-body PBM sibling variant**.

**Findings:** Forty-two participants were recruited into a triple-blinded placebo-controlled fibromyalgia trial and followed for up to 6 months after 12 whole-body PBM sessions. The article reports significant pain improvement after treatment, at 2 weeks, and again at 6 months; quality of life improved from mid-treatment through all later assessments; leisure physical activity improved from post-treatment onward; and kinesiophobia and self-efficacy improved across later follow-up windows. Pain catastrophizing did not clearly differ earlier but improved at the 6-month timepoint.

**Why it matters:** This paper extends the fibromyalgia RCT program beyond short-term change and preserves mixed time-course details that matter for realistic expectations.

**Potential experiment signals:** pain, quality of life, leisure physical activity, pain catastrophizing, kinesiophobia, self-efficacy.

**Protocol takeaway:** Treat as the best long-term controlled signal in this sibling variant, while keeping the fibromyalgia/supervised-context boundary explicit.

**Safety / adverse events:** A dedicated adverse-event table was not evident in the accessible extraction used here, so the paper contributes clearer long-term symptom direction than safety-rate detail.

**Limitations:** Sample size calculation was anchored to the primary outcome; age and menopausal status may influence results; baseline kinesiophobia differed between groups; placebo-group pain also improved.

**Population mismatch / directness:** Whole-body red/NIR delivery is direct, but the cohort remains a supervised fibromyalgia population and therefore should not be promoted into generalized protocol efficacy claims.
