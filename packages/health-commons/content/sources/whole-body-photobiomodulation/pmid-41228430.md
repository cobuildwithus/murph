This source is included for **Emerging disease-specific whole-body PBM variants**.

**Findings:** This source reports a direct whole-body red and near-infrared PBM exposure in a women-only metabolic cohort. After a single 12-minute front-and-back session, women with obesity showed a 9.3% increase in resting energy expenditure, while respiratory exchange ratio did not change. Rate of perceived exertion and flexibility improved after PBM, and no adverse events were observed. The main generalizability limit is that the signal was acute, disease-specific, and restricted to women.

**Why it matters:** It expands direct human whole-body PBM recall beyond pain and performance into acute metabolic outcomes, but also sets a clear boundary against overgeneralizing from a small obesity-specific cohort.

**Potential experiment signals:** resting energy expenditure, oxygen consumption, respiratory exchange ratio, perceived exertion, flexibility, skin temperature

**Protocol takeaway:** Treat as direct but narrow evidence. It is best used to shape outcome choice and dosing ideas for supervised or obesity-adjacent contexts, not to promise a durable metabolic effect in the general population.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
