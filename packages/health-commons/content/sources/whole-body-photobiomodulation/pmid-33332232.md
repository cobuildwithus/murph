This source is included for **Exercise-timed whole-body PBM sibling variant**.

**Findings:** Thirteen young male water polo athletes were randomized during a 5-day championship to full-body PBM or placebo after each match. The active protocol used six Joovv panels positioned about 30 cm from the body for 2.5 minutes front and 2.5 minutes back, delivering red 660 nm and near-infrared 850 nm light at about 46.17 mW/cm^2 and 6.9 J/cm^2. Most outcomes showed no significant interaction or group effect, including testosterone, cortisol, TNF-alpha, IL-6, CK, maximal isometric force, squat jump, and heart-rate variability. The only reported advantage was an isolated LDH decrease after the first match. This makes the paper a mostly null preliminary athlete study rather than a positive efficacy record.

**Why it matters:** It is one of the few real-world tournament studies of whole-body PBM and shows that most recovery markers did not improve under short post-match dosing.

**Potential experiment signals:** CK, LDH, IL-6, TNF-alpha, HRV, jump performance, isometric force, subjective soreness.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as adjacent-variant recovery-boundary evidence, not as proof that full-body PBM speeds post-match recovery.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
