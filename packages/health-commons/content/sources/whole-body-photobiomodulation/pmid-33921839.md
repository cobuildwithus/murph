This source is included for **Skin/cosmetic large-area or full-body PBM sibling variant**.

**Findings:** This study enrolled 30 adult women from 39 screened candidates and used a 630 nm/850 nm LED neck device daily for 9 minutes over 16 weeks, followed by 8 weeks after discontinuation. The device contained 120 LEDs and delivered a total irradiance of 25 mW/cm². Thyroid and parathyroid safety was the primary outcome. Across laboratory testing and ultrasound, mean thyroid hormones, PTH, antibody levels, gland size, vascularity, echogenicity, lymph nodes, and incidental nodules did not change significantly during the study. One participant developed an elevated PTH value that investigators attributed to vitamin D insufficiency rather than device exposure. Local cosmetic outcomes improved: the blinded Lemperle neck-fold score fell significantly after 16 weeks and remained below baseline after discontinuation, while hydration, elasticity, and skin density also improved significantly. The major limitation was the lack of a control group and the restriction to healthy adult women without prior thyroid disease.

**Why it matters:** It is especially useful for Murph because it monitored an adjacent gland exposure boundary directly instead of assuming safety from cosmetic outcomes alone.

**Potential experiment signals:** thyroid function tests, thyroid ultrasound, wrinkle scale, hydration, elasticity, skin density.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as a thyroid-safety boundary source and local neck-device context, not as direct controlled evidence for whole-body red/NIR benefit.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.
