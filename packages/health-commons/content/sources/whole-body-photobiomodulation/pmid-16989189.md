This source is included for **Skin/cosmetic large-area or full-body PBM sibling variant**.

**Findings:** Thirty-six subjects received nine combined 633-nm and 830-nm LED treatments over five weeks and were evaluated for final clinical improvement 12 weeks after treatment. Five subjects also underwent biopsies for ultrastructural collagen assessment. The study reported statistically significant wrinkle improvement by profilometric analysis. Most subjects reported better softness, smoothness, and firmness at all time points. Electron microscopy in the biopsy subset showed thicker collagen fibers after treatment. The accessible abstract did not provide a detailed adverse-event table, so this record is best kept as adjacent facial context rather than a safety-defining source.

**Why it matters:** It is a concise adjacent record for combined 633/830 nm facial use and collagen-fiber ultrastructure.

**Potential experiment signals:** wrinkles, softness, smoothness, firmness, collagen fiber ultrastructure.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use only as adjacent facial red-plus-NIR evidence for wrinkles and tissue-structure endpoints, not as a direct Murph protocol source.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Skin Photobiomodulation Note

This source is included for **core facial LED benefit evidence; clinic-supervised/non-wearable geometry**.

**Findings:** Thirty-six subjects received nine combined 633 nm and 830 nm LED treatments over 5 weeks, with final evaluation 12 weeks after treatment and five biopsy participants reported in accessible summaries. Extracted material reports significant wrinkle improvement by profilometry, participant-reported improvements in softness/smoothness/firmness, and thicker collagen fibers on electron microscopy.

**Why it matters:** It is another early red+NIR photoaging source using the same wavelength pair that appears in several foundational LED rejuvenation studies.

**Potential experiment signals:** Wrinkle/profilometry, skin feel ratings, and clear separation between clinical outcomes and biopsy-based mechanism.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as supportive but lower-certainty clinical evidence because randomization/sham control details were not extracted.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.
