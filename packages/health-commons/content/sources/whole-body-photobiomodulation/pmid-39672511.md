This source is included for **Safety, contraindication, and population-boundary evidence**.

**Findings:** The review screened 689 records and analyzed 20 studies involving 2380 participants, including 1436 individuals undergoing repeated low-level red-light therapy. Median follow-up was 9 months and the longest follow-up was 24 months. No permanent vision loss was reported, but two case reports described an identical patient with reversible visual acuity decline and OCT abnormalities that resolved after stopping therapy. Temporary afterimage was the most common ocular symptom, usually resolving within six minutes, and the reported side-effect incidence was 0.088 per 100 patient-years.

**Why it matters:** This is a recent safety-focused review that helps Murph keep eye-monitoring guidance explicit and evidence-based.

**Potential experiment signals:** visual acuity change, afterimage duration, OCT changes, fundus photography.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as adjacent ocular safety evidence: no irreversible visual harm was identified in the reviewed literature, but monitoring and prompt discontinuation for visual symptoms remain important.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

## Skin Photobiomodulation Note

This source is included for **eye and face-adjacent ocular safety boundary**.

**Findings:** Extracted summaries report no permanent vision loss, temporary afterimage as the most common ocular symptom resolving within minutes, and reversible visual-acuity/OCT abnormalities in case reports after treatment cessation.

**Why it matters:** It is a directly safety-focused synthesis in the adjacent repeated-red-light retinal literature.

**Potential experiment signals:** temporary afterimage, afterimage duration, visual acuity, OCT abnormality, permanent vision loss.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as safety-only or context-only evidence; do not use for direct skin-outcome claims.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.
