This source is included for **Chronic pain/fibromyalgia whole-body PBM sibling variant**.

**Findings:** Forty-nine people were screened, 21 entered the study, and 19 completed a course of 18 whole-body PBMT sessions over roughly 6 weeks. The paper reports positive changes in fibromyalgia-specific quality of life, pain, tenderness, stiffness, fatigue, sleep disturbance, anxiety, depression, and cognitive impairment. Patient global impression improved after treatment and remained improved at 24 weeks, and fibromyalgia-specific quality of life was still better than baseline at 24 weeks.

**Why it matters:** This paper captures the practical tolerability and symptom domains that changed in a real-world fibromyalgia feasibility cohort, which helps with endpoint recall even though the design is uncontrolled.

**Potential experiment signals:** fibromyalgia impact, pain, tenderness, stiffness, fatigue, sleep disturbance, anxiety, depression, cognitive impairment.

**Protocol takeaway:** Treat as signal-generating and acceptability-focused evidence rather than as confirmatory efficacy evidence.

**Safety / adverse events:** The accessible record supports feasibility and acceptability, but a detailed adverse-event table was not clearly exposed in the extraction used here.

**Limitations:** Uncontrolled feasibility design; small sample; 2 participants did not complete treatment; disease-specific supervised cohort.

**Population mismatch / directness:** Direct whole-body delivery is relevant to the protocol family, but the cohort consists of symptomatic fibromyalgia patients in supervised care rather than a broader protocol population.

**Artifact candidates / rights:** A public PMC article page is available and is the safest manifest candidate. The underlying article is open access under CC BY terms.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
