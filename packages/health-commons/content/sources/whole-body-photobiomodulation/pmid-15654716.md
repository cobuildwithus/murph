This source is included for **Skin/cosmetic large-area or full-body PBM sibling variant**.

**Findings:** This prospective clinical trial studied 90 patients with photoaged skin treated by a full-panel 590 nm non-thermal full-face LED array delivering 0.1 J/cm² with a specific pulsing sequence. Participants received eight treatments over four weeks and were evaluated through 12 months of follow-up. Digital imaging showed reduction of photoaging signs in 90% of subjects, with smoother texture, reduced peri-orbital rhytids, and less erythema and pigmentation; optical profilometry showed an average 10% improvement. Biopsy and immunofluorescence findings showed increased papillary dermal collagen in all 10 post-treatment biopsy specimens, a 28% average increase in collagen I staining, and an average 4% reduction in MMP-1. Investigators recorded no side effects or pain, although two patients reported temporary exacerbation of papular acne and 17 reported temporary quenching of papular acne.

**Why it matters:** It is one of the clearer early examples of biopsy-backed PBM skin data, even though it remains an adjacent 590 nm facial protocol.

**Potential experiment signals:** wrinkles, skin texture, collagen I, MMP-1, acne flares.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use it only for adjacent cosmetic and mechanistic context; do not translate its facial 590 nm findings into direct whole-body red/NIR claims.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Skin Photobiomodulation Note

This source is included for **wavelength sibling: red-only, amber/yellow, or broad-spectrum evidence**.

**Findings:** The source reports 90 participants treated with a 590 nm full-face LED array at 0.1 J/cm² for eight treatments over 4 weeks. Extracted summaries report photoaging reduction in 90% of patients, about 10% optical profilometry improvement, collagen I increases averaging 28%, MMP-1 reductions averaging 4%, and no side effects or pain. Source key: `source_artifact:pmid-15654716`.

**Why it matters:** It links clinical appearance endpoints with mechanistic biopsy endpoints, but in an amber-light protocol rather than the target wavelength combination.

**Potential experiment signals:** digital imaging photoaging score, optical profilometry, collagen I, MMP-1, pain/side effects.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as mechanism and endpoint context for collagen remodeling; do not use as direct red+NIR efficacy evidence.
