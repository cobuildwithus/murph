This source is included for **Safety, contraindication, and population-boundary evidence**.

**Findings:** The WALT position paper reviews PBM use for cancer therapy-related side effects and states that clinical trial results have not demonstrated adverse events attributable to PBM. At the same time, it recommends caution in cancer patients and explicitly notes the need for more in vivo work and large clinical trials with follow-up of at least five years before declaring that PBM has no effect on progression or survival. This makes it a strong boundary-setting guidance source, not a direct whole-body protocol paper.

**Why it matters:** This is a major expert-practice source that helps Murph keep oncology supportive-care claims and cancer-related caution language aligned with a recognized PBM organization.

**Potential experiment signals:** adverse events, parameter reporting, long-term recurrence follow-up.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as oncology supportive-care safety context: reassuring on reported adverse events, but explicit that cancer populations still warrant caution and longer follow-up.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.
