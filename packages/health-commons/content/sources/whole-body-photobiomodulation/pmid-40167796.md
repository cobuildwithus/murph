This source is included for **Skin/cosmetic large-area or full-body PBM sibling variant**.

**Findings:** This randomized sham-controlled double-blind facial rejuvenation trial included 95 women aged 45–60. The active intervention used a red LED mask (660 ± 10 nm, 6.4 mW/cm², 8.05 J/cm², 21 minutes). Group 1 received three sessions per week for four weeks (12 sessions), Group 2 received two sessions per week for four weeks (8 sessions), and the control group received sham photobiomodulation twice per week for four weeks. No significant differences among groups were found on the photographic Wrinkle Assessment Scale. In contrast, ImageJ analysis showed significant reductions in glabellar and right periorbital wrinkle length in the active groups versus control, and FACE-Q satisfaction rates were 79.6% and 73.4% in Groups 1 and 2, respectively, both significantly better than control. The authors concluded that the number of sessions did not materially change the positive results and that two weekly sessions appeared sufficient for improving patient satisfaction.

**Why it matters:** It is a good Murph example of why mixed results must stay visible instead of being flattened into a single positive headline.

**Potential experiment signals:** wrinkle assessment scale, image-based wrinkle length, FACE-Q satisfaction, session frequency.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use only as adjacent frequency and endpoint-behavior context; preserve the null photographic result and do not promote it into a direct whole-body efficacy claim.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
