This source is included for **Safety, contraindication, and population-boundary evidence**.

**Findings:** This study characterized the output of two repeated low-level red-light myopia devices and compared them with ocular maximum permissible exposure limits. For both instruments, three minutes of continuous viewing approached or exceeded the luminance dose limit relevant to retinal photochemical damage. That makes it a strong eye-safety boundary source, but it is still an adjacent-variant device analysis rather than a direct whole-body PBM clinical trial.

**Why it matters:** It is the clearest recent red-light boundary source for retinal exposure limits and direct-gaze risk.

**Potential experiment signals:** direct-gaze exposure, retinal photochemical risk, device-output checks.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as adjacent ocular safety-boundary evidence: do not allow direct staring into high-intensity red-light sources and keep eye-protection language explicit.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.
