This source is included for **Chronic pain/fibromyalgia whole-body PBM sibling variant**.

**Findings:** Sixteen participants from the trial cohort were interviewed during treatment, producing a reflexive thematic analysis centered on a positive spiral the authors termed 'recomposition'. The paper describes increased motivation, feeling proud, improved confidence, and feeling more like one's old self. It also notes that positive high-level processes coexisted with some residual aches, stiffness, sleep disturbance, fatigue, and mental struggles.

**Why it matters:** This source preserves participant-language and implementation context that are usually absent from efficacy papers, especially around motivation, confidence, and identity-related change.

**Potential experiment signals:** participant experience, motivation, confidence, pain, stiffness, sleep, fatigue.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use for acceptability and lived-experience context only, not for efficacy claims.

**Safety / adverse events:** This was not a dedicated adverse-event study. The accessible text instead highlights treatment experience and notes real-world disruptions such as postponed sessions related to illness or COVID, not formal safety rates.

**Limitations:** Small convenience sample; no placebo comparator for interview responses; interviews themselves may have influenced reflection; some real-world factors disrupted treatment timing.

**Population mismatch / directness:** The study directly concerns supervised whole-body PBMT but is restricted to fibromyalgia participants and experiential outcomes rather than generalized efficacy or safety.
