This source is included for **Safety, contraindication, and population-boundary evidence**.

**Findings:** The review screened a large light-therapy literature and included 43 articles. Ocular complaints such as discomfort or vision problems were reported variably, but the review did not identify evidence of ocular damage in most studies. The key caution came from a case report of maculopathy in a person taking the photosensitizing antidepressant clomipramine, plus poor reporting of light parameters and limited evidence for people with preexisting ocular abnormalities.

**Why it matters:** This is an important eye-safety boundary source because it preserves both the generally reassuring record and the need for caution in photosensitized or preexisting eye-disease settings.

**Potential experiment signals:** ocular complaints, visual symptoms, photophobia, eye discomfort, vision changes.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as adjacent eye-safety context: generally reassuring for healthy users, but not a license to ignore eye protection, medication review, or ocular disease history.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

## Skin Photobiomodulation Note

This source is included for **eye and face-adjacent ocular safety boundary**.

**Findings:** The review included 43 articles; ocular complaints ranged from 0% to 45%, with no clear dose relationship and no evidence of ocular damage except one maculopathy case in a person using clomipramine.

**Why it matters:** It preserves a mixed safety picture: symptoms can occur, serious damage was rare in studied populations, and susceptible groups remain uncertain.

**Potential experiment signals:** ocular discomfort, vision problems, photosensitizing medication, preexisting eye condition.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as safety-only or context-only evidence; do not use for direct skin-outcome claims.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.
