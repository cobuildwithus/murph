This source is included for **Skin/cosmetic large-area or full-body PBM sibling variant**.

**Findings:** This systematic review searched the literature on September 13, 2017 and included 31 original randomized controlled trials of LED treatments for skin conditions. Acne vulgaris, herpes simplex and zoster, and acute wound healing received grade B recommendations, while other skin conditions received only grade C or D recommendations. The review emphasized frequent design limitations, including small sample sizes often below 20 participants, absent blinding, no sham placebo, and widely varied treatment parameters. The authors also noted few incidences of adverse events across the included dermatology LED trials, but the review was not specific to whole-body red/NIR exposure.

**Why it matters:** It is useful for documenting the boundary between a generally reassuring dermatology safety profile and a weakly standardized efficacy literature.

**Potential experiment signals:** adverse events, sham control use, population fit, skin outcomes.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as safety-context and methodology-context only; do not turn this heterogeneous dermatology review into a direct whole-body PBM claim.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

## Skin Photobiomodulation Note

This source is included for **review or consensus context for dermatology PBM**.

**Findings:** The review included 31 randomized controlled trials. It reported stronger evidence for some dermatologic indications such as acne and wound-related uses than for many other indications, and noted recurring limitations including small samples, variable parameters, absent sham controls, and incomplete blinding.

**Why it matters:** It is a controlled-trial review that helps separate stronger LED indications from weaker or indirect cosmetic evidence.

**Potential experiment signals:** condition-specific evidence grade, adverse-event reporting, trial blinding, sham/control use, wavelength and treatment schedule.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use condition-specific evidence cautiously; do not treat general LED dermatology support as direct evidence for photoaging unless the underlying trial is direct.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
