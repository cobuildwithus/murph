This source is included for **Chronic pain/fibromyalgia whole-body PBM sibling variant**.

**Findings:** Forty-two participants with fibromyalgia were randomized in a triple-blinded placebo-controlled trial and received 12 sessions of whole-body PBM or placebo over 4 weeks. Pain improved significantly after treatment and again at the 2-week follow-up, while quality of life improved by session 6 and remained better immediately after treatment and at follow-up. Kinesiophobia improved after treatment and at follow-up, self-efficacy improved at follow-up, and pain catastrophizing showed no between-group difference.

**Why it matters:** This is one of the strongest controlled whole-body fibromyalgia papers in the batch for short-term symptom direction and sham-controlled comparator structure.

**Potential experiment signals:** pain, quality of life, kinesiophobia, self-efficacy, pain catastrophizing.

**Protocol takeaway:** This trial supports short-term symptom improvement in a supervised fibromyalgia setting, but the claim boundary should stay within that context.

**Safety / adverse events:** The accessible article text used here did not expose a dedicated adverse-event table, so the trial contributes stronger efficacy than safety detail in this extraction.

**Limitations:** Single-site private-clinic cohort; modest sample; 2-week follow-up only in this paper; pain catastrophizing null result; fibromyalgia-only population.

**Population mismatch / directness:** Direct whole-body red/NIR exposure is relevant, but the source remains a clinically supervised fibromyalgia RCT rather than a generalized protocol implementation study.
