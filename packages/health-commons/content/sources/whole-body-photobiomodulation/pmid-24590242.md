This source is included for **Safety, contraindication, and population-boundary evidence**.

**Findings:** The authors revisited earlier BMLA-style caution and searched PubMed for evidence about laser use in people taking photosensitizing medication. Only four publications were found, and none reported complications from laser treatment in those patients. That supports a more nuanced medication-screening approach, but the evidence base is very small and mostly about non-essential laser therapy rather than whole-body red/NIR photobiomodulation.

**Why it matters:** This source helps Murph avoid unsupported blanket bans while still being explicit that the available literature is thin and wavelength-dependent.

**Potential experiment signals:** photosensitivity reactions, burns, delayed healing, treatment intolerance.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as adjacent-variant evidence for medication-screening nuance, not as proof that photosensitizing drugs are harmless with whole-body PBM.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.
