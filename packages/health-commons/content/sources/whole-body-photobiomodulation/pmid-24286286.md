This source is included for **Skin/cosmetic large-area or full-body PBM sibling variant**.

**Findings:** A total of 136 volunteers participated, with 113 subjects randomized into four active treatment groups and 23 serving as controls. Active groups received twice-weekly large-area or full-body PBM using either 611–650 nm red light or broader 570–850 nm polychromatic light normalized to approximately 9 J/cm² in the 611–650 nm band, although irradiance and treatment duration varied by group. After 30 sessions, treated subjects showed significantly improved skin complexion and skin feeling, lower profilometric skin roughness, higher ultrasonographic collagen density, and better blinded photographic ratings than controls. The broader polychromatic source showed no advantage over the red-only spectrum. The abstract characterized both light sources as safe, but the accessible record did not provide a detailed adverse-event table.

**Why it matters:** It gives Murph a conservative way to reference a large-area/full-body sibling protocol without blurring cosmetic skin results into systemic whole-body benefit claims.

**Potential experiment signals:** skin roughness, collagen density, patient satisfaction, skin feel.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use only as adjacent cosmetic evidence for large-area/full-body PBM feasibility and skin endpoints; preserve the mixed result that broader-spectrum exposure did not beat red-only treatment.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
