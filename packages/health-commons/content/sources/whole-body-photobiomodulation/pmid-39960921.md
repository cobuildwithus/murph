This source is included for **Skin/cosmetic large-area or full-body PBM sibling variant**.

**Findings:** This multicenter randomized double-blind sham-controlled trial included 60 Asian adults aged 30–65 years with Fitzpatrick II–V skin and crow’s-feet grading scores of 2–4. The experimental device combined 630 nm LED and 850 nm IRED output, while the comparator was a sham mask. By week 16, both independent-rater and investigator crow’s-feet grading scores differed significantly between groups at weeks 8, 12, and 16. The prespecified success criterion was also met: independent raters found improvement rates of at least 69.2%, with 86.2% in the full-analysis set and 89.3% in the per-protocol set, exceeding the required between-group margin. The authors described the treatment as effective, safe, well tolerated, and painless, but the study remained limited to a local periocular cosmetic indication.

**Why it matters:** It is a modern sham-controlled face-mask trial with explicit response thresholds, but it still belongs in the adjacent local-device bucket.

**Potential experiment signals:** crow’s-feet grading, investigator ratings, participant ratings, tolerability.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use only as local cosmetic mask context with sham control and tolerability reporting; it does not directly inform whole-body red/NIR protocol claims.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.
