This source is included for **Exercise-timed whole-body PBM sibling variant**.

**Findings:** Forty-eight healthy active adults completed a single-blind crossover study comparing 20 minutes of whole-body PBM before repeated Wingate cycling against placebo and baseline conditions. The abstract reports no significant condition-by-time interactions for power output, blood lactate, perceived exertion, or most acute heart-rate variability measures. Perceived recovery and perceived stress also did not differ. Two signals did emerge: peak heart rate during the exercise protocol was slightly higher during active PBM than placebo, and next-morning rMSSD was higher after PBM than placebo (p=0.043). This makes the study important as a mixed rather than positive result.

**Why it matters:** It is one of the biggest direct human whole-body PBM exercise studies in the batch and it clearly separates null performance findings from a possible next-day autonomic recovery effect.

**Potential experiment signals:** heart rate variability, peak power, average power, lactate, perceived recovery, perceived stress, peak heart rate.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use it to bound claims: pre-exercise whole-body PBM did not improve maximal anaerobic performance in this setting.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
