This source is included for **Adjacent-variant and exclusion boundary anchors**.

**Findings:** The review summarizes human transcranial PBM studies in which even a single session could change measured brain activity. Accessible abstract text reports that focal and helmet approaches increased regional cerebral blood flow and altered neuronal activity, and that some focal interventions induced wider functional-connectivity changes. Population mismatch is substantial because the intervention is focal head stimulation rather than whole-body skin exposure. The review also notes that patient studies were much sparser than healthy-participant studies and that more randomized controlled trials are needed.

**Why it matters:** It anchors the transcranial-mechanism boundary: relevant for understanding how PBM can affect neurophysiology, but not appropriate for direct whole-body benefit claims.

**Potential experiment signals:** brain activity, regional cerebral blood flow, functional connectivity, acute session effects.

**Protocol takeaway:** Keep this as same-mechanism context only. Do not translate transcranial brain-activity findings into direct whole-body red/NIR protocol claims.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
