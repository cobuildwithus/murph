This source is included for **Adjacent-variant and exclusion boundary anchors**.

**Findings:** The meta-analysis reported improved depression scores with standardized mean difference -0.37 (95% CI -0.63 to -0.12) across 18 studies and 610 patients, and improved response rate with relative risk 1.42 (95% CI 1.08 to 1.85) across 16 studies and 559 patients. Reported adverse effects were generally mild to moderate and included headache, irritability or hypomanic symptoms, hyperactivity, nausea, eyestrain, insomnia, and nervousness. The authors also note important limitations: short study durations, small average sample sizes, heterogeneity in illumination and timing, and recurrent risk-of-bias concerns. Population and modality mismatch are explicit because this is bright-light treatment for seasonal affective disorder, not whole-body red/NIR photobiomodulation.

**Why it matters:** It keeps a legitimate positive light-therapy meta-analysis in the right intervention bucket and helps avoid wavelength or modality confusion.

**Potential experiment signals:** depression symptoms, response rate, headache, eyestrain, insomnia.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use only as bright-light mood-disorder context and adverse-effect boundary, not as direct PBM support.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
