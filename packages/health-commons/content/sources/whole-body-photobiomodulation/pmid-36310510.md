This source is included for **Skin/cosmetic large-area or full-body PBM sibling variant**.

**Findings:** This meta-analysis assessed 554 records and included 31 studies. The pooled acne vulgaris analysis found a statistically significant overall standardized mean difference of −2.42 (95% CI −2.64 to −2.15; I²=17%). Yellow LED and near-infrared device studies showed favorable direction for herpes simplex, psoriasis, and skin rejuvenation, with low to moderate heterogeneity reported for some of those analyses. In contrast, wound healing and atopic dermatitis analyses were highly heterogeneous (I²=85% and 90%), which the authors linked to publication and reporting limitations. The review concluded that LEDs are useful in dermatology and may have future cosmetic applications, but it remained a local-skin evidence synthesis rather than a whole-body exposure study.

**Why it matters:** It captures the strongest quantitative pooled effect in this batch while also preserving the heterogeneity that limits extrapolation.

**Potential experiment signals:** adverse events, effect-size reporting, heterogeneity, skin outcomes.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use only for background on dermatology effect sizes and study heterogeneity; do not treat the pooled skin outcomes as direct support for whole-body red/NIR exposure.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.
