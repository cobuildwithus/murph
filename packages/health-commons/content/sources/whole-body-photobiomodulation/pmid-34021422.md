This source is included for **Safety, contraindication, and population-boundary evidence**.

**Findings:** This retrospective single-center study followed 693 hematopoietic cell transplantation patients treated with PBMT for oral mucositis control and surveyed the oral cavity for secondary malignancies during a 15-year follow-up. The study reported no immediate adverse effects and no secondary malignancy associated with PBMT. Severe oral mucositis was infrequent, but when it occurred it was associated with worse overall survival. The paper is therefore a strong long-term oncology safety source, while still being a population mismatch for healthy-user whole-body protocols.

**Why it matters:** This is the best long-term empirical anchor in the batch for delayed oncology safety concerns such as secondary malignancy.

**Potential experiment signals:** immediate adverse events, secondary malignancy surveillance, oral mucositis severity, overall survival.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as long-term clinical-supervised safety evidence for oncology contexts; do not generalize it into direct safety proof for unsupervised whole-body wellness protocols.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.
