This source is included for **Skin/cosmetic large-area or full-body PBM sibling variant**.

**Findings:** This open-access multicenter trial enrolled 70 Asian participants aged 30–65 years with Fitzpatrick II–V skin and neck wrinkle scores of 2–4. Participants used a home LED neck device for 9 minutes per day, 5 times per week, for 60 sessions. The test device delivered 630 nm LED and 850 nm IRED output, while the sham device looked the same but delivered far lower active output. Investigator-rated Lemperle wrinkle improvement favored the study group at weeks 8, 12, and 16, and the baseline-corrected week-12 wrinkle score differed significantly between groups. GAIS favored the treatment group in investigator ratings but not in participant ratings, and repeated-measures analysis found a significant group difference only in investigator assessment. No significant thyroid-related complications were observed. The study was funded by the device manufacturer Y&J BIO, which is a design and interpretation caveat.

**Why it matters:** It is one of the better sham-controlled neck studies for retaining both the thyroid-safety null result and the mixed efficacy interpretation.

**Potential experiment signals:** neck wrinkle scores, investigator GAIS, participant GAIS, thyroid ultrasound.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as a safety-boundary and device-implementation source for adjacent neck exposure, while preserving the mixed efficacy interpretation and cosmetic population mismatch.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

## Skin Photobiomodulation Note

This source is included for **anatomy-specific sibling: neck, under-eye, or periocular evidence**.

**Findings:** The source reports 70 participants randomized to an active LED neck device or sham device. The active schedule was 9 minutes per day, 5 days per week, for 60 sessions. Extracted summaries report significantly higher proportions of improved Lemperle Wrinkle Scale scores at Week 12 and across Weeks 8, 12, and 16, while investigator GAIS separated from sham more clearly than participant GAIS. **Why it matters:** It is a relatively rigorous home-use LED trial in adjacent neck anatomy and includes safety monitoring around the thyroid region.

**Potential experiment signals:** Lemperle Wrinkle Scale, investigator GAIS, participant GAIS, thyroid ultrasound safety monitoring, adherence to home-use schedule.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as anatomy-specific context for home-use LED adherence, neck outcomes, and thyroid-adjacent safety monitoring; do not pool as direct facial-mask efficacy evidence.
