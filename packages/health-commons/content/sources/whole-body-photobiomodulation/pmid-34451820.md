This source is included for **Safety, contraindication, and population-boundary evidence**.

**Findings:** This review describes how drug-induced photosensitivity can arise through phototoxic or photoallergic mechanisms and notes that the triggering light can extend into visible wavelengths for some compounds. It is not a PBM or whole-body device study, so it cannot quantify protocol-specific risk. Its practical value is screening: it supports medication review and a more cautious stance in users with known photosensitizers or prior light-reactive skin history.

**Why it matters:** It provides the broader mechanistic context for why Murph should ask about photosensitizing medications instead of assuming red/NIR devices are risk-free for everyone.

**Potential experiment signals:** photosensitivity reactions, rash, burning, erythema, itching.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as background mechanism and screening context, not as direct evidence about whole-body PBM harms or benefits.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

## Skin Photobiomodulation Note

This source is included for **photosensitizing-medication and retinoid safety boundary**.

**Findings:** Review describes how photoexcited drugs can trigger oxidative stress, inflammation, and melanin-related changes that manifest as erythema, blisters, burning, itching, and hyperpigmentation.

**Why it matters:** Use for adverse-event symptom language and medication screening, with wavelength caveats.

**Potential experiment signals:** burning/itching stop rule, blistering stop rule, PIH after reaction.

**Protocol takeaway:** Drug photosensitivity mechanisms can produce inflammatory and pigmentary skin reactions after light exposure. Do not claim red/NIR PBM triggers these reactions without direct evidence.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.
