This source is included for **Safety, contraindication, and population-boundary evidence**.

**Findings:** Two phase I single-blind dose-escalation randomized controlled trials exposed healthy volunteers' forearms to LED red light three times weekly for three weeks. STARS 1 randomized 60 participants across all skin types to 160-640 J/cm^2, and STARS 2 enrolled 55 non-Hispanic Caucasian participants at 480-640 J/cm^2. There were no serious adverse events, but predefined dose-limiting toxicities occurred at 480 J/cm^2 in STARS 1 and at 640 J/cm^2 in STARS 2, with blistering and prolonged erythema alongside milder transient erythema and hyperpigmentation. The authors concluded that safe upper bounds in these trials were 320 J/cm^2 for skin of color and 480 J/cm^2 for non-Hispanic Caucasian participants.

**Why it matters:** This is the strongest human safety anchor in the batch for red-light skin exposure and the clearest reminder that dose tolerance can vary across populations.

**Potential experiment signals:** erythema, hyperpigmentation, blistering, delayed skin recovery, session tolerability.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as same-mechanism human skin-safety evidence: high-fluence red light can produce dose-limiting skin reactions, so whole-body protocols should stay conservative and monitor skin response.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

## Skin Photobiomodulation Note

This source is included for **red-light skin safety and dose-escalation boundary**.

**Findings:** Dose-limiting adverse events occurred at 480 J/cm² in STARS 1 (n=1) and 640 J/cm² in STARS 2 (n=2). The authors concluded LED-RL was safe up to 320 J/cm² for skin of color and 480 J/cm² for non-Hispanic Caucasian individuals; no serious adverse events were reported.

**Why it matters:** Use as a safety-boundary source for dose ceilings, skin-of-color caution, and monitoring for erythema, hyperpigmentation, or blistering—not as anti-aging efficacy evidence.

**Potential experiment signals:** post-session erythema log, hyperpigmentation/darkening log, stop rule for blistering or prolonged redness, skin-type-specific dose ceiling.

**Protocol takeaway:** High-fluence red LED safety differed by skin phenotype, with dose-limiting reactions at very high fluences. Forearm safety results do not prove facial texture or wrinkle improvement.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

### Extraction notes

- **Population:** Healthy adults with normal forearm skin; STARS 1 included all skin types and STARS 2 enrolled non-Hispanic Caucasian participants.
- **Intervention/exposure:** High-fluence 633 nm ± 15 nm LED red-light exposure to the forearm, three times weekly for three weeks; STARS 1 tested 160-640 J/cm² and STARS 2 tested 480-640 J/cm².
- **Comparator/control:** Mock irradiation control.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Nine irradiation visits over three weeks, with safety follow-up for predefined dose-limiting toxicities.
- **Endpoints:** Primary endpoint was incidence of adverse events and maximum tolerated dose; monitored erythema, hyperpigmentation, blistering/prolonged erythema, warmth, pain, and other local reactions.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** Transient erythema and hyperpigmentation were mild; dose-limiting blistering/prolonged erythema occurred at high fluences; no serious adverse events reported.
- **Limitations:** Safety trial on forearm skin, not a facial photoaging efficacy trial.; Short treatment and follow-up window for consumer home-device safety.; High-fluence protocol does not directly establish daily low-dose mask or panel dosing.
- **Population mismatch/directness:** Direct for red LED skin safety and skin-type dose boundaries; indirect for home facial photoaging outcomes.
- **Artifact/rights status:** open_access.
