This source is included for **Dose, device, and implementation reporting**.

**Findings:** This 2025 consensus combined a systematic Embase and MEDLINE review with a Delphi process involving 21 experts and reached 38 consensus statements on PBM core principles, safety, and efficacy. It states that PBM uses nonionizing light sources including lasers, LEDs, and broadband light, with visible 400-700 nm and near-infrared 700-1100 nm ranges, and that red 600-700 nm plus near-infrared 780-1100 nm are the most commonly used wavelengths. It also states that PBM is generally safe for adults when applied as directed, that lower fluences are generally associated with stimulation while higher fluences are associated with inhibition, and that fluence, distance, wavelength, irradiation time, beam area or spot size, treatment frequency, and treatment duration should all be measured and reported. Importantly for whole-body pain claims, the musculoskeletal consensus was based on low-quality level IV evidence rather than strong whole-body trial evidence.

**Why it matters:** This is the cleanest source in the batch for conservative safety language and for clarifying that not all PBM indications are on equal evidence footing.

**Potential experiment signals:** adverse events, eye comfort, skin tolerance, wavelength, fluence, treatment frequency and duration.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as a safety and reporting boundary source. It should constrain wording and data fields, not act as a substitute for direct whole-body efficacy evidence.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

## Skin Photobiomodulation Note

This source is included for **review or consensus context for dermatology PBM**.

**Findings:** The consensus used a systematic review, two Delphi rounds, and meetings with a 21-member international multidisciplinary panel. It supports general adult PBM safety framing and notes that red-light PBM was not considered DNA-damaging in the consensus context, while cosmetic photoaging was not a principal endorsed indication.

**Why it matters:** Consensus guidance helps define safety and clinical-boundary language for PBM protocols.

**Potential experiment signals:** adult safety, DNA-damage concern, clinical indication, consensus strength, adverse-event monitoring.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as a safety/context anchor; do not cite it as direct proof that red/NIR improves facial texture or photoaging.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
