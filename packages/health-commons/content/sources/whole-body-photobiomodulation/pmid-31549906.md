This source is included for **Adjacent-variant and exclusion boundary anchors**.

**Findings:** The review screened 871 records and included nine publications, with meta-analysis performed on six full-text publications in young healthy adults. The pooled estimate reported improved cognition with standardized mean difference 0.833 (95% CI 0.458 to 1.209) across 14 comparisons. At the same time, the authors reported heterogeneity, modest overall study quality, and funnel-plot or statistical signals consistent with publication bias. Population mismatch remains clear: this is transcranial head-only PBM in cognition studies, not whole-body exposure.

**Why it matters:** It is the main reason cognition claims need a scope boundary in a whole-body PBM protocol page.

**Potential experiment signals:** cognitive performance, attention, executive function, reaction time.

**Protocol takeaway:** Keep the result as transcranial cognition context only. It should not be used as direct evidence that whole-body red/NIR exposure improves cognitive performance.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
