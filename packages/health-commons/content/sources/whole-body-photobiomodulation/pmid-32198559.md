This source is included for **Safety, contraindication, and population-boundary evidence**.

**Findings:** This short commentary responded to tumor-response concerns by arguing that in vivo and clinical studies with follow-up are more informative than isolated in vitro findings when assessing PBMT safety in cancer patients. It did not report new participants or new adverse-event data. Its value is interpretive: it helps set an evidence-hierarchy boundary, but it cannot by itself justify safety claims for whole-body red/NIR exposure.

**Why it matters:** It helps explain why Murph should weight human and animal follow-up data more heavily than isolated cell-culture concerns when discussing cancer-related safety questions.

**Potential experiment signals:** tumor progression concerns, recurrence surveillance, evidence-quality weighting.

**Protocol takeaway:** Treat as background commentary on evidence hierarchy, not as an independent safety or efficacy study.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.
