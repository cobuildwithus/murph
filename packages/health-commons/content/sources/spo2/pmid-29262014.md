This clinical review is the main general reference for how pulse oximetry works and why it can be wrong.

For Murph, the most important takeaway is that SpO₂ should be interpreted as an estimate and as a trend signal. It is useful because it is noninvasive and widely available, but it can be distorted by low perfusion, skin pigmentation, nail polish, movement, ambient light, dyshemoglobins, and other measurement conditions.
