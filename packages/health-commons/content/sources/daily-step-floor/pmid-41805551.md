This source is included for **direct_step_floor_trials**.

**Findings:**

- `finding:pmid-41805551-week12-steps` — The published abstract reported 156 randomized participants, 124 completers, and valid step data for 117; an unadjusted mixed model showed higher week-12 step change in the intervention group (difference 579 steps/day, 95% CI 36 to 1,123; P=.04).
- `finding:pmid-41805551-secondary-null` — No significant differences emerged for total MET-minutes per week (difference 646, 95% CI -12 to 1,303; P=.054) or walking at least 30 minutes/day (OR 1.56, 95% CI 0.63 to 3.90; P=.33) in the published abstract.

**Why it matters:** Current same-mechanism evidence on peer support and daily step goals in older adults, with modest effect and null secondary activity outcomes.

**Potential experiment signals:**

- daily steps
- walking ≥30 minutes/day
- MET-minutes/week
- app engagement
- retention

**Protocol takeaway:** Digital peer support modestly improved step counts at week 12 in older adults, but secondary physical-activity outcomes were not clearly different.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction boundaries

- **Directness:** `same_mechanism`.
- **Population:** Community-dwelling Japanese adults aged 60 years or older who could walk independently and used smartphones
- **Intervention/exposure:** Baseline smartphone lecture plus two sessions using a digital peer support app with daily step goals, peer sharing, and group encouragement.
- **Comparator/control:** Baseline smartphone lecture plus two standard follow-up smartphone lectures.
- **Duration/follow-up:** 12 weeks
- **Endpoints extracted:** weekly average daily step count, MET-minutes/week, walking at least 30 minutes/day, smartphone use.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse-event signal was extracted from the accessible source record; absence of extracted adverse events should not be interpreted as proof of no adverse events unless the finding explicitly says so.
- **Limitations:** Cluster trial in older Japanese smartphone users; missing step data ranged from 5.1% to 29.1%; effect was modest and secondary activity outcomes were null/near-null.
- **Population mismatch:** Older Japanese adults who attended smartphone lectures; not general adults.
- **Artifact/rights boundary:** `open_access`; no copyrighted PDF is stored in Git in this draft.
