This source is included for **cardiometabolic_fitness_endpoints**.

**Findings:** An RCT in sedentary older adults with mild/moderate hypertension compared pedometer step goals with or without lottery-style reinforcement. Reinforcement improved goal attainment and reported health-index improvements, but the incentive component makes it a direct step-goal variant rather than plain Daily Step Floor evidence.

**Why it matters:** Shows that behavioral reinforcement can materially affect adherence to a step goal, which is important when interpreting step-floor effects.

**Potential experiment signals:** biomarker:daily-steps, biomarker:morning-blood-pressure, biomarker:body-weight, biomarker:estimated-vo2max.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as supportive but qualified direct step-goal evidence: adherence architecture may matter as much as the target.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

**Directness boundary:** `direct_protocol`. This source should not be promoted beyond that scope in the Daily Step Floor protocol.

**Safety/adverse events:** No adverse-event signal extracted from accessible abstract text.

**Limitations and population mismatch:** Small sample and incentive/reinforcement design; not an isolated test of a simple step floor. Population mismatch: Sedentary older adults with hypertension; results may not generalize to younger, normotensive, or already-active users.
