This source is included for **sedentary_activity_pattern**.

**Findings:** Both TV-commercial stepping and walking increased daily steps over 6 months, while TV viewing and diet decreased; there was no clear between-group step-count difference in the accessible abstract.

**Why it matters:** Shows that sedentary time can be turned into stepping opportunities, but the tested behavior was TV-commercial stepping rather than a standing daily minimum.

**Potential experiment signals:** daily step count, TV viewing, anthropometrics, body fat, waist circumference, hip circumference.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context-only evidence that prompts embedded in sedentary routines may increase steps; do not treat it as proof that a daily step floor works.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Sedentary overweight adults in a randomized pilot sedentary-behavior substitution study.
- **Intervention/exposure:** TV-commercial stepping during approximately 90 minutes/day of television viewing versus walking 30 minutes/day.
- **Comparator/control:** Active walking comparator; both arms received monthly contact and study materials.
- **Duration/follow-up:** 6 months
- **Endpoints:** daily step count, TV viewing, anthropometrics, body fat, waist circumference, hip circumference
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Both TV-commercial stepping and walking increased daily steps over 6 months, while TV viewing and diet decreased; there was no clear between-group step-count difference in the accessible abstract.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse events or safety outcomes were reported in the accessible extract.
- **Limitations:** Pilot sample with attrition (47 of 58 retained in accessible abstract).; Active comparator rather than no-treatment control.; Sedentary replacement during TV viewing is not the same as a daily step floor.
- **Population mismatch:** Adjacent sedentary-replacement RCT in overweight adults, not a Daily Step Floor protocol.
- **Artifact candidates and rights status:** open_access; no copyrighted PDF is included in this draft artifact package.
