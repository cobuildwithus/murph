This source is included for **dose_response_cut_points**.

**Findings:** Among 2110 middle-aged adults, participants taking at least 7000 steps/day had about 50% to 70% lower mortality risk than those taking fewer than 7000 steps/day; taking ≥10,000 steps/day or higher step intensity was not associated with additional mortality reduction in this cohort.

**Why it matters:** Context for 7000-step threshold and possible plateau below 10,000.

**Potential experiment signals:** daily step count, days meeting step floor, weekly mean steps, all-cause mortality, step intensity.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context for attainable floor targets with mortality caveats.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Black and White middle-aged adults in CARDIA; mean age about 45 years.
- **Exposure/intervention:** Accelerometer-measured daily steps, categorized as <7000, 7000–9999, and ≥10,000 steps/day.
- **Comparator/control:** Low step category <7000 steps/day.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Mean follow-up 10.8 years.
- **Endpoints:** all-cause mortality, daily step count, step intensity
- **Safety/adverse events:** No adverse-event signal specific to a step-floor intervention was extracted from this source in this batch.
- **Limitations:** Small number of deaths, observational design, and cohort-specific age/race composition.
- **Population mismatch/directness:** Middle-aged CARDIA cohort associations are not a Daily Step Floor intervention.
- **Boundary:** This source is observational, review-based, registry-only, or otherwise adjacent unless explicitly noted; it must not be promoted into direct Daily Step Floor protocol evidence.
