This source is included for **cadence_intensity_bouts**.

**Findings:** Narrative review supports >=100 steps/min as a practical adult heuristic for moderate-intensity walking, while emphasizing context and individual variability.

**Why it matters:** Gives the protocol a simple optional explanation of what “brisk” might mean without changing the protocol target.

**Potential experiment signals:** walking_cadence, activity_intensity_classification, mvpa_minutes.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use >=100 steps/min only as optional cadence context for adults; do not make cadence part of the base Daily Step Floor.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Adult walking cadence calibration and epidemiologic literature; no single participant sample because this is a review.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Walking cadence, especially around 100 steps/min, as an estimate of moderate-intensity ambulatory activity.
- **Comparator/control:** Not applicable; narrative synthesis.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Narrative review; source studies varied.
- **Endpoints:** Moderate-intensity walking; cadence-based intensity classification; public-health messaging.
- **Effect estimates or direction:** The review concluded that >=100 steps/min is a reasonable heuristic for absolutely-defined moderate-intensity walking in adults during sustained rhythmic ambulation.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse events or intervention safety outcomes were reported.
- **Limitations:** Narrative review; thresholds come largely from calibration settings and may not individualize for age, body size, fitness, terrain, assistive devices, or clinical status.
- **Population mismatch:** Same-mechanism intensity context, not direct Daily Step Floor intervention evidence.
