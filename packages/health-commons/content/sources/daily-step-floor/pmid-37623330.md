This source is included for **baseline_plus_ramp_trials**.

**Findings:** A 20-week single-arm e-health pilot in 21 sedentary older adults with hypertension targeted +3000 steps/day on 5 days/week and reported reduced blood pressure, but the uncontrolled design limits causal claims.

**Why it matters:** Modern direct +3000-step/day e-health implementation source with major single-arm limitations.

**Potential experiment signals:** steps/day, home blood pressure, five-days-per-week completion.

**Protocol takeaway:** A +3000/day target may be feasible for hypertensive older adults, but efficacy claims need controlled trials.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

## Extracted study details

- **Population:** Sedentary older adults with hypertension, mean age about 73 years.
- **Intervention or exposure:** E-health lifestyle walking intervention targeting an additional 3000 steps/day on 5 days/week.
- **Comparator or control:** No control group.
- **Duration or follow-up:** 20 weeks.
- **Endpoints:** steps/day, systolic blood pressure, diastolic blood pressure, self-reported step/BP data
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Accessible article and snippets report blood-pressure reduction during the 20-week +3000-step/day intervention; exact effect tables were not extracted here. Secondary snippets report approximate systolic BP reductions of 5 mm Hg at 10 weeks and 7 mm Hg at 20 weeks, but those values should be verified in full text before protocol synthesis.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** Adverse-event reporting was not extracted from accessible records.
- **Limitations:** Single-arm pilot, small sample, self-reported/objectively assessed home data, no causal control.
- **Population mismatch:** Older adults with hypertension; not general population.
- **Directness to Daily Step Floor:** `direct_protocol`
