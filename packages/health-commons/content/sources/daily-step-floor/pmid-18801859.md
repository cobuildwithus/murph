This source is included for **safety_special_populations**.

**Findings:** In the Feet First RCT of 79 people with diabetes and peripheral neuropathy, a supervised strengthening, balance, and graduated walking program did not significantly increase foot-ulcer incidence compared with control.

**Why it matters:** Directly tests whether a supervised weight-bearing walking/balance program increased ulcer incidence in diabetic peripheral neuropathy.

**Potential experiment signals:** daily-step-count, bout-related steps, foot-ulcer incidence, falls, adverse-events.

**Protocol takeaway:** Daily Step Floor should not generalize this to unsupervised neuropathy; if used, foot-risk users need assessment, counseling, and monitoring.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

**Directness boundary:** clinical_supervised adjacent walking intervention. Do not promote this source into direct Daily Step Floor claims beyond the stated claim-use boundary.

**Safety/adverse events:** Foot ulcer incidence did not significantly differ, suggesting supervised weight-bearing activity can be considered after adequate assessment and counseling.

**Limitations and mismatch:** Special population with neuropathy; small trial; intervention included balance/strength and foot-care components, not a simple daily step floor. Diabetes peripheral neuropathy; safety boundary only.
