This source is included for **cardiometabolic_fitness_endpoints**.

**Findings:** A systematic review/meta-analysis found pedometer walking interventions reduced SBP and DBP overall, but achieving 10,000 steps/day did not significantly distinguish BP change. The key extracted signal is increasing step count rather than treating 10,000 as a guaranteed BP threshold.

**Why it matters:** Directly informs BP expectations and avoids overstating the 10,000-step number as a threshold.

**Potential experiment signals:** biomarker:daily-steps, biomarker:morning-blood-pressure.

**Protocol takeaway:** Frame BP effects around step-count increases and sustained walking, not a categorical 10,000-step guarantee.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness boundary:** `direct_protocol`. This source should not be promoted beyond that scope in the Daily Step Floor protocol.

**Safety/adverse events:** No adverse-event signal extracted from accessible abstract text.

**Limitations and population mismatch:** Meta-analysis of heterogeneous trials; 10,000-step threshold not confirmed as a distinct BP cutoff. Population mismatch: Healthy adults and trial interventions may not map to hypertensive, diabetic, older, or mobility-limited users.
