This source is included for **direct_step_floor_trials**.

**Findings:**

- `finding:pmid-27658677-young-adult-app-steps` — The intervention arm had a significant time-by-group interaction (P=.04) and an additional roughly 2,000 steps/day increase compared with control; both groups increased walking.

**Why it matters:** Same-mechanism evidence for app-based step feedback and translating time goals into step goals.

**Potential experiment signals:**

- daily steps
- goal feedback engagement
- guideline attainment

**Protocol takeaway:** Step-count feedback in a smartphone app can increase walking in young adults, but the sample and pilot design are not a direct general Daily Step Floor test.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction boundaries

- **Directness:** `same_mechanism`.
- **Population:** Young adults aged 17-26 years with mobile phones
- **Intervention/exposure:** Smartphone app recording steps plus equivalent step goal and feedback.
- **Comparator/control:** Smartphone app with a 30-minutes/day goal but without equivalent step-count goal feedback.
- **Duration/follow-up:** 5 weeks
- **Endpoints extracted:** daily steps, walking behavior, physical activity guideline attainment.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse-event signal was extracted from the accessible source record; absence of extracted adverse events should not be interpreted as proof of no adverse events unless the finding explicitly says so.
- **Limitations:** Pilot trial; young adults; short duration; comparator also increased activity.
- **Population mismatch:** Young adult smartphone users only.
- **Artifact/rights boundary:** `open_access`; no copyrighted PDF is stored in Git in this draft.
