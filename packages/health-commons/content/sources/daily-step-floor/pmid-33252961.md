This source is included for **baseline_plus_ramp_trials**.

**Findings:** A 20-person N-of-1 series manipulated daily step-goal difficulty within person to study the tradeoff between activity increases and goal achievement; it is goal-mechanics evidence rather than a standard efficacy trial.

**Why it matters:** Reusable source for adaptive goal-difficulty tuning and the achievement-vs-difficulty tradeoff.

**Potential experiment signals:** goal difficulty multiplier, steps/day, goal achievement ratio, valid assessment days.

**Protocol takeaway:** Floor difficulty should be individualized and monitored as a tradeoff between motivating more steps and preserving achievement.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extracted study details

- **Population:** Inactive overweight adults; mostly female, mean BMI about 33.8 and baseline mean around 4863 steps/day.
- **Intervention or exposure:** Daily step goals experimentally manipulated from each participant’s baseline median steps multiplied by pseudo-random factors from 1 to 2.6.
- **Comparator or control:** Within-person variation in prescribed goal difficulty.
- **Duration or follow-up:** 2-week baseline plus 80-day intervention phase.
- **Endpoints:** daily steps, goal difficulty, goal achievement
- **Effect estimates or direction:** The study examined person-specific links between goal difficulty, activity, and goal achievement; accessible snippets indicate high adherence to step assessment but did not provide a single pooled efficacy estimate for Daily Step Floor.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** Adverse-event reporting was not extracted.
- **Limitations:** N-of-1 series, overweight mostly female participants, and not a conventional between-person efficacy trial.
- **Population mismatch:** Inactive overweight adults; highly individualized within-person design.
