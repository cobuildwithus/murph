This source is included for **sedentary_activity_pattern**.

**Findings:** The harmonised meta-analysis reported dose-response associations between accelerometer-measured activity/sedentary time and all-cause mortality; this is observational context, not intervention evidence.

**Why it matters:** Shows why activity and sedentary exposure matter, while preserving the observational boundary.

**Potential experiment signals:** sedentary time, light physical activity, moderate-to-vigorous physical activity, all-cause mortality.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context-only mortality/epidemiology evidence, not as proof of protocol benefit.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Middle-aged and older adults in accelerometer cohorts included in a harmonised mortality meta-analysis.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Observed accelerometer-measured physical activity and sedentary time exposures.
- **Comparator/control:** Lower or higher dose categories of physical activity and sedentary time.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Follow-up varied by cohort; not extracted in this pass.
- **Endpoints:** sedentary time, light physical activity, moderate-to-vigorous physical activity, all-cause mortality
- **Effect estimates or direction:** The harmonised meta-analysis reported dose-response associations between accelerometer-measured activity/sedentary time and all-cause mortality; this is observational context, not intervention evidence.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** Mortality was an outcome; no intervention adverse events apply.
- **Limitations:** Observational cohorts cannot establish causal effects of a step-floor intervention.; Participant count and detailed dose estimates were not extracted in this pass.; Accelerometer cut-points and populations may not map to consumer step trackers.
- **Population mismatch:** Observational dose-response context; not direct protocol evidence.
