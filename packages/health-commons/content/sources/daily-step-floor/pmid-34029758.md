This source is included for **dose_response_cut_points**.

**Findings:** Among 768 older adults, each additional 1000 steps/day was associated with lower hospitalization and mortality risk in adjusted models; high step volume plus high intensity was associated with lower hospitalization and mortality versus low volume plus low intensity.

**Why it matters:** Context for hospitalization and mortality signals in older adults.

**Potential experiment signals:** daily step count, days meeting step floor, weekly mean steps, hospitalization, all-cause mortality, step cadence.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as adjacent context, noting intensity/volume correlation.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Community-dwelling Spanish older adults from the Toledo Study for Healthy Aging; mean age 78.8 years.
- **Exposure/intervention:** Hip accelerometer steps/day and average step cadence.
- **Comparator/control:** Higher versus lower step volume and intensity; joint high-volume/high-intensity groups.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Mean follow-up 3.1 years for hospitalization and 5.7 years for mortality.
- **Endpoints:** hospitalization, all-cause mortality, daily step count, step cadence
- **Safety/adverse events:** No adverse-event signal specific to a step-floor intervention was extracted from this source in this batch.
- **Limitations:** Observational cohort; step volume and intensity highly correlated; single baseline device window.
- **Population mismatch/directness:** Older Spanish cohort and joint volume/intensity categories are not a Daily Step Floor trial.
- **Boundary:** This source is observational, review-based, registry-only, or otherwise adjacent unless explicitly noted; it must not be promoted into direct Daily Step Floor protocol evidence.
