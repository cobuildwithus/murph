This source is included for **cardiometabolic_fitness_endpoints**.

**Findings:** A 2×2 factorial RCT in older adults with hypertension and diabetes found the largest 12-month BP reductions when pedometer use was combined with home BP monitoring; pedometer-only lowered DBP and had a near-null/confidence-crossing SBP estimate. It is adjacent because it tests device self-monitoring in a clinical combination, not a simple step floor.

**Why it matters:** Useful for BP endpoints and for the idea that step tracking may pair with home BP monitoring, but the strongest result was the combination arm.

**Potential experiment signals:** biomarker:daily-steps, biomarker:morning-blood-pressure.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as adjacent clinical context for BP monitoring; do not promote the combination result as evidence for a stand-alone step floor.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness boundary:** `adjacent_variant`. This source should not be promoted beyond that scope in the Daily Step Floor protocol.

**Safety/adverse events:** No adverse-event signal extracted from accessible abstract text.

**Limitations and population mismatch:** Clinical older diabetes/hypertension population and self-monitoring combination design; pedometer-only SBP CI crossed zero. Population mismatch: Older hypertensive adults with diabetes in one setting; not general-population Daily Step Floor evidence.
