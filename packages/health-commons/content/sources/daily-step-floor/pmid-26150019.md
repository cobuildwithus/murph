This source is included for **baseline_plus_ramp_trials**.

**Findings:** This randomized study suggests low goals may inhibit walking for some people, while higher difficulty can increase activity but reduce achievement; 94 were randomized and 53 completed.

**Why it matters:** Important mixed boundary evidence for avoiding floors that are too low or too high.

**Potential experiment signals:** goal difficulty, goal achievement, steps/day, dropout.

**Protocol takeaway:** Set floors in an attainable-but-challenging zone rather than minimizing difficulty or defaulting high.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extracted study details

- **Population:** Self-selected able-bodied students, faculty, and staff at a small rural university; 53 completed.
- **Intervention or exposure:** Incentivized pedometer-monitored walking goals of varying levels.
- **Comparator or control:** Different randomized low/high or easier/harder step-count goals.
- **Duration or follow-up:** Duration not extracted from accessible records.
- **Endpoints:** steps/day, goal difficulty, goal achievement
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Accessible abstract reports 94 randomized and 53 completed. Prior extraction snippets indicate goal difficulty was positively associated with physical activity for many participants and negatively associated with goal achievement, suggesting an optimal goal-setting zone.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** Adverse-event reporting was not extracted.
- **Limitations:** High attrition, self-selected able-bodied university sample, and detailed effect estimates not fully extracted.
- **Population mismatch:** Able-bodied university volunteers; not low-active clinical or general population.
