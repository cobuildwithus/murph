This source is included for **cadence_intensity_bouts**.

**Findings:** Older-adult relative-intensity calibration suggests cadence thresholds above the simple 100 spm heuristic may better represent relative moderate/vigorous effort.

**Why it matters:** Highlights that older adults’ relative effort may require different cadence interpretation than absolute MET thresholds.

**Potential experiment signals:** walking_cadence, relative_intensity, heart_rate, rating_of_perceived_exertion.

**Protocol takeaway:** Do not use one universal cadence target as a protocol rule; relative intensity should remain optional and individualized.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Ostensibly healthy adults aged 61-85 years in treadmill relative-intensity calibration.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Cadence during treadmill walking, evaluated against relative intensity indicators.
- **Comparator/control:** Relative moderate and vigorous intensity indicators based on heart rate and perceived exertion.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Single laboratory protocol with up to nine 5-minute treadmill bouts.
- **Endpoints:** Cadence thresholds for relatively-defined moderate and vigorous intensity in older adults.
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Accessible summaries report heuristic moderate thresholds around >=105 spm and vigorous thresholds around >=115 or >=120 spm depending on sensitivity/specificity trade-offs.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse events or safety outcomes were reported in accessible summaries.
- **Limitations:** Ostensibly healthy lab sample; relative-intensity definitions; threshold choice depends on classification trade-offs; not direct step-floor efficacy evidence.
- **Population mismatch:** Context-only same-mechanism calibration; not a Daily Step Floor intervention.
