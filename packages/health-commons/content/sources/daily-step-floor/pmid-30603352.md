This source is included for **cardiometabolic_fitness_endpoints**.

**Findings:** A 12-week quasi-experimental pedometer walking study in overweight Thai adults asked participants to accumulate at least 10,000 steps/day. Among 30 achievers, SBP and blood glucose decreased significantly, but the small non-randomized design and achiever analysis limit causal strength.

**Why it matters:** Directly tests a 10,000-steps/day accumulation target with BP and glucose endpoints, but with weak causal design.

**Potential experiment signals:** biomarker:daily-steps, biomarker:morning-blood-pressure, biomarker:fasting-glucose.

**Protocol takeaway:** Can support cautious hypothesis language for BP/glucose monitoring, not a strong causal claim.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

**Directness boundary:** `direct_protocol`. This source should not be promoted beyond that scope in the Daily Step Floor protocol.

**Safety/adverse events:** No adverse-event signal extracted from accessible abstract text.

**Limitations and population mismatch:** Small quasi-experimental design, achiever-only result for 30 of 35 participants, and no extracted randomized control arm. Population mismatch: Sedentary overweight Thai adults; not necessarily general, older, normoweight, diabetic, or hypertensive users.
