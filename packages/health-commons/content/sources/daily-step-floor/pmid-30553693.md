This source is included for **direct_step_floor_trials**.

**Findings:**

- `finding:pmid-30553693-participation-effect` — Participation was 5.94% in the personal incentive group (OR 1.96, 95% CI 1.55 to 2.49) and 4.98% in the charity incentive group (OR 1.59, 95% CI 1.25 to 2.01) versus 3.23% in control.
- `finding:pmid-30553693-behavior-limited` — At program start, charity incentives increased the chance of walking more than 10,000 steps/day (OR 1.68, 95% CI 1.23 to 2.30), but the effect dissipated after 3 months; steps/day and non-usage attrition did not differ significantly between groups.

**Why it matters:** Adjacent population-scale 10,000-step threshold trial that separates participation effects from behavior-change effects.

**Potential experiment signals:**

- program participation
- daily steps
- 10,000-step days
- attrition

**Protocol takeaway:** Small incentives increased participation but did not produce durable behavior change; charity incentives briefly improved 10,000-step probability.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction boundaries

- **Directness:** `adjacent_variant`.
- **Population:** German-speaking adult insureds of a Swiss health insurer enrolled in complementary insurance plans and registered online
- **Intervention/exposure:** Small personal or charity financial incentives for monthly average step thresholds above 10,000 or 7,500 steps/day.
- **Comparator/control:** No incentive control during the first 3 months.
- **Duration/follow-up:** 6-month pilot program; primary behavior analyses over first 3 months
- **Endpoints extracted:** participation rate, daily steps, days above 10,000 steps, non-usage attrition.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse-event signal was extracted from the accessible source record; absence of extracted adverse events should not be interpreted as proof of no adverse events unless the finding explicitly says so.
- **Limitations:** Clustered by Swiss canton; incentive values were small and monthly; participants were insureds with online accounts and often devices; not a simple no-incentive floor.
- **Population mismatch:** Swiss insureds in a health-insurer program; self-selection into participation was a primary issue.
