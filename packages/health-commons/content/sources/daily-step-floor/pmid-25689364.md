This source is included for **baseline_plus_ramp_trials**.

**Findings:** PACE-Lift increased steps and MVPA in 60- to 75-year-old primary care adults at 3 and 12 months, with similar adverse events and pain versus control.

**Why it matters:** Direct, higher-quality evidence for sustained pedometer-based walking gains and safety monitoring in older adults.

**Potential experiment signals:** steps/day, MVPA bout minutes, pain, adverse events, mood.

**Protocol takeaway:** A supported pedometer walking plan can sustain step gains for 12 months in older adults, with adverse-event monitoring.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

## Extracted study details

- **Population:** Primary care adults aged 60 to 75 years.
- **Intervention or exposure:** Nurse-delivered walking intervention with pedometer step-count feedback, accelerometer intensity feedback, and four physical-activity consultations.
- **Comparator or control:** Usual-care control.
- **Duration or follow-up:** 3-month intervention with 12-month follow-up.
- **Endpoints:** steps/day, MVPA bouts, anthropometry, mood, pain, adverse events
- **Effect estimates or direction:** At 3 months, intervention participants increased by about +1037 steps/day versus control (95% CI 513 to 1560) and +63 min/week of MVPA bouts; at 12 months, about +609 steps/day (95% CI 104 to 1115) and +40 min/week persisted. Adverse events, anthropometry, mood, and pain were similar between groups.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** Adverse events and pain were similar between groups in accessible results.
- **Limitations:** Relatively affluent/non-diverse sample and no significant changes in several health outcomes.
- **Population mismatch:** Older primary care adults; may not generalize to younger populations or high-risk groups.
