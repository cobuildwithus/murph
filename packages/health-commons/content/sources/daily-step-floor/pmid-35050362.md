This source is included for **dose_response_cut_points**.

**Findings:** In OPACH older women, more steps/day were associated with lower incident diabetes risk; confounder-adjusted models reported a lower hazard per 2000 steps/day, with attenuation after BMI adjustment, and moderate-to-vigorous-intensity steps appeared most strongly associated.

**Why it matters:** Adds metabolic-risk context for older women.

**Potential experiment signals:** daily step count, days meeting step floor, weekly mean steps, incident diabetes, step intensity.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context and preserve attenuation after BMI adjustment.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Older women in the Objective Physical Activity and Cardiovascular Health Study.
- **Exposure/intervention:** Accelerometer-derived daily steps and step intensity.
- **Comparator/control:** Per-2000-step increments and intensity categories.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Prospective follow-up for incident diabetes; exact duration varied in cohort reporting.
- **Endpoints:** incident diabetes, daily step count, step intensity
- **Safety/adverse events:** No adverse-event signal specific to a step-floor intervention was extracted from this source in this batch.
- **Limitations:** Observational women-only cohort; BMI and adiposity may mediate or confound associations; not randomized.
- **Population mismatch/directness:** Diabetes outcome in older women is adjacent to general step-floor behavior.
- **Boundary:** This source is observational, review-based, registry-only, or otherwise adjacent unless explicitly noted; it must not be promoted into direct Daily Step Floor protocol evidence.
