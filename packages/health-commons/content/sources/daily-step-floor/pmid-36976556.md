This source is included for **cadence_intensity_bouts**.

**Findings:** NHANES cohort analysis found lower mortality risk among adults who achieved at least 8000 steps on some or most days of the week versus none.

**Why it matters:** Provides context that frequency of higher-step days may matter, but does not prescribe cadence or bouts.

**Potential experiment signals:** daily_step_count, weekly_step_floor_days, all_cause_mortality, cardiovascular_mortality.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as observational context for consistency of step-floor days; do not claim causality.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** US adults in NHANES 2005-2006 with accelerometer-measured steps and mortality follow-up.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Number of days per week with at least 8000 steps.
- **Comparator/control:** 0 days per week with at least 8000 steps compared with 1-2 days and 3-7 days.
- **Duration/follow-up:** One week of accelerometer monitoring; mortality follow-up through December 31, 2019.
- **Endpoints:** All-cause mortality; cardiovascular mortality.
- **Effect estimates or direction:** The study reported lower 10-year all-cause and cardiovascular mortality risk among adults taking at least 8000 steps on 1-2 days/week or 3-7 days/week compared with 0 days/week, with a plateau around 3 days/week.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse events or intervention safety outcomes were reported.
- **Limitations:** Observational cohort; weekly 8000-step-day pattern rather than a daily step-floor intervention; residual confounding possible.
- **Population mismatch:** Adjacent observational step-pattern evidence, not direct causal Daily Step Floor evidence.
