This source is included for **dose_response_cut_points**.

**Findings:** In 6042 Fitbit-linked participants, higher step-count volumes were associated with lower risk across several chronic diseases; public summaries reported inverse linear patterns for obesity, sleep apnea, GERD, and major depressive disorder, and nonlinear patterns for diabetes and hypertension with limited additional reductions above roughly 8000–9000 steps/day.

**Why it matters:** Digital-health context for using consumer step data in Murph-like settings.

**Potential experiment signals:** daily step count, days meeting step floor, weekly mean steps, obesity, sleep apnea, gastroesophageal reflux disease, major depressive disorder.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context for wearable-derived chronic-disease signals only.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** All of Us Research Program participants with Fitbit step data linked to electronic health records.
- **Exposure/intervention:** Step counts over time captured by participants’ own Fitbit devices.
- **Comparator/control:** Lower versus higher habitual step-count volumes.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Longitudinal Fitbit/EHR follow-up; exact monitoring duration varied by participant.
- **Endpoints:** obesity, sleep apnea, gastroesophageal reflux disease, major depressive disorder, diabetes, hypertension, daily step count
- **Safety/adverse events:** No adverse-event signal specific to a step-floor intervention was extracted from this source in this batch.
- **Limitations:** Volunteer wearable cohort, EHR ascertainment, confounding by health status and device use, and observational design.
- **Population mismatch/directness:** Consumer-wearable cohort is not a randomized step-floor protocol.
- **Boundary:** This source is observational, review-based, registry-only, or otherwise adjacent unless explicitly noted; it must not be promoted into direct Daily Step Floor protocol evidence.
