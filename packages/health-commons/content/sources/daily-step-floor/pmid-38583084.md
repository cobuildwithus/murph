This source is included for **baseline_plus_ramp_trials**.

**Findings:** BE ACTIVE showed gamification, financial incentives, and especially combined interventions increased steps more than control in high cardiovascular-risk adults, using personalized baseline-derived goals.

**Why it matters:** Large modern adjacent trial with baseline-derived goals but strong non-floor components.

**Potential experiment signals:** steps/day, MVPA minutes/week, goal percent above baseline, 18-month maintenance.

**Protocol takeaway:** Baseline-derived goals can work at scale, but gamification/incentives and high-risk status should not be collapsed into core Daily Step Floor evidence.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extracted study details

- **Population:** Adults with established cardiovascular disease or elevated 10-year ASCVD risk and baseline average daily steps below 7500.
- **Intervention or exposure:** Wearable-connected interventions with personalized goals 33% to 50% above baseline plus gamification, financial incentives, or both.
- **Comparator or control:** Control group receiving daily text only.
- **Duration or follow-up:** 12-month intervention plus 6-month follow-up.
- **Endpoints:** steps/day, MVPA minutes/week, maintenance at 18 months
- **Effect estimates or direction:** At 12 months, changes from baseline were +1418 steps/day in control, +1954 gamification, +1915 incentives, and +2297 combined; differences versus control were +538, +492, and +868 steps/day. MVPA also increased more in intervention arms; changes persisted at 18 months but control also improved.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** Adverse-event details were not extracted from accessible summaries.
- **Limitations:** Adjacent incentive/gamification design, high cardiovascular-risk population, and a large control-group step increase.
- **Population mismatch:** High cardiovascular-risk clinical population with behavior-economics intervention components.
