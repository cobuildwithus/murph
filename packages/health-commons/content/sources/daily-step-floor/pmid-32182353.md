This source is included for **baseline_plus_ramp_trials**.

**Findings:** In an optimization trial of a walking app with personalized step goals, daily cash incentives increased goal achievement, action planning helped only without incentives, and several components had no effect with substantial app disengagement.

**Why it matters:** Directly useful for implementation components and engagement boundaries, but not a core floor-only efficacy source.

**Potential experiment signals:** goal achievement, cash incentive status, planning engagement, app discontinuation.

**Protocol takeaway:** Support components matter: cash incentives can improve short-term achievement, while prompts/planning may not work without engagement.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extracted study details

- **Population:** Health-insurance insurees in Switzerland using a smartphone walking app; more active/healthier than the general Swiss population.
- **Intervention or exposure:** Ally smartphone app with personalized daily step goals plus cash incentives, charity incentives, planning modules, and self-monitoring prompts.
- **Comparator or control:** No-incentive and alternative randomized app-component conditions.
- **Duration or follow-up:** 2-week run-in/baseline plus 6-week intervention within an 8-week study.
- **Endpoints:** personalized daily step-goal achievement, steps/day, engagement
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Daily cash incentives increased step-goal achievement by 8.1 percentage points (95% CI 2.1 to 14.1). Action planning increased goal achievement only in the no-incentive group by 5.8 percentage points. Charity incentives, self-monitoring prompts, and coping planning did not affect physical activity; 30% stopped using the app.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** Adverse-event reporting was not extracted.
- **Limitations:** Component optimization trial, incentive/app engagement effects, selected healthier participants, and not a pure daily-floor efficacy RCT.
- **Population mismatch:** Swiss insured app users; healthier/more active than general population.
