This source is included for **direct_protocol_reviews**.

**Findings:** Daily step count: The review reported a small positive effect on daily step count (SMD 0.24; 95% CI 0.16 to 0.33).; MVPA and energy expenditure: The review reported positive effects on MVPA (SMD 0.27; 95% CI 0.15 to 0.39) and energy expenditure (SMD 0.28; 95% CI 0.03 to 0.54).; Sedentary behavior: Sedentary behavior was lower in tracker groups but not statistically significant (SMD -0.20; 95% CI -0.43 to 0.03), with very low certainty and heterogeneous measurement.

**Why it matters:** This source helps position modern consumer wearables as an adjacent feedback tool, while preserving uncertainty about sedentary time and long-term adherence.

**Potential experiment signals:** daily-step-count, weekly-mvpa-minutes, sedentary-minutes, wearable-adherence.

**Protocol takeaway:** Consumer trackers can increase steps and activity participation, but this should be treated as adjacent context rather than direct Daily Step Floor evidence.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
