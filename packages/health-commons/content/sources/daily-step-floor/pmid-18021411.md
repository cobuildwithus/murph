This source is included for **baseline_plus_ramp_trials**.

**Findings:** An internet-mediated pedometer program in sedentary adults with type 2 diabetes found lifestyle accumulated-step goals as effective as structured bout goals over 6 weeks and more acceptable, with no serious adverse events reported.

**Why it matters:** Direct precedent for baseline-derived step goals and for allowing accumulated daily steps rather than requiring structured bouts.

**Potential experiment signals:** total steps/day, bout steps/day, goal satisfaction, wear adherence, musculoskeletal symptoms.

**Protocol takeaway:** Accumulated daily-step goals can be at least as acceptable as structured walking-bout goals for a step-floor protocol.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

## Extracted study details

- **Population:** Sedentary adults with type 2 diabetes.
- **Intervention or exposure:** Internet-mediated pedometer walking program; lifestyle goals used previous 7-day average +1200 steps/day, rounded to nearest 100 and capped at 10,000, while structured goals used bout-step targets.
- **Comparator or control:** Structured bout-walking goals versus lifestyle accumulated-step goals.
- **Duration or follow-up:** 6 weeks.
- **Endpoints:** bout steps/day, total steps/day, goal satisfaction, pedometer wear, adverse events
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Thirty-five participants were randomized and 30 completed. Completers increased about 1921 bout steps/day and 1938 total steps/day overall, with no significant between-group step difference; lifestyle accumulated-step goals were more satisfying and had more pedometer wear than structured goals.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** Twelve possible adverse events were reported; none were serious and most were musculoskeletal.
- **Limitations:** Small short pilot, internet access requirements, baseline imbalance, and a low bout threshold.
- **Population mismatch:** Adults with type 2 diabetes; not a general wellness cohort.
