This source is included for **safety_special_populations**.

**Findings:** In the Feet First RCT, the walking/strength/balance intervention did not produce statistically significant differences in falls during follow-up among people with diabetes and peripheral neuropathy.

**Why it matters:** Adds falls/balance context for neuropathy users, a group at higher fall and foot-injury risk.

**Potential experiment signals:** falls, balance, daily-step-count, adverse-events.

**Protocol takeaway:** Do not promise falls prevention from a daily step floor in diabetic neuropathy; pair walking with appropriate balance/safety assessment.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

**Directness boundary:** clinical_supervised falls-safety context. Do not promote this source into direct Daily Step Floor claims beyond the stated claim-use boundary.

**Safety/adverse events:** Falls were monitored; the intervention did not demonstrate a clear falls-prevention advantage.

**Limitations and mismatch:** The parent intervention was designed for physical activity and ulcer-risk questions, not powered as a falls-prevention program; intensity may have been insufficient. Diabetes peripheral neuropathy; not general adult Daily Step Floor evidence.
