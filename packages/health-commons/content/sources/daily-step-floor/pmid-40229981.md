This source is included for **sedentary_activity_pattern**.

**Findings:** Tracker plus motivational interviewing reduced sedentary behavior by about 81 minutes/day and increased steps by about 3117/day versus control; tracker-only did not show a beneficial sedentary-behavior effect in accessible summaries.

**Why it matters:** Highlights that goal support/coaching may matter more than wearing a tracker alone.

**Potential experiment signals:** sedentary behavior, steps, standing, light physical activity, moderate-to-vigorous physical activity, cardiometabolic health.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as adjacent/context evidence that a step-floor protocol may need support components to shift sedentary time.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Free-living sedentary adults randomized to control, consumer wearable tracker only, or tracker plus motivational interviewing.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Consumer wearable activity tracker alone or combined with motivational interviewing.
- **Comparator/control:** Control group maintaining daily routine.
- **Duration/follow-up:** 12 weeks
- **Endpoints:** sedentary behavior, steps, standing, light physical activity, moderate-to-vigorous physical activity, cardiometabolic health
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Tracker plus motivational interviewing reduced sedentary behavior by about 81 minutes/day and increased steps by about 3117/day versus control; tracker-only did not show a beneficial sedentary-behavior effect in accessible summaries.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** Adverse events were not extracted from the accessible summary.
- **Limitations:** Small trial.; Multicomponent tracker plus motivational interviewing, not simple daily floor.; Sedentary adults may not represent already active users.
- **Population mismatch:** Adjacent consumer-wearable RCT; not direct Daily Step Floor evidence.
- **Artifact candidates and rights status:** open_access; no copyrighted PDF is included in this draft artifact package.
