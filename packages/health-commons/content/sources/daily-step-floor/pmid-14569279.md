This source is included for **baseline_plus_ramp_trials**.

**Findings:** In overweight/obese adults with type 2 diabetes, the First Step Program increased daily walking by more than 3000 steps/day versus control during the 16-week intervention, but maintenance waned by 24-week follow-up.

**Why it matters:** Directly demonstrates a pedometer-delivered step increment in a clinical population and highlights maintenance needs.

**Potential experiment signals:** steps/day change, goal adherence, waist/hip circumference, follow-up relapse.

**Protocol takeaway:** Baseline-plus pedometer programs can produce clinically meaningful step increases, but maintenance supports or boosters may be needed.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

## Extracted study details

- **Population:** Overweight or obese sedentary adults with type 2 diabetes.
- **Intervention or exposure:** First Step Program pedometer-based daily physical activity intervention focused on increasing daily steps.
- **Comparator or control:** Control group.
- **Duration or follow-up:** 16-week intervention with 24-week follow-up.
- **Endpoints:** steps/day, waist circumference, hip circumference, body composition, blood pressure, follow-up maintenance
- **Effect estimates or direction:** The First Step Program increased walking by more than 3000 steps/day versus control during the 16-week intervention (reported p<.0001); waist and hip circumference decreased within the intervention group but between-group differences were not significant, and step gains relapsed during follow-up.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** Adverse-event reporting was not extracted from accessible records.
- **Limitations:** Type 2 diabetes population, attrition/follow-up relapse, and full adverse-event details unavailable from accessible records.
- **Population mismatch:** Adults with type 2 diabetes and obesity/overweight; not a broad low-risk population.
