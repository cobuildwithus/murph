This source is included for **measurement_validity**.

**Findings:** Pedometer step-count error increased markedly at slower walking cadences in older adults, reaching high mean percent errors at 50 and 66 steps/min.

**Why it matters:** Slow cadence can make a step-floor appear lower than true ambulatory effort.

**Potential experiment signals:** daily_step_count, step_count_accuracy, device_placement_or_carriage, gait_speed_or_gait_aid_context.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context-only evidence to flag slow walking as a reason for device validation or sensitivity analysis.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Eighteen older adults (6 male, 12 female) with mean age 63.6 years.
- **Exposure/intervention:** Five pedometer brands during slow walking at preset cadences and self-selected cadence.
- **Comparator/control:** Manual/observed step count over an 80 m indoor track.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Single-session walking protocol
- **Endpoints:** pedometer percent error, walking cadence/speed effects
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Mean percent error across devices was reported as 56%, 40%, 19%, and 9% at cadences of 50, 66, 80 steps/min, and self-selected cadence, respectively; accuracy decreased with slower walking speed.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse events or safety outcomes were reported in the accessible extract.
- **Limitations:** Small older-adult sample.; Short indoor protocol rather than free-living measurement.; Device models may be older.
- **Population mismatch:** Slow-walking older adult measurement context; not protocol efficacy evidence.
- **Artifact rights:** open_access
