This source is included for **direct_step_floor_trials**.

**Findings:**

- `finding:pmid-21453540-step-message` — At week 12, the 10,000-step group increased steps by 4,616/day (43%) versus 2,761/day (35%) in the 30-minute group; the between-group difference was reported as P=0.045.
- `finding:pmid-21453540-null-health-endpoints` — The accessible source reported no significant anthropometric or blood-pressure changes despite increased steps.

**Why it matters:** This is a direct framing comparison between a daily step floor and a time-based activity prescription.

**Potential experiment signals:**

- daily steps
- body weight
- waist circumference
- blood pressure
- lipids

**Protocol takeaway:** The step-count message produced a larger step increase than the time-based message, but short-term anthropometric and blood-pressure outcomes were null.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

## Extraction boundaries

- **Directness:** `direct_protocol`.
- **Population:** Sedentary overweight or obese women aged 35-55 years with BMI 25-35 kg/m²
- **Intervention/exposure:** Pedometer feedback plus a 10,000-steps/day message.
- **Comparator/control:** 30-minutes/day physical activity health message.
- **Duration/follow-up:** 12 weeks
- **Endpoints extracted:** daily steps, body weight, BMI, waist circumference, blood pressure, lipids.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse-event signal was extracted from the accessible source record; absence of extracted adverse events should not be interpreted as proof of no adverse events unless the finding explicitly says so.
- **Limitations:** Small women-only sample; 12 weeks; body-composition and blood-pressure outcomes were not improved in the accessible source.
- **Population mismatch:** Sedentary overweight middle-aged women only.
