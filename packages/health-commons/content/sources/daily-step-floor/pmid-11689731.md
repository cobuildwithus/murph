This source is included for **baseline_plus_ramp_trials**.

**Findings:** A 24-week daily walking intervention in postmenopausal women with borderline/stage 1 hypertension was reported to lower systolic blood pressure, but the accessible records did not provide exact sample size or step-dose details.

**Why it matters:** Foundational daily-walking blood-pressure evidence for a baseline-plus walking floor, with extraction caveats.

**Potential experiment signals:** steps/day or added walking days, home blood pressure, adherence over 24 weeks.

**Protocol takeaway:** Can support the plausibility of health benefit from progressive daily walking, but should not be used for a precise step-floor dose without full-text extraction.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

## Extracted study details

- **Population:** Sedentary postmenopausal women with borderline to stage 1 hypertension.
- **Intervention or exposure:** Daily walking program intended to meet ACSM/CDC physical activity recommendations; exact step-dose details were not extracted from accessible records.
- **Comparator or control:** Control/usual-activity comparator reported in secondary descriptions; full comparator details were not extracted.
- **Duration or follow-up:** 24 weeks
- **Endpoints:** daily walking volume, systolic blood pressure, diastolic blood pressure, body composition
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Accessible abstracts/secondary records report lower systolic blood pressure after the daily walking program; sample size and exact step dose were not extracted from accessible records.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** Adverse-event reporting was not extracted from accessible records.
- **Limitations:** Older permission-required article; exact sample size, randomization details, and adverse-event data were not extracted in this run.
- **Population mismatch:** Postmenopausal hypertensive women; not a general wellness population.
