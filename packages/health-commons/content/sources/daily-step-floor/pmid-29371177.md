This source is included for **baseline_plus_ramp_trials**.

**Findings:** A mobile-app RCT found machine-learning personalized goals had a smaller post-run-in step decline than a constant 10,000-step goal, yielding about a +960 steps/day relative advantage, but both groups declined from run-in.

**Why it matters:** Direct evidence favoring personalized goals over a fixed 10,000-step target, with important measurement/run-in caveats.

**Potential experiment signals:** steps/day change from run-in, goal personalization algorithm, phone-carry adherence.

**Protocol takeaway:** Personalization can outperform a fixed 10,000-step goal, but app trials must define a true baseline.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

## Extracted study details

- **Population:** Adults using a mobile app, mostly university staff, with a high proportion of women and highly educated participants.
- **Intervention or exposure:** Machine-learning-based personalized daily step goals delivered through a mobile app.
- **Comparator or control:** Active control with constant 10,000 steps/day goal.
- **Duration or follow-up:** 10 weeks after a run-in period.
- **Endpoints:** steps/day, app-delivered goal response
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Steps decreased from run-in in both arms, but less in the personalized-goal arm (-390) than the 10,000-step control (-1350), yielding a net about +960 steps/day advantage (95% CI 90 to 1830; P=.03).
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** Adverse-event reporting was not extracted from accessible records.
- **Limitations:** Small sample, no natural baseline, phone-carry measurement issues, mostly highly educated women, and short duration.
- **Population mismatch:** University-affiliated app users; not a representative general population.
