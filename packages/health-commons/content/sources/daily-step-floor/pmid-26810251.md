This source is included for **baseline_plus_ramp_trials**.

**Findings:** StepWise used a staged +1500 to +3000 steps/day baseline-plus ramp and maintained higher step counts than comparison at 24 weeks, but the pilot had mixed secondary outcomes and goal-adjustment limitations.

**Why it matters:** Direct precedent for a staged baseline-plus ramp and a caution about inflexible automatic escalation.

**Potential experiment signals:** steps/day, website use, ramp stage completion, cholesterol, disruption/illness pauses.

**Protocol takeaway:** A ramp from smaller increments to +3000/day is plausible, but floor algorithms should allow recovery after illness or disruption.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

## Extracted study details

- **Population:** Low-active adults with mean age around 46 years.
- **Intervention or exposure:** StepWise web-based pedometer walking program with baseline-tailored ramps from +1500 to +3000 extra steps/day across 12 weeks.
- **Comparator or control:** Comparison physical-activity condition; full comparator details were not extracted in this run.
- **Duration or follow-up:** 12-week intervention with 24-week follow-up.
- **Endpoints:** steps/day, physical outcomes, psychological outcomes, cholesterol
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Both groups increased steps; at week 24 StepWise participants took more steps than comparison participants, reported as an 8939-step/day difference in accessible text (95% CI 274 to 17604; p=.04). Cholesterol increased and other physical/psychological changes were mixed.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** Adverse-event reporting was not extracted from accessible records.
- **Limitations:** Pilot RCT; automatic goals could increase without stepping back after illness or disruption; success depended on website use.
- **Population mismatch:** Low-active adult pilot sample.
