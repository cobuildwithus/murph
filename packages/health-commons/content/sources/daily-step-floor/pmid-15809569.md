This source is included for **direct_step_floor_trials**.

**Findings:**

- `finding:pmid-15809569-steps-vs-time` — The 10,000-step group averaged 10,159 ± 292 steps/day versus 8,270 ± 354 steps/day in the 30-minute group (P<0.005); on goal-achievement days the 10,000-step group averaged 11,775 ± 207 steps.
- `finding:pmid-15809569-thirty-minute-days` — The 30-minute group averaged 9,505 ± 326 steps/day on days when a 30-minute walk occurred and 5,597 ± 363 steps/day when it did not, suggesting the time-based message produced near-10,000-step days only when the walk was completed.

**Why it matters:** This is a direct protocol-style trial: the intervention is a daily step floor and the comparator is a common time-based walking recommendation.

**Potential experiment signals:**

- daily steps
- days meeting the step floor
- step count on non-goal days
- self-reported adherence

**Protocol takeaway:** A specific 10,000-step floor can elicit more walking than a 30-minute walking message in inactive middle-aged women, but this short trial does not establish long-term health outcomes.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

## Extraction boundaries

- **Directness:** `direct_protocol`.
- **Population:** Previously inactive women, mean age about 45 years, averaging at or below 7,000 baseline steps/day
- **Intervention/exposure:** Daily pedometer-based recommendation to walk 10,000 steps per day.
- **Comparator/control:** Recommendation to take a brisk 30-minute walk on most, preferably all, days of the week.
- **Duration/follow-up:** 2-week sealed-pedometer baseline plus 4-week intervention
- **Endpoints extracted:** daily step count, goal-achievement days, walking recommendation adherence.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse-event signal was extracted from the accessible source record; absence of extracted adverse events should not be interpreted as proof of no adverse events unless the finding explicitly says so.
- **Limitations:** Short 4-week intervention; all participants were women; health outcomes and adverse events were not extracted from the accessible abstract-level record.
- **Population mismatch:** Middle-aged inactive women only; not enough to generalize to men, older adults, or clinical populations.
