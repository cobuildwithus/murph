This source is included for **sedentary_activity_pattern**.

**Findings:** Higher total sedentary time and longer sedentary bout duration were associated with higher all-cause mortality; the study is observational and cannot establish intervention causality.

**Why it matters:** Explains why prolonged bouts are meaningful to track, but it does not validate any specific step-goal intervention.

**Potential experiment signals:** total sedentary time, sedentary bout duration, all-cause mortality.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as observational context for sedentary-bout endpoints only.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** U.S. middle-aged and older adults in an accelerometer-measured national cohort.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Observed accelerometer-measured total sedentary time and sedentary bout duration.
- **Comparator/control:** Lower sedentary-time and shorter-bout exposure categories.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Median follow-up about 4 years in accessible abstract.
- **Endpoints:** total sedentary time, sedentary bout duration, all-cause mortality
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Higher total sedentary time and longer sedentary bout duration were associated with higher all-cause mortality; the study is observational and cannot establish intervention causality.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** Mortality was an outcome; no intervention adverse events apply.
- **Limitations:** Observational cohort; residual confounding possible.; Associations do not prove that a step floor reduces mortality.; Middle-aged/older cohort may not generalize to all users.
- **Population mismatch:** Observational sedentary-pattern risk context, not intervention evidence.
- **Artifact candidates and rights status:** open_access; no copyrighted PDF is included in this draft artifact package.
