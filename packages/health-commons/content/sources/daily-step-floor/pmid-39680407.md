This source is included for **dose_response_cut_points**.

**Findings:** Across 33 observational studies including 96,173 adults, higher daily steps were associated with fewer depressive symptoms; prospective summaries reported lower depression risk for ≥7000 versus <7000 steps/day and lower risk per additional 1000 steps/day.

**Why it matters:** Adds mental-health context to step-volume thresholds.

**Potential experiment signals:** daily step count, days meeting step floor, weekly mean steps, depressive symptoms, depression risk.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context for potential mood signals while preserving observational caveats.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Adults from observational studies, including cross-sectional and longitudinal designs.
- **Exposure/intervention:** Daily step count categories and per-1000-step increments.
- **Comparator/control:** Lower step-count categories such as <5000 or <7000 steps/day.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Searches through 18 May 2024; longitudinal follow-up varied.
- **Endpoints:** depressive symptoms, depression risk, daily step count
- **Safety/adverse events:** No adverse-event signal specific to a step-floor intervention was extracted from this source in this batch.
- **Limitations:** Mostly observational and many cross-sectional studies; reverse causation and residual confounding remain plausible.
- **Population mismatch/directness:** Mental-health associations are adjacent to, not direct evidence for, a step-floor protocol.
- **Boundary:** This source is observational, review-based, registry-only, or otherwise adjacent unless explicitly noted; it must not be promoted into direct Daily Step Floor protocol evidence.
