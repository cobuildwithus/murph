This source is included for **dose_response_cut_points**.

**Findings:** This preliminary study assigned 35 overweight participants to a 12-week 10,000 steps/day program and reported improved mood-related measures among those who achieved the target, but the design was preliminary and subject to adherence selection.

**Why it matters:** Adjacent intervention context for 10,000-step prescriptions and mood/physical signals.

**Potential experiment signals:** daily step count, days meeting step floor, weekly mean steps, physical health measures, mental health/mood measures.

**Protocol takeaway:** Context-only; do not use as strong efficacy evidence.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Sedentary overweight adults in a community setting.
- **Exposure/intervention:** 12-week pedometer-based walking program targeting 10,000 steps/day.
- **Comparator/control:** Baseline status and achievers/non-achievers as reported; no robust randomized control extracted.
- **Duration/follow-up:** 12 weeks.
- **Endpoints:** daily step count, physical health measures, mental health/mood measures
- **Safety/adverse events:** No adverse-event signal specific to a step-floor intervention was extracted from this source in this batch.
- **Limitations:** Small preliminary study, limited control structure, and target achiever analyses may be biased.
- **Population mismatch/directness:** Overweight community sample and 10,000-step target differ from a flexible daily floor protocol.
- **Boundary:** This source is observational, review-based, registry-only, or otherwise adjacent unless explicitly noted; it must not be promoted into direct Daily Step Floor protocol evidence.
