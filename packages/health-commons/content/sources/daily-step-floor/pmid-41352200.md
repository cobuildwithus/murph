This source is included for **mental_health_sleep_qol**.

**Findings:** A 36-study meta-analysis of 8,192 adults found higher daily steps correlated with better sleep quality and reduced daytime dysfunction, but sleep latency, efficiency, and disturbances were not significantly associated in the accessible abstract.

**Why it matters:** Current meta-analysis directly connecting daily steps and sleep endpoints with mixed outcome-specific findings.

**Potential experiment signals:** biomarker:daily-steps, biomarker:sleep-quality, biomarker:sleep-duration, biomarker:daytime-dysfunction.

**Protocol takeaway:** Daily steps may relate to sleep quality and daytime dysfunction, but evidence is mixed by sleep endpoint and should not support broad sleep-improvement claims.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.
