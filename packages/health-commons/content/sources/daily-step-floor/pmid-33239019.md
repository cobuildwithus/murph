This source is included for **safety_special_populations**.

**Findings:** A WHO-informing systematic review found overall exercise reduced older-adult falls, but walking-only programs had very low-certainty evidence and did not clearly reduce falls.

**Why it matters:** Prevents overclaiming that a walking floor alone prevents falls in older adults.

**Potential experiment signals:** falls, daily-step-count, balance, adverse-events.

**Protocol takeaway:** Daily Step Floor should not be marketed as a falls-prevention substitute; older adults with fall risk may need balance/strength programming and clinical screening.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

**Directness boundary:** general_guideline safety boundary for older adults. Do not promote this source into direct Daily Step Floor claims beyond the stated claim-use boundary.

**Safety/adverse events:** Falls-prevention evidence favors balance and functional exercise rather than walking-only prescriptions for older adults.

**Limitations and mismatch:** Falls-prevention review, not daily step-floor evidence; walking-only subgroup small and very low certainty. Older-adult falls-prevention evidence; use as safety/guideline context only.
