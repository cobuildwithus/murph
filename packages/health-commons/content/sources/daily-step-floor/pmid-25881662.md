This source is included for **measurement_validity**.

**Findings:** Android smartphone activity tracking showed strong laboratory correlations with ActiGraph but more limited free-living agreement in accessible extracts, supporting context-only measurement use.

**Why it matters:** Smartphone-based step or activity outcomes are vulnerable to phone carriage and context effects.

**Potential experiment signals:** daily_step_count, step_count_accuracy, device_placement_or_carriage, gait_speed_or_gait_aid_context.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context-only evidence for smartphone measurement; do not promote as direct Daily Step Floor evidence.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Study 1 included 15 adults; Study 2 included 23 adults in free-living conditions.
- **Exposure/intervention:** Android smartphone physical-activity tracking.
- **Comparator/control:** ActiGraph accelerometer.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Laboratory activity protocol plus 7-day free-living monitoring.
- **Endpoints:** activity-classification validity, smartphone-ActiGraph correlation, sedentary/MVPA estimates
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Laboratory smartphone-ActiGraph correlations were strong to very strong (rho=.77-.82). Free-living agreement was more moderate in accessible extracts, suggesting lower certainty outside controlled conditions.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse events or safety outcomes were reported in the accessible extract.
- **Limitations:** Activity tracking study, not purely a step-count validation.; Android devices and algorithms from 2015 may not represent current phones.; Free-living agreement depends on phone carriage.
- **Population mismatch:** Measurement context for smartphone tracking; no Daily Step Floor intervention.
- **Artifact rights:** open_access
