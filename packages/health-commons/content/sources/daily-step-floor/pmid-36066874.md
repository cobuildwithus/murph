This source is included for **dose_response_cut_points**.

**Findings:** Among 78,430 UK adults, higher daily steps and higher step intensity were associated with lower incident dementia risk; public summaries reported an optimal dose near 9826 steps/day and a minimum dose near 3826 steps/day associated with lower risk.

**Why it matters:** Cognitive-health context for step count and intensity.

**Potential experiment signals:** daily step count, days meeting step floor, weekly mean steps, incident dementia, step intensity.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as observational dementia context only.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** UK Biobank adults living in the UK, initially free of dementia.
- **Exposure/intervention:** Wrist accelerometer daily step count and intensity.
- **Comparator/control:** Lower step counts and modeled dose-response reference levels.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Approximately seven years of follow-up in public summaries.
- **Endpoints:** incident dementia, daily step count, step intensity
- **Safety/adverse events:** No adverse-event signal specific to a step-floor intervention was extracted from this source in this batch.
- **Limitations:** Observational cohort, UK Biobank selection bias, dementia ascertainment limitations, and residual confounding/reverse causation concerns.
- **Population mismatch/directness:** Dementia incidence association is adjacent and not direct proof for a step-floor protocol.
- **Boundary:** This source is observational, review-based, registry-only, or otherwise adjacent unless explicitly noted; it must not be promoted into direct Daily Step Floor protocol evidence.
