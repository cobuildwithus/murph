This source is included for **safety_special_populations**.

**Findings:** In a 274-participant high-risk home-based walking study, 87 participants reported 121 adverse events over 1,110 person-months; one serious event was considered study-related and non-serious related events were mainly minor musculoskeletal complaints.

**Why it matters:** Provides concrete adverse-event categories and pause/resume precedent for high-risk home walking interventions.

**Potential experiment signals:** adverse-events, temporary protocol suspension, musculoskeletal pain, cardiovascular symptoms, return-to-walking timing.

**Protocol takeaway:** Daily Step Floor should track minor musculoskeletal symptoms, cardiovascular symptoms, and temporary suspensions, especially in high-risk users.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

**Directness boundary:** clinical_supervised safety context for home-based walking. Do not promote this source into direct Daily Step Floor claims beyond the stated claim-use boundary.

**Safety/adverse events:** The single serious study-related event was atrial fibrillation; the participant resumed walking within 3 days. Fifty temporary suspensions averaged 26 days.

**Limitations and mismatch:** All-male high-risk veteran sample; descriptive safety analysis; not a direct test of a minimum daily step floor. High-risk veterans with cardiovascular risk; supervised/safety context only.
