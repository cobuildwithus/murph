This source is included for **sedentary_activity_pattern**.

**Findings:** The SMS prompt arm produced short-lived activity gains in the accessible abstract, with between-group differences evident at week 1 but not sustained across the full 6 weeks; no durable sedentary-time reduction was extracted.

**Why it matters:** Prompts can create short-term movement, but persistence appears uncertain without stronger behavior design.

**Potential experiment signals:** steps, moderate-to-vigorous physical activity, total physical activity, sedentary time.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context-only evidence that prompts alone may be insufficient for sustained activity-pattern change.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Overweight and obese adults in a 6-week wearable-sensor and SMS-prompt randomized trial.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Fitbit One self-monitoring plus SMS text-message prompts.
- **Comparator/control:** Fitbit One without SMS prompts.
- **Duration/follow-up:** 6 weeks
- **Endpoints:** steps, moderate-to-vigorous physical activity, total physical activity, sedentary time
- **Effect estimates or direction:** The SMS prompt arm produced short-lived activity gains in the accessible abstract, with between-group differences evident at week 1 but not sustained across the full 6 weeks; no durable sedentary-time reduction was extracted.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse events or safety outcomes were reported in the accessible extract.
- **Limitations:** Small sample and short follow-up.; SMS prompting was adjunctive to a tracker and was not a daily step floor.; Sedentary-time-specific effect estimates were not extracted here.
- **Population mismatch:** Adjacent wearable/prompting RCT in overweight and obese adults, not a Daily Step Floor trial.
- **Artifact candidates and rights status:** open_access; no copyrighted PDF is included in this draft artifact package.
