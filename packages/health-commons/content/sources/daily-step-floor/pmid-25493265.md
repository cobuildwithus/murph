This source is included for **baseline_plus_ramp_trials**.

**Findings:** A 16-week RCT in type 2 diabetes found progressive pedometer-based walking improved quality of life and wellbeing, with supervised exercise stronger than self-reported pedometer use.

**Why it matters:** Adjacent clinical evidence for progressive pedometer walking and QoL endpoints.

**Potential experiment signals:** steps/day or steps/session, quality of life, wellbeing, supervision level.

**Protocol takeaway:** Progressive pedometer walking may improve wellbeing in diabetes, but supervised/contextual elements limit direct translation.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extracted study details

- **Population:** Type 2 diabetes outpatients aged 40 to 70 years.
- **Intervention or exposure:** Progressive pedometer-based walking: supervised exercise plus pedometer target around 4000 steps/session or self-reported pedometer target of 10,000 steps/day.
- **Comparator or control:** Control group.
- **Duration or follow-up:** 16 weeks.
- **Endpoints:** steps/session or steps/day, quality of life, general wellbeing
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Ninety of 102 participants completed. Quality-of-life and general wellbeing improved in intervention groups, with supervised exercise more effective. The self-reported pedometer group increased by about +4208 steps/day, while supervised group increased about +922 steps/session.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** Adverse-event reporting was not extracted.
- **Limitations:** Clinical diabetes sample, supervised exercise arm, QoL focus, and not a simple daily floor-only intervention.
- **Population mismatch:** Type 2 diabetes outpatients in clinical exercise context.
