This source is included for **dose_response_cut_points**.

**Findings:** Across 20,152 adults and 1523 cardiovascular disease events, higher steps were associated with lower CVD risk among adults aged 60 years or older; the highest quartile had an adjusted HR around 0.51 versus the lowest quartile, while associations were not apparent among younger adults after adjustment.

**Why it matters:** Important context for older-adult cardiovascular risk gradients.

**Potential experiment signals:** daily step count, days meeting step floor, weekly mean steps, incident cardiovascular disease.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as age-aware CVD context; avoid causal floor claims.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Adults from eight prospective studies with device-measured steps; mean age about 63 years and majority non-Hispanic White.
- **Exposure/intervention:** Daily steps measured for 3–7 days.
- **Comparator/control:** Step quartiles, with lowest quartile as reference.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Mean follow-up 6.2 years.
- **Endpoints:** incident cardiovascular disease, daily step count
- **Safety/adverse events:** No adverse-event signal specific to a step-floor intervention was extracted from this source in this batch.
- **Limitations:** Observational harmonized cohorts, limited age-specific power for younger adults, device/protocol heterogeneity, residual confounding.
- **Population mismatch/directness:** CVD-event association is not direct evidence of a step-floor protocol effect.
- **Boundary:** This source is observational, review-based, registry-only, or otherwise adjacent unless explicitly noted; it must not be promoted into direct Daily Step Floor protocol evidence.
