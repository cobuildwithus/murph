This source is included for **baseline_plus_ramp_trials**.

**Findings:** A pilot RCT in older adults suggested pedometer feedback variants increased step trajectories, but accessible summaries did not show significant quality-of-life differences.

**Why it matters:** Adjacent feedback-design evidence for older adults; not a pure floor/ramp source.

**Potential experiment signals:** step slope, feedback engagement, quality of life.

**Protocol takeaway:** Pedometer feedback can support walking behavior, but motivational tailoring should be treated as an adjacent implementation option.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extracted study details

- **Population:** Older adults in a motivationally matched pedometer-feedback pilot.
- **Intervention or exposure:** Pedometer feedback with standard or motivationally matched materials, with or without telephone support.
- **Comparator or control:** Standard literature/no pedometer or less intensive feedback groups.
- **Duration or follow-up:** Duration not fully extracted from accessible snippets.
- **Endpoints:** steps/day trajectory, quality of life, motivational feedback response
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Pedometer-feedback groups showed positive daily step slopes in accessible regression summaries; quality-of-life outcomes did not show significant between-group differences in accessible summaries.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** Adverse-event reporting was not extracted.
- **Limitations:** Pilot size, older adult sample, partial accessible data only.
- **Population mismatch:** Older-adult feedback intervention rather than a clear daily floor prescription.
- **Directness to Daily Step Floor:** `adjacent_variant`
