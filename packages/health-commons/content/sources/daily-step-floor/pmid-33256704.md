This source is included for **dose_response_cut_points**.

**Findings:** In 3055 community-dwelling 70-year-olds, higher daily step count was associated with lower incident diabetes risk, with the greatest benefit described at the lower end of the activity range and before 10,000 steps/day.

**Why it matters:** Metabolic-outcome context for lower-end step increases.

**Potential experiment signals:** daily step count, days meeting step floor, weekly mean steps, incident diabetes.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as diabetes-risk context and preserve attenuation caveats.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Community-dwelling 70-year-olds, about 52% women.
- **Exposure/intervention:** Daily step count at baseline.
- **Comparator/control:** Higher versus lower step counts; nonlinear models and cut-points.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Prospective follow-up for incident diabetes; exact follow-up varied in cohort reporting.
- **Endpoints:** incident diabetes, daily step count
- **Safety/adverse events:** No adverse-event signal specific to a step-floor intervention was extracted from this source in this batch.
- **Limitations:** Observational cohort; adjustment for visceral adipose tissue and other factors attenuated associations in reported models.
- **Population mismatch/directness:** Older-adult diabetes association does not establish Daily Step Floor efficacy.
- **Boundary:** This source is observational, review-based, registry-only, or otherwise adjacent unless explicitly noted; it must not be promoted into direct Daily Step Floor protocol evidence.
