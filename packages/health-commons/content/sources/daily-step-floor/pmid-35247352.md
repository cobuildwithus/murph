This source is included for **dose_response_cut_points**.

**Findings:** Among 47,471 adults and 3013 deaths, higher step quartiles were associated with lower all-cause mortality versus the lowest quartile; risk reductions appeared to level around 6000–8000 steps/day for adults aged 60 years or older and 8000–10,000 steps/day for younger adults, while stepping-rate metrics attenuated after adjustment for total steps.

**Why it matters:** Backbone source for age-stratified step-volume context.

**Potential experiment signals:** daily step count, days meeting step floor, weekly mean steps, all-cause mortality, stepping rate.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use for context that lower-than-10,000 thresholds may be meaningful by age, not causal proof.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Adults from 15 international cohorts with device-measured steps.
- **Exposure/intervention:** Daily step count quartiles and stepping-rate metrics.
- **Comparator/control:** Step quartiles with lowest quartile as reference.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Median follow-up 7.1 years.
- **Endpoints:** all-cause mortality, daily step count, stepping rate
- **Safety/adverse events:** No adverse-event signal specific to a step-floor intervention was extracted from this source in this batch.
- **Limitations:** Observational cohorts, single baseline step assessment in many cohorts, residual confounding, and age-stratified cut-points rather than intervention targets.
- **Population mismatch/directness:** International mortality cohorts do not test a Daily Step Floor assignment.
- **Boundary:** This source is observational, review-based, registry-only, or otherwise adjacent unless explicitly noted; it must not be promoted into direct Daily Step Floor protocol evidence.
