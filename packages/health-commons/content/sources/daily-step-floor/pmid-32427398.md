This source is included for **dose_response_cut_points**.

**Findings:** Among 2183 adults, higher daily steps were associated with lower all-cause mortality in a nonlinear pattern; the second quartile, about 2200 steps/day above the least-active quartile, had about half the mortality risk in adjusted models.

**Why it matters:** Adds middle-aged/older cohort evidence for gains from modest increases above low baseline.

**Potential experiment signals:** daily step count, days meeting step floor, weekly mean steps, all-cause mortality.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context for incremental floor/ramp framing.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Norwegian middle-aged and older adults; mean age about 57 years.
- **Exposure/intervention:** Waist-mounted accelerometer daily steps measured for seven consecutive days.
- **Comparator/control:** Quartiles of daily steps from about 4651 to 11,467 median steps/day.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Median follow-up 9.1 years.
- **Endpoints:** all-cause mortality, daily step count
- **Safety/adverse events:** No adverse-event signal specific to a step-floor intervention was extracted from this source in this batch.
- **Limitations:** Observational cohort, relatively healthy and active population, short baseline measurement window.
- **Population mismatch/directness:** Cohort dose-response evidence is not a step-floor intervention.
- **Boundary:** This source is observational, review-based, registry-only, or otherwise adjacent unless explicitly noted; it must not be promoted into direct Daily Step Floor protocol evidence.
