This source is included for **measurement_validity**.

**Findings:** In persons with MS, Fitbit Inspire HR step counts correlated strongly but agreed poorly with ActiGraph-derived counts; validity may vary by severity and devices should not be treated as interchangeable.

**Why it matters:** A tracker can be valid for a construct without being numerically interchangeable with a research-grade comparator.

**Potential experiment signals:** daily_step_count, step_count_accuracy, device_placement_or_carriage, gait_speed_or_gait_aid_context.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context-only evidence to avoid equating Fitbit and ActiGraph steps, especially across severity strata.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Forty-five persons with multiple sclerosis.
- **Exposure/intervention:** Fitbit Inspire HR-derived step counts and physical-activity intensity metrics.
- **Comparator/control:** ActiGraph-derived metrics and construct-validation comparisons.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Rehabilitation and home/free-living monitoring; exact duration not extracted.
- **Endpoints:** construct validity, agreement with ActiGraph, correlations, severity subgroup performance
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Fitbit-derived step counts had strong correlations but poor agreement with ActiGraph-derived counts. Authors inferred Fitbit-derived step count may outperform ActiGraph-derived step count during free-living physical activity in mild or moderate MS, but caution is needed in severe walking impairment.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse events or safety outcomes were reported in the accessible extract.
- **Limitations:** Construct validity rather than direct observed-step criterion validation for all settings.; True step count may lie between imperfect ActiGraph methods and Fitbit methods.; Performance differed by MS severity.
- **Population mismatch:** MS measurement context; not Daily Step Floor intervention evidence.
- **Artifact rights:** open_access
