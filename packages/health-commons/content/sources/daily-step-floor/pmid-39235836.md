This source is included for **sedentary_activity_pattern**.

**Findings:** Pilot results showed high satisfaction and feasibility; weekly step count increased (reported effect size d about 0.54, P≈.05), while sedentary time reduction was not statistically significant in the accessible abstract.

**Why it matters:** Preserves mixed efficacy: step gains may be feasible while sedentary-time benefit remains uncertain.

**Potential experiment signals:** sedentary time, weekly step count, physical activity, executive function, feasibility.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context-only evidence for culturally tailored prompts and feasibility, not definitive sedentary-time efficacy.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Spanish-speaking midlife and older Latino adults in a pilot randomized controlled trial.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Six-week web-based Spanish ecological momentary intervention using Fitbit data, SMS, and coaching to replace sedentary time with activity.
- **Comparator/control:** Guidelines education control.
- **Duration/follow-up:** 6 weeks
- **Endpoints:** sedentary time, weekly step count, physical activity, executive function, feasibility
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Pilot results showed high satisfaction and feasibility; weekly step count increased (reported effect size d about 0.54, P≈.05), while sedentary time reduction was not statistically significant in the accessible abstract.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No serious adverse events were extracted from the accessible summary.
- **Limitations:** Pilot sample and feasibility focus.; Latino older/midlife population with culturally tailored Spanish intervention.; Sedentary-time estimate was nonsignificant.
- **Population mismatch:** Adjacent ecological momentary stepping intervention; not a daily floor protocol.
- **Artifact candidates and rights status:** open_access; no copyrighted PDF is included in this draft artifact package.
