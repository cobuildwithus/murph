This source is included for **measurement_validity**.

**Findings:** Among inpatient rehabilitation participants, StepWatch, ankle Fitbit One, and ActivPAL agreed better with observed steps than several hip/wrist or research-grade alternatives.

**Why it matters:** Step floors may be misclassified in slow-walking or rehabilitating users if the wrong device/placement is chosen.

**Potential experiment signals:** daily_step_count, step_count_accuracy, device_placement_or_carriage, gait_speed_or_gait_aid_context.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context-only evidence favoring validated placement in slow-gait clinical populations.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** One hundred sixty-six adult inpatients in rehabilitation, with mean walking speed approximately 0.4 m/s in accessible extract.
- **Exposure/intervention:** Sixteen monitor configurations covering seven devices/placements during 6-minute and 6-metre walks.
- **Comparator/control:** Observed steps by physical therapist and gait parameters from GAITRite.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Single inpatient walking assessment
- **Endpoints:** step-count agreement, device placement, gait parameter effects
- **Effect estimates or direction:** StepWatch, Fitbit One at ankle, and ActivPAL showed excellent agreement with observed steps (ICC 0.98, 0.92, and 0.78). Fitbit Charge, Fitbit One at hip, G-Sensor, Garmin Vivofit, and ActiGraph showed poor agreement (ICC 0.12-0.40).
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse events or safety outcomes were reported in the accessible extract.
- **Limitations:** Inpatient rehabilitation context may not generalize to healthy adults.; Small counts in some gait-parameter subgroups.; No intervention outcome.
- **Population mismatch:** Clinical rehabilitation measurement context; not a direct Daily Step Floor trial.
