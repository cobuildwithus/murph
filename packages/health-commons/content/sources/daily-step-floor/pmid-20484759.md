This source is included for **cardiometabolic_fitness_endpoints**.

**Findings:** A randomized study compared supervised fitness training, pedometer-monitored walking up to 10,000 steps/day, and usual-activity control with matched energy expenditure. Supervised fitness training improved ventilatory threshold, peak VO2, submaximal RPE, and systolic exercise BP more than pedometer walking, making this adjacent evidence that step volume may not substitute for structured fitness training.

**Why it matters:** Important negative/contrast evidence: a step-count walking program may not produce the same fitness adaptations as supervised aerobic training.

**Potential experiment signals:** biomarker:daily-steps, biomarker:estimated-vo2max, biomarker:morning-blood-pressure, biomarker:fasting-glucose, biomarker:resting-heart-rate.

**Protocol takeaway:** Do not claim a step floor replaces structured aerobic fitness training for VO2/ventilatory-threshold outcomes.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness boundary:** `adjacent_variant`. This source should not be promoted beyond that scope in the Daily Step Floor protocol.

**Safety/adverse events:** No adverse-event signal extracted from accessible abstract text.

**Limitations and population mismatch:** Adjacent comparison of structured fitness versus pedometer walking; not an isolated Daily Step Floor intervention. Population mismatch: Sedentary adults in an exercise trial; not necessarily clinical cardiometabolic users.
