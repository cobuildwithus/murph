This source is included for **measurement_validity**.

**Findings:** In community-dwelling older adults, hip-worn ActiGraph and Digiwalker monitors underestimated steps at slower gait speeds, while the ankle-worn StepWatch was less biased in the accessible extract.

**Why it matters:** Step-floor adherence can be underestimated in people who walk slowly, especially with hip-worn pedometers or accelerometers.

**Potential experiment signals:** daily_step_count, step_count_accuracy, device_placement_or_carriage, gait_speed_or_gait_aid_context.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context-only evidence to flag slow gait as a measurement-risk condition.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Thirty-four community-dwelling older adults with mean age 79.2 years.
- **Exposure/intervention:** Yamax Digiwalker hip pedometer, ActiGraph hip accelerometer, and StepWatch ankle monitor during walking.
- **Comparator/control:** Observed steps during a 100-step walking test.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Single-session observed walking test
- **Endpoints:** step-count error, gait-speed subgroup accuracy
- **Effect estimates or direction:** At gait speeds below 0.80 m/s, ActiGraph and Digiwalker underestimated by 19.1% and 31.2%, respectively, while StepWatch overestimated by 6.5%. At 0.80-1.0 m/s, StepWatch overestimated by 6.6% and ActiGraph/Digiwalker underestimated by 5.7% and 12.7%.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse events or safety outcomes were reported in the accessible extract.
- **Limitations:** Older adult sample; device models are older.; Short observed-walk test may not capture free-living behavior.
- **Population mismatch:** Older-adult measurement-validity evidence; not a Daily Step Floor efficacy trial.
