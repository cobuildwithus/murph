This source is included for **direct_step_floor_trials**.

**Findings:**

- `finding:pmid-23802053-steps-decline` — Average steps were 12,256 ± 3,180 steps/day in month 1 and declined over the 6-month program.
- `finding:pmid-23802053-bp-fitness-mixed` — The accessible record reported significant time trends in systolic/diastolic blood pressure and estimated VO2max; steps were significantly related to VO2max changes and approached significance for systolic blood-pressure change.

**Why it matters:** Direct 10,000-step worksite context with clinical signal tracking, but design makes causal protocol claims weaker than RCT evidence.

**Potential experiment signals:**

- daily steps
- blood pressure
- estimated VO2max

**Protocol takeaway:** A 10,000-step worksite program can produce high early steps, but step counts may decline and health associations are mixed/non-randomized.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction boundaries

- **Directness:** `direct_protocol`.
- **Population:** U.S. and Swedish adults participating in the ASUKI Step workplace program
- **Intervention/exposure:** Worksite pedometer walking study with a goal of more than 10,000 steps/day.
- **Comparator/control:** Within-person trends over time; no simple randomized daily-floor comparator extracted.
- **Duration/follow-up:** 6 months
- **Endpoints extracted:** daily steps, systolic blood pressure, diastolic blood pressure, estimated VO2max, cardiorespiratory fitness.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse-event signal was extracted from the accessible source record; absence of extracted adverse events should not be interpreted as proof of no adverse events unless the finding explicitly says so.
- **Limitations:** Quasi-experimental substudy; workplace sample; no clean randomized comparison for the Daily Step Floor protocol.
- **Population mismatch:** Worksite adult participants in the U.S. and Sweden, not general direct-to-user protocol testers.
- **Artifact/rights boundary:** `open_access`; no copyrighted PDF is stored in Git in this draft.
