This source is included for **sedentary_activity_pattern**.

**Findings:** At 12 months, sitting time was lower than control by about 22.2 minutes/day for SWAL and 63.7 minutes/day for SWAL plus desk in accessible summaries; the desk arm was more effective than SWAL alone.

**Why it matters:** Shows that sedentary outcomes may be driven by environmental supports rather than step targets.

**Potential experiment signals:** daily sitting time, workplace sitting, physical activity, wellbeing, pain.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context-only workplace sedentary-time evidence, not proof of Daily Step Floor efficacy.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Office workers in a three-arm cluster randomized sitting-time reduction trial.
- **Intervention/exposure:** SMArT Work and Life intervention, with or without a height-adjustable desk.
- **Comparator/control:** Control arm and intervention-without-desk arm.
- **Duration/follow-up:** 12 months
- **Endpoints:** daily sitting time, workplace sitting, physical activity, wellbeing, pain
- **Effect estimates or direction:** At 12 months, sitting time was lower than control by about 22.2 minutes/day for SWAL and 63.7 minutes/day for SWAL plus desk in accessible summaries; the desk arm was more effective than SWAL alone.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** Accessible summaries noted some pain/wellbeing outcomes but no extracted adverse-event table.
- **Limitations:** Office-worker environmental intervention, not a step-floor protocol.; Sedentary-time reduction largely depends on workplace context and desk availability.; Effects may not generalize to non-office settings.
- **Population mismatch:** Adjacent workplace sitting intervention; not direct Daily Step Floor evidence.
