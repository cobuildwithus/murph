This source is included for **cardiometabolic_fitness_endpoints**.

**Findings:** A 12-week workplace pedometer intervention in sedentary workers increased daily steps and reported reductions in BMI, waist circumference, and resting heart rate. It is adjacent-variant evidence because it used a workplace/self-control program rather than a standalone Daily Step Floor rule.

**Why it matters:** Shows that a pedometer-supported step increase in a workplace setting can move cardiometabolic-adjacent markers, but the design limits protocol-level causal strength.

**Potential experiment signals:** biomarker:daily-steps, biomarker:bmi, biomarker:waist-circumference, biomarker:resting-heart-rate, biomarker:morning-blood-pressure.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context that step-count self-monitoring can increase activity and may improve body-size/resting-HR markers; do not cite as direct proof for a simple daily floor.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness boundary:** `adjacent_variant`. This source should not be promoted beyond that scope in the Daily Step Floor protocol.

**Safety/adverse events:** No adverse-event signal extracted from accessible abstract text.

**Limitations and population mismatch:** Non-randomized/self-control workplace program, 59% completion, and possible selection/adherence bias. Population mismatch: Sedentary workers may not represent clinical, older, diabetic, hypertensive, or mobility-limited users.
