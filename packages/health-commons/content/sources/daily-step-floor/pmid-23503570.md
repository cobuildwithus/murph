This source is included for **guidelines_external_protocol_context**.

**Findings:** The Flanders state-wide evaluation reported higher self-reported physical activity, more pedometer-based daily steps, and a higher proportion reaching 10,000 steps/day versus a historical comparison sample.

**Why it matters:** Useful external context for 10,000-step campaigns while requiring careful caveats about historical comparison and multicomponent dissemination.

**Potential experiment signals:** daily_step_count, step_goal_attainment, physical_activity_minutes.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as adjacent-variant context only; do not treat positive dissemination findings as direct Daily Step Floor evidence.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Random sample of adults aged 25-75 years in Flanders in 2011 compared with baseline intervention/control data from 10,000 Steps Ghent in 2005; pedometer data were available from 269 Flemish and 1,236 comparison respondents.
- **Exposure/intervention:** State-wide dissemination of the 10,000 Steps physical-activity intervention in Flanders.
- **Comparator/control:** Historical 2005 comparison sample from the 10,000 Steps Ghent intervention/control groups.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Statewide dissemination assessed in 2011 against 2005 historical data.
- **Endpoints:** International Physical Activity Questionnaire domains; pedometer daily step counts; proportion reaching 10,000 steps/day
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Compared with the 2005 comparison sample, the 2011 Flemish sample reported more walking, moderate, vigorous, work-related, leisure-time, and household PA; step-count analyses showed more daily steps and a higher proportion reaching 10,000 steps/day.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse events reported in the accessible abstract.
- **Limitations:** Historical comparison rather than randomized contemporaneous control; self-reported activity for several endpoints; external state-wide campaign.
- **Population mismatch:** External state-wide 10,000 Steps campaign, not a personal Daily Step Floor protocol.
