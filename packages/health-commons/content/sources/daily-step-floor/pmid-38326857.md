This source is included for **cadence_intensity_bouts**.

**Findings:** Small overground study in healthy young men found a VO2-reserve moderate-intensity cadence near 129 steps/min, higher than the simple 100 spm absolute-MET heuristic.

**Why it matters:** Shows that cadence thresholds differ when moderate intensity is defined relative to individual fitness.

**Potential experiment signals:** walking_cadence, vo2_reserve, relative_intensity, mvpa_minutes.

**Protocol takeaway:** Do not equate 100 spm with all definitions of moderate intensity; keep cadence optional and definition-specific.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Healthy young men, mean age about 25 years, free of major cardiac, metabolic, respiratory, or musculoskeletal issues.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Overground walking at free pace and at prescribed 100, 120, and 140 steps/min cadences.
- **Comparator/control:** Moderate intensity defined using VO2 reserve rather than only absolute METs.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Within-subject overground protocol with 6-minute walking trials at free pace and prescribed cadences.
- **Endpoints:** VO2 reserve; METs; cadence corresponding to moderate walking intensity.
- **Effect estimates or direction:** The study reported that moderate walking intensity for young healthy men corresponded to 128.9 steps/min by VO2 reserve; 100 and 120 spm did not reach 40% VO2 reserve, while 140 spm did.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse events or safety outcomes were reported in accessible summaries.
- **Limitations:** Small all-male healthy sample; VO2 reserve definition differs from absolute MET heuristic; overground track protocol; some higher-BMI participants struggled at 140 spm.
- **Population mismatch:** Adjacent population- and definition-specific calibration, not direct Daily Step Floor evidence.
