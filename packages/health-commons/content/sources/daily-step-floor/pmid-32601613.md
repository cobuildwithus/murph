This source is included for **measurement_validity**.

**Findings:** A systematic review of Garmin activity trackers found relatively higher validity for step counts than for distance, energy expenditure, or heart rate, with reliability evidence still limited.

**Why it matters:** A Daily Step Floor can use step counts more defensibly than less-valid derived metrics, but Garmin validity still depends on model and context.

**Potential experiment signals:** daily_step_count, step_count_accuracy, device_placement_or_carriage, gait_speed_or_gait_aid_context.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context-only review evidence for Garmin step measurement, not as direct protocol support.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Adults in 32 validity studies of Garmin activity trackers; sample sizes ranged from 1 to 95 for validity studies in accessible extract.
- **Exposure/intervention:** Garmin activity trackers measuring steps and other features.
- **Comparator/control:** Criterion/reference methods varied across included studies.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Review; included study durations varied.
- **Endpoints:** step validity, distance validity, energy expenditure validity, heart-rate validity, feature reliability
- **Effect estimates or direction:** The review concluded Garmin trackers had higher validity for steps, fewer studies for speed/elevation/sleep, and lower validity for distance, energy expenditure, and heart rate. Intra- and inter-device reliability needed more testing.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse events or safety outcomes were reported in the accessible extract.
- **Limitations:** Device models and criterion measures varied across included studies.; Review evidence is secondary and heterogeneous.; Results may not reflect later Garmin models or firmware.
- **Population mismatch:** Device-validity review, not Daily Step Floor protocol evidence.
- **Artifact rights:** open_access
