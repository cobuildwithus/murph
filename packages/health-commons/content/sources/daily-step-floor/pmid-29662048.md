This source is included for **measurement_validity**.

**Findings:** Different activity monitors may use proprietary and non-equivalent step-detection rules, weakening the assumption that a step count from one device is interchangeable with another.

**Why it matters:** A Daily Step Floor treats steps as a common unit, but device definitions may not be identical.

**Potential experiment signals:** daily_step_count, step_count_accuracy, device_placement_or_carriage, gait_speed_or_gait_aid_context.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as measurement-context evidence to avoid cross-device comparisons without calibration.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Methodological measurement analysis; participant count was not available in the accessible extraction.
- **Exposure/intervention:** Three popular activity-monitor brands and their step-detection behavior.
- **Comparator/control:** Device-specific step-detection parameters and the conceptual definition of a step.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Not applicable
- **Endpoints:** step-detection thresholds, device-specific definitions, inter-study comparability
- **Effect estimates or direction:** The source argues that different proprietary strategies for defining and detecting a step can invalidate steps as a universal metric and compromise inter-study comparisons.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse events or safety outcomes were reported.
- **Limitations:** Mechanistic/methodological evidence, not a user intervention.; Full device-level numerical outputs were not extracted for this draft.; Consumer device algorithms may change over time.
- **Population mismatch:** Mechanistic measurement context; no Daily Step Floor trial.
- **Artifact rights:** open_access
