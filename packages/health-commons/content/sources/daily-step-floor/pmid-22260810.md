This source is included for **guidelines_external_protocol_context**.

**Findings:** In a two-year prospective study of 348 10,000 Steps users, individual challenges were associated with higher logged-step and active-user engagement, while team challenges were not significantly associated.

**Why it matters:** Useful for implementation context about self-monitoring and challenge features that may sustain step logging.

**Potential experiment signals:** program_engagement, daily_step_count, step_goal_adherence.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context-only implementation evidence; do not infer causal health or step-floor efficacy from website engagement.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Registered 10,000 Steps Australia Step Log users who completed a web-based survey in June-July 2006.
- **Exposure/intervention:** Website engagement with individual challenges, team challenges, virtual walking buddies, and online Step Log features.
- **Comparator/control:** Users exposed to specific interactive features versus users not exposed; no randomized comparator.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Two-year website engagement follow-up.
- **Endpoints:** average steps logged; days logging steps; active user status; 10,000-step logged-day average; website logons
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Participants logged steps on average 169 days over two years. Individual challenges were associated with average 10,000 steps/day (OR 2.80, 95% CI 1.45-5.40), more logging days (OR 6.81, 95% CI 2.87-13.31), and remaining active (OR 4.36, 95% CI 2.17-8.71); team challenges were not significantly associated.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse events reported.
- **Limitations:** Engagement study rather than efficacy trial; logged steps are website records and may not capture overall physical activity; self-selection and nonrandomized feature exposure.
- **Population mismatch:** External eHealth program engagement, not a Murph Daily Step Floor outcome study.
- **Artifact rights:** open_access
