This source is included for **cadence_intensity_bouts**.

**Findings:** Overground walking-speed validation in 248 adults found moderate-intensity walking speeds around 1.29 m/s, with 3 mph/5 km/h outperforming lower public thresholds.

**Why it matters:** Provides intensity context adjacent to cadence: brisk walking often requires meaningful pace as well as steps.

**Potential experiment signals:** walking_speed, activity_intensity_classification, mvpa_minutes.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use walking speed only as optional context; do not add a speed requirement to Daily Step Floor.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Healthy adults aged 21-85 years (49% women) completing a self-paced overground walking trial.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Overground walking speed measured using an electronic gait mat with indirect calorimetry.
- **Comparator/control:** Moderate intensity defined as >=3 METs.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Single 5-minute overground walking trial.
- **Endpoints:** Walking-speed thresholds associated with moderate-intensity overground walking.
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Accessible summaries report a whole-sample threshold around 1.29 m/s and conclude that 3 mph or 5 km/h better identifies moderate intensity than 2.5 mph or 4.5 km/h.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse events or safety outcomes were reported in accessible summaries.
- **Limitations:** Walking-speed rather than step-count exposure; healthy overground lab sample; single trial; context-only for cadence and step-floor interpretation.
- **Population mismatch:** Same-mechanism walking-intensity context but not step-floor or cadence-prescription evidence.
