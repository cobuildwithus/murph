This source is included for **dose_response_cut_points**.

**Findings:** In 4840 US adults, compared with 4000 steps/day, 8000 steps/day was associated with lower all-cause mortality (HR 0.49) and 12,000 steps/day with still lower risk (HR 0.35); step intensity was not associated with mortality after accounting for total steps.

**Why it matters:** Major US cohort context for 8000 and 12,000 step comparisons.

**Potential experiment signals:** daily step count, days meeting step floor, weekly mean steps, all-cause mortality, cardiovascular mortality, cancer mortality, stepping intensity.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context for volume-associated mortality gradients; avoid causal claims.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** US adults aged 40 years or older in NHANES with accelerometer step data.
- **Exposure/intervention:** Daily step count and step-intensity metrics.
- **Comparator/control:** Modelled comparisons to 4000 steps/day and lower step categories.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Mortality follow-up through National Death Index; approximately 10 years in reporting context.
- **Endpoints:** all-cause mortality, cardiovascular mortality, cancer mortality, daily step count, stepping intensity
- **Safety/adverse events:** No adverse-event signal specific to a step-floor intervention was extracted from this source in this batch.
- **Limitations:** Observational cohort; residual confounding and reverse causation remain possible despite adjustments.
- **Population mismatch/directness:** National cohort associations do not test adherence to a daily floor.
- **Boundary:** This source is observational, review-based, registry-only, or otherwise adjacent unless explicitly noted; it must not be promoted into direct Daily Step Floor protocol evidence.
