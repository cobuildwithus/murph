This source is included for **dose_response_cut_points**.

**Findings:** Among 16,741 older women, higher step quartiles were associated with lower mortality; about 4400 steps/day was associated with lower mortality than about 2700 steps/day, and mortality risk decreased until roughly 7500 steps/day before leveling. Step-intensity associations mostly attenuated after adjustment for total steps.

**Why it matters:** Key cohort for lower threshold and plateau context in older women.

**Potential experiment signals:** daily step count, days meeting step floor, weekly mean steps, all-cause mortality, stepping intensity.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as observational context that volume may matter more than intensity in older women.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Older US women from the Women’s Health Study; mean age 72 years.
- **Exposure/intervention:** Hip accelerometer steps/day and step-intensity metrics over 7 days.
- **Comparator/control:** Quartiles of steps/day and intensity metrics.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Mean follow-up 4.3 years.
- **Endpoints:** all-cause mortality, daily step count, stepping intensity
- **Safety/adverse events:** No adverse-event signal specific to a step-floor intervention was extracted from this source in this batch.
- **Limitations:** Observational cohort of women only; residual confounding, healthy-volunteer bias, and single baseline accelerometer window.
- **Population mismatch/directness:** Older women only and not a randomized step-floor intervention.
- **Boundary:** This source is observational, review-based, registry-only, or otherwise adjacent unless explicitly noted; it must not be promoted into direct Daily Step Floor protocol evidence.
