**Findings:** The review summarized evidence that habitual moderate-intensity activity and daily steps in older adults are associated with several chronic-disease and functional-health outcomes, while noting uncertainty about exact patterns of activity required for benefit.

**Why it matters:** Context for age-sensitive step goals and older-adult health endpoints.

**Potential experiment signals:** daily step count, days meeting step floor, weekly mean steps, senior health outcomes, functional and cardiometabolic markers.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use only as older-adult landscape context.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

- **Population:** Older adults, with emphasis on senior-health outcomes.
- **Exposure/intervention:** Daily steps and walking patterns in older adults.
- **Comparator/control:** Lower versus higher step volumes in reviewed studies.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Not applicable; narrative review.
- **Endpoints:** senior health outcomes, daily step count, functional and cardiometabolic markers
- **Safety/adverse events:** No adverse-event signal specific to a step-floor intervention was extracted from this source in this batch.
- **Limitations:** Narrative review; many underlying studies are observational and outcome-specific.
- **Population mismatch/directness:** Senior-health context may not generalize to younger adults or to a specific Daily Step Floor protocol.
- **Boundary:** This source is observational, review-based, registry-only, or otherwise adjacent unless explicitly noted; it must not be promoted into direct Daily Step Floor protocol evidence.
