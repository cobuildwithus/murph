This source is included for **dose_response_cut_points**.

**Findings:** In 2576 free-living adults, higher baseline daily steps were linearly associated with lower all-cause mortality, with adjusted HR 0.94 per additional 1000 steps/day; a subset analysis also linked increases in steps over time with lower mortality risk.

**Why it matters:** Early objective-step prospective mortality context.

**Potential experiment signals:** daily step count, days meeting step floor, weekly mean steps, all-cause mortality.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as observational support for step-volume gradients only.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Free-living adults in Tasmania, Australia, mean age about 58.8 years at baseline.
- **Exposure/intervention:** Pedometer-measured daily steps at baseline; repeated steps available in a subset.
- **Comparator/control:** Lower versus higher daily steps and change over time.
- **Duration/follow-up:** About 10 years mortality follow-up; repeated monitoring after about 3.7 years in a subset.
- **Endpoints:** all-cause mortality, daily step count
- **Safety/adverse events:** No adverse-event signal specific to a step-floor intervention was extracted from this source in this batch.
- **Limitations:** Observational cohort; possible residual confounding and selection bias; step assessment was limited to short measurement periods.
- **Population mismatch/directness:** Not an intervention assigning a step floor.
- **Boundary:** This source is observational, review-based, registry-only, or otherwise adjacent unless explicitly noted; it must not be promoted into direct Daily Step Floor protocol evidence.
