This source is included for **baseline_plus_ramp_trials**.

**Findings:** PACE-UP found that a postal pedometer programme and a nurse-supported version both increased steps and MVPA at 12 months versus usual care, with no clear advantage of nurse support.

**Why it matters:** Large direct RCT evidence for a +3000/day step-goal programme in primary care.

**Potential experiment signals:** steps/day, MVPA bout minutes, delivery mode, adverse events.

**Protocol takeaway:** A low-touch postal/digital step-floor program can be effective without intensive nurse support.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

## Extracted study details

- **Population:** Primary care adults aged 45 to 75 years.
- **Intervention or exposure:** 12-week pedometer walking plan delivered by post or with nurse support; both aimed to add about 3000 steps/day or a 30-minute walk on at least 5 days/week.
- **Comparator or control:** Usual-care control.
- **Duration or follow-up:** 12-week intervention with 12-month assessment.
- **Endpoints:** steps/day, MVPA bouts, sedentary time, adverse events, body composition, quality of life, mood
- **Effect estimates or direction:** At 12 months, postal and nurse groups increased about +642 and +677 steps/day versus control; MVPA bouts increased about +33 and +35 min/week. There was no meaningful postal-versus-nurse difference.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** Adverse events were measured, but detailed adverse-event results were not extracted from accessible abstract sections.
- **Limitations:** Only about 10% of invited patients were recruited; nurse support did not clearly outperform postal delivery.
- **Population mismatch:** UK primary care adults aged 45-75; recruitment/participation selection may apply.
