This source is included for **mental_health_sleep_qol**.

**Findings:** A two-year cohort of older adults found a dose-response association between higher accelerometer-assessed daily steps and lower depressive-symptom risk, including lower risk above 3,500 and 7,000 steps/day versus <3,500.

**Why it matters:** Same-mechanism dose-response context connecting daily steps with depressive symptoms over time.

**Potential experiment signals:** biomarker:daily-steps, biomarker:depressive-symptoms.

**Protocol takeaway:** Higher daily steps are associated with lower depressive-symptom risk in older adults, but this should be treated as observational context, not a causal protocol result.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

**Directness boundary:** This source is classified as `same_mechanism` for Daily Step Floor. Do not promote adjacent, observational, registry/protocol, or clinical-population findings into direct protocol claims.

**Safety/adverse events:** Observational exposure study; adverse events were not applicable/extracted.
