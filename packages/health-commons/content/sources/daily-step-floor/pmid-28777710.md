This source is included for **measurement_validity**.

**Findings:** In people after stroke, Fitbit Zip and Garmin Vivofit reliability and accuracy depended on walking speed and placement side, with poorer accuracy in slower or more impaired walking.

**Why it matters:** Step floors in neurologic gait impairment may be affected by hemiparetic gait and tracker placement.

**Potential experiment signals:** daily_step_count, step_count_accuracy, device_placement_or_carriage, gait_speed_or_gait_aid_context.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context-only evidence to validate tracker placement in post-stroke or asymmetric gait populations.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Twenty-four people with stroke-induced hemiparesis.
- **Exposure/intervention:** Fitbit Zip on nonparetic hip and Garmin Vivofit on both wrists during walking tasks.
- **Comparator/control:** Video-determined step counts.
- **Duration/follow-up:** 6-minute walk test plus two 50-step reliability trials.
- **Endpoints:** step-count accuracy, test-retest reliability, walking-speed subgroup effects
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Fitbit Zip had excellent reliability and 4.2% error for walkers faster than 0.35 m/s. Garmin Vivofit reliability was high but accuracy varied by side and speed; paretic-side Garmin accuracy was poor in slower walkers and better in faster walkers.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse events or safety outcomes were reported in the accessible extract.
- **Limitations:** Small post-stroke sample.; Accuracy findings depend on placement side and walking speed.; Short walking tasks, not free-living daily steps.
- **Population mismatch:** Post-stroke measurement context; not general Daily Step Floor efficacy evidence.
