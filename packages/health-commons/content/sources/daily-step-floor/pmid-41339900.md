This source is included for **guidelines_external_protocol_context**.

**Findings:** In the 10,000 Steps Duesseldorf nonrandomized trial, intervention participants averaged 462 more steps/day than controls at follow-up but the 95% CI crossed zero, and the proportion reaching 10,000 steps/day was similar between cities.

**Why it matters:** Important contemporary external-protocol result that preserves a no-clear-advantage finding for 10,000-step community intervention replication.

**Potential experiment signals:** daily_step_count, step_goal_attainment, physical_activity_minutes.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as adjacent-variant evidence showing no clear advantage for a multilevel 10,000-step community intervention; do not generalize to direct Daily Step Floor efficacy.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Community-dwelling adults aged 25-75 years in Duesseldorf intervention and Wuppertal control city districts; 627 completed baseline and 553 follow-up, about 60% female and 60% intervention across timepoints/cities.
- **Exposure/intervention:** 12-month multilevel 10,000 Steps Duesseldorf intervention with website self-monitoring, workplace/community group competitions, media campaign, street signage, and 10,000 steps/day messaging.
- **Comparator/control:** Control city districts in Wuppertal.
- **Duration/follow-up:** 12-month intervention from April 2022 to April 2023 with follow-up assessments beginning May 2023.
- **Endpoints:** 7-day pedometer-recorded daily steps; proportion reaching 10,000 steps/day; physical activity promotion outcomes
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Propensity-score analyses found intervention participants walked an average of 462 steps/day more than controls at follow-up (95% CI -146 to 1070). The proportion reaching 10,000 steps/day was comparable between Duesseldorf and Wuppertal (26.4% vs 25.6%; OR 1.04, 95% CI 0.41 to 2.66).
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse events were reported in the accessible abstract/results extract.
- **Limitations:** Nonrandomized design; confidence intervals crossed no effect; multicomponent community intervention; pandemic/recruitment context and active baseline sample may limit generalizability.
- **Population mismatch:** External multilevel community intervention, not an individual Daily Step Floor protocol.
