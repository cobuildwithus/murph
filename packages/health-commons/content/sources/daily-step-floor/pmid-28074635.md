This source is included for **baseline_plus_ramp_trials**.

**Findings:** SMARTER found physician step-count prescription and pedometer monitoring increased steps by about +1190/day over 1 year versus control and improved some glycemic outcomes in type 2 diabetes, while arterial-stiffness results were inconclusive.

**Why it matters:** Direct clinical evidence for step-count prescription and baseline-plus +3000/day annual goal.

**Potential experiment signals:** steps/day, HbA1c, HOMA-IR, arterial stiffness, gait limitations.

**Protocol takeaway:** Step prescriptions can work in cardiometabolic clinical care, but Daily Step Floor should not imply arterial-stiffness benefit.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

## Extracted study details

- **Population:** Adults with type 2 diabetes and/or hypertension, BMI 25 to 40, without gait impairment.
- **Intervention or exposure:** Physician-reviewed pedometer logs with written step-count prescriptions targeting an overall +3000 steps/day over 1 year at an individualized rate.
- **Comparator or control:** Control/usual-care condition.
- **Duration or follow-up:** 1 year.
- **Endpoints:** steps/day, arterial stiffness/cfPWV, HbA1c, HOMA-IR, new type 2 diabetes
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Among 275 completers, active-arm steps increased by a net about +1190 steps/day (20%) versus control. cfPWV results were inconclusive; in participants with type 2 diabetes, HbA1c was lower by about -0.38% versus control and HOMA-IR improved.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** Eligibility excluded gait impairment; adverse-event details were not extracted from accessible records.
- **Limitations:** Clinical population, physician prescription, open-label design, and completion attrition.
- **Population mismatch:** Higher-risk adults with diabetes and/or hypertension; medical monitoring context.
