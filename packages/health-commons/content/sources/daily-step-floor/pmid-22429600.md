This source is included for **direct_step_floor_trials**.

**Findings:**

- `finding:pmid-22429600-maintained-steps` — Mean steps rose from 6,941 ± 3,047 at baseline to 9,327 ± 4,136 at 12 weeks, 8,804 ± 4,145 at 24 weeks, and 8,450 ± 3,855 at 48 weeks.
- `finding:pmid-22429600-counselling-boundary` — Both interventions increased and maintained step counts; accessible results reported no other significant health-related outcome effects, while consultations may have supported other activity or reduced sitting.

**Why it matters:** Shows a realistic step-increment floor can be sustained over a year in low-active adults.

**Potential experiment signals:**

- daily steps
- maintenance at 12 months
- quality of life
- mood
- sitting time

**Protocol takeaway:** A pedometer-based step-increment program maintained higher steps at 12 months; added consultations may help non-walking activity but were not a clear step-count enhancer.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

## Extraction boundaries

- **Directness:** `direct_protocol`.
- **Population:** Low-active adults in Scotland, mostly women
- **Intervention/exposure:** Pedometer-based walking program aiming to add about 3,000 steps/day above baseline, with physical activity consultations in one arm.
- **Comparator/control:** Pedometer walking program with minimal advice after waiting-list control.
- **Duration/follow-up:** 12-week intervention with 24- and 48-week follow-up
- **Endpoints extracted:** daily steps, self-reported physical activity, sitting time, mood, quality of life, health-related outcomes.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse-event signal was extracted from the accessible source record; absence of extracted adverse events should not be interpreted as proof of no adverse events unless the finding explicitly says so.
- **Limitations:** Only 61% returned final 12-month data; both groups eventually received pedometer materials; no long-term untreated control.
- **Population mismatch:** Low-active Scottish adult sample, mostly women; not necessarily smartphone-measured or unsupervised wellness users.
- **Artifact/rights boundary:** `open_access`; no copyrighted PDF is stored in Git in this draft.
