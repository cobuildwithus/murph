This source is included for **baseline_plus_ramp_trials**.

**Findings:** An 8-week clustered trial in older adults used 5000 then 7500 steps/day goals and improved light activity, sedentary time, BMI/waist, and sarcopenia indicators, but not MVPA, with several feasibility limitations.

**Why it matters:** Modern fixed-floor step-up implementation in older adults with body composition/sarcopenia signals.

**Potential experiment signals:** 5000/7500 step-goal adherence, sedentary time, light PA, MVPA, muscle strength, waist circumference.

**Protocol takeaway:** Step-by-step fixed floors may benefit older adults, but MVPA should not be assumed.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

## Extracted study details

- **Population:** Community-dwelling adults older than 60 years from four Taipei community centers.
- **Intervention or exposure:** Wearable activity tracker with step-by-step goals: 5000 steps/day for 4 weeks, then 7500 steps/day for 4 weeks.
- **Comparator or control:** Control group.
- **Duration or follow-up:** 8 weeks.
- **Endpoints:** total physical activity, light-intensity physical activity, MVPA, sedentary time, skeletal muscle index, muscle strength, BMI, waist circumference
- **Effect estimates or direction:** The intervention increased total and light-intensity physical activity, skeletal muscle index, and muscle strength, and reduced sedentary time, BMI, and waist circumference versus control; MVPA did not improve.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** Adverse-event reporting was not extracted from accessible records.
- **Limitations:** Small cluster trial, subjective physical-activity measurement, unknown wearable adherence, COVID-related community-center closures, and age imbalance.
- **Population mismatch:** Older Taiwanese community-center participants; sarcopenia-focused indicators may not generalize.
