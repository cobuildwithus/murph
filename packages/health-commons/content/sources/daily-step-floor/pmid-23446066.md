This source is included for **safety_special_populations**.

**Findings:** In a 57-participant chronic low-back-pain feasibility RCT, an 8-week pedometer walking program increased steps by about 2,776 steps/day and reported eight minor related adverse events in five of 40 walking participants.

**Why it matters:** Provides step-increase feasibility and minor adverse-event data for a chronic-pain population.

**Potential experiment signals:** daily-step-count, pain intensity, disability, walking adherence, minor adverse-events.

**Protocol takeaway:** Daily Step Floor can use chronic-pain walking trials as safety context, but should monitor pain flares and musculoskeletal events.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

**Directness boundary:** clinical_supervised adjacent walking intervention. Do not promote this source into direct Daily Step Floor claims beyond the stated claim-use boundary.

**Safety/adverse events:** Eight minor related adverse events were reported in five of 40 walking-program participants (13%).

**Limitations and mismatch:** Feasibility trial; underpowered for clinical endpoints; chronic-pain population and education/advice cointervention. Chronic low back pain clinical sample; adjacent safety/feasibility evidence only.
