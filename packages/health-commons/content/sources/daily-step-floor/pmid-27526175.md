This source is included for **sedentary_activity_pattern**.

**Findings:** Workplace sitting decreased by approximately 99.1 minutes per 8-hour workday at 3 months and 45.4 minutes at 12 months; standing and prolonged-sitting patterns improved, while stepping effects were not significant or meaningful in the accessible abstract.

**Why it matters:** Strong reminder that sedentary-time interventions can succeed through standing, not necessarily through step accumulation.

**Potential experiment signals:** workplace sitting, daily sitting, standing time, prolonged sitting, stepping.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context-only evidence that Daily Step Floor should measure steps and sedentary bouts separately.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Desk-based office workers in 14 worksites.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Cluster-randomized multi-component workplace sitting-time reduction intervention.
- **Comparator/control:** Control worksites.
- **Duration/follow-up:** 3 months and 12 months
- **Endpoints:** workplace sitting, daily sitting, standing time, prolonged sitting, stepping
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Workplace sitting decreased by approximately 99.1 minutes per 8-hour workday at 3 months and 45.4 minutes at 12 months; standing and prolonged-sitting patterns improved, while stepping effects were not significant or meaningful in the accessible abstract.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse events were extracted from the accessible summary.
- **Limitations:** Office-worker and environmental context.; The intervention targeted sitting/standing rather than daily step minimums.; Reduced sitting did not imply increased stepping.
- **Population mismatch:** Adjacent workplace sitting-pattern intervention; not direct Daily Step Floor evidence.
