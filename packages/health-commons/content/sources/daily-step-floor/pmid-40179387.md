This source is included for **direct_protocol_reviews**.

**Findings:** Daily step count and physical activity time: The review reported increased physical activity time (SMD 0.28; 95% CI 0.10 to 0.47) and daily step count (SMD 0.58; 95% CI 0.33 to 0.83) immediately after intervention versus usual care.; Physical activity outcomes: Compared with conventional interventions, wearable tracker-based interventions were not superior for physical activity outcomes in the extracted results.; Body composition and physical function: The review found no significant effects on BMI, body fat, timed up and go, or chair-stand performance in the extracted results.

**Why it matters:** This source tests the same wearable-feedback mechanism in older adults and keeps special-population claims separated from general adult protocol claims.

**Potential experiment signals:** daily-step-count, weekly-active-minutes, sedentary-minutes, body-mass-index, physical-function.

**Protocol takeaway:** Wearable activity trackers can improve steps and activity in older adults versus usual care, but this should be treated as context-only and not a direct general-population claim.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
