This source is included for **sedentary_activity_pattern**.

**Findings:** Secondary analysis found sedentary time and prolonged sedentary-bout changes were not statistically significant or meaningfully different between intervention groups; step increases were associated with lower sedentary time and prolonged bouts regardless of randomized assignment.

**Why it matters:** Important negative/mixed evidence: simply giving a tracker may not change sedentary patterns.

**Potential experiment signals:** sedentary time, prolonged sedentary bouts, step count.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context-only evidence that tracker ownership alone is insufficient for sedentary-bout reduction.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Employees in a 12-month consumer-wearable activity tracker randomized controlled trial.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Consumer wearable activity tracker, with or without incentives.
- **Comparator/control:** Control or non-tracker arms from the parent trial.
- **Duration/follow-up:** 12 months
- **Endpoints:** sedentary time, prolonged sedentary bouts, step count
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Secondary analysis found sedentary time and prolonged sedentary-bout changes were not statistically significant or meaningfully different between intervention groups; step increases were associated with lower sedentary time and prolonged bouts regardless of randomized assignment.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse events were extracted from the accessible summary.
- **Limitations:** Secondary analysis of a parent trial not designed primarily for sedentary outcomes.; Employee population and incentives may not generalize.; Associational step-sedentary relationships within the trial do not prove causal protocol effects.
- **Population mismatch:** Adjacent consumer-tracker RCT secondary analysis, not a Daily Step Floor protocol.
