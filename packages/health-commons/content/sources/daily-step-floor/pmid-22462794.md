This source is included for **cadence_intensity_bouts**.

**Findings:** Narrative review framing cadence as a useful free-living descriptor of ambulatory intensity patterns, including cadence bands and peak cadence metrics.

**Why it matters:** Cadence can help interpret whether a step floor was achieved slowly or briskly without changing the primary protocol target from daily step volume.

**Potential experiment signals:** walking_cadence, cadence_bands, peak_cadence, daily_step_count.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use cadence as optional descriptive context, not as a required Daily Step Floor target.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Free-living ambulatory behavior literature; no single participant sample because this is a review.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Cadence (steps/minute) as a representation of free-living ambulatory behavior and activity intensity patterns.
- **Comparator/control:** Not applicable; narrative review.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Review; source studies varied.
- **Endpoints:** Cadence bands; peak cadence; mean cadence; free-living ambulatory behavior descriptors.
- **Effect estimates or direction:** The review argued that time-stamped step data can describe ambulatory intensity patterns through cadence metrics, while emphasizing the need for standardized outputs and calibration.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse events or intervention safety outcomes were reported.
- **Limitations:** Narrative review; not a Daily Step Floor intervention; cadence methods and reporting standards were still developing.
- **Population mismatch:** Mechanism and measurement context only; does not test a daily minimum step prescription.
