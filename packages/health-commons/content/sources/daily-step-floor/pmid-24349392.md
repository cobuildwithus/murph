This source is included for **baseline_plus_ramp_trials**.

**Findings:** In a 20-person pilot RCT, adaptive daily step goals produced larger step increases and much higher goal attainment than a static 10,000-step target, with no adverse events reported.

**Why it matters:** Direct evidence for adaptive/baseline-responsive daily step goals over a fixed high floor.

**Potential experiment signals:** steps/day, daily goal attainment, adaptive goal success rate, adverse events.

**Protocol takeaway:** Adaptive floors may improve adherence compared with an immediate 10,000-step goal.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

## Extracted study details

- **Population:** Inactive overweight adults aged 18 to 65 years.
- **Intervention or exposure:** Adaptive daily step goals calculated from the 60th percentile of a moving 9-day window plus micro-incentives.
- **Comparator or control:** Static 10,000 steps/day goal with comparable monitoring/incentive structure.
- **Duration or follow-up:** 6 months.
- **Endpoints:** steps/day, goal attainment, adverse events
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Static intervention increased about 1598 steps/day and adaptive intervention increased about 2728 steps/day; between-group difference was about +1130 steps/day (Cohen d=.74). Adaptive goals were met on 58.2% of days versus 22.5% for static 10,000-step days.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse events were reported in the accessible full-text summary.
- **Limitations:** Very small pilot sample, overweight adults, adaptive algorithm plus incentives, and not a pure floor-only intervention.
- **Population mismatch:** Inactive overweight adults; not a broad general population.
