This source is included for **safety_special_populations**.

**Findings:** In a 60-participant dialysis pilot RCT, pedometers plus weekly step goals increased daily steps by about 2,256 steps/day at 3 months versus usual care, but symptom and physical-performance differences were not significant and steps reverted after follow-up.

**Why it matters:** Shows step-goal walking can increase steps in dialysis patients short-term but requires clinical supervision and durability monitoring.

**Potential experiment signals:** daily-step-count, weekly steps, symptoms, physical performance, heart-rate variability, adverse-events.

**Protocol takeaway:** Do not apply Daily Step Floor generically to dialysis patients without nephrology/clinical clearance and symptom off-ramps.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

**Directness boundary:** clinical_supervised adjacent variant. Do not promote this source into direct Daily Step Floor claims beyond the stated claim-use boundary.

**Safety/adverse events:** Dialysis patients are a medically fragile population; walking goals should be clinically supervised and symptom-monitored.

**Limitations and mismatch:** Pilot trial in one geographic setting; walking does not capture all physical activity; effects did not persist after intervention. Dialysis population; safety-only special-population evidence.
