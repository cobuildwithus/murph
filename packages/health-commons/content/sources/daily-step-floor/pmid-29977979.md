This source is included for **measurement_validity**.

**Findings:** StepWatch was much more accurate than ActivPAL or ActiGraph in very old adults during observed walking trials, with gait speed and assistive-device use affecting accuracy.

**Why it matters:** The same daily step floor could be undercounted by some monitors in older adults.

**Potential experiment signals:** daily_step_count, step_count_accuracy, device_placement_or_carriage, gait_speed_or_gait_aid_context.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context-only evidence favoring validated devices in slow-gait older-adult contexts.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Forty-three older adults, mean age 87.0 years, predominantly female.
- **Exposure/intervention:** StepWatch, ActivPAL, and ActiGraph monitors during 100-step walking trials.
- **Comparator/control:** Directly observed steps.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Two walking trials in a single assessment
- **Endpoints:** step-count accuracy, gait-speed subgroup accuracy, assistive-device subgroup accuracy
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Overall accuracy was reported as StepWatch 99.0% ± 1.5, ActivPAL 93.7% ± 11.1, and ActiGraph 51.4% ± 35.7. Accuracy varied by assistive-device use and gait-speed category, while StepWatch remained highly accurate across conditions.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse events or safety outcomes were reported in the accessible extract.
- **Limitations:** Short observed trials rather than free-living daily monitoring.; Very old adult sample may not generalize to younger users.; Monitor models and placement matter.
- **Population mismatch:** Older-adult measurement context, not Daily Step Floor intervention evidence.
- **Artifact rights:** open_access
