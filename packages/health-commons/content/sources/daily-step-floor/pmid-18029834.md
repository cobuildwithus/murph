This source is included for **direct_protocol_reviews**.

**Findings:** Daily step count: Across 8 RCTs with 277 participants, pedometer users increased physical activity by a mean difference of 2491 steps/day versus control (95% CI 1098 to 3885; p<0.001); observational studies showed a 2183 steps/day increase from baseline.; BMI and blood pressure: The review reported modest reductions in BMI (mean difference -0.38) and systolic blood pressure (mean difference -3.8 mmHg); diastolic blood pressure change was small (-0.3 mmHg).; Lipids and fasting glucose: The review did not find statistically significant changes in lipid levels or fasting glucose in the extracted summary.

**Why it matters:** This is an early, dense synthesis of pedometer interventions and anchors the claim that self-monitoring daily steps can raise step counts in adults.

**Potential experiment signals:** daily-step-count, body-mass-index, morning-blood-pressure, fasting-glucose, serum-lipids.

**Protocol takeaway:** A step-count floor is aligned with trial evidence that pedometer-based self-monitoring increases daily steps, but cardiometabolic effects should be treated as modest and mixed.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.
