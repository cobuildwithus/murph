This source is included for **direct_step_floor_trials**.

**Findings:**

- `finding:pmid-26895847-walking-program-steps` — Both groups significantly increased daily steps from baseline; the online-support group increased by 1,401 steps/day and the no-support group by 2,461 steps/day, with no significant between-group difference.
- `finding:pmid-26895847-support-tools-low-use` — The accessible source reported low use of the online social support tools and no greater psychosocial or health improvements in the support arm.

**Why it matters:** Direct walking self-monitoring trial that tests whether online support adds benefit to step goals.

**Potential experiment signals:**

- daily steps
- support-tool engagement
- psychosocial outcomes

**Protocol takeaway:** Core walking-program elements increased steps, but online social support did not add a clear advantage.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction boundaries

- **Directness:** `direct_protocol`.
- **Population:** Insufficiently active adults
- **Intervention/exposure:** Walking program with pedometer, step goals, relevant websites, and online social support tools.
- **Comparator/control:** Walking program with pedometer, step goals, and relevant websites without online social support tools.
- **Duration/follow-up:** 12 weeks
- **Endpoints extracted:** daily steps, psychosocial outcomes, health outcomes, support tool use.
