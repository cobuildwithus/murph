This source is included for **baseline_plus_ramp_trials**.

**Findings:** A 12-week graduated pedometer and consultation program targeting +3000 steps/day above baseline produced an approximately +3022 steps/day between-group change versus control and improved leisure walking, sitting, and positive affect.

**Why it matters:** Direct RCT evidence for a +3000/day baseline-plus step target.

**Potential experiment signals:** steps/day, five-day weekly success, leisure walking minutes, sitting time, positive affect.

**Protocol takeaway:** A +3000/day baseline-plus floor can produce measurable step gains over 12 weeks in low-active adults.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

## Extracted study details

- **Population:** Low-active Scottish community adults, mostly women, mean age about 49 years.
- **Intervention or exposure:** Pedometer-based community walking programme with consultation targeting +3000 steps/day above baseline on 5 days/week.
- **Comparator or control:** Control group.
- **Duration or follow-up:** 12 weeks.
- **Endpoints:** steps/day, leisure walking minutes, sitting time, positive affect, other health outcomes
- **Effect estimates or direction:** The intervention increased steps from about 6802 to 9977 steps/day while control changed from about 6924 to 7078; mean between-group change was about +3022 steps/day (p<.001). Leisure walking and positive affect increased and sitting decreased, while other health outcomes were not significant.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** Adverse-event reporting was not extracted from accessible records.
- **Limitations:** Twelve-week community sample, attrition (15 withdrew by week 12), mostly female.
- **Population mismatch:** Low-active Scottish community adults; not a clinical population.
