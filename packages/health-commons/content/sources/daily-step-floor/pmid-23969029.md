This source is included for **safety_special_populations**.

**Findings:** In a 229-participant chronic low-back-pain RCT, an internet-mediated pedometer walking program improved disability at 6 months in complete-case analysis, but all-case analysis and 12-month persistence were not clearly significant.

**Why it matters:** Shows a pedometer-mediated walking intervention in chronic pain can have mixed functional effects and requires separate safety tracking.

**Potential experiment signals:** daily-step-count, pain-related disability, walking adherence, adverse-events.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as adjacent chronic-pain context; avoid direct protocol efficacy claims and cite the separate adverse-event extraction for safety rates.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

**Directness boundary:** clinical_supervised adjacent walking intervention. Do not promote this source into direct Daily Step Floor claims beyond the stated claim-use boundary.

**Safety/adverse events:** Adverse events from this parent trial were reported in a separate descriptive safety paper; do not infer event rates from this source alone.

**Limitations and mismatch:** Chronic low back pain veteran sample; internet-mediated intervention; mixed persistence and sensitivity analyses. Clinical pain population; adjacent, not general protocol evidence.
