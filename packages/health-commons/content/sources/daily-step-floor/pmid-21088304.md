This source is included for **baseline_plus_ramp_trials**.

**Findings:** A 12-week worksite pedometer program using incremental progression toward 10,000 steps/day found favorable changes in some metabolic syndrome components among women who increased steps, but the design was quasi-experimental and systolic blood-pressure change was not independent of weight loss.

**Why it matters:** Supports gradual ramping toward a large fixed floor while preserving non-randomized limitations.

**Potential experiment signals:** step increase >3000/day, waist circumference, fasting glucose, resting heart rate, weight/BMI.

**Protocol takeaway:** A progressive ramp may be more feasible than immediate 10,000 steps/day for sedentary participants.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

## Extracted study details

- **Population:** Sedentary overweight middle-aged women in a worksite pedometer program.
- **Intervention or exposure:** Gradual weekly progression toward 10,000 steps/day; active grouping was based on increasing daily activity by more than 3000 steps/day.
- **Comparator or control:** Participants remaining sedentary/control group after program classification.
- **Duration or follow-up:** 12 weeks.
- **Endpoints:** steps/day, waist circumference, fasting glucose, resting heart rate, weight, BMI, systolic blood pressure, metabolic syndrome components
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Active participants showed favorable changes in some metabolic syndrome components. Between-group changes were reported for resting heart rate, weight, BMI, and systolic blood pressure; systolic blood pressure changes were not independent of weight loss.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** Adverse-event reporting was not extracted from accessible records.
- **Limitations:** Quasi-experimental post hoc grouping, worksite sample, overweight women only.
- **Population mismatch:** Sedentary overweight women; not all adults.
