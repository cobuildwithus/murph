This source is included for **measurement_validity**.

**Findings:** A 20-participant controlled study assessed accelerometer pedometer step accuracy and reliability across wrist, waist, and shoe-midsole placements during walking, running, and stair climbing.

**Why it matters:** Attachment location and activity type can affect step counts, especially when stairs or running are part of a daily floor.

**Potential experiment signals:** daily_step_count, step_count_accuracy, device_placement_or_carriage, gait_speed_or_gait_aid_context.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context-only evidence to standardize pedometer placement and activity context.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Twenty healthy adults.
- **Exposure/intervention:** Accelerometer-based pedometers placed on the wrist, waist, and shoe midsole during walking, running, and stair climbing.
- **Comparator/control:** Simultaneously video-recorded visual step counts for accuracy; repeated pedometer recording one week later for reliability.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Initial activity protocol plus repeat recording one week later.
- **Endpoints:** step-count accuracy, attachment location, walking/running/stair-climbing conditions, test-retest reliability
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Accessible extracts report that accuracy and reliability were assessed across wrist, waist, and midsole locations during walking, running, and stair climbing; detailed numerical estimates were not fully extracted for this draft.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse events or safety outcomes were reported in the accessible extract.
- **Limitations:** Healthy adult sample.; Detailed condition-specific numerical results were not fully extracted.; Short controlled activities may not match free-living step-floor behavior.
- **Population mismatch:** Healthy placement validation; not Daily Step Floor efficacy evidence.
- **Artifact rights:** open_access
