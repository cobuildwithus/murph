This source is included for **measurement_validity**.

**Findings:** The Omron HJ-203-ED showed placement- and speed-dependent step-count accuracy; free-living absolute percentage error remained high for neck and pants-pocket placement in the accessible extract.

**Why it matters:** Step-floor counts can change when a device is moved from hip/pocket to neck, bag, or other positions.

**Potential experiment signals:** daily_step_count, step_count_accuracy, device_placement_or_carriage, gait_speed_or_gait_aid_context.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context-only evidence to keep pedometer placement consistent and document nonstandard positions.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Forty healthy adults in controlled tests and 54 adults in free-living testing.
- **Exposure/intervention:** Omron HJ-203-ED pedometer worn in pants pocket, carrier bag, and/or around the neck.
- **Comparator/control:** Observed or reference step count during 20-step, stair, treadmill, and free-living conditions.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Controlled tests plus one-day free-living monitoring
- **Endpoints:** step-count validity by wearing position, reliability, controlled versus free-living accuracy
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Controlled absolute percentage error ranged from 0.1% to 14.0%; accuracy varied by wearing position and walking speed, with poor pants-pocket accuracy at slower speeds and better performance around the neck. Free-living APE was 30.7%-36.9%, with no statistical difference between pants pocket and neck.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse events or safety outcomes were reported in the accessible extract.
- **Limitations:** Healthy samples may not represent people with gait impairment.; One-day free-living monitoring has limited stability.; Device model and algorithm may not represent current pedometers.
- **Population mismatch:** Measurement context only; no Daily Step Floor intervention or outcome.
