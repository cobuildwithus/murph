This source is included for **safety_special_populations**.

**Findings:** In a 29-participant RCT of diabetes with peripheral neuropathy, supervised weight-bearing exercise increased daily steps and six-minute walk distance more than non-weight-bearing exercise, while non-weight-bearing exercise showed greater HbA1c improvement.

**Why it matters:** Shows that supervised weight-bearing exercise can raise steps in diabetic peripheral neuropathy, but with clinical context and mixed metabolic results.

**Potential experiment signals:** daily-step-count, six-minute-walk-distance, HbA1c, foot symptoms, adverse-events.

**Protocol takeaway:** Do not use as direct Daily Step Floor evidence; use as a supervised special-population comparator and safety boundary.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

**Directness boundary:** clinical_supervised adjacent variant. Do not promote this source into direct Daily Step Floor claims beyond the stated claim-use boundary.

**Safety/adverse events:** Diabetic peripheral neuropathy requires foot monitoring; this small trial supports supervised rather than unsupervised weight-bearing exercise.

**Limitations and mismatch:** Very small sample and supervised exercise design; not a daily step-floor trial and not generalizable to active ulcers. Diabetes peripheral neuropathy; adjacent supervised exercise evidence only.
