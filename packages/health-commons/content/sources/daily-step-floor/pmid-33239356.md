This source is included for **sedentary_activity_pattern**.

**Findings:** Harmonised meta-analysis found sedentary time and MVPA were jointly associated with all-cause mortality; observational results do not establish a causal effect of a step-floor intervention.

**Why it matters:** Supports the importance of measuring sedentary and activity exposure together.

**Potential experiment signals:** sedentary time, moderate-to-vigorous physical activity, all-cause mortality.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context-only observational evidence; do not claim Daily Step Floor reduces mortality.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Middle-aged and older adults from 9 accelerometer cohorts.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Observed joint categories of accelerometer-measured sedentary time and moderate-to-vigorous physical activity.
- **Comparator/control:** Lower sedentary time and higher/lower physical activity categories.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Follow-up ranged across cohorts; accessible summaries reported 4.0 to 14.5 years.
- **Endpoints:** sedentary time, moderate-to-vigorous physical activity, all-cause mortality
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Harmonised meta-analysis found sedentary time and MVPA were jointly associated with all-cause mortality; observational results do not establish a causal effect of a step-floor intervention.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** Mortality was the outcome; no intervention adverse events apply.
- **Limitations:** Observational cohort design with residual confounding possible.; Moderate-to-vigorous activity and sedentary time are not daily step-floor adherence.; Middle-aged/older cohorts may not generalize to all Daily Step Floor users.
- **Population mismatch:** Observational context only.
- **Artifact candidates and rights status:** open_access; no copyrighted PDF is included in this draft artifact package.
