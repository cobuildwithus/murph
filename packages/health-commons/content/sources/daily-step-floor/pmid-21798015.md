This source is included for **dose_response_cut_points**.

**Findings:** The review reported that healthy adults typically accumulate about 4000–18,000 steps/day, characterized 10,000 steps/day as a reasonable healthy-adult target, summarized pedometer interventions as increasing activity by roughly 2000–2500 steps/day, and used 100 steps/minute and 3000 steps in 30 minutes as practical moderate-intensity heuristics.

**Why it matters:** Key background for the 10,000-step convention and more attainable incremental targets.

**Potential experiment signals:** daily step count, days meeting step floor, weekly mean steps, moderate-to-vigorous physical activity translation, pedometer intervention step increases.

**Protocol takeaway:** Supports contextual threshold framing and ramp design, but not direct protocol efficacy.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Apparently healthy adults aged roughly 20–65 years.
- **Exposure/intervention:** Daily step count, pedometer intervention step changes, and cadence-based moderate-intensity translations.
- **Comparator/control:** Normative adult step ranges, intervention changes, and guideline-equivalent step levels.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Not applicable; narrative review of adult literature.
- **Endpoints:** daily step count, moderate-to-vigorous physical activity translation, pedometer intervention step increases
- **Safety/adverse events:** No adverse-event signal specific to a step-floor intervention was extracted from this source in this batch.
- **Limitations:** Narrative synthesis; cut-points and translations are approximations and not individualized prescriptions.
- **Population mismatch/directness:** Healthy adult context may not apply to older adults, special populations, or users starting from very low baselines.
- **Boundary:** This source is observational, review-based, registry-only, or otherwise adjacent unless explicitly noted; it must not be promoted into direct Daily Step Floor protocol evidence.
