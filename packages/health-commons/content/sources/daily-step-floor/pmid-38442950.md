This source is included for **dose_response_cut_points**.

**Findings:** Among 72,174 participants, any steps above the 2200 steps/day reference were associated with lower mortality and incident CVD risk across low and high sedentary-time groups; optimal mortality risk occurred around 9000–10,500 steps/day and minimal meaningful doses around 4000–4500 steps/day.

**Why it matters:** Important context for step floors among high-sedentary people.

**Potential experiment signals:** daily step count, days meeting step floor, weekly mean steps, all-cause mortality, incident cardiovascular disease, sedentary time.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context for sedentary-time-aware thresholds and caveats.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** UK Biobank adults with valid wrist accelerometer data, excluding baseline CVD or cancer in main analysis.
- **Exposure/intervention:** Daily steps and sedentary time levels (<10.5 or ≥10.5 h/day).
- **Comparator/control:** 2200 steps/day reference; low versus high sedentary time strata.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Mean follow-up about 6.9 years.
- **Endpoints:** all-cause mortality, incident cardiovascular disease, daily step count, sedentary time
- **Safety/adverse events:** No adverse-event signal specific to a step-floor intervention was extracted from this source in this batch.
- **Limitations:** Observational UK Biobank cohort, single accelerometer window, residual confounding, and limited representativeness.
- **Population mismatch/directness:** Sedentary-time-stratified cohort context is not a Daily Step Floor intervention.
- **Boundary:** This source is observational, review-based, registry-only, or otherwise adjacent unless explicitly noted; it must not be promoted into direct Daily Step Floor protocol evidence.
