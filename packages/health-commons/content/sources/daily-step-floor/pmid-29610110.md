This source is included for **measurement_validity**.

**Findings:** In 130 people with chronic diseases, nine commercial trackers showed low validity for ADL step counts against video; most trackers underestimated and only Fitbit One lacked a significant systematic difference in the accessible extract.

**Why it matters:** Step floors occur during real-world ADLs, not just straight walking; ADL patterns can degrade tracker validity.

**Potential experiment signals:** daily_step_count, step_count_accuracy, device_placement_or_carriage, gait_speed_or_gait_aid_context.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context-only evidence that ADL step counts in chronic disease can be substantially unreliable across many commercial trackers.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** One hundred thirty persons with chronic diseases receiving physiotherapy.
- **Exposure/intervention:** Nine commercially available trackers during standardized activities of daily living.
- **Comparator/control:** Video-recorded step count as gold standard.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Standardized ADL protocol; data collection over a 1-year period.
- **Endpoints:** ADL step count, correlation with video gold standard, Bland-Altman agreement, systematic bias
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Correlations between tracker step counts and gold standard were low (range −.02 to .33). All trackers except Fitbit One had a significant systematic difference; Activ8 overestimated on average and the other eight trackers underestimated.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** The accessible extract reports no adverse events; all patients were able to perform the protocol.
- **Limitations:** Participants using walking aids and asymmetric gait were excluded.; ADL protocol is standardized rather than fully free-living.; Device algorithms and time slots were not available.
- **Population mismatch:** Chronic-disease ADL measurement context; not a Daily Step Floor intervention.
- **Artifact rights:** open_access
