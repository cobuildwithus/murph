This source is included for **measurement_validity**.

**Findings:** In older adults, step monitor accuracy varied by device and walking context; accessible extracts emphasize careful device selection for slower walking paces.

**Why it matters:** Older adults may walk at speeds where some monitors lose accuracy.

**Potential experiment signals:** daily_step_count, step_count_accuracy, device_placement_or_carriage, gait_speed_or_gait_aid_context.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context-only evidence to consider gait speed and device choice in older adults.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Twenty-nine community-dwelling older adults aged 67.70 ± 6.07 years.
- **Exposure/intervention:** Three pedometers and an Actical accelerometer during indoor track and treadmill walking.
- **Comparator/control:** Actual/manual step counts.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Single-session 200 m indoor track walk plus treadmill walking at three speeds.
- **Endpoints:** step-count accuracy, device and walking-speed effects
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Accessible extracts report no statistical difference between monitor step counts and actual steps during self-selected pace walking; the study emphasizes deliberate monitor selection, especially at slower paces.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse events or safety outcomes were reported in the accessible extract.
- **Limitations:** Short walking tests rather than free-living measurement.; Detailed device-level estimates were not fully available in the accessible extract.; Older adult sample only.
- **Population mismatch:** Measurement-context evidence; no step-floor intervention.
- **Artifact rights:** open_access
