**Findings:**

- `finding:pmid-22200586-walking-time-mixed` — The abstract-level evidence indicates the pedometer/toolkit increased walking time in older individuals and among completers, but reported no effect on aerobic fitness or self-rated health.

**Why it matters:** Direct pedometer self-monitoring/toolkit trial in a low-activity population, with mixed endpoint results.

**Potential experiment signals:**

- walking time
- daily steps
- aerobic fitness
- self-rated health

**Protocol takeaway:** A pedometer/toolkit package may increase walking time in some low-activity adults, especially older/completer groups, but it did not clearly improve aerobic fitness or self-rated health.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

## Extraction boundaries

- **Directness:** `direct_protocol`.
- **Population:** Danish adults with low levels of physical activity or fitness
- **Intervention/exposure:** Pedometer combined with a self-guided intervention toolkit and walking-step materials.
- **Comparator/control:** Control condition without the pedometer/toolkit package.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Intervention/follow-up duration not fully extracted from accessible abstract-level source
- **Endpoints extracted:** walking time, physical activity, aerobic fitness, self-rated health.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** No adverse-event signal was extracted from the accessible source record; absence of extracted adverse events should not be interpreted as proof of no adverse events unless the finding explicitly says so.
- **Limitations:** Accessible extraction did not verify an explicit daily step floor or full follow-up details; effects varied by age/completion; no clear fitness/self-rated health benefit.
