This source is included for **safety_special_populations**.

**Findings:** In a 60-participant randomized study of older adults with cognitive impairment, a telemonitoring-based walking prescription increased daily steps at 12 and 24 weeks compared with control conditions, with no between-group withdrawal difference reported.

**Why it matters:** Shows supervised/mobile walking prescription can raise steps in a cognitively impaired older-adult population, but this is a special population.

**Potential experiment signals:** daily-step-count, withdrawals, falls, adherence, caregiver/supervision needs.

**Protocol takeaway:** Daily Step Floor should not assume cognitively impaired users can self-manage step floors without supervision and fall-risk planning.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness boundary:** clinical_supervised adjacent variant. Do not promote this source into direct Daily Step Floor claims beyond the stated claim-use boundary.

**Safety/adverse events:** Withdrawals did not differ among groups; cognitive impairment raises adherence, supervision, and fall-risk considerations.

**Limitations and mismatch:** Older adults with cognitive impairment; small study; mobile-health prescription rather than generic daily floor. Cognitive-impairment special population; context-only for general protocol.
