This source is included for **measurement_validity**.

**Findings:** Fitbit step counts were accurate without aids at the hip but became unreliable with gait aids, especially wrist counts during four-wheeled walker use.

**Why it matters:** Gait aids can disrupt wrist/hip step detection, causing major errors in a step-floor protocol.

**Potential experiment signals:** daily_step_count, step_count_accuracy, device_placement_or_carriage, gait_speed_or_gait_aid_context.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context-only evidence that cane/crutch/walker use is a measurement boundary requiring validation.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Nine healthy participants.
- **Exposure/intervention:** Fitbit step counters worn on wrist and hip while walking with no aid, canes, crutches, and walkers.
- **Comparator/control:** Actual/observed step count using Bland-Altman analyses.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Eight walking tests
- **Endpoints:** step-count bias, limits of agreement, gait-aid effects, wrist versus hip placement
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Hip-worn Fitbit agreement without gait aids was excellent (bias −2.9, LOA −8.72 to 2.95). Canes/crutches showed wide bias and limits of agreement. Wrist counter during four-wheeled walker testing showed very little agreement with actual steps (bias 91.33, LOA 64.1 to 118.6), indicating exceptionally unreliable counts.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse events or safety outcomes were reported in the accessible extract.
- **Limitations:** Very small healthy sample; gait aids were simulated rather than used by clinical patients.; Short walking tests.; Fitbit model and placement specifics may limit generalizability.
- **Population mismatch:** Healthy simulated gait-aid context; relevant to measurement boundary, not direct Daily Step Floor efficacy.
- **Artifact rights:** open_access
