This source is included for **sedentary_activity_pattern**.

**Findings:** All groups tended to improve steps, MVPA, and sedentary behavior, but device comparisons were highly uncertain; at 16 weeks, consumer tracker arms did not show a clear advantage over pedometer and had wide confidence intervals for sedentary behavior.

**Why it matters:** Directly cautions that device sophistication alone may not change activity patterns.

**Potential experiment signals:** steps, moderate-to-vigorous physical activity, sedentary behavior, device comparison.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context-only evidence that a tracker is a measurement/support tool, not the intervention by itself.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Inactive adults allocated by workplace to pedometer or consumer activity tracker arms.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Fitbit One or Jawbone UP consumer activity tracker for 8 weeks without additional active behavior-change components.
- **Comparator/control:** Digi-Walker pedometer.
- **Duration/follow-up:** 8-week intervention with outcomes assessed to 16 weeks.
- **Endpoints:** steps, moderate-to-vigorous physical activity, sedentary behavior, device comparison
- **Effect estimates or direction:** All groups tended to improve steps, MVPA, and sedentary behavior, but device comparisons were highly uncertain; at 16 weeks, consumer tracker arms did not show a clear advantage over pedometer and had wide confidence intervals for sedentary behavior.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse events were extracted from the accessible full-text summary.
- **Limitations:** Small RCT.; No active behavior-change components beyond device use.; Workplace allocation and wide confidence intervals limit inference.
- **Population mismatch:** Adjacent device-comparison RCT; not a Daily Step Floor protocol.
- **Artifact candidates and rights status:** open_access; no copyrighted PDF is included in this draft artifact package.
