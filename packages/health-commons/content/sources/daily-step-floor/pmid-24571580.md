This source is included for **cardiometabolic_fitness_endpoints**.

**Findings:** A meta-analysis of RCTs in type 2 diabetes found step counters increased daily steps, especially when paired with a physical-activity goal, but did not significantly improve HbA1c. Safety reporting did not identify major step-counter-related adverse effects.

**Why it matters:** Directly tests step counters in a cardiometabolic clinical population and distinguishes physical activity gains from glycemic non-response.

**Potential experiment signals:** biomarker:daily-steps, biomarker:hba1c, adverse-event:musculoskeletal-injury, adverse-event:hypoglycemia.

**Protocol takeaway:** Supports daily-step tracking for increasing physical activity in type 2 diabetes, but protocol claims should not promise HbA1c improvement from step counters alone.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness boundary:** `direct_protocol`. This source should not be promoted beyond that scope in the Daily Step Floor protocol.

**Safety/adverse events:** No major adverse effects related to step-counter use were reported; extracted adverse-event terms included musculoskeletal injury, shin soreness, hypoglycemia, and minor poor health not associated with intervention.

**Limitations and population mismatch:** Heterogeneity across RCT interventions and goal-setting designs; type 2 diabetes population limits generalization. Population mismatch: Clinical type 2 diabetes sample; may not apply to non-diabetic users or people with contraindications to walking.
