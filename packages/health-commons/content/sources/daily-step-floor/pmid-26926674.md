This source is included for **cardiometabolic_fitness_endpoints**.

**Findings:** A meta-analysis in overweight/obese adults with type 2 diabetes found small pooled reductions in BMI and body weight after pedometer interventions. The authors identified dietary counselling, not step goal-setting, as the key predictor of weight reduction in extracted summaries.

**Why it matters:** Separates modest weight effects from the step target itself and flags the role of diet/coaching.

**Potential experiment signals:** biomarker:body-weight, biomarker:bmi, biomarker:daily-steps.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use cautiously as context: pedometer interventions may support small weight/BMI changes in T2D, but not as evidence that a step floor alone drives weight loss.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness boundary:** `direct_protocol`. This source should not be promoted beyond that scope in the Daily Step Floor protocol.

**Safety/adverse events:** No adverse-event signal extracted from accessible abstract text.

**Limitations and population mismatch:** Modest effect sizes, clinical T2D/overweight-obese population, and unclear relationship between physical-activity change and weight loss; dietary counselling appeared influential. Population mismatch: Overweight/obese adults with T2D are not equivalent to general Daily Step Floor users.
