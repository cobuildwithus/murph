This source is included for **direct_protocol_reviews**.

**Findings:** Daily step count through 1 year: In multivariable-adjusted meta-analysis, interventions increased steps/day by 1126 at up to 4 months (95% CI 787 to 1466), 1050 at 6 months (95% CI 602 to 1498), and 464 at 1 year (95% CI 301 to 626).; Daily step count at 2 years and 3-4 years: The adjusted pooled estimate was not significant at 2 years (121 steps/day; 95% CI -64 to 306) but was positive at 3-4 years (434 steps/day; 95% CI 191 to 676).; Daily step count difference: Meta-regression found body-worn tracker or smartphone interventions were less effective than pedometers (-834 steps/day; 95% CI -1542 to -126), and adding counseling or incentives was not associated with greater effect (-812 steps/day; 95% CI -1503 to -122); the authors caution these analyses are observational.

**Why it matters:** This is the most directly aligned modern synthesis: adult community RCTs of step-count monitoring, with follow-up windows relevant to a Daily Step Floor protocol.

**Potential experiment signals:** daily-step-count, wearable-adherence, weekly-active-minutes.

**Protocol takeaway:** Step-count monitoring reliably increases daily steps in adult community RCTs through at least 1 year; claims beyond that need caveats because the 2-year estimate was not significant and long-term effects vary.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.
