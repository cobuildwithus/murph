This source is included for **baseline_plus_ramp_trials**.

**Findings:** Long-term follow-up of PACE-UP and PACE-Lift found sustained MVPA gains and sustained steps in PACE-UP, while PACE-Lift step gains at 4 years were smaller and not statistically clear.

**Why it matters:** Direct evidence on durability of pedometer walking interventions beyond the initial ramp.

**Potential experiment signals:** steps/day at 3-4 years, MVPA bout minutes, sedentary time, follow-up retention.

**Protocol takeaway:** Behavioral gains can persist for years, but persistence may vary by cohort and endpoint.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

## Extracted study details

- **Population:** Adults and older adults from two UK primary care pedometer-based walking trials.
- **Intervention or exposure:** Prior PACE-UP postal/nurse pedometer interventions and PACE-Lift nurse-supported pedometer intervention.
- **Comparator or control:** Original trial control groups.
- **Duration or follow-up:** 3-year follow-up for PACE-UP and 4-year follow-up for PACE-Lift.
- **Endpoints:** steps/day, MVPA bouts, sedentary time, long-term maintenance
- **Effect estimates or direction:** PACE-UP retained significant step gains at 3 years (+627 postal and +670 nurse vs control) and MVPA gains. PACE-Lift showed +407 steps/day at 4 years with confidence intervals crossing no effect, but MVPA bouts remained higher (+32 min/week). Sedentary time did not differ.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** Adverse-event reporting was not extracted from accessible follow-up sections.
- **Limitations:** Incomplete long-term follow-up and mixed step persistence between the two original trials.
- **Population mismatch:** UK primary care adults and older adults.
