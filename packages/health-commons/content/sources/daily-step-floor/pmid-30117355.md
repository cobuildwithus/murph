This source is included for **measurement_validity**.

**Findings:** In older adults, commercial activity monitors showed limitations in both step-count accuracy and usability/acceptability, especially for wrist-worn monitors noted in accessible extracts.

**Why it matters:** A tracker used for a Daily Step Floor must be accurate enough and acceptable enough to wear consistently.

**Potential experiment signals:** daily_step_count, step_count_accuracy, device_placement_or_carriage, gait_speed_or_gait_aid_context.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context-only evidence that commercial monitor choice in older adults requires both accuracy and usability review.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Forty-three older adults with mean age approximately 87 years and varied walking ability.
- **Exposure/intervention:** Seven commercial-grade physical activity monitors.
- **Comparator/control:** Observed walking trials and participant usability/acceptability questionnaires.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Single assessment with walking trials and acceptability survey
- **Endpoints:** step-count accuracy, usability, acceptability
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Accessible extracts report limitations in both step-count accuracy and usability for current commercial monitors in older adults; wrist-worn monitors such as Fitbit Charge and Garmin Vivofit were specifically noted as limited.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse events or safety outcomes were reported in the accessible extract.
- **Limitations:** Predominantly female and all-White sample noted in accessible extracts.; Short walking trials and questionnaire data.; Some device-level numerical results not extracted for this draft.
- **Population mismatch:** Older-adult measurement and usability context; not intervention efficacy evidence.
- **Artifact rights:** open_access
