This source is included for **mental_health_sleep_qol**.

**Findings:** A 12-week primary-care pedometer walking pilot RCT in adults aged 65+ increased walking by about 2,000 steps/day and reduced sedentary time, with quality-of-life improvement but unclear sensitivity for mood/loneliness measures.

**Why it matters:** Direct pedometer walking RCT with older-adult implementation, QoL, adherence, and adverse-event information.

**Potential experiment signals:** biomarker:daily-steps, biomarker:quality-of-life, biomarker:adverse-events.

**Protocol takeaway:** Primary-care pedometer support can increase walking in older adults, but knee/back pain and monitor irritation show why a Daily Step Floor needs off-ramps and symptom guidance.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

**Directness boundary:** This source is classified as `direct_protocol` for Daily Step Floor. Do not promote adjacent, observational, registry/protocol, or clinical-population findings into direct protocol claims.

**Safety/adverse events:** Six adverse events were reported; three were attributed to the intervention or research monitoring: withdrawal after increased knee pain, temporary low-back pain, and skin irritation from an activity monitor.
