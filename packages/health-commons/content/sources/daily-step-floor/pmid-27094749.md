This source is included for **sedentary_activity_pattern**.

**Findings:** The intervention reduced work sitting by about 71 minutes/day at 1 month and 48 minutes/day at 3 months versus control in accessible summaries, largely by replacing sitting with standing rather than increasing physical activity.

**Why it matters:** Sedentary-time reduction can occur without meaningful step increases, so sitting and step outcomes should not be conflated.

**Potential experiment signals:** work sitting time, prolonged sitting, standing time, sit-to-stand transitions, physical activity.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context-only evidence for separating standing/sitting endpoints from daily step-count endpoints.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Office workers in 19 offices participating in a cluster-randomized workplace sitting-time intervention.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Take a Stand! multi-component workplace intervention to reduce sitting time.
- **Comparator/control:** Control offices with no multi-component intervention.
- **Duration/follow-up:** 3 months primary follow-up, with 1-month and 3-month activity outcomes reported in accessible summaries.
- **Endpoints:** work sitting time, prolonged sitting, standing time, sit-to-stand transitions, physical activity
- **Effect estimates or direction:** The intervention reduced work sitting by about 71 minutes/day at 1 month and 48 minutes/day at 3 months versus control in accessible summaries, largely by replacing sitting with standing rather than increasing physical activity.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse events were extracted from the accessible summary.
- **Limitations:** Workplace environmental and organizational intervention, not a step-floor intervention.; Effects were occupational and may not transfer to non-work hours.; Activity increases were not the main mechanism.
- **Population mismatch:** Adjacent workplace sitting intervention; not direct Daily Step Floor evidence.
