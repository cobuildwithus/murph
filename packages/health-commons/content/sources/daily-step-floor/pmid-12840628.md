This source is included for **safety_special_populations**.

**Findings:** A two-year cohort of 400 people with diabetes and prior foot-ulcer history found that higher self-reported daily weight-bearing activity did not increase re-ulceration risk.

**Why it matters:** Counters the assumption that all weight-bearing activity necessarily increases ulcer risk, while still requiring diabetic-foot risk screening.

**Potential experiment signals:** daily-step-count, foot-ulcer incidence, skin breakdown, adverse-events.

**Protocol takeaway:** Prior ulcer history is not automatically a reason to ban all walking, but Daily Step Floor should rely on clinician-supervised foot-risk assessment rather than unsupervised escalation.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

**Directness boundary:** clinical_supervised observational safety context. Do not promote this source into direct Daily Step Floor claims beyond the stated claim-use boundary.

**Safety/adverse events:** Foot lesions were actively tracked; the study did not support blanket avoidance of daily weight-bearing activity in this high-risk cohort.

**Limitations and mismatch:** Observational design; activity was self-reported; population already had diabetes and ulcer history; does not test a daily step floor. Diabetic-foot history cohort; safety context rather than direct general-adult protocol evidence.
