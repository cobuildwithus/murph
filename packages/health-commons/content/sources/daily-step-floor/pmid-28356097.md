This source is included for **baseline_plus_ramp_trials**.

**Findings:** In a 4-month factorial RCT, both static and adaptive goal groups increased steps, with adaptive goals showing slower decline over time and immediate rewards adding about 746 steps/day.

**Why it matters:** Direct adaptive-goal evidence with important mixed timing signal.

**Potential experiment signals:** steps/day trajectory, daily goal attainment, reward timing, drop-off slope.

**Protocol takeaway:** Adaptive goals may protect against later decline even if static goals create a larger early jump.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

## Extracted study details

- **Population:** Overweight or obese insufficiently active adults.
- **Intervention or exposure:** Adaptive goals calculated from recent behavior and immediate versus delayed rewards.
- **Comparator or control:** Static 10,000 steps/day goals and delayed/non-contingent reward timing.
- **Duration or follow-up:** 4 months.
- **Endpoints:** steps/day, daily goal attainment, reward timing effects
- **Effect estimates or direction:** All groups increased by about +2389 steps/day. Static goals produced a larger early increase, but adaptive goals declined more slowly and performed better by study end; immediate rewards were associated with about +746 steps/day versus delayed rewards.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** Adverse-event reporting was not extracted from accessible records.
- **Limitations:** Reward/incentive components confound pure goal-setting; overweight/obese sample.
- **Population mismatch:** Overweight/obese insufficiently active adults.
