This source is included for **safety_special_populations**.

**Findings:** In a 66-participant postpartum RCT, a 12-week pedometer walking program increased steps from about 3,249 to 9,960 steps/day and improved energy expenditure and anthropometric measures versus routine care.

**Why it matters:** Shows progressive pedometer walking after childbirth but is not general-adult evidence.

**Potential experiment signals:** daily-step-count, weight, BMI, waist circumference, adherence, postpartum symptoms.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use only as postpartum context; Daily Step Floor should not prescribe postpartum floors without postpartum recovery and clinician guidance.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

**Directness boundary:** adjacent_variant special-population context. Do not promote this source into direct Daily Step Floor claims beyond the stated claim-use boundary.

**Safety/adverse events:** No safety signal was extracted; postpartum/breastfeeding status requires population-specific considerations.

**Limitations and mismatch:** Postpartum sample; culturally/contextually specific trial; high step target rather than a generic minimum floor. Postpartum special population; context-only/low-priority for general Daily Step Floor.
