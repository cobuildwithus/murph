This source is included for **dose_response_cut_points**.

**Findings:** The review supported <5000 steps/day as a step-defined sedentary lifestyle index for adults and summarized observational and acute step-reduction evidence linking very low step counts with unfavorable cardiometabolic and body-composition signals.

**Why it matters:** Key context for defining a minimum activity floor and “too low” step ranges.

**Potential experiment signals:** daily step count, days meeting step floor, weekly mean steps, sedentary step index, cardiometabolic risk markers, insulin sensitivity, body composition.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as boundary context for avoiding very low steps; do not treat <5000 as a universal clinical cutoff.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Adults, with limited evidence for children and adolescents.
- **Exposure/intervention:** Daily step count below 5000 steps/day and acute step-reduction studies.
- **Comparator/control:** Higher step-count categories or pre/post step reductions.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Acute transition studies ranged roughly 3–14 days; observational contexts varied.
- **Endpoints:** sedentary step index, cardiometabolic risk markers, insulin sensitivity, body composition
- **Safety/adverse events:** No adverse-event signal specific to a step-floor intervention was extracted from this source in this batch.
- **Limitations:** Index is a classification heuristic; acute restriction studies and observational associations do not prove long-term intervention effects.
- **Population mismatch/directness:** Sedentary-index context is a boundary marker, not a full Daily Step Floor prescription.
- **Boundary:** This source is observational, review-based, registry-only, or otherwise adjacent unless explicitly noted; it must not be promoted into direct Daily Step Floor protocol evidence.
