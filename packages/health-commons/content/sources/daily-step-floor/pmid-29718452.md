This source is included for **measurement_validity**.

**Findings:** In persons with Parkinson disease, tracker accuracy varied widely by device, placement, and task type, with poorer performance during discontinuous household-like tasks.

**Why it matters:** Daily Step Floor counts can be inaccurate in altered gait, freezing-like patterns, or discontinuous household walking.

**Potential experiment signals:** daily_step_count, step_count_accuracy, device_placement_or_carriage, gait_speed_or_gait_aid_context.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context-only evidence to validate device choice in Parkinson disease rather than prescribe trackers indiscriminately.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Thirty-three persons with Parkinson disease, Hoehn & Yahr stages 1–3.
- **Exposure/intervention:** Four activity trackers worn on the less affected side during continuous and discontinuous walking tasks.
- **Comparator/control:** Observed/manual steps during standardized outpatient tasks.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Single outpatient activity protocol
- **Endpoints:** step-count accuracy, continuous versus discontinuous tasks, waist versus wrist placement
- **Effect estimates or direction:** ICC ranged from −0.03 to 0.98. The accessible extract reported Fitbit Zip as most accurate overall and Jawbone Up Move as least accurate during a simulated household course; trackers were more accurate in continuous than discontinuous tasks, and waist-mounted monitors were more accurate than wrist-mounted monitors for continuous tasks.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse events or safety outcomes were reported in the accessible extract.
- **Limitations:** Mild-to-moderate Parkinson disease sample; may not generalize to severe disease.; Outpatient tasks are not full free-living monitoring.; Device models may be older.
- **Population mismatch:** Parkinson disease measurement context; not Daily Step Floor efficacy evidence.
