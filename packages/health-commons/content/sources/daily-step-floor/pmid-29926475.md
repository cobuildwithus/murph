This source is included for **sedentary_activity_pattern**.

**Findings:** Cochrane review found sit-stand desks reduced sitting at work in the short and medium term, but evidence quality was low/very low and policy, counseling, walking, and activity-tracker approaches had inconsistent or nonsignificant effects.

**Why it matters:** Helps prevent overclaiming that trackers automatically reduce sitting; environmental interventions may have different mechanisms.

**Potential experiment signals:** sitting at work, total sitting, prolonged sitting, standing, stepping, adverse events.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context-only evidence for workplace sedentary endpoints and uncertainty.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Workers in sedentary occupations across workplace sitting-reduction studies.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Workplace interventions including sit-stand desks, policy changes, walking strategies, prompts, counseling, and activity trackers.
- **Comparator/control:** Usual care, no intervention, or alternative workplace strategies.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Short-, medium-, and longer-term follow-up varied by study.
- **Endpoints:** sitting at work, total sitting, prolonged sitting, standing, stepping, adverse events
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Cochrane review found sit-stand desks reduced sitting at work in the short and medium term, but evidence quality was low/very low and policy, counseling, walking, and activity-tracker approaches had inconsistent or nonsignificant effects.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** Adverse-event evidence was limited and sparsely reported in the review.
- **Limitations:** Low or very-low certainty for many outcomes.; Workplace-specific interventions may not generalize to daily-life step floors.; Activity-tracker evidence within the review did not clearly reduce sitting.
- **Population mismatch:** Workplace sitting-reduction review; adjacent to Daily Step Floor and not direct efficacy.
