This source is included for **dose_response_cut_points**.

**Findings:** This UK Biobank cohort reported that accumulating more daily steps, up to about 10,000 steps/day, was associated with lower all-cause, cancer, and cardiovascular mortality and incidence outcomes; higher intensity may have provided additional associations.

**Why it matters:** Broad outcome context for step volume and intensity.

**Potential experiment signals:** daily step count, days meeting step floor, weekly mean steps, all-cause mortality, cancer incidence, cancer mortality, cardiovascular disease incidence.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context for outcome breadth; do not claim causality.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** UK Biobank adults aged 40–79 years in England, Scotland, and Wales with wrist accelerometer data.
- **Exposure/intervention:** Daily step count and step intensity.
- **Comparator/control:** Lower versus higher daily steps and intensity metrics.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Median follow-up about seven years.
- **Endpoints:** all-cause mortality, cancer incidence, cancer mortality, cardiovascular disease incidence, cardiovascular disease mortality, daily step count, step intensity
- **Safety/adverse events:** No adverse-event signal specific to a step-floor intervention was extracted from this source in this batch.
- **Limitations:** Observational UK Biobank cohort, possible healthy-volunteer bias, single device window, residual confounding.
- **Population mismatch/directness:** Multiple disease outcomes are adjacent to a daily step-floor behavior and not protocol efficacy evidence.
- **Boundary:** This source is observational, review-based, registry-only, or otherwise adjacent unless explicitly noted; it must not be promoted into direct Daily Step Floor protocol evidence.
