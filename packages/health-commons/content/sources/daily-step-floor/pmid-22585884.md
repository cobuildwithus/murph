This source is included for **baseline_plus_ramp_trials**.

**Findings:** In low-active adults aged ≥65, a pedometer-based Green Prescription produced a greater 12-month leisure-walking increase than a standard time-based prescription, while BMI was unchanged and blood-pressure improvements were similar between groups.

**Why it matters:** Shows step-based prescription can outperform time-based advice for walking in older adults.

**Potential experiment signals:** leisure walking minutes/week, overall physical activity, falls, blood pressure, BMI.

**Protocol takeaway:** Pedometer-based advice may improve walking behavior in older adults, but biomarker claims require caution.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

## Extracted study details

- **Population:** Low-active adults aged 65 years and older recruited through primary care.
- **Intervention or exposure:** Pedometer step-based Green Prescription with primary care visit and three telephone counseling sessions.
- **Comparator or control:** Standard time-based Green Prescription.
- **Duration or follow-up:** 12-week intervention with 12-month follow-up.
- **Endpoints:** leisure walking minutes, overall physical activity, blood pressure, BMI, quality of life, physical function, falls
- **Effect estimates or direction:** At 12 months, leisure walking increased 49.6 min/week in the pedometer group versus 28.1 min/week in the standard group (P=.03). Both groups increased physical activity and improved blood pressure; BMI did not change and blood-pressure differences were not between-group significant.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** Falls were measured as an outcome, but detailed fall/adverse-event results were not extracted from accessible records.
- **Limitations:** Older adults only; pedometer improved leisure walking but not overall activity level beyond standard prescription.
- **Population mismatch:** Low-active older adults in New Zealand primary care.
