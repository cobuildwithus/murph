This source is included for **measurement_validity**.

**Findings:** Fitbit Charge HR and Garmin vívosmart HR had low step error at self-selected pace in mild-to-moderate Parkinson disease, but Garmin performed across a broader cadence range and intensity estimates were limited.

**Why it matters:** Cadence and disease-specific gait can affect whether a tracker can support a step floor.

**Potential experiment signals:** daily_step_count, step_count_accuracy, device_placement_or_carriage, gait_speed_or_gait_aid_context.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context-only evidence that tracker choice matters in Parkinson disease, especially outside self-selected walking speed.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Thirty-three adults with mild-to-moderate Parkinson disease.
- **Exposure/intervention:** Fitbit Charge HR and Garmin vívosmart HR during indoor cadence-controlled walks and outdoor terrain-challenge walking.
- **Comparator/control:** ActivPAL3 for steps; other clinical/intensity references for intensity.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Six 2-minute indoor walks plus one 500 m outdoor walk
- **Endpoints:** step-count error, cadence effects, intensity estimation
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Both trackers had low error (<3%) and moderate-high consistency at self-selected indoor/outdoor pace (ICC .88-.97). Garmin was more accurate across a wider cadence range than Fitbit; neither tracker strongly reflected walking intensity.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse events or safety outcomes were reported in the accessible extract.
- **Limitations:** Mild-to-moderate Parkinson disease only.; Short walking protocols; not long-term free-living daily steps.; Device generation may not represent current models.
- **Population mismatch:** Parkinson disease measurement context; no step-floor intervention.
