This source is included for **guidelines_external_protocol_context**.

**Findings:** The call to action promotes walking and wheelchair rolling as accessible ways to increase physical activity and emphasizes safe, convenient places and gradual increases, but it does not test a daily step-count floor.

**Why it matters:** Supports the idea that walking is accessible and scalable, while separating community/public-space strategy from a personal step-floor experiment.

**Potential experiment signals:** walking_behavior, built_environment_access, physical_activity_minutes, safety_boundary.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context-only evidence for walking accessibility and gradual-increase safety; not as efficacy evidence for a step floor.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** U.S. population across ages and abilities, including people who walk or use wheelchairs for mobility.
- **Exposure/intervention:** Walking, wheelchair rolling, and walkable-community strategies to increase physical activity; no daily step-floor intervention.
- **Comparator/control:** No comparator or control group; public-health call to action.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Not applicable; public-health report.
- **Endpoints:** walking behaviour; wheelchair rolling; access to safe and convenient places for physical activity; public-health implementation goals
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Promotes walking and walkable communities as a broadly accessible physical-activity strategy; it does not quantify a tested step-floor effect.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** Walking is framed as generally low risk, with gradual increases suggested to minimize injury risk; no adverse-event dataset is reported.
- **Limitations:** Policy and implementation context only; no trial, no participant-level comparator, and no daily step-count floor.
- **Population mismatch:** General walking and built-environment guidance, not a self-tracked daily step minimum protocol.
