This source is included for **dose_response_cut_points**.

**Findings:** Across 17 prospective studies, higher daily steps were associated with lower mortality and cardiovascular disease risk; reported per-1000-step associations ranged from about 6% to 36% lower mortality risk and 5% to 21% lower cardiovascular disease risk, but the review judged evidence insufficient to identify a minimum threshold.

**Why it matters:** Backbone context for prospective dose-response and uncertainty about thresholds below 10,000 steps.

**Potential experiment signals:** daily step count, days meeting step floor, weekly mean steps, all-cause mortality, cardiovascular disease, dysglycemia.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context for graded associations and threshold uncertainty, not as causal protocol support.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Adults in non-patient prospective cohorts; more than 30,000 adults across included studies.
- **Exposure/intervention:** Daily step count measured prospectively.
- **Comparator/control:** Lower versus higher daily step counts; per-1000-step increments in some studies.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Follow-up ranged roughly 3 months to 10 years by outcome and study.
- **Endpoints:** all-cause mortality, cardiovascular disease, dysglycemia, daily step count
- **Safety/adverse events:** No adverse-event signal specific to a step-floor intervention was extracted from this source in this batch.
- **Limitations:** Prospective observational designs; heterogeneous devices, populations, outcomes, and limited threshold certainty.
- **Population mismatch/directness:** Population-level prospective associations do not prove that assigning a daily step floor changes outcomes.
- **Boundary:** This source is observational, review-based, registry-only, or otherwise adjacent unless explicitly noted; it must not be promoted into direct Daily Step Floor protocol evidence.
