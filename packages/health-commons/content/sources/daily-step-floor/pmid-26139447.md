This source is included for **baseline_plus_ramp_trials**.

**Findings:** Two randomized walking experiments found that high goals and social comparison feedback increased steps versus comparison conditions, while medium goals did not clearly outperform low goals.

**Why it matters:** Context for goal difficulty and social comparison; reinforces that goal level matters and can be nonlinear.

**Potential experiment signals:** goal difficulty, steps/day, social comparison feedback.

**Protocol takeaway:** Daily Step Floor should choose goal levels carefully; medium or low goals may not always create the intended response.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extracted study details

- **Population:** University staff in Study 1 and adult participants in Study 2; full population details beyond accessible snippets were not extracted.
- **Intervention or exposure:** Pedometer feedback with low, medium, or high walking goals, and social comparison feedback.
- **Comparator or control:** Lower goal levels or individual feedback alone.
- **Duration or follow-up:** Duration not extracted from accessible records.
- **Endpoints:** steps/day, goal difficulty response, social comparison response
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Study 1 (N=148) reported participants walked 1358 more steps/day with a high goal than a medium goal (95% CI 729 to 1985), while medium did not clearly exceed low. Study 2 (N=64) reported social comparison feedback increased walking by 1120 steps/day versus individual feedback only (95% CI 538 to 1703).
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** Adverse-event reporting was not extracted.
- **Limitations:** Behavioral experiments, permission-required article, goal/social comparison context rather than health endpoint trial.
- **Population mismatch:** University/study volunteers rather than a clinical or general health cohort.
