This source is included for **sedentary_activity_pattern**.

**Findings:** The primary 12-month sedentary-time outcome and most secondary outcomes did not differ significantly between groups in the accessible summary.

**Why it matters:** It cautions that education, self-monitoring, and a single motivational call may not be enough to change sedentary time long term.

**Potential experiment signals:** accelerometer-measured sedentary time, prolonged sitting, physical activity, cardiometabolic risk.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context-only evidence that sedentary behavior change may require more than step awareness or brief counseling.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Young adults aged 18–40 at risk of type 2 diabetes, with overweight plus diabetes risk or obesity.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Project STAND sedentary-time reduction program including workshop, self-monitoring, and motivational telephone contact.
- **Comparator/control:** Usual-care or control condition.
- **Duration/follow-up:** 12 months
- **Endpoints:** accelerometer-measured sedentary time, prolonged sitting, physical activity, cardiometabolic risk
- **Effect estimates or direction:** The primary 12-month sedentary-time outcome and most secondary outcomes did not differ significantly between groups in the accessible summary.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse events or safety outcomes were reported in the accessible extract.
- **Limitations:** Young at-risk metabolic population may not generalize to all Daily Step Floor users.; Brief education and self-monitoring lacked a broad environmental component.; Null/mixed result should be preserved.
- **Population mismatch:** Adjacent sedentary-time intervention; not a step-floor or pedometer-goal intervention.
