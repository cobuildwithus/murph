This source is included for **cadence_intensity_bouts**.

**Findings:** Systematic review retained 31 articles and concluded that about 100 steps/min is commonly used for adult moderate-intensity walking, with possible higher needs in older adults and limited intervention evidence.

**Why it matters:** Provides the main evidence basis for treating cadence as an optional intensity marker in a step-floor protocol.

**Potential experiment signals:** walking_cadence, mvpa_minutes, activity_intensity_classification.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use 100 steps/min as a rough adult MVPA heuristic only when discussing optional intensity context, not as a required floor component.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Adult and older-adult walking-cadence literature; 31 included articles in accessible summaries.
- **Intervention/exposure:** Walking cadence thresholds used to identify or prescribe moderate-intensity walking.
- **Comparator/control:** Not applicable across heterogeneous calibration and intervention studies.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Systematic review; included study durations varied.
- **Endpoints:** Moderate-intensity walking; cadence thresholds; physical activity recommendation attainment.
- **Effect estimates or direction:** The review found that 100 steps/minute is generally prescribed for adults to reach moderate intensity, while older adults may require higher cadence and intervention evidence was sparse.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse events or safety outcomes were central to the accessible summary.
- **Limitations:** Limited intervention studies; heterogeneity across populations and protocols; cadence threshold is a heuristic rather than an individualized guarantee.
- **Population mismatch:** Same-mechanism cadence evidence, not direct evidence that a daily step-floor intervention improves outcomes.
