This source is included for **cadence_intensity_bouts**.

**Findings:** Secondary analysis of a supervised walking trial found prefrail/frail older adults could increase cadence and that increased cadence was associated with improved 6MWT functional capacity.

**Why it matters:** Shows cadence can be meaningful in supervised older-adult walking interventions, but clinical context matters.

**Potential experiment signals:** walking_cadence, functional_capacity_6mwt, frailty_context, safety_screening.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as clinical-supervised context; do not generalize fast-walking cadence targets to unsupervised frail users without screening.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Prefrail and frail older adults in retirement-community settings, included in a supervised walking trial analysis.
- **Intervention/exposure:** High-intensity walking cadence versus comfortable-speed walking cadence during supervised sessions.
- **Comparator/control:** Comfortable-speed walking group.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Four-month supervised walking intervention in accessible summaries.
- **Endpoints:** Walking cadence; six-minute walk test minimum clinically important difference; functional capacity.
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Accessible summaries report high-intensity walking cadence around 100 [88,111] spm versus comfortable-speed walking around 77 [65,86] spm, and that increasing cadence by about 14 spm from comfortable cadence increased odds of improving 6MWT MCID.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** Safety events were not extracted from accessible summaries; supervised setting and frail/prefrail status imply clinical caution.
- **Limitations:** Secondary analysis; supervised retirement-community sample; frail/prefrail older adults; not an unsupervised general-population step-floor protocol.
- **Population mismatch:** Clinical/supervised older adult evidence; not direct Daily Step Floor evidence.
