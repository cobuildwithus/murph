This source is included for **baseline_plus_ramp_trials**.

**Findings:** PACE-UP protocol specified a 12-week pedometer programme in primary care using a start-low-go-slow approach and targets including +3000 steps/day, with adverse events tracked.

**Why it matters:** High-value implementation and safety-boundary source for a daily step-floor ramp.

**Potential experiment signals:** steps/day, sedentary time, MVPA, adverse events, mood/QoL.

**Protocol takeaway:** Daily Step Floor should include conservative ramping and explicit safety exclusions/referral boundaries.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

## Extracted study details

- **Population:** Less-active primary care patients aged 45 to 75 years.
- **Intervention or exposure:** Pedometer-alone postal 12-week programme or pedometer plus nurse-support programme using a walking plan, handbook, diary, and targets including +3000 steps/day.
- **Comparator or control:** Usual-care control.
- **Duration or follow-up:** 12-week programme with 12-month outcome assessment planned.
- **Endpoints:** steps/day, sedentary time, MVPA, BMI, body fat, quality of life, mood, adverse events
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Protocol source; no efficacy result.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** Start-low-go-slow messaging and exclusions for high fall risk, recent cardiac events, unstable conditions, cognitive impairment, blindness, pregnancy, and other safety concerns.
- **Limitations:** Protocol-only source; outcome evidence comes from later PACE-UP trial.
- **Population mismatch:** Primary care adults aged 45-75; excludes several higher-risk groups.
