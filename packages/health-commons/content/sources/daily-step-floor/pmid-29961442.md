This source is included for **safety_special_populations**.

**Findings:** In the 1,023-participant PACE-UP cluster RCT, postal and nurse-supported pedometer walking programs increased steps at 12 months by roughly 642-677 steps/day versus control and did not show an increase in adverse events.

**Why it matters:** Large pedometer walking trial with explicit adverse-event and falls monitoring.

**Potential experiment signals:** daily-step-count, moderate-to-vigorous-physical-activity, falls, adverse-events, cardiovascular events.

**Protocol takeaway:** PACE-UP supports careful adverse-event/falls monitoring around pedometer walking programs; do not overinterpret small safety differences as causal protection.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.

**Directness boundary:** adjacent_variant with safety monitoring. Do not promote this source into direct Daily Step Floor claims beyond the stated claim-use boundary.

**Safety/adverse events:** The trial reported no increase in adverse events; falls and primary-care cardiovascular events were not increased, with some small differences requiring cautious interpretation.

**Limitations and mismatch:** Cluster trial in 45-75-year-old primary-care adults; recruitment and demographic representation limitations; not a special-population safety trial. Midlife/older primary-care sample rather than all Daily Step Floor users; safety context is useful but not special clinical clearance.
