This source is included for **measurement_validity**.

**Findings:** In Parkinson-related step-count estimation, normal walkers had lower median error than abnormal walkers, supporting gait-abnormality as a measurement-risk factor.

**Why it matters:** Movement-disorder gait can change step-count error and may be detectable from monitoring data.

**Potential experiment signals:** daily_step_count, step_count_accuracy, device_placement_or_carriage, gait_speed_or_gait_aid_context.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context-only evidence for gait-abnormality measurement boundaries in Parkinson disease.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** People with Parkinson disease and comparison walkers; participant count was not available in the accessible extraction.
- **Exposure/intervention:** Ambulatory monitoring and step-count estimation algorithm.
- **Comparator/control:** Reference step-count accuracy categories for normal versus abnormal walkers.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Ambulatory monitoring; exact duration not available in accessible extraction.
- **Endpoints:** step-count error rate, normal versus abnormal walker classification, predictors of step-count accuracy
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Accessible abstract text reported median step-count error rates <3% in normal walkers but ≥3% in abnormal walkers; simultaneous accelerometry data and characteristics were used to predict accuracy.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse events or safety outcomes were reported in the accessible extract.
- **Limitations:** Participant count and full model details were not extracted.; Parkinson disease gait-specific context.; Algorithm and sensor setup may not match consumer trackers.
- **Population mismatch:** Parkinson measurement/algorithm context; not a Daily Step Floor intervention.
- **Artifact rights:** open_access
