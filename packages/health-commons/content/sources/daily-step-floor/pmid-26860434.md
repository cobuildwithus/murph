This source is included for **baseline_plus_ramp_trials**.

**Findings:** A 6-week adaptive tracker/internet walking program increased daily steps by about +970 steps/day versus no-treatment control and improved the proportion achieving a ≥1000-step/day increase.

**Why it matters:** Direct implementation evidence for adaptive tracker-enabled step increases.

**Potential experiment signals:** steps/day, ≥1000-step/day responder status, baseline activity subgroup.

**Protocol takeaway:** Tracker-backed adaptive programs can produce short-term step increases, especially when baseline activity is low.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.

## Extracted study details

- **Population:** Workplace adults, average age about 40 years, with many sedentary or low-active participants.
- **Intervention or exposure:** Activity tracker- and internet-based adaptive walking program.
- **Comparator or control:** No-treatment control instructed not to wear the tracker and to maintain usual routine.
- **Duration or follow-up:** 6-week treatment period.
- **Endpoints:** steps/day, 1000-step/day increase, subgroup response by baseline activity
- **Effect estimates or direction:** The intervention produced an adjusted mean difference of about +970 steps/day versus control (P<.001). In sedentary participants, changes were about +595 versus +47 steps/day (p=.04); 29.9% of intervention participants versus 16.4% of controls increased by at least 1000 steps/day.
- **Adverse events or safety notes:** Adverse-event reporting was not extracted from accessible records.
- **Limitations:** Short workplace study; some effect reflected control-group decline/reactivity; not a clinical endpoint trial.
- **Population mismatch:** Workplace adults may not represent older or clinical populations.
