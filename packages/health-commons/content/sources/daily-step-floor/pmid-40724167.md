This source is included for **measurement_validity**.

**Findings:** In a small healthy-sample obstacle-course study, Fitbit Inspire 3 accuracy during cane/walker use depended on body placement; ankle or waist placement appeared more accurate than wrist placement in accessible extracts.

**Why it matters:** Assistive devices and wrist placement can bias step-floor measurements.

**Potential experiment signals:** daily_step_count, step_count_accuracy, device_placement_or_carriage, gait_speed_or_gait_aid_context.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context-only evidence to prefer ankle/waist validation over wrist placement when assistive devices are used.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Eleven healthy participants.
- **Exposure/intervention:** Fitbit Inspire 3 recorded step counts while using a two-wheeled walker, standard cane, and no-device control trial at wrists, hips, and ankles.
- **Comparator/control:** Visually recorded steps during a 50 m obstacle course with turns and a step up/down.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Three 50 m obstacle-course trials: walker, cane, and control.
- **Endpoints:** Fitbit step-count accuracy, assistive-device condition, body placement, visual step count
- **Effect estimates or direction:** Accessible extracts and peer review summary reported the Fitbit Inspire 3 was accurate when worn at ankle or waist but not at the wrist during assistive-device use.
- **Adverse events/safety notes:** No adverse events or safety outcomes were reported in the accessible extract.
- **Limitations:** Small healthy sample using simulated assistive-device conditions.; Brief obstacle course, not free-living monitoring.; Published in 2025; details should be revisited if integrating into stable protocol text.
- **Population mismatch:** Healthy simulated assistive-device measurement context; not Daily Step Floor intervention evidence.
- **Artifact rights:** open_access
