This source is included for **dose_response_cut_points**.

**Findings:** Among 419 older Japanese adults, 76 deaths occurred over 9.8 years. The highest step quartile (>7972 steps/day) had a lower adjusted mortality hazard than the lowest quartile (<4503 steps/day), but the linear trend and per-1000-step association were not statistically significant.

**Why it matters:** Adds null/mixed details alongside positive highest-quartile findings.

**Potential experiment signals:** daily step count, days meeting step floor, weekly mean steps, all-cause mortality.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context with mixed statistical strength; do not overstate dose-response certainty.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.

## Extraction notes

- **Population:** Physically independent, community-dwelling Japanese adults aged 71 years at baseline.
- **Exposure/intervention:** Waist-mounted pedometer steps for seven consecutive days at baseline.
- **Comparator/control:** Quartiles of average steps/day, including <4503 and >7972 steps/day.
- **Duration/follow-up:** Mean follow-up 9.8 years.
- **Endpoints:** all-cause mortality, daily step count
- **Safety/adverse events:** No adverse-event signal specific to a step-floor intervention was extracted from this source in this batch.
- **Limitations:** Small sample, selection bias, few deaths, single baseline step measurement, and limited generalizability to less healthy older adults.
- **Population mismatch/directness:** Older, physically independent Japanese cohort is not a general Daily Step Floor trial.
- **Boundary:** This source is observational, review-based, registry-only, or otherwise adjacent unless explicitly noted; it must not be promoted into direct Daily Step Floor protocol evidence.
