This source is included for **Reviews, definitions, and variant taxonomy**.

**Findings:** The review summarizes evidence that the original Tabata intermittent exercise can strongly tax both aerobic and anaerobic systems; any numerical training effects should be traced back to the primary trials.

**Study design and population:** Narrative review of Tabata energetics, history, and adaptation studies; Mixed populations and experiments discussed in a narrative review, with emphasis on the original laboratory cycling protocol.

**Intervention or exposure:** Tabata training, especially repeated 20-second high-intensity cycling bouts separated by 10 seconds of rest and calibrated to exhaustion near the seventh or eighth bout.

**Comparator or control:** Narrative comparison with other intermittent exercise protocols and historical training approaches.

**Duration or follow-up:** Review source; individual studies discussed include acute energetic tests and multiweek training studies.

**Endpoints:** Oxygen uptake, maximal accumulated oxygen deficit, VO2max, anaerobic capacity, exercise energetics.

**Safety notes:** No adverse-event rate extracted from the review abstract; high intensity implies a screening and readiness boundary for practical use.

**Limitations and mismatch:** Narrative review by the protocol originator; not a pooled effect estimate and not a direct test of unsupervised consumer protocols. Population mismatch: Laboratory cycling intensity calibration is not the same as bodyweight or group-fitness Tabata-style use.

**Why it matters:** It is an authoritative terminology and mechanism source from the named protocol originator.

**Potential experiment signals:** estimated VO2max, session intensity fidelity, RPE and fatigue, heart-rate response as secondary context.

**Protocol takeaway:** The original Tabata idea is not merely any 20/10 circuit; it was a calibrated, exhaustive high-intensity cycling stimulus.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
