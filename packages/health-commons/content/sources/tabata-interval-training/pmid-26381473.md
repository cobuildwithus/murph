This source is included for **Rhabdomyolysis, heat, and serious exertional adverse-event safety**.

**Findings:** The position statement addresses prevention, recognition, and treatment of exertional heat illnesses in physically active settings. It is not efficacy evidence for Tabata but provides safety boundaries for heat exposure, dehydration, inadequate acclimatization, and exertional heat-stroke response.

**Why it matters:** It supplies safety language for heat illness recognition and prevention when a high-intensity protocol is performed in hot or humid settings.

**Potential experiment signals:** heat symptoms, environmental heat load, hydration status, session stoppage, and need for urgent cooling or medical evaluation.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as a safety boundary: stop or modify intense intervals when heat illness risk factors or symptoms appear.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.
