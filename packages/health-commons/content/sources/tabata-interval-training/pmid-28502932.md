This source is included for **Rhabdomyolysis, heat, and serious exertional adverse-event safety**.

**Findings:** The case involved a previously healthy 37-year-old man who developed severe myalgia and dark urine after intense resistance exercise, with CK reported at 95,100 U/L and urine myoglobin at 160,000 ng/mL. He improved with intravenous saline and did not have reported acute renal failure.

**Why it matters:** It illustrates that healthy adults can develop clinically significant muscle breakdown after intense resistance exercise.

**Potential experiment signals:** severe myalgia, dark urine, CK, urine myoglobin, renal function, and need for IV hydration.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as safety-only case evidence for abrupt high-intensity resistance overload.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.
