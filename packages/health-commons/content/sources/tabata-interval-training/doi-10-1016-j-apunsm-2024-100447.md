This source is included for **Rhabdomyolysis, heat, and serious exertional adverse-event safety**.

**Findings:** The review identified 26 studies and 63 reported rhabdomyolysis cases after high-intensity functional training. It is adjacent-variant evidence: useful for safety warnings around CrossFit/HIFT-style overload, but not direct evidence about canonical Tabata efficacy or incidence.

**Why it matters:** It captures high-recall adverse-event evidence for functional high-intensity training formats that users may call Tabata in practice.

**Potential experiment signals:** training volume, movement novelty, severe pain, swelling, dark urine, CK if medically indicated, and hospitalization.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use for adjacent-variant safety warnings, especially when Tabata is implemented as high-volume functional training.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.
