This source is included for **wearable_recovery_autonomic**.

**Findings:** Fifteen male students completed randomized Tabata, HIIT, and MICT tests; Tabata had higher overall energy-expenditure rate across exercise plus recovery but lower total energy expenditure than longer HIIT/MICT sessions, with higher 20-minute recovery fat-oxidation rate than HIIT/MICT in the accessible report. **Why it matters:** Acute metabolism context in overweight/obese male students; not chronic fat-loss evidence. [source_artifact:pmid-38476670]

**Potential experiment signals:** energy-expenditure, fat-oxidation, glucose-oxidation, heart-rate, rpe.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context-only evidence only: A 4-minute 20/10 Tabata session had higher per-minute expenditure but lower total exercise energy expenditure than longer HIIT/MICT sessions; recovery metabolism differed by window.
