This source is included for **direct_original_dose_evidence**.

**Findings:** Seven volunteers completed isometric, isotonic, and jump squat 20s:10s routines. Total energy cost was higher for jump and isotonic squats than for isometric squats, with isometric squats significantly lower than the other two formats.

**Why it matters:** It shows why 20/10 timing alone cannot define energy expenditure: isometric, isotonic, and jump formats produce different costs.

**Potential experiment signals:** energy cost by movement mode, aerobic cost, EPOC, lactate-estimated anaerobic cost, RPE.

**Protocol takeaway:** Mode selection matters; bodyweight Tabata-style routines should not inherit energy-cost claims from cycle-ergometer Tabata.

**Claim use:** `supports-protocol`.
