This source is included for **cardiometabolic_body_composition**.

**Findings:** Interval training was associated with modest adiposity reductions in apparently healthy adults, with clinical significance and review quality caveats. Body-fat percentage reductions were reported versus MICT and non-exercise controls; effects appeared stronger in overweight/obesity, programs ≥12 weeks, cycling modality, and low-volume HIIT subgroups.

**Why it matters:** This recent umbrella review provides the broadest adiposity context while explicitly flagging modest effects and methodological limitations.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- body-fat-percentage
- fat-mass
- visceral-adipose-tissue
- waist-circumference

**Protocol takeaway:** Use only as broad interval-training adiposity context; body-composition claims for Tabata should remain cautious.

**Safety and limitations:** No adverse-event counts or safety event rates were extracted from accessible metadata/abstract details.  Key limitations: Most included systematic reviews were rated critically low or low quality.; Effects were modest and may be close to measurement error.; Clinical significance was uncertain.; Not specific to Tabata 20/10.

**Population mismatch:** Apparently healthy adult interval-training evidence may not match specific Tabata programming or clinical populations.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
