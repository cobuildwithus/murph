This source is included for **Safety: musculoskeletal injury / HIFT-CrossFit boundary**.

**Findings:** Among 406 analyzed participants, 133 reported at least one new CrossFit-related musculoskeletal injury and 247 unique injuries were recorded over 13,041 hours; incidence proportion was 32.8% and incidence density was 18.9 injuries per 1000 hours. Switching between prescribed and scaled loads and previous injuries were associated with increased injury risk, while greater CrossFit experience was protective in the reported model. The source is classified as `adjacent_variant` and `safety-only` for the Tabata 20/10 protocol. Citation key: `source_artifact:pmid-32284938`.

**Why it matters:** Use as a safety boundary for functional or loaded Tabata-style circuits: track prior injury, load scaling, shoulder/back symptoms, and medical-care triggers.

**Potential experiment signals:** Track shoulder/back/knee symptoms, pain severity, missed workouts, medical-care seeking, and load-scaling decisions when the Tabata-style workout is implemented as a functional circuit.

**Protocol takeaway:** Safety-only adjacent evidence: prior injury and load-scaling changes should be treated as risk flags before functional Tabata-style circuits.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`. This is CrossFit facility training, not the original Tabata 20/10 protocol, and should not be generalized to lower-impact modalities.
