This source is included for **Safety: musculoskeletal injury / HIFT-CrossFit boundary**.

**Findings:** All competition groups had similar injury incidence in this analysis. Reported injury rates were 0.21-0.54 injuries per 1000 training hours for competitors and 0.39-1.30 per 1000 training hours for non-competitors. Short participation length was associated with higher odds of injury (OR 1.82, 95% CI 1.15 to 2.92), while official affiliate training appeared protective but imprecise (OR 0.85, 95% CI 0.65 to 1.10). The source is classified as `adjacent_variant` and `safety-only` for the Tabata 20/10 protocol. Citation key: `source_artifact:pmid-32577301`.

**Why it matters:** Use to avoid overclaiming that every functional HIFT or competitive setting is necessarily higher risk; emphasize progression and experience.

**Potential experiment signals:** Track shoulder/back/knee symptoms, pain severity, missed workouts, medical-care seeking, and load-scaling decisions when the Tabata-style workout is implemented as a functional circuit.

**Protocol takeaway:** Safety-only adjacent evidence: progression and experience may matter more than competition status in this CrossFit dataset.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`. Cross-sectional design and CrossFit-specific population prevent direct inference for Tabata 20/10.
