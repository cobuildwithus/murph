This source is included for **Rhabdomyolysis, heat, and serious exertional adverse-event safety**.

**Findings:** The review describes exertional rhabdomyolysis as exercise-induced skeletal-muscle damage with clinically important kidney-risk implications. It supports warning signs, risk-factor screening, and prevention language rather than a Tabata efficacy claim.

**Why it matters:** It ties intense exertion to clinically important muscle-breakdown and kidney-injury risks without overclaiming causality for a specific interval template.

**Potential experiment signals:** severe muscle pain, weakness, swelling, dark urine, CK/myoglobin testing when ordered by clinicians, and renal-function follow-up when indicated.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as safety-only evidence for rhabdomyolysis warning signs and risk-factor screening.

**Claim use:** `safety-only`.
