This source is included for **Reviews, definitions, and variant taxonomy**.

**Findings:** Review found only five studies explicitly used the original protocol; variations appeared capable of improving aerobic power similarly to traditional aerobic training while being less time consuming, but weight-loss claims were not substantiated.

**Study design and population:** Systematic review of human Tabata-protocol applications and variations; Human studies using original or modified Tabata-style protocols across mixed populations and settings.

**Intervention or exposure:** Original Tabata protocol or Tabata-style variations, including cycle-ergometer and non-cycle protocols.

**Comparator or control:** Traditional aerobic training, alternative HIIT variants, controls, or no formal comparator depending on included study.

**Duration or follow-up:** Included intervention durations varied by study; the review searched literature from 1996 to October 2017.

**Endpoints:** Protocol fidelity, aerobic power, anaerobic capacity, body composition or weight-loss claims, intensity control.

**Safety notes:** No batch-level adverse-event estimate extracted from the abstract-accessible material.

**Limitations and mismatch:** Heterogeneous protocols, populations, intensity controls, and outcomes; review-level source cannot replace primary trials for effect estimates. Population mismatch: Mixed populations and many protocol variants do not map cleanly to an unsupervised consumer 20/10 experiment.

**Why it matters:** It prevents over-generalizing the Tabata label and anchors the source ledger around variant separation.

**Potential experiment signals:** estimated VO2max or field-test fitness, session intensity fidelity, body weight/body composition as exploratory only, adherence and symptoms.

**Protocol takeaway:** Treat Tabata-labeled evidence as heterogeneous unless a source clearly matches the original 20-second/10-second, supramaximal cycling protocol.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
