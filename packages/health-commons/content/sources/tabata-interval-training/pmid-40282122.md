## Findings

- [source_artifact:pmid-40282122] Sixty male adolescents were randomized to a 12-week Tabata-style HIIT warm-up or control warm-up in PE classes.
- [source_artifact:pmid-40282122] The Tabata program used two 4-minute sequences of eight high-intensity exercises with 20 seconds work and 10 seconds rest, separated by 1 minute recovery.
- [source_artifact:pmid-40282122] Social recognition showed a significant time-by-group interaction; affiliation and competition improved in the HIIT group, while other motivation factors were not significant.

## Why it matters

It helps separate adolescent school-delivery evidence from adult self-directed Tabata claims while retaining useful adherence-context signals.
