This source is included for **Reviews, definitions, and variant taxonomy**.

**Findings:** Compared with no exercise, WB-HIIT improved cardiorespiratory fitness, fat-free mass, fat mass, and musculoskeletal fitness; compared with traditional aerobic training, it had lower CRF effect, similar body-composition effects, and higher musculoskeletal-fitness effect. The review did not provide evidence of metabolic-risk improvement.

**Study design and population:** Systematic review and meta-analysis; General and clinical populations across whole-body HIIT studies; 19 studies and 657 participants in the meta-analysis.

**Intervention or exposure:** Whole-body high-intensity interval training using bodyweight or multi-joint whole-body exercises.

**Comparator or control:** No-exercise controls or traditional aerobic training depending on analysis.

**Duration or follow-up:** Intervention durations varied across included studies.

**Endpoints:** Cardiorespiratory fitness, fat-free mass, fat mass, musculoskeletal fitness, metabolic risk factors, total training time dose-response.

**Safety notes:** No source-level adverse-event rate extracted from the batch material.

**Limitations and mismatch:** Whole-body HIIT is an adjacent variant; protocols, populations, and comparator types varied. Population mismatch: Does not isolate original Tabata 20/10 cycling or a single consumer protocol.

**Why it matters:** It is one of the stronger adjacent syntheses for bodyweight or whole-body interval protocols.

**Potential experiment signals:** estimated VO2max, body composition, musculoskeletal fitness proxy, metabolic biomarkers if measured, adherence and symptoms.

**Protocol takeaway:** Whole-body Tabata-style workouts should remain in an adjacent-variant bucket unless the exact protocol is tested.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
