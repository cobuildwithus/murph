This source is included for **cardiometabolic_body_composition**.

**Findings:** Low-volume HIIT improved fasting glucose and HbA1c versus non-exercise controls, while comparisons with MICT showed no clear advantage for several glycemic markers. Against controls, fasting glucose and HbA1c decreased; against MICT, insulin sensitivity improved but fasting glucose, fasting insulin, HbA1c, and HOMA-IR did not clearly differ.

**Why it matters:** This is one of the more relevant adjacent sources for glucose, insulin, and HbA1c tracking in a time-efficient HIIT context.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- fasting-glucose
- HbA1c
- fasting-insulin
- HOMA-IR
- adherence
- tolerability

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as context for possible glycemic endpoint tracking, not as proof that Tabata 20/10 lowers glucose or insulin.

**Safety and limitations:** No adverse-event counts or safety event rates were extracted from accessible metadata/abstract details.  Key limitations: Only 30% of included studies were rated high quality.; Subgroup sample sizes were small.; Protocol and population heterogeneity were substantial.; Compliance and affective-response reporting were limited.

**Population mismatch:** Adult non-athlete clinical and nonclinical populations may not generalize to healthy users doing unsupervised Tabata-style circuits.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
