This source is included for **Reviews, definitions, and variant taxonomy**.

**Findings:** Across 28 studies and 723 participants, HIT improved VO2max versus no exercise and showed a small advantage over continuous endurance training in the pooled estimate; this does not isolate Tabata 20/10.

**Study design and population:** Systematic review and meta-analysis of controlled trials; Adults in controlled HIIT and continuous endurance training trials; mean baseline fitness and age varied across included studies.

**Intervention or exposure:** High-intensity interval training protocols across controlled trials.

**Comparator or control:** Continuous endurance training or no-exercise control.

**Duration or follow-up:** Trial durations varied; review-level source.

**Endpoints:** VO2max improvements.

**Safety notes:** No adverse-event extraction in the batch-level abstract material.

**Limitations and mismatch:** Heterogeneous HIIT protocols and populations; direct Tabata 20/10 effects cannot be inferred. Population mismatch: General HIIT literature includes protocols that differ substantially from supramaximal 20/10 cycling.

**Why it matters:** It provides a broader HIIT benchmark for VO2max claims while preserving directness boundaries.

**Potential experiment signals:** estimated VO2max, field-test aerobic fitness, training adherence, session intensity.

**Protocol takeaway:** General HIIT evidence supports plausibility for VO2max improvement, but protocol-specific claims still require Tabata-matched sources.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
