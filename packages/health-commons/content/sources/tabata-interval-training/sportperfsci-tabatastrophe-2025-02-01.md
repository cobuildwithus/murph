This source is included for **Reviews, definitions, and variant taxonomy**.

**Findings:** Argues that many public Tabata-style workouts may not reproduce the metabolic effects of the original method because mode and intensity are not calibrated.

**Study design and population:** Expert narrative report; General fitness users, group-fitness settings, athletes, and practitioners discussed conceptually.

**Intervention or exposure:** Public Tabata-style workouts contrasted with original calibrated 20-second/10-second cycling at approximately 170% of power associated with VO2max.

**Comparator or control:** Original laboratory Tabata protocol and modern uncalibrated public variants.

**Duration or follow-up:** Narrative source; no intervention follow-up.

**Endpoints:** Protocol fidelity, metabolic demand, exercise mode, active muscle mass, intensity calibration, weight-loss claims, public translation.

**Safety notes:** No adverse-event incidence extracted; report frames excessive suffering and poor calibration as practical concerns.

**Limitations and mismatch:** Expert narrative/report, not peer-reviewed primary outcome evidence; some applied recommendations are external protocol claims. Population mismatch: Public fitness contexts and elite-athlete calibration tools may not fit all Murph self-experiment users.

**Why it matters:** It is directly relevant to how a consumer-facing page should avoid public-protocol overload.

**Potential experiment signals:** exercise mode, completion of 20/10 bouts, RPE, session power/pace where available, fatigue and symptoms.

**Protocol takeaway:** Public bodyweight Tabata should be labeled as a variant unless it has calibrated intensity and mode evidence.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
