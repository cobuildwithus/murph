This source is included for **cardiometabolic_body_composition**.

**Findings:** Low-volume HIIT improved cardiorespiratory fitness but did not clearly improve body composition versus controls or MICT. Cardiorespiratory fitness improved versus control/MICT; body fat mass, body fat percentage, and lean body mass did not show clear improvement.

**Why it matters:** This source helps separate likely fitness adaptations from less certain fat-mass or lean-mass changes.

**Potential experiment signals:**
- VO2max
- cardiorespiratory-fitness
- body-fat-percentage
- fat-mass
- lean-body-mass

**Protocol takeaway:** Use as adjacent evidence for low-volume HIIT fitness context; avoid claiming body-composition effects from Tabata 20/10.

**Safety and limitations:** No adverse-event counts or safety event rates were extracted from accessible metadata/abstract details.  Key limitations: Low-volume HIIT definitions vary.; Included populations include healthy and clinical adults.; No direct Tabata 20/10 subgroup was extracted.

**Population mismatch:** Low-volume HIIT trials often use supervised cycling/running protocols and may not match bodyweight Tabata circuits.
