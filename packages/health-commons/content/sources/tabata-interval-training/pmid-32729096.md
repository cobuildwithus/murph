This source is included for **Reviews, definitions, and variant taxonomy**.

**Findings:** Critiques common intensity-prescription methods and supports caution when translating fixed percentages or wearable targets into protocol fidelity.

**Study design and population:** Narrative methods critique; Exercise-prescription contexts across populations; no single participant cohort.

**Intervention or exposure:** Methods for determining and prescribing exercise intensity, including relative percentage approaches.

**Comparator or control:** Different intensity-prescription frameworks and their ability to provoke consistent physiological perturbation.

**Duration or follow-up:** Review source; no single intervention duration.

**Endpoints:** Exercise intensity classification, homeostatic perturbation, internal-load interpretation, percent HR/VO2 limitations.

**Safety notes:** No adverse-event rate extracted; misclassification can affect overload or underdose risk.

**Limitations and mismatch:** General intensity-methods review, not Tabata-specific. Population mismatch: Does not directly test a Tabata protocol or consumer wearable setup.

**Why it matters:** It explains why simple HR or VO2 percentages can mislead practical 20/10 dosing.

**Potential experiment signals:** RPE, heart-rate response, power/pace if available, completion rate, symptoms.

**Protocol takeaway:** Use multiple intensity signals and label uncertainty; avoid pretending that one wearable percentage proves original Tabata fidelity.

**Claim use:** `context-only`.
