This source is included for **practical_or_population_boundary_tabata**.

## Findings

- [source_artifact:doi-10.25035/ijare.09.03.06] The workout used a 20-minute shallow-water Tabata-style format in 9 females and measured acute cardiorespiratory responses.
- [source_artifact:doi-10.25035/ijare.09.03.06] Reported signals included heart-rate and oxygen-uptake reserve, energy expenditure, perceived exertion, and post-session lactate.
- [source_artifact:doi-10.25035/ijare.09.03.06] The water setting substantially changes loading, so this is adjacent-variant evidence only.

## Why it matters

It helps define a lower-impact aquatic boundary: the timing resembles Tabata-style interval work, but the medium changes loading and intensity interpretation.

## Potential experiment signals

- session heart-rate response
- oxygen-uptake or perceived-intensity fidelity
- blood lactate
- energy expenditure
- post-session symptoms

## Protocol takeaway

Mention only as aquatic variant context. Do not use it to claim that standard land-based Tabata improves VO2max or other longitudinal outcomes.

## Population and directness boundary

Population mismatch is high for default adult self-directed Tabata: small female sample, water-supported exercise, and no chronic training follow-up. Directness is `adjacent_variant`.
