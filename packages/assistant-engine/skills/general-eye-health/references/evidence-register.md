# General Eye-Health Evidence Register

Evidence review date: 2026-07-10. Scope: general adult guidance, not diagnosis or individualized treatment. Prefer the newest applicable clinician guidance when a user's age, disease, surgery, pregnancy, medicine, or procedure changes the risk.

## How to Use This Register

- **Higher confidence** means an authoritative public-health or specialty source, a systematic review, or a strong randomized trial supports the practical conclusion.
- **Moderate confidence** often means consensus or ergonomics guidance is reasonable but not proven as a precise therapeutic dose.
- **Limited evidence** means Murph may offer a low-risk option or memory cue without promising benefit.
- Safety routing outranks comfort evidence. Read `triage-and-contact-lenses.md` for active symptoms.

## Evidence Matrix

| Digital eye strain | Reduced or incomplete blinking, sustained near work, ocular-surface dryness, uncorrected refractive error, binocular factors, glare, airflow, and ergonomics can contribute. Symptoms and signs are nonspecific. | Say symptoms "can fit" a digital-eye-strain pattern. Do not diagnose it or assume a single screen cause. | [TFOS digital-environment report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37062428/); [NEI dry eye](https://www.nei.nih.gov/eye-health-information/eye-conditions-and-diseases/dry-eye) |
| Screen damage | Ordinary monitor use can produce fatigue or dryness, but it is not evidence of permanent eye damage in a healthy adult. | Reassure only after red flags are absent. Do not say screens caused the user's myopia or retinal damage. | [NEI Healthy Vision booklet](https://www.nei.nih.gov/sites/default/files/2019-06/NEI_Healthy-Vision_booklet_WEB_508%20%281%29.pdf); [TFOS digital-environment report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37062428/) |
| Visual breaks and blinking | Periodic distance changes and complete blinking are low-risk comfort practices. The exact `20-20-20` formula has limited evidence as a treatment dose. Small studies are mixed. | Offer self-paced brief breaks; use `20-20-20` only as an optional memory cue, never a protection threshold or myopia treatment. | [NEI Healthy Vision booklet](https://www.nei.nih.gov/sites/default/files/2019-06/NEI_Healthy-Vision_booklet_WEB_508%20%281%29.pdf); [20-second break trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36473088/); [reminder intervention](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35963776/) |
| Workstation setup | Readable text, glare control, looking away, blinking, and a comfortable screen roughly 20–40 inches away with its top at or below eye level are standard ergonomics guidance. | Optimize for comfort and accessibility; do not turn measurements into medical thresholds. | [OSHA computer monitor guidance](https://www.osha.gov/etools/computer-workstations/components/monitors) |
| Blue-light-filtering lenses | A Cochrane review found they may not reduce short-term computer eyestrain; sleep results were inconsistent and the trials did not establish macular protection. | Do not recommend them as treatment or retinal protection. A tint can remain a preference if the user likes it. | [Cochrane blue-light-filtering lens review](https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD013244_blue-light-filtering-spectacle-lenses-visual-performance-macular-back-part-eye-protection-and) |
| Digital-eye-strain products | A systematic review of 45 randomized trials found no evaluated intervention supported by high-certainty evidence; blue blockers were not beneficial and supplement or specialty-lens evidence was weak or negative. | Prefer low-cost environmental changes over product stacks and communicate uncertainty. | [Ophthalmology systematic review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35597519/) |
| Artificial tears | Lubricating drops commonly help mild dry-eye symptoms, but evidence does not identify one universally best OTC formulation. Contact compatibility matters. | Mention a cautious labeled option, not a prescription or a substitute for evaluation of pain, redness, discharge, or vision change. | [NEI dry eye](https://www.nei.nih.gov/eye-health-information/eye-conditions-and-diseases/dry-eye); [systematic review of artificial tears](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9840372/) |
| Omega-3 for dry eye | In the 535-participant DREAM randomized trial, high-dose omega-3 was no better than placebo over 12 months; broader reviews remain inconsistent. | Do not routinely recommend fish oil for screen-related dryness. | [NIH DREAM trial summary](https://www.nih.gov/news-events/news-releases/omega-3s-fish-oil-supplements-no-better-placebo-dry-eye); [Cochrane omega-3 review](https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD011016_omega-3-and-omega-6-polyunsaturated-fatty-acid-supplements-dry-eye-disease) |
| "Eye vitamins" | AREDS2 supplements are for specific stages of clinician-diagnosed age-related macular degeneration, not general eye health, myopia, or screen strain. | Do not offer generic eye supplements. Defer stage-specific AREDS2 decisions to the eye clinician. | [NEI AREDS/AREDS2 FAQ](https://www.nei.nih.gov/eye-health-information/clinical-trials/age-related-eye-disease-studies-aredsareds2/aredsareds2-frequently-asked-questions) |
| Myopia and a lens number | Myopia is a refractive error corrected by glasses or contacts. A contact-lens power is only one prescription parameter and does not show lens fit, full refraction, eye health, or symptom cause. | Treat `-2.75` as correction context, not a diagnosis or urgency score. Never change power, brand, or fit from chat. | [NEI nearsightedness](https://www.nei.nih.gov/eye-health-information/eye-conditions-and-diseases/nearsightedness-myopia); [FDA buying contact lenses](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/contact-lenses/buying-contact-lenses) |
| Myopia exercises and under-correction | Eye exercises have limited-to-no evidence for controlling or reversing myopia, and intentional under-correction is not an established control strategy. | Separate useful distance breaks from claims that exercises or weaker lenses reverse an adult prescription. | [Myopia exercise systematic review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10858027/); [International Myopia Institute intervention report](https://myopiainstitute.org/imi-whitepaper/interventions-for-myopia-onset-and-progression/) |
| Contact-lens infection prevention | Hand hygiene, no water, no unauthorized overnight wear, fresh solution, case care, prescribed replacement, and follow-up exams reduce avoidable risk. Pain, redness, light sensitivity, a new vision change, tearing, or discharge lowers the care threshold. | Give prevention when relevant; active symptoms use the triage reference first. | [CDC prevention](https://www.cdc.gov/contact-lenses/prevention/index.html); [CDC infection symptoms](https://www.cdc.gov/contact-lenses/causes/index.html); [NEI contact lenses](https://www.nei.nih.gov/eye-health-information/healthy-vision/contact-lenses) |
| Prevention and exams | Eye-exam timing depends on age and risk. Contact-lens users generally need at least yearly review. Protective eyewear, sunglasses blocking 99–100% UVA/UVB, smoking avoidance, and management of diabetes and blood pressure are established prevention guidance. | Supply evidence to `Arrange a routine eye exam` in the triage Decision Order. Do not assign exam timing or type outside that outcome. | [NEI dilated eye exam](https://www.nei.nih.gov/eye-health-information/healthy-vision/finding-eye-doctor/get-dilated-eye-exam); [NEI healthy vision](https://www.nei.nih.gov/eye-health-information/healthy-vision/8-things-you-can-do-right-now-protect-your-vision); [CDC eye care](https://www.cdc.gov/vision-health/prevention/taking-care-of-your-eyes.html) |

## Important Scope Limits

- Child and adolescent myopia prevention or control is a separate clinician-led problem; adult screen-strain advice must not be repurposed into a pediatric treatment plan.
- Severe dry-eye treatment, prescription drops, contact-lens refitting, binocular-vision therapy, glaucoma, cataract, retinal disease, infection, surgery, or post-procedure care requires professional evaluation.
- A normal-looking eye or a reassuring photo cannot rule out corneal, pressure-related, retinal, or neurologic disease.
