# Evidence register

**Last searched/reviewed:** 2026-06-25  
**Purpose:** provenance for the generalized reasoning, triage, remote-care, exercise, follow-up, adherence, privacy, and product-boundary choices in this skill. This is not a complete clinical guideline library and does not replace region-specific clinical review.

## Evidence-use rules

- Prefer current clinical guidelines, standards, systematic reviews, and authoritative public-health sources.
- Record population, setting, intervention, outcomes, and limitations; do not generalize by title alone.
- Human-led telehealth evidence does not validate autonomous AI diagnosis.
- Exercise evidence is usually condition-, stage-, population-, and dose-dependent.
- Red flags raise suspicion and guide referral; isolated historical items rarely diagnose a serious condition.
- Every future regional/condition module requires its own evidence review and owner.

## Physical-therapy patient/client management

### American Physical Therapy Association. Standards of Practice for Physical Therapy. 2020.

- https://www.apta.org/siteassets/pdfs/policies/standards-of-practice-pt.pdf

The standard describes collaborative patient/client management, examination/evaluation, diagnosis/prognosis, management and plan of care, intervention aligned with goals and response, reexamination, referral/consultation, outcomes, communication, and documentation.

**Skill implication:** Murph's useful approximation is an iterative loop—screen, history, function, working hypotheses, plan, response, re-examination, and referral—not an exercise lookup. Murph must not claim the professional examination, diagnosis, or legal responsibilities of a physical therapist.

### American Physical Therapy Association. Documentation: Initial Examination and Evaluation. Updated web resource.

- https://www.apta.org/your-practice/documentation/defensible-documentation/elements-within-the-patientclient-management-model/initial-examination

The resource lists history/review of systems, physical examination, tests/measures, outcomes, management planning, progress based on response, and referral/consultation as parts of the ongoing process.

**Skill implication:** Intake, outcomes, and referral logic belong in the core skill; exercise selection is downstream.

### American Physical Therapy Association. Elements of Documentation Within the Patient-Client Management Model. 2024.

- https://www.apta.org/your-practice/documentation/defensible-documentation/elements-within-the-patientclient-management-model

**Skill implication:** Store a concise episode state, response history, re-examination decision, and conclusion/handoff while preserving source and uncertainty.

### World Physiotherapy. Description of Physical Therapy.

- https://world.physio/policy/ps-descriptionPT

**Skill implication:** Physical therapy includes assessment, diagnosis/prognosis, planning, intervention, education, reevaluation, referral, prevention, and self-management. An AI product should not reduce the profession to exercise videos.

## Function and outcomes

### World Health Organization. International Classification of Functioning, Disability and Health (ICF).

- https://www.who.int/standards/classifications/international-classification-of-functioning-disability-and-health

**Skill implication:** Track body symptoms together with activity, participation, personal goals, and context. Pain intensity alone is not an adequate outcome.

### Stratford PW, Gill C, Westaway MD, Binkley JM. Assessing Disability and Change on Individual Patients: A Report of a Patient Specific Measure. Physiotherapy Canada. 1995.

**Skill implication:** User-selected functional activities can provide a meaningful longitudinal signal. Murph should not call an improvised conversational version a validated PSFS administration unless it follows the official instrument and scoring.

## Triage and red-flag reasoning

### Finucane LM, et al. International Framework for Red Flags for Potential Serious Spinal Pathologies. Journal of Orthopaedic & Sports Physical Therapy. 2020.

- DOI: 10.2519/jospt.2020.9971
- https://www.jospt.org/doi/10.2519/jospt.2020.9971

**Skill implication:** Serious spinal pathology screening requires categories, context, combinations, and clinical reasoning rather than one generic checklist.

### Andreoletti F, et al. Screening for Referral of Serious Pathology by Physical Examination in Patients With Musculoskeletal Disorders: A Scoping Review. 2022.

- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9779291/

**Skill implication:** Individual red flags often have limited accuracy. Murph should use targeted, clustered reasoning and avoid saying one absent sign rules a condition out.

### Henschke N, et al. Red flags to screen for malignancy in patients with low-back pain. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews. 2013.

- https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD008686.pub2/full

**Skill implication:** Many historical red flags are nonspecific; meaningful prior cancer and combinations of findings deserve more weight than isolated symptoms.

### U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention. Deep Vein Thrombosis and Pulmonary Embolism; Testing and Diagnosis for Venous Thromboembolism. Updated 2024–2025.

- https://www.cdc.gov/yellow-book/hcp/travel-air-sea/deep-vein-thrombosis-and-pulmonary-embolism.html
- https://www.cdc.gov/blood-clots/testing-diagnosis/index.html
- https://www.cdc.gov/blood-clots/about/index.html

DVT symptoms can include one-sided pain/tenderness, swelling, warmth, and redness/discoloration; PE can include unexplained breathlessness, pleuritic chest pain, cough/hemoptysis, or syncope. Symptoms are nonspecific and professional testing/imaging is needed.

**Skill implication:** Do not teach a self-test, massage, or exercise when a clot pattern is plausible. Route urgent/emergency care based on the cluster.

### NHS. Joint pain. Reviewed 2026.

- https://www.nhs.uk/symptoms/joint-pain/

The NHS advises urgent assessment for a hot swollen joint or systemic illness and emergency assessment after serious injury with inability to bear weight, deformity, or altered sensation.

**Skill implication:** Hot/red swelling, fever/systemic illness, deformity, inability to use the joint, and post-injury sensory change override a routine exercise plan.

### American Academy of Orthopaedic Surgeons. Compartment Syndrome.

- https://orthoinfo.aaos.org/en/diseases--conditions/compartment-syndrome/

**Skill implication:** Severe escalating pain, especially with stretch after trauma/swelling/cast context, is an emergency pattern.

### Buckinghamshire Healthcare NHS Trust. Cauda Equina Syndrome.

- https://www.buckshealthcare.nhs.uk/pifs/cauda-equina-syndrome/

**Skill implication:** New saddle sensory change, bladder/bowel disturbance, and progressive leg weakness in a compatible spinal presentation require emergency evaluation.

## Telerehabilitation and remote assessment

### Cottrell MA, et al. Real-time telerehabilitation for the treatment of musculoskeletal conditions is effective and comparable to standard practice: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Clinical Rehabilitation. 2017.

- DOI: 10.1177/0269215516645148
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27141087/

**Skill implication:** Human-led telerehabilitation can support selected musculoskeletal care, but its evidence does not transfer automatically to unsupervised AI.

### Mani S, et al. Validity and reliability of Internet-based physiotherapy assessment for musculoskeletal disorders: a systematic review. Journal of Telemedicine and Telecare. 2017.

- DOI: 10.1177/1357633X16642369
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27036879/

**Skill implication:** Some observation and movement measures can be feasible remotely, while palpation, special tests, and several examination components remain limited.

### Systematic review/meta-analysis of clinician-led remote versus in-person musculoskeletal diagnosis and management concordance. Journal of Medical Internet Research. 2024.

- https://www.jmir.org/2024/1/e52964/

**Skill implication:** Remote assessment may agree reasonably with in-person assessment in selected populations, but evidence on red/yellow-flag detection and generalizability remains limited. Murph should specialize in stable, lower-risk use cases and preserve an in-person off-ramp.

### Stakeholder research on remote initial musculoskeletal assessment. 2025.

**Skill implication:** Common concerns include incomplete examination, self-administered tests, diagnostic error, digital access, privacy, and therapeutic relationship. Use simple cases, low-risk observations, and hybrid escalation.

## Exercise, loading, and pain response

### Smith BE, et al. Should exercises be painful in the management of chronic musculoskeletal pain? A systematic review and meta-analysis. British Journal of Sports Medicine. 2017.

- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28596288/

Some painful-exercise protocols showed small short-term benefits in selected chronic musculoskeletal conditions, but the evidence does not support one universal pain allowance.

**Skill implication:** Avoid both “all pain means harm” and “up to X/10 is always safe.” Use stability, symptom quality, function, swelling/neurological findings, and recovery.

### Silbernagel KG, et al. Continued Sports Activity, Using a Pain-Monitoring Model, During Rehabilitation in Patients With Achilles Tendinopathy. American Journal of Sports Medicine. 2007.

- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17307888/

**Skill implication:** Pain-monitoring models can be useful in selected conditions but cannot be generalized to every injury or body region.

### American College of Sports Medicine. Progression Models in Resistance Training for Healthy Adults. Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise. 2009.

- DOI: 10.1249/MSS.0b013e3181915670

**Skill implication:** Progressive overload and specificity are useful principles, but healthy-adult training prescriptions are not automatically acute-rehabilitation prescriptions. Use conservative fallback ranges and response-based adjustment.

### Sherrington C, et al. Exercise for preventing falls in older people living in the community. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews. 2019.

- https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD012424.pub2/full

**Skill implication:** Balance and functional exercise can be valuable, but remote balance work must use environmental and support safeguards.

## Return to activity

### Ardern CL, et al. 2016 Consensus Statement on Return to Sport From the First World Congress in Sports Physical Therapy, Bern. British Journal of Sports Medicine. 2016.

- DOI: 10.1136/bjsports-2016-096278
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27226389/

**Skill implication:** Return to sport is a continuum and a multifactorial risk-management process, not one end-stage test. Murph can support graded exposure but cannot issue clearance.

## Adherence, shared decision-making, and alliance

### Ley C, Putz P. Efficacy of Interventions and Techniques on Adherence to Physiotherapy in Adults: An Overview of Systematic Reviews and Panoramic Meta-Analysis. Systematic Reviews. 2024;13:137.

- DOI: 10.1186/s13643-024-02538-9
- https://systematicreviewsjournal.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s13643-024-02538-9

**Skill implication:** Adherence interventions are heterogeneous. Do not assume reminders alone solve the problem; match support to the actual barrier.

### Chester R, et al. Behaviour Change Techniques to Promote Self-Management and Home Exercise Adherence for People Attending Physiotherapy With Musculoskeletal Conditions. Musculoskeletal Science and Practice. 2023;66:102776.

- DOI: 10.1016/j.msksp.2023.102776
- PMID: 37301059

**Skill implication:** Identify modifiable determinants such as knowledge, confidence, environment, goals, monitoring, and context; then choose a targeted behavior-support technique.

### Moore AJ, Holden MA, Foster NE, Jinks C. Therapeutic Alliance Facilitates Adherence to Physiotherapy-Led Exercise and Physical Activity for Older Adults With Knee Pain. Journal of Physiotherapy. 2020;66(1):45–53.

- DOI: 10.1016/j.jphys.2019.11.004
- PMID: 31843425

**Skill implication:** Listening, collaboration, continuity, and perceived relevance are functional parts of the intervention.

## AI and digital rehabilitation

### Pros and Cons of Using Artificial Intelligence Chatbots for Musculoskeletal Rehabilitation Management. Review article.

- https://cris.unibo.it/retrieve/handle/11585/942174/b374afab-147d-427a-b1c3-2796f7891206/Pros%20and%20Cons%20of%20Using%20Artificial%20Intelligence%20Chatbots%20for%20Musculoskeletal%20Rehabilitation%20Management.pdf

**Skill implication:** AI may support education, communication, reminders, and reasoning support, but inaccurate or misleading answers require guardrails, clinical review, and escalation.

### How AI-Based Digital Rehabilitation Improves End-User Adherence: Rapid Review. JMIR Rehabilitation and Assistive Technologies. 2025.

- https://rehab.jmir.org/2025/1/e69763/

**Skill implication:** Tracking, personalization, reminders, usability, communication, and objective adherence measurement may help, but evidence remains early and heterogeneous.

## Product, privacy, and regulatory boundary

### U.S. Food and Drug Administration. Device Software Functions and Mobile Medical Applications; Software as a Medical Device.

- https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/digital-health-center-excellence/device-software-functions-including-mobile-medical-applications
- https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/digital-health-center-excellence/software-medical-device-samd

**Skill implication:** Regulatory status depends on intended use and actual function. A patient-specific system that diagnoses, treats, or directs rehabilitation may require medical-device analysis; disclaimers alone do not settle classification.

### U.S. Department of Health and Human Services. HIPAA Privacy Rule resources.

- https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/index.html

**Skill implication:** Product implementation requires jurisdiction-appropriate privacy, security, consent, retention, and access controls. The skill itself follows minimum-necessary collection and separates source from inference.

## Known evidence gaps

- High-quality evidence for fully autonomous AI-led musculoskeletal triage and rehabilitation is limited.
- Human clinician telehealth evidence cannot be assumed to validate AI diagnosis or emergency exclusion.
- Red-flag definitions and referral thresholds vary by condition, guideline, and health system.
- Optimal exercise type, dose, and acceptable symptom response differ by condition, stage, and person.
- Conversational outcome tracking is useful but may not be equivalent to validated clinical measures.
- Media-based movement assessment can be affected by camera angle, environment, user performance, and model error.
- Adherence interventions are context-dependent and often poorly standardized.
- Pediatric, postoperative, neurological, cardiopulmonary, pelvic-health, pregnancy/postpartum, and complex medical rehabilitation need separate reviewed pathways.

These gaps justify conservative scope, explicit uncertainty, clinician escalation, versioned modules, safety evaluation, adverse-event monitoring, and rollback.

## Maintenance rules

- Review the core safety sources at least annually and after major guideline changes.
- Review every condition/regional module before activation and assign a licensed clinical owner.
- Record search date, content-review date, version, and limitations.
- Link adverse events and near misses to the exact skill/module/catalog versions used.
- Deprecate unsafe or outdated content with a migration note; do not silently overwrite active plans.
