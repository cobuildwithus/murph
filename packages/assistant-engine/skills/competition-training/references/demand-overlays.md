# Demand overlays

Use this reference to represent a competition with a small set of stable demands. Brands, race names, distances, rules, and sponsors change; the underlying work changes more slowly.

## Build the demand card

Record only dimensions that change training or execution:

1. **Time and density:** expected duration; continuous, staged, repeated heats, or multi-day; recovery between efforts.
2. **Locomotion and terrain:** running, cycling, swimming, rowing/paddling, mixed; road, track, trail, technical terrain, open water, indoor, elevation.
3. **Intensity pattern:** steady, progressive, surging/stochastic, interval-station, repeated maximal/submaximal, or unknown.
4. **Skill and transitions:** low to high technical demand; equipment changes; drafting/pack skill; movement standards; judging.
5. **External load and muscular demand:** low, supportive, material, or dominant; grip, carries, obstacles, gymnastics, lifting, eccentric load.
6. **Execution and risk:** cutoffs, aid access, navigation, penalties, partner/team synchronization, consequence of error, environmental exposure.

Add the user's expected duration rather than relying on the nominal distance. A trail half marathon, road half marathon, and mountain half marathon may require different overlays because their durations and risks differ.

## Overlay A: continuous endurance

Fits events dominated by sustained locomotion for a known duration: road running, steady trail running, gran fondos, time trials, long swims, rowing races, paddling, and much of long-course triathlon.

### Decisive demands

- sustainable aerobic output and economy
- durability at the event's expected duration
- pacing restraint and late-event decision quality
- fueling/hydration tolerance when duration warrants it
- terrain, position, equipment, and environmental tolerance

### Assess

- current frequency and total easy work
- longest recent exposure and next-day response
- sustainable benchmark relevant to the mode
- history of late-race fade, GI issues, cramps, or pacing error
- whether the event duration exceeds anything recently tolerated

### Training emphasis

Use mostly absorbable aerobic work, one or more targeted sustainable-intensity exposures when appropriate, progressively longer or more specific durability, supportive strength, and selected race rehearsals. Separate easy and demanding work clearly enough that the athlete can absorb both.

Completion plans emphasize frequency, durability, cutoff margin, conservative pacing, and logistics. Performance plans add stronger limiter-specific work, closer event-pace calibration, and more precise fatigue-resistant execution.

### Common errors

- making the long session the only meaningful training
- turning easy work into constant moderate work
- chasing target pace before the athlete can tolerate the duration
- adding volume, intensity, and terrain difficulty together
- leaving fueling, shoes/equipment, or position changes until race day

## Overlay B: surges, pack, and terrain

Fits road races and criteriums, mountain bike, cyclocross, technical trail and mountain racing, variable-gradient cycling, tactical running, and events where position or terrain forces repeated changes in output.

### Decisive demands

- repeated changes above and below sustainable output
- recovery while still moving
- acceleration, climbing, descending, cornering, or pack-position skill
- decision quality under local fatigue
- terrain-specific muscular and eccentric tolerance

### Assess

- steady capacity plus repeatability of short surges
- technical skill and safe access to practice
- confidence and judgment in packs, descents, turns, obstacles, or uneven terrain
- local limiter: power, braking/handling, climbing, descending, footwork, or recovery after surges

### Training emphasis

Keep the continuous-endurance base, then add controlled stochastic work, terrain-specific exposure, and supervised skill practice. Separate fitness work from high-consequence technical practice when combining them would degrade safety.

Completion plans prioritize safe handling, terrain familiarity, conservative positioning, and sufficient durability. Performance plans add tactical rehearsal, repeatability, race-position decisions, and event-specific surges.

### Common errors

- using indoor fitness as proof of outdoor handling skill
- practicing technical risk only when exhausted
- copying elite tactical sessions without the base or supervision
- ignoring downhill/eccentric load because uphill fitness looks good
- treating every group ride or technical session as a race

## Overlay C: multi-discipline and transition

Fits triathlon, duathlon, swimrun, run-bike-run, relays with changing modes, and events where one discipline alters the next.

### Decisive demands

- discipline-specific capacity and the cost of each discipline on the next
- transitions, equipment, mounting/dismounting, and setup
- pacing across the whole event rather than winning one segment
- fueling and hydration while changing modes
- open-water, pack, drafting, or equipment rules where applicable

### Assess

- ability and safety in each discipline
- the weakest discipline versus the discipline that creates the largest downstream cost
- transition fluency and equipment reliability
- bike-to-run, swim-to-bike, or other mode-change response
- access constraints such as pool, open water, safe roads, bike fit, or transport

### Training emphasis

Do not divide training equally by default. Allocate work by event contribution, current limiter, technical risk, and recoverability. Use short, purposeful transition sessions and occasional integrated rehearsals; not every weekend needs to become a maximal brick.

Completion plans emphasize safe competence in every discipline, transitions, equipment, cutoff margin, and conservative whole-race pacing. Performance plans use discipline-specific benchmarks, optimized interaction between disciplines, and more precise transition execution.

### Common errors

- training the favorite discipline and avoiding the weakest
- overusing long bricks while undertraining each discipline well
- treating transition practice as trivial logistics
- racing the bike and surviving the run
- trying new open-water, equipment, or fueling choices near the event

## Overlay D: hybrid station, obstacle, and external load

Fits HYROX-style fixed run-station events, Spartan-style competitive OCR, Tough Mudder-style participation events, loaded endurance, adventure races, and mixed locomotion with carries, crawls, walls, sleds, or implements.

### Decisive demands

- locomotion while carrying local muscular fatigue
- strength endurance and movement economy under elevated breathing
- grip, carries, obstacles, sleds, lunges, burpees, or other known standards
- transition and station execution
- terrain, mud, weather, penalty, and safety demands

### Assess

- locomotion capacity in isolation
- strength/skill capacity in isolation
- which stations or obstacles create disproportionate time, failure, or risk
- ability to resume efficient movement after local fatigue
- access to implements and safe substitutes

### Training emphasis

Build the locomotion and strength foundations separately enough to improve them, then integrate selectively. Use technique-first station practice, controlled compromised-running/locomotion work, grip and tissue durability, and simulations that answer a specific question.

For a runner-dominant athlete, local strength endurance or standards may limit. For a strength-dominant athlete, running durability and pacing often deserve more protected work. Do not assume one profile from appearance.

Completion plans prioritize safe completion of standards, efficient movement, penalty avoidance, and conservative transitions. Performance plans target bottleneck stations, compromised locomotion, station order, and time lost in transitions without making every session a full simulation.

### Common errors

- doing frequent full-event simulations instead of training limiters
- replacing aerobic development with random circuits
- practicing technical obstacles only under extreme fatigue
- ignoring movement standards and penalties
- adding high-impact running, heavy carries, and high-rep strength together too quickly

## Overlay E: repeated heat and unknown task

Fits CrossFit Open-style events, local or multi-day functional-fitness competitions, strength-endurance meets, and formats with several events, variable recovery, or partly unknown tasks.

### Decisive demands

- broad capacity across likely domains
- repeatability across events and recovery between heats/days
- movement standards, judging, transitions, and scoring strategy
- warm-up logistics and rapid task interpretation
- high-skill movements and heavy lifts under competition constraints

### Assess

- event history and likely task envelope
- aerobic/anaerobic capacity, strength, power, gymnastics, and movement-specific gaps
- skill consistency when fresh before testing under fatigue
- recovery between efforts and multi-day tolerance
- competition logistics and ability to warm up without draining the event

### Training emphasis

Develop broad capacity while making the largest gaps explicit. Practice likely standards, transitions, and pacing across varied time domains. Use selected back-to-back events or mock heats to rehearse recovery and decisions. Preserve technical quality; fatigue is not a substitute for skill.

Completion plans emphasize valid reps, safe scaling/division choice, basic pacing, and logistics. Performance plans emphasize scoring leverage, weak-domain development, repeated-event recovery, and execution under judging pressure.

### Common errors

- random variation with no diagnosed gap
- testing more often than training
- learning high-skill or maximal lifts under fatigue
- chasing leaderboard strategy before valid movement and pacing
- neglecting between-event food, cooling, clothing, and warm-up timing

## Overlay F: strength, power, or skill dominant

Fits weightlifting, powerlifting, strongman-adjacent events, short sprint/power competitions, and mixed events where maximal or near-maximal strength, power, or technical execution dominates more than endurance.

Use this competition skill only when event preparation and execution are the main question. This packet is not a complete strength-sport coaching system.

### Decisive demands

- event-specific technical proficiency
- strength/power expression and attempt selection
- fatigue management across attempts or events
- equipment, commands, standards, weigh-in, and warm-up timing
- high consequence of technical or loading error

### Assess

- current coached proficiency and safe recent exposures
- competition-valid performance rather than gym estimates alone
- injury/return-to-sport status
- event order, attempt rules, and equipment familiarity
- access to qualified coaching and spotters where appropriate

### Training emphasis

Preserve technical quality, build competition-valid exposures, reduce nonessential fatigue near the event, and rehearse attempts/logistics. Do not prescribe maximal attempts, novel lifts, extreme weight cuts, or high-risk implements from chat.

Completion plans prioritize valid attempts, safe loading, commands, and a conservative opener. Performance plans require more precise attempt strategy and usually benefit from direct qualified coaching.

## Cross-cutting modifiers

Apply modifiers to any overlay rather than creating another event chapter.

### Long duration and fueling

When the event materially exceeds stored-fuel/tolerance limits, add progressive fueling, hydration, aid-station, and GI rehearsal. Use `fueling-recovery-safety.md` for detail.

### Heat, cold, altitude, water, or remoteness

Add acclimation, equipment, conservative pacing, emergency access, and route-specific safety. Environment can override nominal performance targets.

### Technical or high-consequence terrain

Fitness does not confer skill. Use supervised, progressive practice and separate technical learning from severe fatigue when needed.

### Team, partner, or relay

Add synchronization, role clarity, communication, handoffs, shared equipment, contingency rules, and the weakest-link effect. Do not expose private health details in team coordination without permission.

### Youth or developmental athlete

Protect long-term development before short-term result chasing. Account for growth and maturation, training age, school and sleep, safeguarding, age-group rules, qualified supervision, and the athlete's own motivation. Do not shrink an adult plan, normalize year-round specialization, or use weight cuts and supplement strategies as proof of seriousness.

### Masters or older athlete

Use training age, recent exposure, health history, recovery response, strength/power needs, and life context rather than chronological age alone. Do not assume fragility or apply an automatic low ceiling; also do not dismiss new symptoms, medication effects, or slower recovery when they are present.

### Adaptive or para format

Start with the athlete's classification, equipment, lived experience, clinician/coach constraints, and the actual organizer rules. Do not infer capacity from diagnosis or disability label, and do not route away solely because the athlete is disabled. Treat equipment fit, transfer demands, skin/pressure risk, thermoregulation, autonomic considerations, medication, and accessibility as decision-changing when relevant.

### Navigation or self-support

Add route finding, mandatory kit, resupply, emergency communication, and decision thresholds. A self-supported event is not just a longer fitness test.

## Example composition map

Use examples to choose overlays, not to fetch a canned plan:

| Event | Typical overlay composition |
|---|---|
| 5K/10K road | continuous endurance; add tactical surges for competitive fields |
| Half marathon/marathon | continuous endurance + long-duration/fueling modifier as needed |
| Trail/mountain/ultra | continuous endurance + terrain/surges + environment/navigation/fueling modifiers |
| Sprint/Olympic triathlon | multi-discipline + continuous endurance; add pack/drafting rules when applicable |
| 70.3/Ironman | multi-discipline + continuous endurance + long-duration/fueling |
| Cycling time trial | continuous endurance + position/equipment execution |
| Road race/criterium | surges/pack + continuous endurance + technical risk |
| Gran fondo/gravel/MTB | continuous endurance + terrain/surges; add pack, navigation, or fueling modifiers |
| HYROX | hybrid station + continuous endurance |
| Spartan/OCR | hybrid obstacle + terrain/surges + technical risk |
| Tough Mudder participation | hybrid obstacle + team/social + safety/logistics; completion usually dominates |
| CrossFit Open | repeated heat/unknown task + strength/power/skill |
| Multi-day functional fitness | repeated heat + strength/power/skill + recovery logistics |
| Adventure race | multi-discipline + terrain + navigation + team + long-duration modifiers |

## Add a new overlay only when

All are true:

- a recurring demand pattern is not representable by the existing overlays and modifiers
- the difference changes training, execution, or safety
- multiple real user cases justify maintenance
- the new overlay can remain short and evidence-linked
- at least one eval fails without it

A new brand, sponsor, distance label, or season is not enough.

## Compact output

When classifying an event, keep the internal result brief:

- primary overlay(s)
- two to four decisive demands
- one primary limiter hypothesis
- one rule/course/environment fact to verify, if any
- one implication for completion and one for performance, only when relevant
