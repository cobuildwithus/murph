# HRV And Resting Heart Rate

Use this as Murph operating guidance, not as a consumer article. Ground the answer in the current conversation, vault context, and wearable data before recommending. Ask at most one missing question when the answer would materially change the next step.

## Owns

- HRV and resting-heart-rate interpretation using personal baselines, trend length, and context.
- Explaining noise versus signal in wearable autonomic markers.
- Illness, stress, heat, altitude, alcohol, sleep debt, training load, and aerobic fitness effects on RHR/HRV.

## Hand Off

- Use sleep-recovery-readiness when the question is whether to train hard today.
- Use aerobic-fitness for VO2 max/cardio fitness estimates and chronic conditioning markers.
- Use substance-load for alcohol/caffeine/nicotine/cannabis-driven changes.
- Route chest pain, fainting, palpitations with symptoms, very high resting HR, arrhythmia concerns, or persistent unexplained changes to clinician support.

## Data First

- Check the user's baseline, measurement device, wear consistency, recent 7-30 day trend, sleep, alcohol, illness, heat, altitude, training load, hydration, and menstrual phase when relevant.
- Do not compare HRV across people; method, age, genetics, and device algorithms dominate.
- Treat a single low HRV day as context, not a verdict.

## If Context Is Thin

Ask: "Is this a one-day dip, a multi-day trend, or a new baseline over several weeks?"

## Practical Levers

- For acute dips with high RHR: screen illness, alcohol, sleep loss, heat, dehydration, and hard training.
- For chronic improvement: aerobic base training, sleep regularity, alcohol reduction, and stress-load management are the main levers.
- For overreaching suspicion: reduce intensity for 2-4 days and watch symptoms plus RHR/HRV rebound.
- For heat/altitude/travel: normalize expectations and reassess after acclimation or return.

## Interpretation Rules

- Higher HRV is not always better; unusually high HRV with fatigue can appear during under-recovery for some users.
- RHR is often easier to interpret than HRV. A sustained rise of roughly 5-10 bpm above baseline with symptoms is meaningful.
- A 20-30 percent HRV drop from personal baseline over several days matters more than one low night.

## Safety Boundaries

- Escalate for chest pain, fainting, severe shortness of breath, irregular heartbeat symptoms, or persistent unexplained tachycardia.
- Do not reassure away symptoms because the wearable score looks normal.

## Answer Shape

- Start with baseline-relative interpretation and the most likely context driver.
- Separate acute explanation from chronic lever.
- Use concrete reassessment windows: tomorrow for acute load, 3-7 days for illness/recovery, weeks to months for fitness change.
