# Vegetarian, pescatarian and flexitarian eating

The rules in `nutrition-strategy/SKILL.md` on evaluating diets, sensitive handling and clinician decisions apply here. This card adds what is specific to vegetarian patterns that keep dairy, eggs or fish. When dairy, eggs and fish are all excluded, read `vegan-plant-based.md` instead.

## Murph's position

- Ask which foods the member keeps. Dairy, eggs and seafood each make planning easier, and none makes the diet adequate on its own.
- Check these when the pattern is narrow or the member is at higher need:
  - Vitamin B12: a regular source from dairy, eggs, fish or B12-fortified foods. Small or occasional amounts of eggs or dairy may not cover it. Unfortified plant foods, fermented foods and seaweed do not count.
  - Iron: plant iron is absorbed less well, and dairy and eggs are poor sources. Heavy periods, pregnancy, adolescence, endurance training, blood donation and previous low iron call for a clinical check. Do not suggest self-prescribed iron.
  - Iodine: many specialty salts are not iodized. Do not suggest kelp or seaweed as a source, because the dose can be excessive. A pattern without dairy needs particular attention.
  - Calcium: a pattern without dairy needs a deliberate replacement, such as fortified soy drink or calcium-set tofu. Check labels, because fortification varies.
  - Omega-3 fats: plant sources convert poorly to EPA and DHA. Fish oil and algae oil questions belong to `micronutrients-supplements`.
- Anchor protein at main meals. Complementary proteins do not need to be paired at each meal.
- For training, use the parent skill's protein and fuel numbers. When high-fiber meals sit badly before hard sessions, suggest lower-fiber options near training.
- For a pescatarian pregnancy or when feeding a child, follow current lower-mercury fish guidance and local catch advisories.

## Screens

The parent skill's clinician list applies. Adult guidance on vegetarian diets does not carry over to pregnancy, breastfeeding, infants, children or adolescents. For those, and for a member feeding a child on a narrow pattern, give ordinary food ideas and involve a clinician and dietitian for the plan. Involve them early as well for previous iron or B12 deficiency, malabsorption, and heavy training with signs of under-fueling. Do not set lab schedules or supplement doses from this card.

## Stop conditions

The member steps back from the restriction and gets assessed for persistent fatigue, dizziness, breathlessness, palpitations, unusual weakness, numbness or tingling, declining performance, repeated injury or illness, menstrual disruption, unexpected weight loss, a growth concern in a child, persistent digestive symptoms, or a food pattern that keeps narrowing out of fear.

## Off-ramp

Widen the pattern one step: lacto or ovo to lacto-ovo, vegetarian to flexitarian, adding fish when the member accepts it, using verified fortified foods, or fewer vegetarian days while intake is restored.
