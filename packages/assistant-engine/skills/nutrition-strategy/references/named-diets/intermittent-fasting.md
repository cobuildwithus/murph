# Intermittent fasting

The rules in `nutrition-strategy/SKILL.md` on evaluating diets, sensitive handling and clinician decisions apply here. This card adds what is specific to fasting patterns: time-restricted eating such as 14:10 and 16:8, 5:2, alternate-day fasting and one meal a day.

## Murph's position

- Fasting patterns give about the same results as ordinary calorie reduction a person can keep up. They suit members who like a simple timing rule. A shorter eating window is not better in itself.
- Start with the least restrictive version. First try ending late-night eating or keeping a 12-hour overnight gap. If the member wants a named pattern, start with 14:10. Move to 16:8 only when they are eating enough, feel well and can say what the tighter window adds.
- This starting point is Murph's default even when Health Commons returns a stricter fasting protocol such as 18:6 or a multi-day fast. Treat that protocol as reference evidence the member can choose later, and do not offer it as the first step.
- Do not suggest 5:2, alternate-day fasting or one meal a day as a first step. If a member with none of the screens below has already chosen 5:2, suggest non-consecutive, lower-demand days and normal eating on the other days, and do not invent a calorie target for fast days. For one meal a day, offer two or three meals in a wider window.
- When an early and a late window fit the member's life equally well, prefer the earlier one. Do not push an early window that costs sleep, food after training or family meals.
- Keep about three meals with protein inside the window. If a strength or muscle goal cannot fit, widen the window.
- Fasted exercise is optional. Place the window so a hard session has carbohydrate before it and carbohydrate plus protein after it. Eating to fuel a session is the right call even when it breaks the fast.
- Prescribed medication, hydration and enough food come before keeping a fast. Do not coach "clean fasting" rules about what breaks a fast.
- Do not promise that a fasting length triggers autophagy, longevity or any other guaranteed effect.
- Religious fasting has its own rules on food, fluid and timing. Support it as the member practices it.
- After a late meal or a broken window, the member resumes at the next normal meal. Do not suggest a longer fast to make up for it.

## Screens

These screens apply to eating windows of 14:10 or tighter, to 5:2, to alternate-day fasting and to one meal a day. A regular 12-hour overnight gap with normal meals is ordinary eating and needs no screen.

Do not give a self-directed fasting plan to a member who:

- is pregnant or breastfeeding, or under 18;
- is frail, eating little, losing weight without trying, or recovering from a period of very low intake;
- shows any sign listed under sensitive handling in the parent skill;
- is already under-fueled or training beyond what they recover from;
- is post-bariatric surgery or on another clinician-managed nutrition plan.

Offer regular meals and the 12-hour overnight gap instead, and say why.

A clinician or dietitian agrees the plan first when the member has diabetes, repeated low blood sugar, kidney disease, advanced liver disease, significant heart disease, a relevant endocrine disorder or a fluid restriction. The same holds when they take insulin or another medicine that can cause low blood sugar, a medicine that must be taken with food, or a medicine affected by dehydration or meal timing.

Suggest pausing the fasting pattern during acute illness, recovery from surgery or injury, heat exposure and endurance events.

## Stop conditions

- Shakiness, sweating, confusion or faintness: the member eats now and follows their existing medical plan.
- Stop the pattern and return to regular meals for repeated dizziness, headaches or nausea, worse sleep, persistent fatigue, declining training, menstrual changes, food preoccupation, rebound or loss-of-control eating, unintended weight loss, heavy caffeine use to suppress hunger, or missed food or medication.

## Off-ramp

Step down one level at a time: 16:8 to 14:10, then to a 12-hour gap or regular meals; 5:2 or alternate-day fasting to a consistent daily pattern; one meal a day to two meals and a snack, then three meals. A member can widen the window whenever the tighter one stops adding anything.
