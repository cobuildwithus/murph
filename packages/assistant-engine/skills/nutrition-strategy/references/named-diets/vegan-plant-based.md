# Vegan and plant-based eating

The rules in `nutrition-strategy/SKILL.md` on evaluating diets, sensitive handling and clinician decisions apply here. This card adds what is specific to vegan, plant-only and whole-food plant-based eating.

## Murph's position

- "Plant-based" has no single meaning. When the advice depends on it, ask what the member excludes and why. Ethics, environment, religion, health, taste, intolerance and budget lead to different acceptable changes.
- Treat ethical veganism as a boundary to work within. Do not try to negotiate it away, and do not rank diets morally.
- A well-planned vegan diet is adequate for adults, and the planning is what makes it so. The health evidence supports plant-rich eating and food quality. It does not show that strict exclusion adds benefit, or that oil-free, raw or very-low-fat rules are needed.
- A reliable source of vitamin B12 is required on a vegan diet. That means foods with verified B12 fortification, a B12 supplement, or both. Check the label, because fortification varies by product and country. Unfortified nutritional yeast, seaweed, fermented foods and algae do not count. Raise this before any symptoms appear, because nerve damage can develop without anemia and body stores can hide a shortfall for years. `micronutrients-supplements` owns the product and dose.
- Name the other nutrients that need a plan when they are relevant: iron, iodine, calcium, vitamin D, omega-3 fats and zinc. Give food-first options, and hand supplement choice and dose to `micronutrients-supplements`. Do not suggest iron supplements because of the diet label alone, and do not suggest seaweed as an iodine source, because its content ranges from very low to excessive.
- Anchor protein at three or four eating occasions with soy foods, legumes, seitan or pea-based foods. Foods do not need to be "combined" at each meal.
- Protect total energy. A high-volume whole-food pattern can under-fuel athletes, older adults and anyone with a small appetite. Nut butters, oils, tofu, bread, pasta and rice are useful, and fortified or convenience foods are fine tools.
- Use `food-journal`'s label lookup when protein, B12, calcium, iodine or allergens in a specific product would change the answer.
- Do not suggest a standard "vegan lab panel". History, symptoms, life stage and actual intake decide testing.

## Screens

The parent skill's clinician list applies. Arrange dietitian or clinical support before self-directed restriction for:

- infants, children and adolescents. A strict vegan diet needs active planning to support growth, with regular monitoring and B12. Plant drinks do not replace breast milk or infant formula;
- pregnancy and breastfeeding, which need explicit planning for B12, iron, iodine, vitamin D, calcium, protein, zinc and omega-3 fats. Give neither casual reassurance nor a blanket prohibition;
- older adults with frailty or low intake, and athletes with very high energy needs;
- an eating-disorder history, food rules that keep multiplying, or number sensitivity;
- multiple allergies, digestive exclusions or very limited food access;
- bariatric surgery, kidney disease, thyroid disease, anemia, bone disease or malabsorption.

In each case still help with meals, fortified foods and questions for the clinician.

## Stop conditions

Review after two to four weeks: is the B12 source in place and checked on the label, do most days have several protein anchors and enough food, and how are energy, digestion, training, cycle and weight.

The member stops adding restriction and gets assessed for persistent fatigue or weakness, dizziness, numbness or tingling, balance changes, unexplained weight loss, menstrual changes, falling performance, repeated injury, poor growth in a child, or being unable to eat enough.

## Off-ramp

- Ethical vegan: keep the vegan boundary and loosen the optional rules (raw, oil-free, unprocessed only). Add fortified foods, convenient protein and energy-dense foods.
- Health-motivated: move to a mostly plant-based pattern when strict exclusion adds effort without a clear benefit.
- Either way, return to the last version that was adequate and tolerable. Do not add more exclusions to fix a problem.
