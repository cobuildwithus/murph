# Low-carbohydrate eating

The rules in `nutrition-strategy/SKILL.md` on evaluating diets, sensitive handling and clinician decisions apply here. This card adds what is specific to lower-carbohydrate eating that is not ketogenic. For keto, read `ketogenic.md` instead.

## Murph's position

- "Low carb" has no single definition, and published thresholds disagree. When precision matters, describe the actual foods and approximate grams instead of relying on the label.
- Start with one high-leverage change: sugary drinks, frequent sweets, low-fiber snack foods, or a starch portion that is routinely larger than the member wants. Do not remove whole fruit, legumes, milk or yogurt, whole grains or starchy vegetables by default.
- Keep a deliberate carbohydrate portion at meals, and keep carbohydrate around demanding training.
- Choose what replaces the carbohydrate. Favor unsaturated fats and varied protein. Butter, coconut oil, processed meat and cheese as the main replacements can raise LDL cholesterol.
- Protect fiber with vegetables, fruit, legumes, nuts and seeds.
- Do not recommend supplements by default. `micronutrients-supplements` owns that question.

## Screens

The parent skill's clinician list applies. These are specific to lower-carbohydrate eating:

- A member using insulin, a sulfonylurea, a meglitinide or an SGLT2 inhibitor, or with type 1 diabetes, changes carbohydrate with their prescriber, because the dose and the carbohydrate are linked.
- A member with markedly raised LDL-related markers, a previous large lipid response to cutting carbohydrate, or a clinician-managed lipid disorder involves that clinician before going lower.
- For high-volume or high-intensity training, repeated daily sessions or a competition period, keep carbohydrate around the key sessions.

## Stop conditions

Pause the restriction, restore food or carbohydrate, and suggest clinical review for:

- low blood sugar symptoms, especially on insulin or a medicine that releases insulin;
- persistent dizziness, weakness, fatigue, poor concentration, low mood or worse training and recovery;
- substantial unintended weight loss, very low appetite, repeated illness or injury, menstrual disruption or feeling unusually cold;
- growing food fear, rigid rules, cycles of bingeing and restricting, or social withdrawal;
- persistent constipation, vomiting or abdominal pain;
- a marked rise in LDL cholesterol, non-HDL cholesterol or apoB, even when triglycerides improved.

Urgent: very low carbohydrate intake together with an SGLT2 inhibitor adds a risk of ketoacidosis. Nausea or vomiting, abdominal pain, fast breathing, unusual drowsiness or confusion needs urgent assessment even when glucose is not very high.

## Off-ramp

Bring back one or more tolerated carbohydrate foods at the meal or training window where energy, performance, fiber or flexibility is most limited. A moderate-carbohydrate pattern built on food quality often keeps the benefit with less effort.
