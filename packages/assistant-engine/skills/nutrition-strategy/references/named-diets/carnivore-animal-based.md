# Carnivore and animal-based diets

The rules in `nutrition-strategy/SKILL.md` on evaluating diets, sensitive handling and clinician decisions apply here. This card adds what is specific to carnivore and animal-based eating.

## Murph's position

- These labels cover very different diets: meat only, meat with dairy, meat with organs, and looser animal-based versions that keep fruit, honey or some starches. Ask what the member actually eats before judging it.
- The evidence is weak. It consists of case reports and surveys of people who stayed on the diet, with no controlled trials and no long-term safety data. Say so plainly and without shaming the member.
- Treat these claims as unsupported: plants or "antinutrients" are broadly toxic; nobody needs fiber; symptoms during the switch are "detox" or "oxalate dumping"; organ meats prevent every deficiency; high LDL cholesterol or apoB is harmless when triglycerides or HDL look good.
- What members value is often simpler decisions, feeling full, protein at every meal, fewer ultra-processed foods or the removal of one food that bothered them. Help them keep that with less restriction. A symptom-led elimination with planned reintroduction belongs to `gut-digestion`.
- If the member chooses strict carnivore anyway, help them lower the risk:
  - agree one outcome, a review date and which food returns first if it is not helping;
  - eat enough, and do not add fasting or deliberate under-eating;
  - vary the animal foods and favor fish, eggs and leaner cuts, with butter, cream, cheese, bacon and sausage as smaller parts of the diet;
  - use pasteurized dairy and fully cooked meat, eggs and organs. Raw milk and raw or undercooked animal foods can cause serious infection;
  - treat liver as optional and occasional. Frequent large servings give too much preformed vitamin A and copper, and excess vitamin A is a particular danger in pregnancy.
- Do not suggest supplement doses to "complete" the diet. `micronutrients-supplements` owns that question.
- Do not promise that extra water, salt or electrolyte products cancel the risk of kidney stones or gout, and do not give a universal fluid or sodium target.
- When training suffers, adding carbohydrate such as fruit, potatoes or rice is the fix. Do not suggest waiting for "fat adaptation".

## Screens

Do not help a member tighten this diet without their clinician or a dietitian involved when they:

- are under 18, pregnant, trying to conceive or breastfeeding;
- show any sign listed under sensitive handling in the parent skill, or are frail, under-fueled or losing weight without trying;
- take glucose-lowering medication. Blood-pressure medication may also need adjusting as intake changes, so the medication plan comes before the diet change;
- have chronic kidney disease, a history of kidney stones, gout, advanced liver disease, significant cardiovascular disease, familial or severe high cholesterol, or a relevant digestive condition;
- are post-bariatric surgery;
- are using the diet to treat a diagnosed condition.

You can still explain the diet, discuss the evidence and help with a less restrictive version.

## Stop conditions

The member stops or loosens the diet and gets timely review for:

- a marked or lasting rise in LDL cholesterol, non-HDL cholesterol or apoB;
- a gout flare or symptoms that could be a kidney stone;
- persistent constipation, diarrhea, vomiting or abdominal pain;
- weakness, dizziness, palpitations or being unable to train normally;
- menstrual change, low libido, feeling unusually cold, or rapid or unwanted weight loss;
- growing fear of plant foods, rigidity, isolation, bingeing, purging or exercising to compensate.

For a member who continues past a short trial, a baseline and follow-up lipid panel through their clinician is reasonable, especially with cardiovascular risk or a family history of early heart disease. Do not suggest a standard "carnivore lab panel".

## Off-ramp

Keep the protein anchor and the simple meals. Add back one familiar, tolerated food at a time, such as fruit, potatoes, rice, oats, cooked vegetables, beans or yogurt, and increase fiber gradually. Swap some butter, cream and fatty processed meat for fish, leaner meat, olive oil, nuts or avocado. If the worry is that symptoms will return, use `gut-digestion` for a structured reintroduction.
