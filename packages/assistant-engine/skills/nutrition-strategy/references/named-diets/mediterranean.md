# Mediterranean-style eating

The rules in `nutrition-strategy/SKILL.md` on evaluating diets, sensitive handling and clinician decisions apply here. This card adds what is specific to Mediterranean-style eating.

## Murph's position

- This is a flexible pattern: vegetables, fruit, legumes, whole grains, nuts and seeds, unsaturated fats in place of saturated fats, fish for those who eat it, and less processed meat, refined grain and sweets than the member eats now.
- Build it from the member's own staples, flavors and budget. It does not require Greek or Italian dishes, imported olive oil or fresh fish, and no food pattern is more "authentic" than another. Frozen and canned produce, dry or canned legumes, canned fish and any affordable unsaturated oil carry the pattern.
- The major trials supplied extra-virgin olive oil and nuts. Another unsaturated oil or seeds keep the same principle, without the same direct trial evidence.
- Wine is optional. Never advise a non-drinker to start drinking for this pattern or for heart health, and do not present alcohol as beneficial.
- "Green Mediterranean", MIND and branded score-based plans are different interventions. Do not carry Mediterranean trial results over to them.
- A "salad and fish" version can under-fuel. Keep grains, potatoes, bread, legumes and fruit, especially for a member who trains hard or has a small appetite.
- Oils, nuts and cheese are useful and energy dense. When the member's goal is weight change, `body-composition` owns the plan.
- Pastries, processed meats and salty convenience foods do not become protective because they are sold as Mediterranean.
- Nuts, sesame, fish, shellfish, dairy and gluten are never required. Use `food-journal`'s label lookup when an allergy or celiac disease makes the exact ingredients matter.

## Screens

The parent skill's clinician list applies, and there are no additional screens for this pattern. When a member plans to use it in place of prescribed care or to treat a disease, help with the food and say that the treatment decision stays with their clinician.

## Stop conditions

Adjust or pause when the pattern leads to eating too little, unintended weight loss, fatigue, declining training, persistent digestive symptoms, a suspected allergic reaction, or growing rigidity around food.

## Off-ramp

Drop the label and keep one or two parts that worked, such as a bean-based meal, more produce or an unsaturated-fat swap. Slow the fiber increase, or suggest another well-supported pattern that fits the member's food culture and budget better.
