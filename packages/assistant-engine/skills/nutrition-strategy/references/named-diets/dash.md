# DASH

The rules in `nutrition-strategy/SKILL.md` on evaluating diets, sensitive handling and clinician decisions apply here. This card adds what is specific to DASH (Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension). When blood-pressure readings, targets or risk are the main question, `cardiometabolic-health` owns it.

## Murph's position

- DASH is a flexible eating pattern for blood pressure. Present it as a set of priorities, with no fixed menu and no adherence score.
- Standard DASH uses a ceiling near 2,300 mg of sodium a day. The lower-sodium version uses about 1,500 mg and lowers blood pressure further, but it is harder to follow. Do not make 1,500 mg the default target, and do not turn either figure into a personal prescription. Sodium, potassium or fluid limits set by a clinician override both.
- Do not start with milligram tracking. Find the one or two foods that supply most of the sodium in the member's week (often bread, sauces, soups, deli or cured foods, cheese, packaged meals or restaurant food) and change those first.
- DASH raises potassium through food, which helps most adults. More potassium is not safer for everyone; see the screens.
- A dairy-free version works with fortified soy milk or yogurt, calcium-set tofu, beans and greens. Say that it is close to, and not the same as, the version tested in the trials. Lactose-free dairy is still dairy and does not suit a milk allergy.
- Add high-fiber foods one at a time over days to weeks.
- Do not let "low fat" become low energy or a diet of sweetened low-fat products. Frailty, low appetite, under-fueling or heavy training come before tighter sodium targets.

## Screens

- Do not encourage a potassium supplement or a potassium-chloride salt substitute without a clinician or pharmacist check when the member has kidney disease, a history of high potassium, significant heart disease, or takes a medicine that affects potassium. Those include ACE inhibitors, ARBs, spironolactone and similar drugs, potassium-sparing diuretics and prescribed potassium.
- Do not personalize sodium, potassium or fluid amounts for a member with kidney disease or dialysis, significant heart disease or a fluid limit, blood-pressure or diuretic medication, or a pregnancy. Their clinician sets those. Meal ideas and the food pattern are still appropriate.
- Do not push a generic low-sodium target on a member who trains hard in heat or loses a lot of sweat. Use the hydration guidance in the parent skill.
- The parent skill's clinician list covers the remaining groups.

## Stop conditions

- Pause the sodium or potassium change and seek prompt clinical guidance for repeated lightheadedness, fainting, unusual weakness, palpitations, confusion or vomiting, especially on blood-pressure or potassium-affecting medicine.
- When blood pressure is the outcome and the member monitors at home, use a validated upper-arm cuff, their clinician's schedule and thresholds, and averages across days. Do not use cuffless wearable estimates for diagnosis or medication decisions.

## Off-ramp

Keep the produce, legume and whole-grain gains and drop a numeric sodium target that has become a burden. Use standard sodium in place of the lower-sodium version. Suggest another heart-healthy pattern when it fits the member's culture, household or budget better. Hand off when blood pressure stays high or medication and kidney decisions become central.
