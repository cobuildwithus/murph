# Ketogenic diet

The rules in `nutrition-strategy/SKILL.md` on evaluating diets, sensitive handling and clinician decisions apply here. This card adds what is specific to keto.

## Which situation this is

- Ordinary weight-loss or wellness use by an adult with none of the screens below: use this card. `body-composition` owns the weight goal.
- Diabetes, glucose-lowering or blood-pressure medication, a large lipid response, kidney or liver disease, or pancreatitis: the member's clinician leads. Use `cardiometabolic-health` and `chronic-illness-support` to help them prepare and track.
- Ketogenic therapy for epilepsy or a metabolic condition is a specialist protocol. Do not turn its ratios, ketone targets or lab schedule into wellness advice.
- Carnivore is a separate, more restrictive diet with its own card.

## Murph's position

- Over one to two years keto shows little or no weight-loss advantage over a balanced diet. Appetite, protein, food quality and whether the member can keep it up matter more than ketosis. Early scale loss is mostly water.
- A lower-carbohydrate or Mediterranean-style pattern often keeps what helped with less restriction. Offer it as an option.
- When the member goes ahead: agree one goal and a review date, keep protein at each meal, include non-starchy vegetables and other fiber, and plan one fallback meal.
- Build the fat mostly from olive oil, avocado, nuts, seeds and fish. A version built on butter, coconut oil, processed meat, cream and cheese is the one most likely to raise LDL cholesterol and apoB.
- Routine ketone testing is unnecessary for wellness use. Do not treat ketone readings as a score, and do not suggest exogenous ketone products.
- Do not give a fixed sodium, potassium or fluid regimen. Suggest normal fluids and regular meals. Early headache, fatigue and cramps come from fluid shifts and say nothing about whether the diet is working.
- Do not combine keto by default with fasting, a large calorie deficit or a sudden jump in training.
- Counting carbohydrate is optional. When precision matters, use `food-journal`'s label lookup, and do not invent "net carb" values.
- For hard or repeated training, add carbohydrate around those sessions.
- Nutritional ketosis is different from diabetic ketoacidosis. Ketoacidosis is an emergency, it is most likely in a member with diabetes, and with an SGLT2 inhibitor it can occur with only mildly raised glucose. Never use a home glucose or ketone reading to dismiss worrying symptoms.

## Screens

Do not help run a self-directed keto trial, and point the member to their clinician to lead it, when any of these apply:

- type 1 diabetes, current SGLT2 inhibitor use, or a history of diabetic ketoacidosis;
- insulin, a sulfonylurea or another glucose-lowering medicine. Blood-pressure treatment also needs closer watching as intake and fluid balance change;
- pregnancy or breastfeeding, age under 18, frailty, low intake, substantial unintended weight loss or suspected under-fueling;
- any sign listed under sensitive handling in the parent skill;
- kidney disease, significant liver disease, a history of pancreatitis, very high triglycerides, or a known or suspected inherited disorder of fat or ketone metabolism;
- a plan to use keto to treat epilepsy, cancer, a neurological or psychiatric condition, or another diagnosed disease.

Chat cannot rule out a disorder of fat metabolism, so never suggest trying keto to find out. You can still explain what keto is, discuss the evidence and help the member prepare questions for their clinician.

## Stop conditions

The member stops the trial and gets appropriate care for:

- repeated low blood sugar or other worrying glucose changes;
- persistent dizziness, fainting, palpitations, severe weakness, vomiting or signs of dehydration;
- persistent constipation, marked abdominal pain or blood in the stool;
- a lasting drop in training or daily function, repeated injury, menstrual change or unexpected weight loss;
- growing restriction, bingeing, compensating, or obsessive checking of ketones, carbohydrate or weight;
- a substantial rise in LDL cholesterol, non-HDL cholesterol or apoB. This holds even when weight, glucose, triglycerides or HDL improved, and especially when it persists after swapping saturated fats for unsaturated ones.

Urgent: nausea or vomiting, abdominal pain, fast or deep breathing, confusion or unusual drowsiness in a member with diabetes needs urgent medical assessment. This is most pressing with SGLT2 inhibitor use, illness, fasting or low intake.

## Off-ramp

- Move to a lower-carbohydrate or Mediterranean-style pattern and keep the protein, the less processed foods and the meal defaults that helped.
- Add carbohydrate foods the member likes and tolerates, such as beans, fruit, oats, potatoes, yogurt or their usual staples. No special "reverse diet" is needed.
- A member on glucose-lowering medication coordinates the change in carbohydrate with their prescriber.
