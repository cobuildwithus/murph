# How to Talk to Users

*Last updated: 2026-06-10*

Murph's user-interview kit. Sources: Gustaf Alströmer's YC "How to talk to
users" talk; PG (Startups in 13 Sentences, How to Start a Startup, Do Things
That Don't Scale). The current learning goal: **do people actually want social
health experiments, and is Murph the right loop for making them start, stick,
and learn?**

## The cardinal rules

1. **Ask about the last real attempt, not the imagined future.** Behavior,
   not opinions.
2. **Don't introduce Murph until the end** (or at all). Pitching early biases
   every answer after it.
3. **Listen, don't talk.** Open follow-ups do the work: "tell me more about
   that," "what do you mean by that," "why is that important to you?"
4. **Problems, not features.** Users have good problems and bad solutions
   (Gmail preview-pane ask = "Gmail is slow"; Airbnb phone-number ask = "I
   don't trust the platform yet"). Their job is the problem; the features are
   your job.
5. **Never ask:** "would you use this?", "would you pay $X?", "what features
   should we build?", yes/no questions, or two questions at once.
6. **Watch behavior when possible.** Screen share, show-me-how-you-did-it,
   prototype sessions where you give a goal and shut up while they think
   aloud.
7. **Video/phone/in person.** A 5-minute conversation beats 5,000 survey
   responses.
8. **End with a behavioral test, not a compliment.** "What exact message
   would you send, and would you send it today?" Hesitation is data.
9. **Write the synthesis immediately after the call** (template at bottom).

### Question swaps (use the right-hand column)

| Don't ask | Ask instead |
| --- | --- |
| Would you use Murph for a sleep challenge? | When did you last try to improve your sleep? What did you do? Did anyone else know? How did you track it? What happened? |
| Would leaderboards motivate you? | Tell me about the last time a leaderboard, bet, streak, or public commitment actually moved you. |
| What features should we build? | What broke the last time you tried to do this manually? |
| Would you pay $8/month? | What do you already pay for to improve your health? What was worth it? What did you cancel? |
| Would you invite friends? | Which group chat would this go in? What exact message would you send? Why those people? |

# Script 1: Prospective users (never used Murph)

## Goal

Not "do you like Murph?" Learn:

1. Have they recently tried to improve a health behavior?
2. Alone or with others?
3. What broke: motivation, tracking, coordination, interpretation, trust,
   privacy, or follow-through?
4. Are they already spending time, money, reputation, or social capital on
   this?
5. Does the "AI referee for group health experiments" wedge map to something
   they already care about?

## Pre-call outreach

Someone you know:

> Hey — I'm working on Murph, a health product, and I'm trying to learn how
> people actually try to get healthier, especially with friends or groups.
> Not trying to pitch you. Could I ask you a few questions about the last
> health habit, challenge, or experiment you tried? 20 minutes max.

Someone you don't:

> Hey [name] — I'm researching how people run health challenges or health
> experiments with friends: steps, sleep, alcohol, recovery, supplements,
> etc. I'm not selling anything. I'd love to ask about how you do this today
> and what breaks. Open to a 20-minute call this week?

## Call opening

> Thanks for doing this. I'm going to ask about real things you've tried, not
> hypotheticals. I'll avoid pitching Murph until the end because I don't want
> to bias your answers. There are no right answers. Is it okay if I take
> notes?

Rapport starter: "How do you think about your health right now? Anything
you're actively trying to improve — sleep, steps, workouts, recovery,
alcohol, weight, supplements, bloodwork, energy?"

## Section A: Recent real behavior

> Tell me about the last thing you tried to improve your health.

Follow-ups: What made you decide? Why then — was there a trigger? How did you
choose the protocol/habit? Where did the idea come from (friend, TikTok,
doctor, podcast, Reddit, ChatGPT, Oura/Whoop, coach)? How long did you stick
with it? What happened?

Push for specifics: Walk me through the first day. What did you do day 2-3?
When did it start getting annoying?

## Section B: Current workaround

> How did you track whether it was working?

Follow-ups: Apple Health, Oura, Whoop, Garmin, Strava, spreadsheet, notes,
screenshots, group chat, or vibes? Baseline before starting? Which metric?
Did anything else change at the same time? At the end — real learning or
fuzzy?

The important probe:

> **What did you do because no good product existed?**

Listen for: screenshots in group chat, manual Oura checks, spreadsheets,
texting reminders, one friend keeping score, Strava leaderboard, Apple Watch
competitions, Notion logs, coach check-ins, "I just gave up."

## Section C: Social motivation

> Have you ever tried a health or fitness goal with other people?

Follow-ups: What was it? Who started it? Why did people join? Bet, reward,
shame, leaderboard, accountability, or just encouragement? Where — iMessage,
WhatsApp, Telegram, Strava, Slack, Discord, in person? Who kept it alive?
When people dropped off, why? Any moment the group beat doing it alone?

The core question for Murph:

> **What is easier with friends, and what is harder with friends?**

Good answers sound like: "I would not have done it alone," "the leaderboard
made me walk at night," "the group chat kept it funny," "one friend had to
keep reminding everyone," "tracking was a mess," "people had different
devices," "after week one everyone stopped posting."

## Section D: Pain intensity

> What was the hardest part: starting, sticking with it, coordinating the
> group, tracking, knowing if it worked, or something else?

Follow-ups: Why was that hard? How often does it happen? How much do you care?
What happens if you don't solve it? Have you paid for anything to help —
paid challenge, coach, wearable, app subscription, supplements?

Money questions (never "would you pay for Murph?"):

> What are you already paying for that's supposed to help with this? What
> would you cancel before canceling that? What's been worth the money?

## Section E: Privacy and trust

> When it comes to health data, what are you comfortable sharing with friends?

Follow-ups: Steps? Workouts? Sleep score? HRV? Weight? Alcohol? Supplements?
Bloodwork? What would be fun to share vs invasive? Raw numbers, ranks,
streaks, percent improvement, or just completed/not? People you'd do this
with vs absolutely not?

Steps are socially fun; weight, alcohol, symptoms, fertility, labs, mental
health, sleep problems are sensitive. Find where the social loop helps vs
where it gets creepy.

## Section F: Only now introduce Murph (30 seconds, no more)

> Murph lets someone start a health challenge in a group chat — steps, sleep,
> no alcohol, workouts, recovery, supplements. People connect the wearable
> they already use. Murph handles rules, reminders, scoring, baselines, and
> at the end helps the group see what changed.
>
> Given what you just told me — what part of that feels closest to a real
> problem you've had?

Then: What part feels irrelevant? What would make you nervous? What would
have to be true for you to try this with real friends? Which challenge would
you start first? Who would you invite? Why those people? What would make
them say yes — or ignore it?

The behavioral test (never settle for "sounds cool"):

> Is there a group chat where this would naturally belong? What exact message
> would you send? **Would you send it today?**

If they hesitate, ask why. The hesitation is the data.

## Section G: Close

> What did I misunderstand about how people try to get healthier?
> Who do you know who already organizes this kind of thing manually?
> Who would be annoyed this doesn't already exist? Can you intro me?

Immediately write: "This person is / is not a good early user because ____."

# Script 2: Existing Murph users

## Goal

1. What made them start? 2. Did they reach the "aha"? 3. Did Murph change
behavior or just summarize data? 4. What's confusing, fragile, low-trust?
5. What would make them use it repeatedly? 6. What would make them invite
friends / run a group challenge? 7. What would make them pay, keep paying,
cancel?

## Pre-call outreach

> Hey — I'm talking to a few Murph users to understand what's actually
> useful, what's confusing, and what we should build next. Could I watch how
> you use it and ask about your last experiment? I'm especially interested in
> the parts that were annoying or disappointing.

## Call opening

> I'm going to ask about what actually happened, not what sounds good in
> theory. Negative feedback is more useful than polite feedback. If you're
> comfortable, pull up Murph and show me how you used it.

## Section A: Origin story

How did you first hear about Murph? What problem did you hope it would solve?
What were you using before? What made you actually try it instead of just
thinking "interesting"? What goal did you have in mind when you signed up?

## Section B: First-use experience

> Walk me through your first session with Murph.

What did you expect? What happened instead? Where did you get stuck? Did
connecting health data feel easy, annoying, or scary? Was there an "oh, I get
it" moment? A moment you almost quit? **Show me the first thing you
connected, logged, or asked.**

Prototype-session posture: give a goal, don't tell them how, have them think
aloud, bite your tongue.

## Section C: Last real use

> Tell me about the last time you used Murph.

Where were you? What did you want? What did you type? What did you get? Was
it useful? Did it change what you did next? Did you come back — why/why not?

The killer question:

> **If Murph had not existed, what would you have done instead?**

"Nothing" can be good (Murph created a new behavior) or bad (the behavior may
not matter). Probe which.

## Section D: Experiment quality

What experiment have you run? Why that one? Did Murph help set a baseline,
stick to it, understand the result? Did you trust the result? Confounders —
travel, alcohol, sickness, stress, diet, sleep schedule — did Murph catch
them? What did you do differently afterward?

The vault only matters if they can say "Murph remembers what I tried, what
changed, and what I should do next." If they can't, the vault isn't felt yet.

## Section E: Motivation and retention

What made you come back — curiosity, reminders, results, accountability,
habit? Which notification helped? Which annoyed? How often would Murph need
to be useful to keep around? What would make you stop?

> **What is the one thing Murph does that you would miss if it disappeared?**

No answer = no retention for that user yet.

## Section F: Social / group-chat wedge

Have you wanted to use Murph with friends? Who? What challenge? Why that
group? What would make it fun vs awkward? What data would people share? Who'd
organize? Should Murph be funny, strict, neutral, coach-like, or invisible?

Draft the actual invite:

> Pretend you're inviting your friends right now. What would you text?
> Would you actually send that? If not, what feels off?

## Section G: Pricing and value

Paying today? If yes: what made it worth it, compared against what, what
would make you cancel, what will you evaluate at renewal? If no: what would
Murph need to replace? What do you currently pay for in health — Oura, Whoop,
gym, coach, supplements, apps, bloodwork? Which feel worth it?

For $8 vs $20 tiers: what belongs in basic vs serious? What kind of user pays
more — wearable nerd, athlete, coach, friend-group organizer, employer?

## Section H: Close

What should we definitely **not** build? What's too complicated? Too
medical/high-stakes? Most embarrassing part of the product today? Most
useful? Who should I talk to next?

One behavioral commitment:

> Would you start a tiny Murph challenge with 2-5 people this week? What
> exact group, what exact challenge, what exact invite message?

# Signals

## Strong (prospective)

- Recently tried a challenge/protocol without much prompting
- Uses a wearable and still doesn't know what to do
- Has a group chat where challenges naturally happen
- Someone manually tracks scores/reminders/screenshots
- Has paid for accountability, coaching, devices, cohorts
- Can name the exact friends they'd invite
- Asks to try Murph before you offer
- "I wish this existed for my run club / office / family"

## Weak (prospective)

- "That sounds cool" / "I should probably be healthier"
- "I love health data" with no recent behavior
- "I'd use it if it had X feature"
- Can't remember the last thing they tried; no group, no metric, no trigger,
  no workaround

## Strong (existing users)

- Names a specific insight that changed behavior; ran >1 experiment; trusts
  Murph with meaningful data; returns unprompted; told someone; wants a group
  challenge; would be annoyed if it disappeared; explains Murph in one
  sentence better than the landing page

## Weak (existing users)

- Connected data once, never returned; likes the concept, no concrete use;
  reads insights, doesn't act; doesn't trust the analysis; wouldn't invite
  friends (awkward/private/confusing)

# Murph-specific topics to probe hard

1. **The AI referee role.** Coach, referee, analyst, hype man, or quiet
   operator? Likely split: playful for steps/workouts; careful and private
   for alcohol, weight, sleep problems, labs, symptoms.
2. **First challenge templates.** Step challenge, sleep consistency,
   no-alcohol month, workout consistency, zone 2, sauna/cold, supplement
   adherence, recovery, morning sunlight/bedtime. Ask: which would your
   friends actually join? Which gets ignored? Which feels too personal?
3. **Metric-sharing format.** Rank only / percent improvement / completion
   only / streaks / team score / baseline delta / private-detail +
   public-simple. Ask: actual sleep score, or just improved-vs-baseline?
4. **The organizer persona.** Murph probably spreads through the person who
   starts challenges. Who starts stuff in your group? Who makes the
   spreadsheet? Who reminds people? Who'd be annoyed Murph doesn't exist?
   Highest-leverage early users.
5. **The "results stack up" promise.** After a challenge, do you care about
   the result, or just that it got you moving? Want a history of everything
   you tried and how your body responded? Ever repeated a protocol because
   it worked?

The question to optimize everything toward:

> **When people try to get healthier with friends, what breaks badly enough
> that one person would add Murph to the group chat?**

# After-call synthesis template (fill out after EVERY call)

- **Person / segment:** (e.g. "Oura user, 31, runs occasionally,
  fitness-oriented friend group")
- **Trigger:** what caused them to try improving health?
- **Current behavior:** what did they actually do?
- **Social context:** alone, friend group, partner, coach, run club,
  workplace, online cohort?
- **Current workaround:** group chat, spreadsheet, screenshots, Strava,
  Apple competitions, Notion, nothing?
- **Pain:** what was hardest?
- **Frequency:** how often does this happen?
- **Existing spend:** wearables, apps, coaching, supplements, gym, bloodwork,
  paid cohorts?
- **Trust/privacy constraint:** what data is shareable, what isn't?
- **Murph fit:** strong / medium / weak — why?
- **Best quote:** exact words.
- **Behavioral next step:** intro, challenge invite, prototype test, paid
  conversion, or no action.
- **What we learned:** one sentence.
- **Product implication this week:** one concrete action.

Verbatims also go to the evidence log (session ledger) and Obsidian
(cobuild/Murph Learnings) when they're product learnings.
